The Shape of the Thing

2026-03-12 Ethan Mollick AI capability, recursive self-improvement, RSI, co-intelligence, managing AI, StrongDM Software Factory, rolling disruption, autonomous agents
Read the source article

Quick summary

AI has crossed from "co-intelligence" (human-AI collaboration) to the threshold of "managing AIs" as autonomous agents—and recursive self-improvement is no longer science fiction but explicitly on major AI companies' roadmaps.

Key takeaways

  • Exponential capability gains across multiple benchmarks: from image generation to complex task completion, AI systems are reaching or exceeding human-level performance on sophisticated challenges.
  • Transition from co-intelligence to managing AIs: autonomous agents now handle multi-hour work tasks in minutes, shifting humans from operators to managers.
  • StrongDM's Software Factory experiment: "code must not be written by humans"—testing agents validate products without human intervention.
  • Rolling disruption: AI capability crossing critical thresholds creates unpredictable cascading effects—a single week in February saw stock market fluctuations, major layoffs, and government conflicts over AI governance.
  • Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is explicitly on major AI companies' roadmaps, no longer science fiction.
  • Uncertainty is not helplessness: organizations establishing norms for AI integration today set precedents for everyone else.

AI Was Supposed to Free My Time. It Consumed It.

March 9, 2026 Katie Parrott AI work intensification, variable-ratio reinforcement, FOBO, digital boundaries, epistemic rabbit holes, AI compulsion, burnout, OpenClaw
Read the source article

Quick summary

New research reveals AI doesn't reduce work — it amplifies it through task expansion, blurred boundaries, and psychological hooks that function like slot machines, keeping knowledge workers compulsively prompting long past the point of productivity.

Key takeaways

  • UC Berkeley researchers identified three ways AI intensifies work: task expansion (people absorb others' responsibilities), blurred boundaries (prompting during lunch/evenings feels like chatting), and multitasking (parallel AI threads create cognitive overload)
  • AI's variable-quality outputs create a "slot machine effect" — unpredictable rewards trigger dopamine in patterns that mirror casino gambling, making it hard to stop prompting
  • The "AI genie phenomenon" describes the irrational feeling that stopping means leaving wishes unfulfilled; "epistemic rabbit holes" trap users in cycles where each answer opens new questions
  • 54% of employees fear falling behind in AI use; 85% learn on their own time; this Fear of Becoming Obsolete (FOBO) and dopamine form a self-reinforcing cycle
  • The author spent 12 hours with her OpenClaw agent Margot on what was supposed to be a quick afternoon setup — a common experience among AI users
  • Countermeasures proposed include "AI practice" with intentional pauses, batched notifications, protected focus windows, and analog activities with natural stopping points

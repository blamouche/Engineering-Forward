Institutional AI vs Individual AI

2026-03-12 George Sivulka institutional AI, individual AI, Hebbia, organizational transformation, AI adoption, coordination, change management, productivity paradox
Read the source article

Quick summary

Individual AI makes workers 10x more productive but organizations haven't captured equivalent value—mirroring 1890s electrification where productivity gains required complete operational restructuring, not just installing new equipment.

Key takeaways

  • Historical parallel: 1890s textile mills saw no productivity gains from electrification until the 1920s, when they redesigned operations around the new technology rather than just replacing steam with electric motors.
  • Seven pillars of institutional intelligence: coordination (structured agent management), signal (filtering AI-generated noise), bias (challenging over-aligned models that reinforce existing beliefs), edge (domain-specific advantages), outcomes (revenue growth over cost reduction), enablement (change management), and unprompted (proactive AI risk/opportunity identification).
  • Organizations fail to capture AI value by distributing tools without redesigning processes.
  • Over-aligned models reinforce organizational biases rather than challenging them—a governance risk as institutions rely on AI for analysis.
  • Real institutional AI value is unprompted: identifying risks and opportunities before humans think to ask.

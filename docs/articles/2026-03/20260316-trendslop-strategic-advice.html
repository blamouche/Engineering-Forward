Researchers Asked LLMs for Strategic Advice. They Got "Trendslop" in Return.

2026-03-16 Angelo Romasanta, Llewellyn D.W. Thomas, Natalia Levina LLMs, strategy, trendslop, AI bias, strategic advice, prompt engineering, management consulting, GPT-5, Claude, decision-making
Read the source article

Quick summary

When researchers tested six leading LLMs on seven core strategic tensions, they found the models consistently recommended the same popular-sounding strategies regardless of company context — a phenomenon they call "trendslop" — revealing that AI-generated strategic advice is a statistical popularity contest dressed as analysis.

Key takeaways

  • Across thousands of simulations with GPT-5, Claude, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, and Mistral, LLMs uniformly favored differentiation, long-term thinking, augmentation, and collaboration — defensible choices that became meaningless because they were applied without regard to specific business context
  • The bias stems from training data: words like "collaboration" and "innovation" appear in overwhelmingly positive contexts, creating a probability landscape where models complete prompts toward popular vocabulary rather than contextually appropriate analysis
  • Prompt engineering barely helps: across 15,000 trials, adding company-specific context shifted biased responses by only 11% from baseline, and better prompting reduced bias by less than 2% on key tensions
  • When allowed to avoid binary choices, models frequently recommended pursuing contradictory strategies simultaneously (both differentiation and cost leadership) — what strategy scholars call being "stuck in the middle" — dressing up evasion as nuance
  • The safest approach is to treat LLM strategic recommendations as a first draft requiring interrogation: use them to generate options, surface risks, and test opposite positions, not as conclusions requiring execution

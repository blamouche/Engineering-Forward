The case for worrying about AI-specific cognitive debt

March 24, 2026 Nate Meyvis cognitive debt, AI coding, software quality, organizational behavior
Read the source article

Quick summary

Nate Meyvis outlines the strongest arguments for AI‑driven cognitive debt risk, even while remaining skeptical that those pessimistic views are ultimately correct.

Key takeaways

  • He frames three broad stances on AI and cognitive debt, personally leaning toward “no major difference.”
  • The post steel‑mans the pessimistic view that AI could worsen cognitive debt.
  • Risks include working at the edges of competence, weak mitigation incentives, and superficial coherence in LLM outputs.
  • Organizational behavior and psychology may favor debt accumulation over debt reduction.
  • The author ultimately remains unconvinced but argues for taking the risks seriously.

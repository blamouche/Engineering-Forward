Study: Firms Often Use Automation to Control Certain Workers' Wages

May 7, 2026 Peter Dizikes (MIT News), covering research by Daron Acemoglu (MIT) and Pascual Restrepo (Yale) automation, inequality, wages, productivity, labor-economics, AI-economics, Acemoglu
Read the source article

Quick summary

Nobel laureate Daron Acemoglu's latest research shows US firms have strategically used automation since 1980 to replace higher-earning workers rather than maximize productivity, accounting for 52% of income inequality growth while offsetting 60-90% of potential productivity gains.

Key takeaways

  • Automation is responsible for 52% of the growth in US income inequality from 1980 to 2016 — a larger contribution than many economists previously estimated
  • About 10 percentage points of that inequality growth comes specifically from firms targeting workers earning a "wage premium" — non-college-educated employees who had negotiated above-market pay
  • Within affected worker groups, the biggest automation impacts hit the 70th-95th salary percentile, showing higher earners bear disproportionate burden
  • Firms' incentive to reduce wage costs via automation has offset 60-90% of the productivity gains that automation could have produced
  • The key insight: "Greater profitability is not the same as increased productivity" — firms can reduce costs while reducing productivity and still increase profits

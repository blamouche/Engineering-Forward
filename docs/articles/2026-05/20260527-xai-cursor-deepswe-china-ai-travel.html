Quick summary

TLDR AI's May 27 digest covers xAI warning staff to limit contact with Cursor employees during acquisition, China expanding AI talent travel restrictions to private firms, the DeepSWE contamination-free coding agent benchmark, OpenRouter doubling to $1.3B valuation, and SpaceX's dual AI compute strategy with a $1.25B/month Anthropic deal.

Key takeaways

  • xAI's top lawyer warned employees to limit interactions with Cursor staff to what's necessary for their technical partnership — standard acquisition protocol but coming late as employees have been co-working for weeks; improper co-mingling could jeopardize the deal
  • China expanded overseas travel restrictions to top AI professionals at private firms (startup founders, researchers, executives) — previously restricted to state firm personnel, now unusual in extending to the private sector
  • DeepSWE is a new contamination-free benchmark for long-horizon software engineering: 91 repos in 5 languages, tasks no model has pre-seen, providing sharper separation metrics than SWE-Bench Pro's clustering
  • OpenRouter raised $113M Series B at $1.3B valuation (2x in a year), providing access to 400+ models and processing 100 trillion tokens monthly — reflects shift toward multi-model AI solutions
  • SpaceX's S-1 reveals two AI compute stories: terrestrial data centers with a $1.25B/month Anthropic deal through May 2029, and orbital AI inference via evolved connectivity satellites — both presented with conviction, neither contingent on the other
  • Anthropic detailed how they contain Claude across products: design for containment at the environment layer first, then steer behavior at the model layer, matching isolation strength to user oversight capacity
  • Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark: Claude Opus 4.7 leads at just 7.1% under "all-pass" standard, signaling legal work is far from saturated by frontier intelligence

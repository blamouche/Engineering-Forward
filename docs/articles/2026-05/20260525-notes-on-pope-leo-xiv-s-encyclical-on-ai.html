Notes on Pope Leo XIV's Encyclical on AI

2026-05-25 Simon Willison AI ethics, Pope Leo XIV, Magnifica Humanitas, Vatican, LLM interpretability, AI regulation, data commons, Catholic social teaching
Read the source article

Quick summary

Simon Willison annotates Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas*, highlighting its remarkably clear and accessible treatment of AI ethics — covering LLM interpretability, cultural bias, environmental impact, algorithmic accountability, and data as a common good — and notes his own accidental prediction that the Pope would weigh in on AI.

Key takeaways

  • The encyclical frames AI as "cultivated" rather than "built" — models grow within frameworks rather than being fully designed, making their internal processes fundamentally opaque.
  • It warns that AI's apparent objectivity masks the cultural assumptions of those who built it, and its simulated empathy can create dangerous illusions of genuine human relationship.
  • Environmental costs of AI are explicitly called out: enormous energy/water consumption and infrastructure demands that require sustainable solutions.
  • The document argues data should be treated as a common or shared good, not a private commodity — a radical framing within Catholic social teaching.
  • Clear human accountability must be traceable through every stage of AI deployment, from design to concrete decisions affecting people's lives.

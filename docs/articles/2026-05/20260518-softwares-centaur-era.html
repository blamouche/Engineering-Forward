Software's Centaur Era

May 18, 2026 Richard Marmorstein ai, software-engineering, coding-agents, centaur, automation, jobs, career
Read the source article

Quick summary

The software industry hasn't even entered its "centaur era" yet — the period between AI surpassing human capability and humans still adding value in collaboration — because coding agents still cannot reliably make independent progress on long-horizon projects, which is the economic core of why engineers are valuable.

Key takeaways

  • The "centaur era" concept comes from chess: after Deep Blue beat Kasparov in 1997, human+engine teams outperformed engines alone for decades — only recently have pure engines definitively surpassed centaurs
  • Coding agents today are not at "Deep Blue beats Kasparov" stage: left alone without human steering, they quickly go off the rails and break codebases
  • The ability to reliably make independent progress on long-horizon projects remains the absolute frontier of what models are beginning to become capable of
  • As long as human+agent teams outperform agent-only teams, there will be room for humans in software
  • Computer science enrollment is shrinking due to AI fears, despite the centaur era not having begun

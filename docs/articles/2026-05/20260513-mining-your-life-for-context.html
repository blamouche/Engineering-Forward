Mining Your Life for Context

2026-05-13 Laura Entis personal context mining, Claude Code as second brain, AI productivity, content workflows, Noah Brier, Chronicle, knowledge management
Read the source article

Quick summary

A practical guide to mining your scattered thoughts — Slack threads, voice memos, meeting transcripts — into coherent drafts by connecting AI agents to every source where your thinking lives, before you ever face the blank page.

Key takeaways

  • By the time you sit down to write, most of your ideas already exist across Slack, Notion, meeting transcripts, and voice memos — the workflow is about surfacing what's already been said
  • Noah Brier has been using Claude Code as a "second brain" for months, connecting it to thousands of personal notes for research, thinking, and writing partnership
  • Codex's Chronicle feature revealed Every's head of growth opens iMessage 671 times per day — leading to an AI-generated productivity regimen to reduce app-switching
  • A three-step workflow: capture everywhere by default, connect every source your agent can read, then name the deliverable and constrain the source to your own past thinking
  • Professional writers are increasingly using "aggressively casual" language (intentional typos, colloquialisms, run-on sentences) to distinguish human writing from AI-generated content

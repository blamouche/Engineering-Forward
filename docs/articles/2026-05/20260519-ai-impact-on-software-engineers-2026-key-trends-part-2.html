AI's impact on software engineers in 2026: key trends, Part 2

May 19, 2026 Gergely Orosz and Elin Nilsson AI tooling, software engineering, codebase quality, AI adoption, developer productivity, AI survey 2026
Read the source article

Quick summary

Analysis of 900+ survey responses reveals that while AI tools reduce tedious work and expand capabilities, they also introduce codebase quality degradation, unrealistic business expectations, and an emerging maintenance crisis where fewer engineers understand increasingly complex codebases.

Key takeaways

  • AI tools reduce time on repetitive work but create unrealistic business expectations and codebase quality issues, with management often prioritizing output over quality.
  • Adopting AI at company scale is hard: benefits depend on pre-existing engineering culture (guardrails, documentation, quality codebases); AI amplifies existing practices rather than fixing broken ones.
  • Codebase quality is decreasing due to AI-generated slop, insufficient code reviews, and more bugs — while maintenance burden falls on a shrinking pool of engineers who understand the code.
  • Less experienced engineers benefit less from AI and rack up higher token bills; they need more mentorship and space to learn, not just AI tools.
  • AI tool usage feels addictive — the "slot machine" effect and pricing models encourage constant prompting despite diminishing returns.
  • Code ownership is eroding and team collaboration is becoming less important as engineers can self-unblock, leading to tooling chaos and workflow fragmentation.
  • Some companies are rolling back AI tools to deal with quality deterioration and costly production incidents.

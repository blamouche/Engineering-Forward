Jury Dismisses All Claims in Elon Musk's Lawsuit Against OpenAI CEO Sam Altman

May 18, 2026 John Ruwitch Elon Musk, Sam Altman, OpenAI, lawsuit, jury verdict, statute of limitations, nonprofit, artificial intelligence, Microsoft
Read the source article

Quick summary

A California jury unanimously dismissed Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, ruling that Musk waited too long — beyond the statute of limitations — to file his 2024 case alleging breach of OpenAI's nonprofit mission.

Key takeaways

  • The nine-member advisory jury deliberated less than two hours before ruling Musk filed beyond the statute of limitations; Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers agreed and dismissed the case
  • The jury sidestepped the core question of whether Altman and co-founder Greg Brockman committed a "breach of charitable trust" by shifting OpenAI toward a for-profit model
  • Musk's claims against Microsoft — which invested $13 billion in OpenAI between 2019 and 2023 — were also dismissed
  • OpenAI's lead lawyer called the suit "a hypocritical attempt to sabotage a competitor"; Musk's attorney promised appeal: "This one is not over"
  • The verdict interrupted a remedies hearing; if Musk had won, OpenAI and Microsoft could have faced up to $150 billion in damages and Altman's removal

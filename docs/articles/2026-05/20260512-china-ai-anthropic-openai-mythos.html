China, AI, and the Battle for Mythos: How Anthropic's Most Powerful Model Became a Geopolitical Flashpoint

May 12, 2026 The New York Times Anthropic, Mythos, China, US-China AI competition, Project Glasswing, cybersecurity, zero-day, Singapore, Carnegie Endowment, Trump administration, AI regulation
Read the source article

Quick summary

A Chinese think tank's informal request for access to Anthropic's Claude Mythos model at a Singapore meeting triggered White House alarm, crystallizing the intensifying US-China AI arms race around a model whose zero-day discovery capabilities are classified as "digital weapon-grade" technology.

Key takeaways

  • At a closed-door Carnegie Endowment meeting in Singapore (April 2026), a Chinese think tank representative informally asked Anthropic to grant access to Mythos — the request was refused on the spot but triggered high alert within the US National Security Council.
  • Mythos, released under Project Glasswing in April 2026, autonomously discovered thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities across all major OS and browsers during testing, with some flaws dating back 27 years; access is limited to ~40 US/UK institutions.
  • China is explicitly excluded as an "adversarial nation," yet much of its critical infrastructure (banks, energy, government) runs on the same software where Mythos identified vulnerabilities — leaving China without access to defense capabilities it arguably needs.
  • The incident coincides with internal Trump administration debates on pre-release AI model security assessments (akin to FDA drug review) and Trump's scheduled visit to China this week.
  • China's cybersecurity market is projected to grow from 1.58 billion yuan in 2025 to 59.35 billion yuan by 2030 (37x growth), but domestic models remain "far from Mythos" in capability.

Study: Firms often use automation to control certain workers' wages

May 7, 2026 Peter Dizikes, MIT News automation, wages, inequality, productivity, Daron Acemoglu, labor economics, wage premium, rent dissipation
Read the source article

Quick summary

An MIT study by Nobel laureate Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo finds that since 1980, US firms have disproportionately used automation to eliminate workers earning a "wage premium"—higher-than-expected pay—rather than to maximize productivity, explaining 52% of income inequality growth while offsetting 60-90% of automation's potential productivity gains.

Key takeaways

  • Automation accounts for 52% of US income inequality growth from 1980-2016, with about 10 percentage points coming specifically from firms targeting workers who earned more than comparable peers.
  • The largest automation effects hit workers in the 70th-95th percentile of their salary range—not the lowest-paid or highest-paid workers.
  • Firms' focus on wage control rather than productivity optimization has "offset 60-90 percent of the productivity gains from automation," helping explain the long-standing productivity paradox.
  • The study analyzed 500 detailed demographic groups (by education, gender, age, ethnicity) across 49 industries, creating one of the most granular pictures of automation's labor market effects.
  • "You can reduce costs while reducing productivity"—the study reveals a basic but underappreciated distinction where greater profitability and increased productivity are not the same thing.

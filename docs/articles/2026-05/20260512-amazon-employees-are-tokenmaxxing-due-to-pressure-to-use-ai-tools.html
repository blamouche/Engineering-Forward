Amazon Employees Are "Tokenmaxxing" Due to Pressure to Use AI Tools

May 12, 2026 Rafe Rosner-Uddin, Financial Times (syndicated to Ars Technica) Amazon, MeshClaw, tokenmaxxing, AI adoption, perverse incentives, internal tools, agentic AI, AI metrics, OpenClaw
Read the source article

Quick summary

Amazon employees are gaming internal AI usage metrics by automating unnecessary tasks — a practice dubbed "tokenmaxxing" — after the company introduced targets requiring 80% of developers to use AI weekly and began tracking token consumption on leaderboards.

Key takeaways

  • Amazon deployed an internal AI agent tool called "MeshClaw" (inspired by OpenClaw) that can automate code deployments, triage emails, and interact with Slack
  • The company introduced targets for 80%+ of developers to use AI weekly and began tracking AI token consumption on internal leaderboards
  • Despite Amazon claiming stats wouldn't affect performance reviews, employees believe managers are monitoring the data, creating "perverse incentives"
  • Meta employees have reportedly engaged in similar "tokenmaxxing" behavior to improve leaderboard standing
  • Employees expressed security concerns about granting an AI tool permission to act on their behalf, with one saying "the default security posture terrifies me"

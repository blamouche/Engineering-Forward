The Pope Takes On AI

May 26, 2026 Alex Kantrowitz and Marty Swant Pope Leo XIV, Vatican, AI ethics, encyclical, AI regulation, Anthropic, disinformation, algorithmic addiction, AI harms
Read the source article

Quick summary

Pope Leo XIV released a 55-page encyclical on AI, calling out its potential harms — war, disinformation, surveillance, algorithmic addiction — and arguing for slower, more deliberate adoption, as the technology's public reputation continues to deteriorate.

Key takeaways

  • Pope Leo XIV released a 55-page encyclical titled "Magnifica Humanitas" that is ambitious, specific, and potentially uncomfortable for AI companies.
  • The pope's growing concern tracks AI's evolution from chatbots to autonomous agents capable of independent action — exactly the shift happening in the industry.
  • He called for AI to be "disarmed," likening it to a weapon of war, and argued that a pause in adoption isn't opposed to progress.
  • The pope explicitly stated AI cannot offer genuine human connection, love, and care — a direct challenge to AI companion and relationship products.
  • Some AI labs, including Anthropic, are reportedly already working with the Vatican, suggesting the encyclical is part of an ongoing dialogue rather than a purely adversarial stance.
  • The encyclical arrives as AI's public reputation craters, adding another critical voice alongside growing regulatory and public concern.

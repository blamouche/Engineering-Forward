Building OpenCode with Dax Raad

2026-05-27 The Pragmatic Engineer opencode, ai-coding-tools, developer-tooling, open-source, positioning, tech-debt, engineering-culture, taste, dax-raad
Read the source article

Quick summary

OpenCode co-founder Dax Raad shares 14 hard-won lessons on building one of the fastest-growing AI developer tools, arguing that taste and engineering judgment matter more than ever — and that AI makes coding easier but doesn't make the hard parts of the job disappear.

Key takeaways

  • AI makes coding easier, but the hard parts of the job (thinking about what to build) don't vanish — Dax still spends ~95% of his energy thinking and only ~4-5% doing
  • Thinking upfront beats building prototypes and seeing what sticks, especially before product-market fit — AI doesn't help much in the "what to build" phase
  • Shipping 10x more features is a recipe for a Frankenstein product; product positioning beats speed of execution — OpenCode won by claiming the open-source category nobody else had
  • Most software engineers profit from AI as time gained, not increased output — until incentive structures change, output stays flat while engineers go home earlier
  • AI code generation mutes the "guilt" of writing hacks, building up tech debt; but agents also make refactoring cheaper than ever, so teams should do more of it
  • Old "enterprise" patterns (domain-driven design, verbose design docs) are coming back because agents are the new junior engineers that need strong guardrails
  • The future-proof tech career: solid software engineering plus deep industry expertise — engineers undervalue how easily they can become domain insiders

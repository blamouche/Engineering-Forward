OpenAI is reportedly preparing legal action against Apple; it wouldn't be the first partner to feel burned

May 14, 2026 Connie Loizos OpenAI, Apple, legal action, partnership, ChatGPT, Siri, platform control, App Store
Read the source article

Quick summary

OpenAI is exploring legal action against Apple after its ChatGPT-Siri integration failed to deliver expected subscriber growth, highlighting Apple's long history of platform control that leaves partners — from Google Maps to Spotify — burned.

Key takeaways

  • OpenAI expected billions in subscription revenue from the Apple deal but claims the integration was "buried" with features hard to find
  • Apple has its own grievances, including privacy concerns over OpenAI and irritation over its hardware push led by ex-Apple execs like Jony Ive
  • The pattern of Apple burning partners is well-established: Google Maps (2012), Adobe Flash (2010), Spotify (2024 EU fine of €1.8B)
  • Google ironically became Apple's AI infrastructure partner in January 2026 with a ~$1B/year deal to power Apple Intelligence with Gemini
  • Any legal move would likely wait until OpenAI's ongoing trial with Elon Musk concludes

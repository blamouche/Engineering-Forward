Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence

2025-11-13 Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, Ruyu Chen AI employment effects, generative AI, labor market, early-career workers, automation vs augmentation, Stanford Digital Economy Lab
Read the source article

Quick summary

A landmark study using high-frequency payroll data finds that early-career workers (22-25) in AI-exposed occupations have experienced a 16% relative employment decline since widespread generative AI adoption, while experienced workers in the same fields remain stable.

Key takeaways

  • Early-career workers (ages 22-25) in the most AI-exposed occupations experienced a 16% relative decline in employment, even after controlling for firm-level shocks
  • Employment for experienced workers in the same AI-exposed occupations has remained stable or grown, suggesting AI disproportionately affects entry-level roles
  • Adjustments occur primarily through employment (fewer hires) rather than compensation (wage cuts), indicating firms are reducing demand for junior workers rather than paying them less
  • Employment declines are concentrated in occupations where AI automates rather than augments human labor — the automation/augmentation distinction has measurable labor market consequences
  • The findings are robust to alternative explanations including excluding tech firms and remote-work-amenable occupations

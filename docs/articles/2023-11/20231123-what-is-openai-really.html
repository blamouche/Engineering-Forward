What is OpenAI, Really?

2023-11-23 Gergely Orosz OpenAI, Sam Altman, corporate governance, nonprofit, ChatGPT, Microsoft, AI safety, board conflict, startup culture, engineering culture
Read the source article

Quick summary

The November 2023 OpenAI leadership crisis—where CEO Sam Altman was fired, nearly all 778 employees threatened to quit, and Altman was reinstated within five days—exposed the fundamental and unresolved tension between OpenAI's nonprofit governance structure and its for-profit commercial ambitions.

Key takeaways

  • OpenAI's board fired CEO Sam Altman on November 17, 2023, triggering a crisis that nearly dissolved the company as 95% of staff signed a letter threatening to join Microsoft
  • The board acted without notifying Microsoft, OpenAI's largest investor with $10B committed, and without the board chair Greg Brockman present
  • Chief Scientist Ilya Sutskever reportedly orchestrated the coup over concerns about the speed of commercialization and AI safety, particularly after the November 6 Dev Day event
  • Within five days, Altman was reinstated as CEO with a restructured board, but the underlying governance tension between nonprofit mission and for-profit execution remained unresolved
  • The crisis revealed OpenAI's unusual corporate structure: a nonprofit board controlling a capped-profit entity, creating a governance framework at odds with the company's commercial trajectory

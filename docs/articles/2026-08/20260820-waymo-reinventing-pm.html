To Invent Waymo, They Had to Reinvent PM

2026-08-20 Nikhyl Singhal / The Skip Podcast Waymo, product management, autonomous driving, evaluation, AI products, leadership, career development, Saswat Panigrahi
Read the source article

Quick summary

Waymo's chief product officer Saswat Panigrahi spent a decade on a problem you couldn't A/B test — and the product management practices the team invented to ship autonomous driving are becoming the playbook for anyone building AI products with year-long feedback loops.

Key takeaways

  • Waymo built evaluation as a product in its own right, with dedicated eval PMs whose entire job is to increase metric fidelity so other teams can hill-climb — a pattern now relevant to any team shipping AI products where "what good looks like" can't be fully specified in advance.
  • The mission was "semi-spiritual" (40,000 US road deaths/year) but the execution was brutally concrete checkpoints: "remove the test driver from just one car" unpacks into checkable weekly milestones.
  • Leadership on impossible problems strips down to three traits: competence (specialists should be surprised you know their field), courage (run toward the biggest problem without waiting for planning cycles), and compassion (kill bets publicly with heroes named).
  • Daily curiosity compounded is the real career moat: 1% per day compounds to 37x per year, and the difference between 20 years of experience and 1 year repeated 20 times is whether you pushed to failure every day.
  • Waymo staffed PMs for range — PhDs in photonics who ran businesses, NASA safety people colliding with internet-era product people — because no single person spans the whole problem.

Nvidia Paid $6B to NOT Own the Company: Jensen Knows His Limitations

2026-08-24 Harry and Anthony (CO/AI Signal/Noise) Nvidia, Poolside, AI deals, licensing, Broadcom, Anthropic, Google, Spirit Airlines, circle of competence
Read the source article

Quick summary

Nvidia paid $6 billion to license Poolside's model factory without acquiring the company, exemplifying a week where four major AI deals were defined by what the buyers refused to do rather than what they bought.

Key takeaways

  • Nvidia paid $6B to license Poolside's model factory, $1B for equity, and acquired 109 engineers—without buying the company
  • This is Nvidia's third such deal in eight months, after Groq and Enfabrica, consistently choosing capability access over ownership
  • Broadcom is parking up to $100B in off-balance-sheet financing through Apollo and Blackstone to put chips in Anthropic's hands—keeping the debt off its own books
  • Google bought bankrupt Spirit Airlines' data for $10M through bankruptcy court—hundreds of millions of emails and billions of flight-pricing records
  • Harvey built a state-of-the-art legal model by taking a Chinese open-weight base and post-training it on law, at a fraction of frontier lab costs
  • The pattern across all four deals: companies refusing to over-reach beyond their core competence, self-imposing discipline that regulators haven't required
  • Etched went from $10.3B to $21B valuation in 26 days with no new product—valuations at the picks-and-shovels layer are pricing compute queue access, not businesses
  • OpenAI paused some frontier RL training for alignment and shipped zero data retention for business accounts

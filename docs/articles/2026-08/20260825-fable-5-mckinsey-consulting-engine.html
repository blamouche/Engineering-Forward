Turn Claude Fable 5 Into a McKinsey-Level Consulting Engine

2026-08-25 Linas Beliūnas Claude Fable 5, McKinsey, consulting frameworks, Claude Skills, Minto Pyramid Principle, strategy
Read the source article

Quick summary

Twelve Claude Skills built on published consulting frameworks (Minto's Pyramid Principle, Playing to Win, Balanced Scorecard, CIA's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses) lift Claude Fable 5 from 38% to 97% on a partner-level checklist, turning a frontier model into a McKinsey-grade strategy consultant.

Key takeaways

  • Companies paid McKinsey, BCG, and Bain about $37 billion last year for advice, where most engagement work was done by associates with less than two years of experience—the client buys the method, not the person
  • The consulting method is not secret: Minto's Pyramid Principle, Lafley and Martin's Playing to Win, Kaplan and Norton's Balanced Scorecard, and Heuer's CIA handbook on competing hypotheses are all published and readable
  • The constraint was never access to the frameworks but the two years it takes to internalize them under partner supervision—and a frontier model can now run that method with more discipline than a second-year associate if the framework is encoded in a form the model can execute
  • Twelve Claude Skills were built on these published frameworks, written to one specification and verified against primary sources; in head-to-head tests, the skills lifted the model from 38-67% to 95-97% on a partner-level checklist
  • All twelve skills are free to download and install, enabling Claude to work through strategy questions with structured checkpoints, partner-level tests, and worked examples rather than answering like a well-read generalist

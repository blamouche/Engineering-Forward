When Artificial Intelligence Is Too Valuable To Sell

2026-07-31 Alex Kantrowitz ai-business-model, api, frontier-models, hoarding, commoditization
Read the source article

Quick summary

As the AI frontier gets crowded with five or more labs producing frontier models, the economics of selling intelligence by the token may flip — and the labs that built their businesses on API access may start hoarding their best models for their own products instead.

Key takeaways

  • The assumption that frontier AI labs will always license their best models via API is being challenged by market dynamics: a crowded frontier (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, DeepSeek) is driving down the marginal value of selling intelligence on a meter
  • OpenAI and Anthropic are already moving upmarket with products like Claude Code, Claude Design, and the ChatGPT superapp — suggesting they see more value in selling the product than the underlying model
  • A crowded frontier doesn't make AI a pure commodity because specialization and compute resources still give certain labs edges, but it does compress the premium that frontier models can charge
  • The "upmarket" move means AI labs will increasingly compete with their own API customers, a dynamic that could reshape the entire SaaS ecosystem
  • The strategic question for every AI-dependent business is whether the model they depend on will still be available via API in 12 months, or whether the lab will reserve it for their own vertical products

Headed for the Exit: The Great Engineering Leader Career Break

2026-08-18 Gergely Orosz engineering leadership, career breaks, CTO, VP Engineering, burnout, AI-native, founder mode
Read the source article

Quick summary

A wave of CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering are quitting high-status roles with nothing lined up — driven by unrealistic AI expectations from founders, shrinking team sizes, the appeal of fractional work, and the sense that ICs at AI startups now out-earn executives at non-AI companies.

Key takeaways

  • 6 out of 10 engineering leaders the author spoke to are either on a career break or seriously considering one — an unprecedented number in the author's ~20 years in the industry.
  • The #1 reason for quitting: unrealistic AI expectations from founders and CEOs, who expect the CTO to "magically transform the company to be AI-native" regardless of the company's actual context.
  • Smaller teams mean fewer leadership roles: AI-augmented engineering means the same output with fewer people, reducing the need for engineering managers and leaders.
  • AI startups pay ICs more than non-AI startups pay executives — senior engineers at AI companies command $400K–$800K+ packages while VPEs at non-AI startups may earn half that.
  • "Founder mode" (where founders bypass management layers) is here to stay, and it's making the CTO/VPE role feel like "low ROI" to founders who see themselves as the primary technical decision-makers.

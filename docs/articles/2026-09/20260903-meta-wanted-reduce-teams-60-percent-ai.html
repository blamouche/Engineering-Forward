The Pulse: Meta Wanted to Reduce Teams by 60% Because of AI

2026-09-03 Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer) Meta, Project OT, Organization Transformation, AI layoffs, Zuckerberg, Reuters, engineering culture, AI-native teams, Myspace, Anthropic
Read the source article

Quick summary

A Reuters investigation reveals Meta's leadership planned to slash team sizes by 60% through "Project Organization Transformation" — assuming AI would let smaller teams do the same work — before Zuckerberg blinked at the last minute, leaving the company with low morale and a mercenary culture.

Key takeaways

  • Meta's "Project OT" (Organization Transformation), hatched in January at Zuckerberg's Hawaii compound, envisioned 60% team reductions with 3-5 person "AI-native" teams replacing 10-20 person groups
  • HR projected layoffs bigger than the 2022-2023 cuts (25% of staff); the plan was for two waves — May and November 2026
  • On the night of May 19, Zuckerberg blinked: Meta laid off 10% but called off the November cuts after employees were "in open revolt"
  • Meta executives were inspired by visits to "AI-native" startups in Asia that built their org charts around AI
  • Downsides of tiny teams: lost domain knowledge, no redundancy, insufficient oncall capacity, zero slack time, stunted professional growth, and untested AI judgment
  • Zuckerberg's likely motivation: fear of being out-executed by a startup, as Facebook did to Myspace in 2008-2011
  • The canceled plan raises questions about AI-related regulation, Meta's culture becoming "mercenary," and whether great employees will stay

The Pragmatic Engineer: Five Years — How Software Engineering Is Changing

2026-09-01 Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer) Pragmatic Engineer, software engineering, newsletter, AI tools, IDEs, code reviews, deepdives, podcast, Pragmatic Summit, essay challenge
Read the source article

Quick summary

The Pragmatic Engineer celebrates five years with 1.1M readers, a podcast, and a summit — while observing that software engineering is changing faster than ever: IDEs are falling out of style, code reviews are becoming optional, and engineers are spending little to no time typing code for the first time since punch cards.

Key takeaways

  • The Pragmatic Engineer has grown from 1,000 paid subscribers in its first six weeks to 1.1M readers, tens of thousands of paid subscribers, a podcast, and 500K+ YouTube subscribers
  • The newsletter's evolution: 2021 product-market fit, 2022 The Pulse launched, 2023 front-door engineering deepdives, 2024 podcast launch, 2025 Pragmatic Summit, 2026 growing the team
  • AI tools are useful for research and summarization but the publication doesn't use AI for writing — "AI can easily lead you down the wrong track by theorizing about non-existent connections"
  • The biggest theme for 2026: IDEs are falling out of style, code reviews are becoming optional, and engineers spend little to no time typing code — unprecedented since punch cards
  • Fundamentals still matter: standout developers are even more in demand, and picking up AI engineering appears easier than learning a new programming language
  • An essay challenge with $10,000 in prizes asks readers to write about how software engineering is changing at their company
  • The Pragmatic Summit returns to San Francisco in February 2027, with a long-term goal of a second summit in Europe

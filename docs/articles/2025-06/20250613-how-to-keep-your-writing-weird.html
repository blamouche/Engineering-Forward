How to Keep Your Writing Weird in the Age of AI

2025-06-13 Katie Parrott AI writing, lectio difficilior, textual criticism, voice, style, content marketing, creative writing, AI homogenization
Read the source article

Quick summary

Katie Parrott draws on a 500-year-old principle of textual criticism — lectio difficilior potior (the harder reading is stronger) — to argue that AI is our era's "timid scribe" that smooths away the specific, weird edges that make writing memorable, and offers a framework for choosing when to preserve friction and when to let it go.

Key takeaways

  • The principle of lectio difficilior potior — used by biblical scholars to identify original texts by preferring the more difficult reading — maps directly onto AI's tendency to simplify and homogenize writing
  • AI consistently transforms specific, memorable language into generic alternatives: "altar-call energy" becomes "enthusiasm," "collecting screenshots like a doomsday prepper" becomes "documenting examples"
  • The "timid scribe" framing positions AI not as malicious but as a well-meaning assistant that, like medieval copyists, smooths away difficulty in the name of clarity
  • Parrott's counter-strategy involves loading AI with a personal style guide, writing samples, and explicit instructions about what to preserve versus what to polish
  • The decision framework: ask what reaction you want (friction for thought-provoking work, smoothness for frictionless communication) and consider context (brand content vs. personal newsletter vs. column)

Building a best-selling game with a tiny team – with Jonas Tyroller

April 8, 2026 Gergely Orosz game development, indie games, prototyping, Unity, software engineering practices, AI tools
Read the source article

Quick summary

Jonas Tyroller’s story is a good reminder that engineering process is context-dependent: for a two-person indie game, rapid prototyping, direct playtesting, and market-sensitive design can matter far more than textbook software practices.

Key takeaways

  • A two-person indie game can succeed by optimizing for fast learning, not process completeness.
  • Gameplay and commercial viability are tested through many tiny prototypes before polishing begins.
  • Traditional practices like code review and unit tests may be optional in small, high-trust, one-shot projects.
  • Pathfinding, balancing, and playtester feedback mattered more than architectural purity.
  • AI tools help with scaffolding and translation, but the hard work remains design judgment and iteration.

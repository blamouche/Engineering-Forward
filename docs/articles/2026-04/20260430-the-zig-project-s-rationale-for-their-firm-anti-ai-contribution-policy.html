The Zig project's rationale for their firm anti-AI contribution policy

April 30, 2026 Simon Willison open-source, llms, zig, governance, contributors
Read the source article

Quick summary

Simon Willison highlights Zig's strict anti-LLM contribution policy as a deliberate bet that open source projects should optimize for developing trusted contributors, not merely landing more code.

Key takeaways

  • Zig bans LLM use across issues, pull requests, and tracker comments, including AI-based translation.
  • The rationale is that review time should help cultivate reliable human contributors rather than validate machine-generated patches.
  • Loris Cro frames open source maintenance as 'betting on the contributor, not the first PR.'
  • The policy contrasts with companies such as Bun, which rely heavily on AI assistance and therefore do not expect easy upstreaming into Zig.
  • The debate reframes AI contribution policies around reviewer return on effort, not only code quality.

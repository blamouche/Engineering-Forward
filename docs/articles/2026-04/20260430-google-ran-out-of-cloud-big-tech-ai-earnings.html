Google Ran Out of Cloud: Big Tech's $130B Q1 AI Spending Spree

April 30, 2026 Grant Harvey (The Neuron Daily) cloud capacity, hyperscaler capex, Q1 2026 earnings, AI infrastructure, Google Cloud, Microsoft, AWS, Meta
Read the source article

Quick summary

Big Tech's unprecedented Q1 2026 earnings cluster revealed $130 billion in single-quarter AI capex, with Google's Sundar Pichai admitting the company literally ran out of cloud capacity — demand for AI infrastructure now exceeds what even the world's largest tech companies can build.

Key takeaways

  • Q1 2026 combined capex from Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Amazon totaled ~$130B — nearly 2x Q1 2025 — with Google raising 2026 guidance to $190B
  • Microsoft's AI business hit a $37B annual revenue run rate (+123% YoY), with 20M paid Copilot enterprise seats (Accenture alone: 740,000)
  • Google Cloud grew 63% to $20B, backlog doubled in one quarter to $462B, and Pichai said revenue would have been higher if the company could build fast enough to meet demand
  • AWS grew 28% (fastest in 15 quarters); Amazon's custom chip business (Trainium, Graviton, Nitro) passed a $20B run rate, with OpenAI committing 2 GW and Anthropic up to 5 GW of Trainium capacity
  • Meta raised 2026 capex to $125-145B; revenue grew 33% to $56.3B
  • Amazon's trailing-twelve-month free cash flow collapsed from $25.9B to $1.2B (-95%) due to infrastructure spending — a warning signal for Big Tech's capex trajectory
  • Claude Mythos Preview found 271 zero-day vulnerabilities in Firefox 150; Mayo Clinic AI detected pancreatic cancer 475 days before formal diagnosis

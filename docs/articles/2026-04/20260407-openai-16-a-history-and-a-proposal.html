OpenAI #16: A History and a Proposal

April 7, 2026 Zvi Mowshowitz OpenAI, Sam Altman, governance, policy, Anthropic, TBPN, AI politics
Read the source article

Quick summary

Zvi Mowshowitz surveys a week of OpenAI news by combining a critique of Sam Altman’s history and credibility, a rejection of OpenAI’s latest policy framing, and commentary on the company’s media and acquisition moves, arguing that governance and trust remain the core unresolved issue.

Key takeaways

  • The post treats the New Yorker’s long profile of Sam Altman as mostly confirmation that long-running concerns about his trustworthiness and governance style remain valid.
  • Zvi revisits episodes around the OpenAI board conflict, internal investigations, safety disputes, and relationships with Musk, Amodei, and Microsoft.
  • He argues that OpenAI’s public policy positioning should be read skeptically because the company’s incentives and history do not support easy trust.
  • The piece frames Anthropic’s Mythos cybersecurity coalition as the day’s most consequential actual news, even while the essay focuses on OpenAI.
  • The overall throughline is that AI power concentration cannot be separated from organizational character and institutional accountability.

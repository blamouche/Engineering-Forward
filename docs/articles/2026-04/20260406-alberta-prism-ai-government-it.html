They Said It Would Cost $54 Million. We Said "No Thanks."

April 6, 2026 Nate Glubish (Alberta Minister of Technology and Innovation) government-IT, AI-development, public-sector-innovation, cost-reduction, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, legacy-modernization
Read the source article

Quick summary

Alberta's government used a small team of public servants with AI development tools to replace two failing legacy systems for $2.64M instead of the $54M vendors quoted — a 95% cost reduction delivered faster.

Key takeaways

  • Three prior attempts since 2016 to replace two legacy government systems (asset tracking and construction project management) had all failed; vendor RFP responses quoted $54M for just one system over 4 years
  • The PRISM Initiative, launched June 2025, deployed both systems in under a year with $858K spent so far — 643 public servants now use them daily
  • AI's key role was accelerant, not replacement: Gemini processed 50+ hours of legacy system screen recordings into structured build specifications in minutes, at roughly one cent per image
  • A cultural shift emerged organically: non-technical government staff started using tools like Loveable to prototype feature ideas, and 1,700+ employees enrolled in the Alberta AI Academy
  • The model is explicitly replicable: domain expertise + modern AI tools + willingness to try, not a rare skill set

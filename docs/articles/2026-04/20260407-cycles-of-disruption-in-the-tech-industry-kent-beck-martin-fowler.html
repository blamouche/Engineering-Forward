Cycles of disruption in the tech industry: with software pioneers Kent Beck & Martin Fowler

April 7, 2026 Gergely Orosz Kent Beck, Martin Fowler, AI, software engineering, agile, disruption, developer workflows
Read the source article

Quick summary

Gergely Orosz summarizes a live discussion with Kent Beck and Martin Fowler, who compare the AI wave to earlier shifts like microprocessors, object-oriented programming, the internet, and agile, while arguing that today’s disruption is broader, faster, and more entangled with organizational incentives.

Key takeaways

  • Beck and Fowler see AI as larger and faster-moving than previous software shifts, even compared with agile and the internet.
  • They argue that skepticism is healthy, but must be paired with active experimentation because capabilities change too quickly for static conclusions.
  • Both expect a widening gap between developers and teams that learn to use AI well and those that do not.
  • They warn that hype cycles, “snake oil,” weak metrics, and managerial fantasies about eliminating programmers are reappearing.
  • They also suggest AI may reshape collaboration patterns, with tension between solo work with agents and more social team practices like pairing.

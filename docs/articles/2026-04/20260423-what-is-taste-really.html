What Is Taste, Really?

Unknown Jack Cheng taste, creativity, AI, judgment, culture
Read the source article

Quick summary

Jack Cheng argues that “taste” in the AI era is not one thing but a blend of personal preference, cultural legitimacy, and practiced discernment built by making things and explaining why they feel right.

Key takeaways

  • The article distinguishes between personal taste and socially recognized “good taste,” arguing that AI discussions often blur the two.
  • Personal taste acts as a filter that helps people navigate abundance and define what feels like “them.”
  • Good taste is culturally constructed and often tied to status, influence, and tastemaking power.
  • Discernment improves when people repeatedly make things and articulate why they prefer one choice over another.
  • AI tools can strengthen taste by forcing users to specify what they want and by moving them from passive consumption toward active creation.

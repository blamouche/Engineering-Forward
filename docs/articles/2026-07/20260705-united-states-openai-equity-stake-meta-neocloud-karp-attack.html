The United States' OpenAI Equity Stake, Meta The Neocloud, Karp's Attack

2026-07-02 Alex Kantrowitz OpenAI, equity stake, US government, Meta, neocloud, Palantir, AI industry, compute, regulation
Read the source article

Quick summary

Three major AI industry shifts converge: OpenAI may give the US government a 5% equity stake, Meta is becoming a neocloud selling excess compute, and Palantir's CEO is attacking frontier labs for competing with their own customers.

Key takeaways

  • OpenAI is considering giving a 5% equity stake to the US government to "clear political obstacles," with similar action expected from Anthropic, Google, Meta, and others—but this would create a fundamental conflict where the government is both a model approver and a vested investor
  • Meta is developing plans for a cloud infrastructure business selling AI computing power and models, similar to SpaceX's approach—significant because it signals excess compute at Meta while Anthropic and OpenAI can't get enough
  • Palantir CEO Alex Karp went after frontier AI labs in a CNBC interview, questioning data ownership, prompt security, and data caching—positioning Palantir as an alternative while some of the same criticisms could apply to Palantir itself
  • The neocloud trend reveals a sorting of AI industry winners: those who sell compute plus a proprietary frontier model (plus sometimes an application) at a markup vs. those who only sell compute and licensed models
  • OpenAI's Greg Brockman discussed the trajectory toward persistent agents that melt the interface away, and why compute will ultimately decide the AI race

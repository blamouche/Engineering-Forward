Tech Jobs Market in 2026, Part 3: Hiring Managers and Job Seekers

2026-07-07 Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer) tech hiring, job market, AI engineering, forward deployed engineers, hiring managers, fake candidates, referrals
Read the source article

Quick summary

The 2026 tech job market is defined by a paradox: hiring managers can't find experienced talent while experienced engineers can't get interviews, with AI-driven resume spam, fake candidates, and a bifurcated market where AI-adjacent roles are red-hot and everything else is painfully slow.

Key takeaways

  • **Catch-22**: Companies receive 800+ applications per role but can't find qualified candidates—AI-generated spam and fake applicants have made inbound hiring nearly useless for many companies
  • **No trust**: AI-enhanced resumes make every candidate look impressive on paper, but interviews reveal fundamental gaps; cover letters are effectively dead since they're always AI-generated
  • **AI engineering is on fire**: ML/AI/FDE roles see 2-3 recruiter messages daily, while non-AI engineers struggle to get callbacks—creating a "tale of two cities" market
  • **Referrals are everything**: Both hiring managers and job seekers report that the only reliable path to interviews is through personal networks; cold applications are largely a black hole
  • **Higher bar, lower comp**: Many companies have raised hiring bars while lowering compensation, except for AI engineering roles and AI-native businesses which still command premium offers

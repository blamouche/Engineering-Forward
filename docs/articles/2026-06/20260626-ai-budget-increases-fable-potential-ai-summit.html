AI Budget Increases, Fable's Potential, AI's Cyber Frontier: Takeaways From Big Technology's AI Summit

2026-06-26 Alex Kantrowitz (Big Technology) AI spending, Big Technology AI Summit, Fable, Anthropic, OpenAI, compute, AI policy, cybersecurity, Moore's Law
Read the source article

Quick summary

At the Big Technology AI Summit in San Francisco, leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, Box, and Corridor agreed that AI spending will only accelerate — even amid a "tokenmaxxing reckoning" — while revealing that Fable reduces hand-holding, compute is the core differentiator, and current US AI policy effectively fulfills the dream of the pause AI movement.

Key takeaways

  • AI spending is only going up: the consensus among summit speakers was that both infrastructure and services spending will continue accelerating, even amid the "tokenmaxxing reckoning" of rationalizing token consumption.
  • Moore's Law is broken for AI: Box CEO Aaron Levie noted that AI's increasing capabilities lead companies to throw it at harder, costlier problems — Box's token usage went from 5,000–20,000 tokens per task to 1–5 million tokens per task with latest agents.
  • Current US AI policy effectively fulfills the pause AI petition: Levie observed that the White House reviewing and approving each new model is effectively the regulatory regime the six-month pause petition sought — achieved through policy rather than legislation.
  • Frontier AI cybersecurity capabilities arrived last year: Alex Stamos argued the real breakthrough came with Opus 4 and GPT-5 series models, giving human engineers "superpowers" — Mythos is the best public model at finding bugs, but it's not unprecedented.
  • Fable gets things done with less back-and-forth: Anthropic Labs lead Mike Krieger said Fable requires less hand-holding than predecessors — he could hand off larger, open-ended tasks and step away, with Fable completing work and "hanging out for the next seven hours."
  • Compute may be the core differentiator: Greg Brockman emphasized that there won't be enough compute to satisfy all demand, with current agent users at 10–20 million — far from planet scale.

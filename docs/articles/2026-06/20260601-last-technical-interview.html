The Last Technical Interview

2026-05-29 Steve Yegge technical-interview, hiring, campfire-model, trial-days, Bar-Raisers, talent-assessment, AI-hiring, Silicon-Valley
Read the source article

Quick summary

Steve Yegge declares the death of the technical interview after 35 years of conducting them, arguing the process was always broken and is finally dying on its own — with the "campfire" model (paid trial days on real codebases with real teams) emerging as what replaces it.

Key takeaways

  • Technical interviews have been broken for decades — Amazon's Bar Raisers and Microsoft's As-Appropriate roles are tacit admissions that you can't trust interview teams to make good hiring decisions
  • The process survives despite producing tons of false positives (unqualified hires) and false negatives (qualified rejections) because it worked just barely well enough to resist overhaul
  • A new "campfire" model is emerging in Silicon Valley: companies hire candidates to work with them for a few days with real pay, on real codebases, with the real team
  • AI is accelerating the shift — candidates can use AI tools during trials, and the evaluation shifts from "can you write code" to "can you ship and collaborate"
  • The replacement is not a silver bullet; the transition will be difficult and messy

SpaceX's Valuation Is Crazy. Maybe That's A Feature?

June 12, 2026 Alex Kantrowitz SpaceX, IPO, AI infrastructure, valuation, capital expenditure, frontier models, price war, retail investors
Read the source article

Quick summary

SpaceX's record $1.77 trillion IPO valuation is disconnected from its current financials, but the willingness of investors to fund improbable bets may be the mechanism that allows both space exploration and the AI buildout to advance at their current pace.

Key takeaways

  • SpaceX IPO'd at $1.77 trillion and rose to $2.1 trillion at close, despite losing nearly $5 billion the prior year and still not turning a profit on rocket launches.
  • The company has been tossing money at AI infrastructure it does not fully know how to use, and has started selling its compute to rivals, building an unexpected AI cloud in the process.
  • Retail investors accounted for roughly 20% of the IPO — far higher than the typical 5-10% — and placed over $100 billion in orders.
  • A similar dynamic drives the AI buildout: some capital expenditures will be written down, but AI would not be advancing at its current rate without financial overexuberance.
  • The risk may be the point: giving companies room to pursue low-probability breakthroughs, even if they incinerate cash along the way, is how satellite internet and space data centers became real.

Token Tightening

2026-06-23 Laura Entis (Every) token economics, AI budget, compute allocation, ROI, enterprise AI, Opus 4.8, Fable
Read the source article

Quick summary

The era of measuring AI adoption by raw token consumption is ending—companies are starting to allocate compute budgets like trading portfolios, reserving the most powerful models for those who can prove the biggest returns.

Key takeaways

  • The "tokenmaxxing" era of measuring AI adoption by token volume is over; companies like Uber, Meta, Amazon, and Walmart have capped employee AI use
  • Mike Taylor predicts token budgets will be managed like trading portfolios, with risk limits, auditing, and approval requirements for large spends
  • Top engineers will receive multiples of their salary in token budgets, similar to how financial traders manage portfolios far exceeding their compensation
  • Opus 4.8 costs 1.7 times more than the previous model, driving up enterprise bills often without proportional results
  • Companies like 8x8 now require employees to prove older models can't do the job before granting access to frontier models
  • Kieran Klaassen spent $2,000 in Cursor credits in two days—a number that will look quaint as models improve
  • The emerging model: interns get Composer 2.5, most employees get Codex-equivalent models, and Fable-grade frontier models go only to an elite few

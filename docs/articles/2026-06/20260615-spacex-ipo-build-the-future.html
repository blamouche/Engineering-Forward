Elon Musk Sold Investors The Future. Now SpaceX Has To Build It.

2026-06-15 Alex Kantrowitz and Marty Swant (Big Technology) SpaceX, IPO, xAI, AI infrastructure, Elon Musk, Anthropic, neocloud, frontier AI, capital expenditure, market cap
Read the source article

Quick summary

Big Technology's analysis of SpaceX's historic IPO—the first AI company to go public—reveals how folding xAI into the offering exposed the staggering economics of frontier AI development and created a new disclosure benchmark for the industry.

Key takeaways

  • SpaceX hit a market cap above $2.4 trillion on its first full trading day, up 14%, placing it among the ten largest companies in the world
  • xAI's financials were revealed for the first time: $12.7B in capex in 2025, $7.7B in Q1 2026, and quarterly operating losses that rocketed from $936M (Q1 2025) to $2.47B (Q1 2026)—a new disclosure benchmark for AI lab economics
  • Anthropic agreed to pay SpaceX $1.25B/month for compute capacity through 2029, turning SpaceX into a neocloud—though either party can cancel with 90 days' notice
  • Musk retained 82% of voting power post-IPO, giving him unilateral control over board composition, shareholder votes, and major financial decisions
  • 20% of IPO shares went to retail investors (vs. the usual 5-7%), potentially making SpaceX more volatile and sentiment-driven

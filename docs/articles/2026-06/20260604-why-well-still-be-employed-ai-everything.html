Why We'll Still Be Employed When AI Can Do Everything

2026-06-04 Laura Entis (Every) ai-employment, automation, enterprise-ai, product-roadmaps, openclaw, microsoft, ai-agents, spiral
Read the source article

Quick summary

Every's Laura Entis argues that even after AI can outwork people at well-run companies, running it will cost so much energy and compute that hiring a person is often cheaper — while alongside, enterprise AI product roadmaps are proving nearly impossible to maintain as the news cycle outpaces any company's ability to plan.

Key takeaways

  • Even when AI can do everything a person does, the energy and compute costs of running it may make hiring a human cheaper for many tasks
  • Enterprise AI product roadmaps are extraordinarily difficult to maintain when the underlying tools change faster than conference planning cycles
  • Microsoft's trajectory with OpenClaw illustrates the roadmap problem: three months from tool launch to "ClawPilot" internal beta to Scout announcement at Build — and already behind the news cycle
  • OpenClaw search traffic spiked in January 2026 and declined quickly, showing how fast AI tool hype cycles compress
  • AI agents as a category aren't dead — traffic has moved to Hermes, Google's Gemini Spark, and Claude/Codex agentic features
  • The essay launches Spiral 4.0 with a new style engine achieving 87% on blind lineup tests, agent-native MCP access, team workspaces, and token-based pricing

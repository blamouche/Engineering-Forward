Why is Meta destroying its engineering organization?

2026-06-16 Gergely Orosz Meta, engineering culture, AI, data labeling, RLHF, layoffs, keystroke tracking, Llama, Scale AI
Read the source article

Quick summary

Meta's leadership has systematically dismantled its once-legendary engineering culture through invasive keystroke tracking, forced reassignment of thousands of engineers to data labeling, a 10% layoff announcement, and token-maxxing incentives—turning engineering from a profit center into a cost center.

Key takeaways

  • Meta is tracking every keystroke and mouse click of engineers with no opt-out, using the data to train AI models, raising serious privacy concerns
  • 30-50% of engineers on core teams have been forcefully reassigned to the ADO (Agent Data Optimisation) org for data labeling and RLHF work—around 4,000-5,000 software engineers, roughly 1 in every 5-6
  • Meta announced 10% layoffs with a month's lead time, creating a toxic waiting period; engineers began "tokenmaxxing" (generating excessive AI token usage) to game performance metrics
  • The most embarrassing outage in Meta's history occurred on May 30, attributed to understaffed infra/security teams and AI-generated code merged with AI-only reviews
  • Meta acquired Scale AI for $14.8B and brought in Alexandr Wang to lead AI strategy, but the approach of surveilling and reassigning engineers is driving top talent away

Silicon Control: The AI Semiconductor Supply Chain Thesis

2026-06-04 Kerman Kohli (Silicon Frontier) semiconductors, ai-hardware, supply-chain, silicon-monopoly, compute-infrastructure, tsmc, ai-investment, manufacturing
Read the source article

Quick summary

An 8,000-word essay arguing that the AI industry faces a decade-long supply constraint in semiconductors, with the future being a silicon monopoly of fewer than 20 companies that control the means of production for machine intelligence.

Key takeaways

  • The common narrative of AI infrastructure overbuild is wrong — the industry faces a decade-long journey of being supply constrained on semiconductors
  • Hardware is the real means of production in the AI race; machine labor is far more scalable and cost-effective than human labor, and demand for it is effectively infinite
  • The limiting factor is not demand but the hardware to support it — the few dozen companies with a monopoly on semiconductor manufacturing
  • GDP has historically been a function of population × productivity, but AI decouples from that maxim — productivity becomes independent of population size as machines substitute for labor
  • The semiconductor supply chain's complexity spans time, capital, and geopolitical constraints, making rapid capacity expansion nearly impossible
  • The future is a silicon monopoly of less than 20 companies controlling the AI compute infrastructure

Steve Yegge on AI Agents and the Future of Software Engineering

2026-02-10 Gergely Orosz Steve Yegge, AI agents, software engineering, workforce reduction, adoption levels, Dracula effect, organizational change, developer productivity
Read the source article

Quick summary

Steve Yegge argues AI's exponential trajectory will require large companies to eliminate ~50% of engineering headcount to afford AI tools and capture their productivity gains—while the Dracula Effect means even maximally productive AI users face physical exhaustion limits around three hours of peak work daily.

Key takeaways

  • No plateau: model release cycles have accelerated from four months to two months between iterations, with no signs of capability growth flattening.
  • The 50% dial: large companies may need to eliminate approximately half their engineering workforce to afford AI tools while maximizing productivity from remaining staff.
  • Eight levels of AI adoption: a spectrum from no AI usage to custom orchestrators managing multiple agents—engineers at lower levels face growing productivity gaps.
  • The Dracula Effect: intensive AI usage creates unexpected physical and mental fatigue; three productive hours per day at maximum AI intensity is a realistic expectation.
  • Big company bottleneck: large organizations cannot absorb the outputs of hyper-productive AI-augmented teams—innovation will increasingly emerge from small, agile teams.

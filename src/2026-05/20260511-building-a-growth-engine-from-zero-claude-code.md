# Building a Growth Engine from Zero: How One COO Boosted ARR 38% Using Claude Code
**Source**: https://www.bvp.com/atlas/building-a-growth-engine-from-zero-how-one-coo-boosted-arr-38-without-a-growth-team-using-claude-code
**Date**: May 11, 2026
**Author**: Bessemer Venture Partners (Atlas)
**Keywords**: Claude Code, growth engine, ARR, AI-native operations, ICP, paid acquisition, attribution

## Elevator pitch
Ascend's COO built a complete growth engine from scratch using Claude Code, boosting ARR 38% to $27.6M in six months with zero dedicated growth hires — demonstrating that AI can replace entire functional teams when deployed as an operational system rather than a productivity tool.

## Takeaways
- Ascend (formerly FlyFlat) grew from $20M to $27.6M ARR in six months, achieving ~5x ROAS with only $13K in Q1 ad spend
- The entire growth stack — ICP research, paid media on Meta and LinkedIn, three outbound channels, CRM automation — was built and operated through Claude Code with zero growth hires
- The key discovery from analyzing 4,582 bookings: 75% of revenue came from executive assistants at PE, VC, and hedge funds, not the "discount flight" customer the brand was targeting
- Claude Code was connected directly to HubSpot, Meta, and LinkedIn APIs, with reusable slash commands (/daily-ad-review, /weekly-growth-report, /new-campaign) making growth ops a continuous compounding process
- A 22-branch attribution rules engine achieved near-100% contact attribution, answering the critical question: "for every paying customer, which channel brought them in and what did it cost?"
- Meta (creative-led) and LinkedIn (identity-led) required fundamentally different campaign architectures — running the same playbook on both is one of the most common early-stage paid media mistakes
- The three-stage sequence (ICP → brand positioning → execution) only works in order; skipping to execution without ICP research is "building on sand"

## Synthesis
Bessemer Venture Partners profiles how Omar Ismail, COO of premium travel concierge Ascend, built a full growth engine from scratch using Claude Code as the sole operational backbone. When Ismail joined, Ascend had $20M ARR, 650+ clients, and a product people loved — but 95% of revenue came from word-of-mouth, with no paid acquisition, no outbound, and no programmatic growth motion of any kind. Six months later, ARR hit $27.6M, with paid channels returning approximately 5x on ad spend and projecting toward 8-10x as pipeline matures.

The method was notable for what it replaced: an entire growth team. The traditional approach would have required hiring specialists for ICP research, paid media, outbound, and CRM operations. Instead, Ismail ran Claude Code connected directly to HubSpot, Meta, and LinkedIn APIs, packaging every operational playbook into reusable slash commands so each session picked up where the last left off.

The work unfolded in three deliberate stages. First, a rigorous analysis of Ascend's 4,582 existing bookings revealed that roughly 75% of revenue came from executive assistants at private equity, venture capital, and hedge funds — a stark mismatch with the brand's "discount flight" positioning. Firecrawl enriched the top 500 customers into six targetable segments. Second, sales call transcripts run through Claude using the Jobs-to-Be-Done framework surfaced three distinct personas with fundamentally different motivations, enabling a rebrand built on actual customer language. A creative angle matrix mapped six psychological hooks across all segments. Third, the acquisition stack came online: paid media on Meta (creative-led, broad targeting) and LinkedIn (identity-led, precise targeting by job title and firm type), three parallel outbound channels (LinkedIn sequences, cold email, warm introductions), and a 22-branch attribution rules engine on HubSpot achieving near-100% contact attribution.

The results were striking: $42-45 cost per lead on Meta, 48.7% MQL-to-booked-call rate, zero growth hires. The biggest remaining challenge — roughly half of qualified leads dropping off before booking — is being addressed by calling high-value leads within five minutes of sign-up and moving to WhatsApp-native onboarding.

The case study is a concrete illustration of the AI-native operating model: a single strategic operator paired with an AI system that handles execution across functions that previously required an entire department. The lesson for founders is that AI doesn't just accelerate existing workflows — it enables entirely new organizational architectures where the make-or-buy decision for company functions is no longer binary.

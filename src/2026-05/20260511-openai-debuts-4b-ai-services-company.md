# OpenAI Debuts $4B AI Services Company As Rival Anthropic Builds Its Own
**Source**: https://www.crn.com/news/ai/2026/openai-launches-services-business-on-heels-of-similar-anthropic-announcement
**Date**: May 11, 2026
**Author**: CRN
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Anthropic, services business, Deployment Company, AI consulting, channel partners, enterprise AI

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI has launched a $4 billion-plus AI services company — the OpenAI Deployment Co. — hot on the heels of a similar $1.5 billion move by Anthropic, marking a new phase where frontier AI labs are building professional services arms to bridge the gap between model capability and enterprise deployment.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI's "DeployCo" is backed by $4B+ from 19 investment firms, consultancies, and system integrators including Capgemini, Bain, McKinsey, TPG, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and SoftBank
- The first acquisition is London-based AI consulting firm Tomoro, bringing 150 forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) with clients including Tesco, Virgin Atlantic, and Supercell
- The move mirrors Anthropic's recent $1.5B services company launch with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs
- Both companies are simultaneously investing in channel partner programs — OpenAI hired Colleen Kapase (ex-Google Cloud, Snowflake) and Anthropic launched its Claude Partner Network with $100M initial investment
- DeployCo engagements follow a structured methodology: AI diagnostics → priority workflow identification → design/build/test → production deployment connecting models to customer data, tools, and business processes
- A rivalry is emerging between traditional solution providers (who bring decades of enterprise process knowledge) and born-in-AI upstarts (who move faster and have deeper AI-native expertise)
- OpenAI reports more than 1 million businesses have adopted its products and APIs, while DeployCo's investor/consulting partners sponsor over 2,000 businesses globally

## Synthesis
Frontier AI labs are entering the professional services business at scale. OpenAI announced the OpenAI Deployment Company (DeployCo) with an initial investment of over $4 billion, backed by a consortium of 19 investment firms, consultancies, and system integrators. The announcement came just days after Anthropic revealed a similar $1.5 billion AI services venture backed by Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs. The message is clear: the bottleneck in enterprise AI is no longer model capability but organizational deployment.

DeployCo's structure reflects a hybrid model. It will acquire existing consulting firms — starting with London-based Tomoro, which brings 150 forward-deployed engineers and clients like Tesco and Virgin Atlantic — and embed these specialists directly into customer organizations. Its methodology follows a deliberate sequence: AI diagnostics to identify the highest-impact opportunities, selection of a small number of priority workflows, and then full-cycle design, build, test, and deployment of production systems that connect OpenAI models to customer data, tools, and business processes.

The strategic significance extends beyond services revenue. OpenAI and Anthropic are simultaneously building channel partner ecosystems — OpenAI hired channel veteran Colleen Kapase from Google Cloud, while Anthropic launched its Claude Partner Network with a $100 million initial investment. This dual investment in direct services and indirect channels suggests both companies recognize that AI deployment at scale requires a multi-layered go-to-market approach that neither product-led growth nor traditional enterprise sales can deliver alone.

A secondary dynamic is the emerging competition between traditional solution providers and born-in-AI upstarts. Traditional firms like CGI argue they bring irreplaceable enterprise trust, security, and decades of workflow knowledge. AI-native firms counter that they move faster and have deeper technical fluency with rapidly evolving models. CGI's Russell Goodenough captured the ambition of the traditional camp: "We want to be the first organization that's attempting to replace an ERP" with AI — not as a hackathon demo, but in a "trustworthy, dependable way." The market appears large enough to accommodate both approaches, but the entry of the model providers themselves into the services layer signals that the AI deployment market is being structured around a new kind of value chain where the line between product and services is increasingly blurred.

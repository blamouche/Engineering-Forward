# How I AI: Felix Rieseberg on Claude Cowork & Google I/O 2026 Recap
**Source**: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-i-ai-how-the-engineer-behind
**Date**: May 25, 2026
**Author**: Lenny Rachitsky
**Keywords**: Claude Cowork, Felix Rieseberg, Anthropic, Google I/O, Gemini, Antigravity, AI workflows, personal AI

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's Felix Rieseberg shares practical Claude Cowork workflows — using email as a personal database, building 3D floor planners and live dashboards, and hacking a $20 hardware buddy — while Lenny's Podcast recaps Google I/O 2026's wave of AI launches, most of which don't fully work yet.

## Takeaways
- Felix Rieseberg's core philosophy: "go one abstraction layer up" — whenever you catch yourself doing tedious work, ask Claude to do it, then ask Claude to figure out how to do it without your telling it.
- He used his email inbox as a personal inventory database: Claude parsed years of purchase receipts to inventory furniture, then built a 3D floor planner with actual dimensions.
- Live Artifacts refresh with real-time data from connected services (Spotify, Gmail, Calendar, Notion), enabling auto-updating personal dashboards.
- Felix's model heuristic: Sonnet for well-scoped problems; Opus when you need Claude to interpret what you actually want, not just what you said.
- Kids are described as the best AI users because they haven't internalized what computers "can't do" — adults have 20 years of learned helplessness to unlearn.
- Google I/O 2026 launched Gemini 3.5 Flash (claims 4x faster than rivals on coding), Antigravity 2.0 IDE (playing catch-up), Omni video generation, Flow video editor, and AI Studio Workspace integration — but half the features didn't work during live testing.

## Synthesis
Lenny's Podcast's "How I AI" series delivers two substantial segments: an interview with Felix Rieseberg, engineering lead for Claude Cowork and Claude Code Desktop at Anthropic, and a day-1 recap of Google I/O 2026.

Rieseberg's interview is a masterclass in practical AI adoption. His core pattern — "go one abstraction layer up, then do it again" — is a framework for progressively offloading cognitive work to AI. The concrete examples are compelling: parsing years of email receipts to inventory furniture for a move, then having Claude build a 3D floor planner with actual dimensions; creating live dashboards that auto-refresh from Gmail, Calendar, and Spotify; and building a $20 hardware "Claude buddy" using a Raspberry Pi. The through-line is that the biggest barrier to AI adoption isn't technical capability but psychological — people haven't built the muscle memory to reach for AI when facing annoying, non-creative tasks.

His model selection heuristic is pragmatic: Sonnet for well-scoped problems, Opus when you need Claude to interpret intent rather than literal instructions. He also emphasizes a collaborative debugging mindset: when Claude makes mistakes, the fix is usually better prompts or cleaner data sources, not blaming the model. His observation that kids are the best AI users — because they haven't learned what computers "can't do" — is a sharp insight about the learned helplessness that limits adult AI adoption.

The Google I/O recap offers a more mixed picture. Gemini 3.5 Flash claims impressive coding benchmarks at 4x speed, positioning it as Google's agentic coding model. Antigravity 2.0 brings Google's IDE to feature parity with Claude Code and Codex, including projects, scheduled Cron tasks, and subagents — but the features match what competitors shipped months ago. The `/grill-me` slash command is an attempt at personality differentiation, promising aggressive requirement interrogation versus Claude Code's polite clarification.

The pattern across the launches is familiar: promising concepts, uneven execution. Omni generates longer, more consistent videos than competitors. Flow offers production-grade conversational video editing. Stitch and Pomelli target design and marketing workflows. But half the features didn't work during live testing, Workspace integration in AI Studio failed entirely, and the gap between announcement and availability remains Google's Achilles' heel. Gemini's multimodal capabilities remain its strongest differentiator, particularly for workflows involving files, video, and cross-modal transformation.

# OpenAI is reportedly preparing legal action against Apple; it wouldn't be the first partner to feel burned
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/openai-is-reportedly-preparing-legal-action-against-apple-it-wouldnt-be-the-first-partner-to-feel-burned/
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Connie Loizos
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Apple, legal action, partnership, ChatGPT, Siri, platform control, App Store

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is exploring legal action against Apple after its ChatGPT-Siri integration failed to deliver expected subscriber growth, highlighting Apple's long history of platform control that leaves partners — from Google Maps to Spotify — burned.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI expected billions in subscription revenue from the Apple deal but claims the integration was "buried" with features hard to find
- Apple has its own grievances, including privacy concerns over OpenAI and irritation over its hardware push led by ex-Apple execs like Jony Ive
- The pattern of Apple burning partners is well-established: Google Maps (2012), Adobe Flash (2010), Spotify (2024 EU fine of €1.8B)
- Google ironically became Apple's AI infrastructure partner in January 2026 with a ~$1B/year deal to power Apple Intelligence with Gemini
- Any legal move would likely wait until OpenAI's ongoing trial with Elon Musk concludes

## Synthesis
The Apple-OpenAI partnership, announced with fanfare at WWDC 2024, has deteriorated to the point where OpenAI is reportedly engaging outside counsel to explore legal options including breach-of-contract notices. The core dispute centers on expectations vs. reality: OpenAI anticipated its ChatGPT integration with Siri would funnel billions in subscriptions through one of the world's most-used mobile platforms. Instead, it claims Apple buried the features, making them hard to discover and resulting in revenue nowhere near projections.

This tension fits a well-worn pattern. Apple's platform control — the iPhone as a fully controlled ecosystem where companies are "only guests" — has repeatedly led to partner fallout. Google Maps was the original iPhone's flagship feature until Apple replaced it with an inferior Apple Maps in 2012. Adobe Flash was famously blocked by Steve Jobs in 2010, effectively killing the technology on mobile. Spotify's years-long antitrust complaints resulted in a €1.8 billion EU fine against Apple in 2024.

The irony is that Google, once Apple's most bitter rival, is now its AI infrastructure partner — powering Apple Intelligence with Gemini models in a deal worth roughly $1 billion annually. Commercial interests can override platform grudges.

For OpenAI, the Apple dispute adds to a growing list of strained relationships. The company is concurrently fighting Elon Musk's lawsuit over its nonprofit mission, navigating tensions with Microsoft as it pushes for independence ahead of an IPO, and now potentially entering a legal fight with one of the world's most valuable companies. The broader lesson: betting distribution on a platform you don't control carries existential risk, even for companies with the leverage of an OpenAI.

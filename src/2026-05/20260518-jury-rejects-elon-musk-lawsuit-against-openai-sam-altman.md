# Jury Rejects Elon Musk's Lawsuit Against OpenAI and Its CEO Sam Altman
**Source**: https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/05/18/jury-rejects-elon-musk-lawsuit-against-openai-its-ceo-sam-altman/
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: Gerrit De Vynck
**Keywords**: Elon Musk, OpenAI, Sam Altman, lawsuit, jury verdict, statute of limitations, breach of charitable trust, federal court, Oakland

## Elevator pitch
A federal judge dismissed all of Elon Musk's claims against OpenAI after a jury found he exceeded the statute of limitations, with Musk attacking the judge online in response to the verdict.

## Takeaways
1. All of Musk's claims against OpenAI were dismissed after a federal jury determined they fell outside the statutory time limit for filing
2. The court did not reach the substantive merits of whether OpenAI and Altman breached any charitable trust obligations
3. Musk reacted by attacking the presiding judge on social media, signaling his intent to continue the legal fight through appeals
4. The case centered on Musk's allegation that OpenAI abandoned its founding nonprofit mission in favor of a for-profit structure
5. The verdict represents a significant legal victory for Altman and OpenAI at a time when both Musk and Altman's companies are racing toward major IPOs

## Synthesis

The Washington Post's coverage of the Musk v. OpenAI trial verdict offers a concise but pointed account of the May 18 ruling that dismissed all claims brought by Elon Musk against the artificial intelligence company he helped found. Federal Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers dismissed the case after a jury determined Musk exceeded the statute of limitations, bringing to a close — at least for the trial court level — a closely watched three-week legal battle.

Gerrit De Vynck's reporting emphasizes that the court never addressed the substantive question at the heart of the case: whether OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman violated a commitment to keep the company as a charitable nonprofit. Instead, the procedural issue of timing proved decisive. The jury found Musk filed his claims too late, and the judge adopted their advisory verdict.

The Post highlights Musk's combative response, noting that he attacked the judge in an online post reacting to the verdict. This detail underscores the personal acrimony that has characterized the feud between the two former collaborators, who were once close friends before becoming bitter rivals in the AI industry.

The legal conflict traces back to 2024, when Musk sued Altman and OpenAI alleging they had abandoned the organization's founding mission to develop AI "for the benefit of humanity" in favor of a for-profit structure that enriched executives. Musk, who contributed roughly $38 million to OpenAI's early efforts, sought to unwind the for-profit restructuring and force the return of what his team characterized as "ill-gotten gains."

The timing of the verdict is particularly consequential given the competitive dynamics between the two men. OpenAI, valued at over $850 billion after its March funding round, is preparing for an IPO. Musk's SpaceX, which absorbed his AI venture xAI in February, is valued at $1.25 trillion and is also moving toward a public offering. The legal battle and its outcome play out against the backdrop of an extraordinary rivalry between two of the most powerful figures in technology, each racing to define the future of artificial intelligence.

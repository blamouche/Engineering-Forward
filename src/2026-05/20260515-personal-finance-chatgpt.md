# A new personal finance experience in ChatGPT
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/personal-finance-chatgpt/
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: OpenAI
**Keywords**: ChatGPT, personal finance, GPT-5.5, Plaid, Intuit, AI financial advisor, privacy

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI launches a personal finance experience in ChatGPT for Pro users in the U.S., allowing users to securely connect financial accounts through Plaid for personalized spending insights, goal planning, and scenario analysis powered by GPT-5.5 Thinking.

## Takeaways
- The feature connects to over 12,000 financial institutions via Plaid (Intuit coming soon), enabling a dashboard of portfolio performance, spending, subscriptions, and upcoming payments
- GPT-5.5 Thinking scored 79/100 on a new internal benchmark developed with 50+ finance professionals; GPT-5.5 Pro scored 82.5/100
- Users control all data: accounts can be disconnected anytime (data deleted within 30 days), conversations respect model training settings, and "financial memories" can be viewed or deleted
- Beyond connected accounts, users can add context like mortgages or savings goals that ChatGPT remembers across sessions
- The vision extends to actions (not just answers): partnerships like Intuit could enable tax estimates, credit card applications, and scheduling live expert sessions directly inside ChatGPT

## Synthesis
On May 15, 2026, OpenAI announced a significant expansion of ChatGPT into personal finance, releasing a preview for Pro users in the United States. The feature represents a shift from ChatGPT as a conversational tool toward a persistent, context-aware financial assistant that integrates real account data with the model's reasoning capabilities.

The architecture is built around three layers: secure account connectivity (via Plaid with Intuit support planned), a real-time dashboard visualizing spending, investments, subscriptions, and cash flow, and the conversational layer powered by GPT-5.5 Thinking. The contrast between generic financial advice and data-grounded advice is striking — the article demonstrates how the same question ("help me save more") yields abstract budgeting tips without connected accounts versus a highly specific, category-by-category savings plan grounded in the user's actual transaction history.

OpenAI took notable steps on evaluation. They built an internal personal finance benchmark with over 50 finance professionals grading response quality on complex tasks. GPT-5.5 Thinking scored 79/100 and GPT-5.5 Pro scored 82.5/100 — providing a quantitative baseline for improvement. The privacy controls are comprehensive: ChatGPT cannot see full account numbers or make account changes, connected accounts can be disconnected at any time, data is deleted within 30 days, and a dedicated "financial memories" feature lets users manage what context persists across sessions.

The strategic ambition extends beyond Q&A. OpenAI is working with ecosystem partners like Intuit to enable actions within ChatGPT — going from a credit card recommendation to an application, or from a stock sale tax question to a live expert session. This represents a platform play, positioning ChatGPT as a financial hub rather than just another chatbot. The feature will roll out to Plus users after the Pro preview period, with the goal of eventually making it available to everyone.

For the AI industry, this launch is significant because it tackles one of the hardest domains for trust: financial data. OpenAI's approach — combining reasoning capability, real financial context, and user-controlled persistence — could set a template for how AI assistants handle sensitive personal data across other verticals.

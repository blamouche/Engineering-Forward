# Everything Google Announced at I/O 2026: Gemini, Search, Android XR, and More
**Source**: https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/google-io-2026-news/
**Date**: May 19, 2026
**Author**: Abner Li
**Keywords**: google, io-2026, gemini, android, search, ai, gemini-spark, antigravity, android-xr

## Elevator pitch
Google I/O 2026 delivered a sweeping set of AI-powered features across Gemini, Search, Workspace, YouTube, and Android, anchored by the Gemini 3.5 Flash model, a new proactive "Spark" agent, and intelligent eyewear running Android XR.

## Takeaways
- Gemini 3.5 Flash combines frontier intelligence with agentic capabilities, surpassing 3.1 Pro in benchmarks at Flash-tier speed
- Gemini Spark is a proactive personal agent that takes actions on users' behalf, integrating with Gmail, Docs, and MCP-enabled third-party tools
- Google shifted from daily prompt limits to a "compute-used" model, factoring prompt complexity and features
- Google AI Ultra dropped from $250 to $200/month, with a new $100/month tier introduced
- Android XR "intelligent eyewear" — audio glasses made with Samsung, Qualcomm, Gentle Monster, and Warby Parker — arriving this fall with iPhone support

## Synthesis
Google I/O 2026 marked a decisive pivot in Google's AI strategy: the company is no longer just showing what its models can do, but embedding AI into every layer of its ecosystem with a coherence that earlier I/O keynotes lacked. The centerpiece is Gemini 3.5 Flash, a model that skips the preview stage and goes directly to powering the Gemini app, Search AI Mode, Antigravity 2.0, and Gemini API simultaneously — reaching billions of users on day one.

The most significant product announcement is Gemini Spark, a proactive personal agent that represents Google's answer to Apple Intelligence and Microsoft Copilot's agentic ambitions. Spark integrates with Gmail, Docs, and other Workspace apps, with MCP-based third-party extensibility coming over the summer. It will be available to AI Ultra subscribers in the US next week. Complementing this is the Daily Brief feature, which sifts through Gmail, Calendar, and Tasks to provide a personalized digest.

Google Search received substantial upgrades: the Search box now expands for longer conversational queries, AI-powered query suggestions anticipate intent beyond autocomplete, and new "information agents" will work 24/7 to monitor the web for changes related to users' specific interests. A Universal Cart feature spans Gemini, YouTube, and Gmail, using Google Wallet to understand payment perks and loyalty programs.

On the hardware side, Android XR's "intelligent eyewear" represents Google's most concrete entry into wearable AI. The first audio glasses arrive this fall, made by Samsung and Qualcomm with externals designed by Gentle Monster and Warby Parker, and notably support both Android and iPhone pairing. Android Halo provides an "at-a-glance" agent progress indicator in the phone's status bar — a subtle but meaningful UX innovation for an agent-driven world.

The pricing restructure is equally telling: Google AI Ultra dropped from $250 to $200/month while a new $100 tier launched, and the company abandoned daily prompt limits in favor of a "compute-used" model that accounts for task complexity. This shift acknowledges that a video generation prompt consumes vastly different resources than a text query, and aligns subscription value with actual compute consumption rather than arbitrary daily caps.

# Musk Slams Altman Trial Verdict as a 'Technicality,' Vows to Appeal
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/18/musk-altman-openai-trial-verdict.html
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: Jeffrey Kopp, Lora Kolodny
**Keywords**: Elon Musk, Sam Altman, OpenAI, trial verdict, statute of limitations, breach of charitable trust, Microsoft, xAI, SpaceX, IPO

## Elevator pitch
A federal jury in Oakland ruled against Elon Musk in his lawsuit against OpenAI and Sam Altman, finding his claims were filed outside the three-year statute of limitations — a verdict Musk dismissed as a "calendar technicality" and immediately vowed to appeal.

## Takeaways
1. The jury deliberated for less than two hours before finding Musk's claims of "breach of charitable trust" were barred by the statute of limitations, without ruling on their substantive merits
2. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers immediately adopted the advisory verdict and expressed skepticism about Musk's appeal prospects, saying she was prepared to dismiss it "on the spot"
3. Musk sought up to $180 billion in "ill-gotten gains" from OpenAI and Microsoft, the removal of Altman and Brockman from leadership, and unwinding of the 2025 for-profit restructuring
4. OpenAI's lawyers portrayed the lawsuit as a competitive attack — Musk started rival xAI (now part of SpaceX) after failing to gain control of OpenAI, and his donations were unrestricted
5. The verdict lands at a critical juncture: OpenAI raised $122B at an $850B+ valuation in March, while SpaceX (merged with xAI, valued at $1.25T) is preparing its own IPO

## Synthesis

The three-week trial pitting Elon Musk against Sam Altman and OpenAI ended decisively on May 18, with a federal jury in Oakland, California finding that Musk waited too long to bring his claims. After less than two hours of deliberation, the advisory jury determined that Musk's allegations fell outside California's three-year statute of limitations. District Court Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers immediately adopted the verdict and dismissed the case.

The core of Musk's argument was that Altman and OpenAI president Greg Brockman "stole a charity" — abandoning OpenAI's founding nonprofit mission in pursuit of personal profit. Musk testified he contributed roughly $38 million to OpenAI on the understanding it would develop AI "for the benefit of humanity" rather than enrich individuals. His legal team sought extraordinary remedies: forcing OpenAI and Microsoft to disgorge up to $180 billion in "ill-gotten gains," removing Altman and Brockman from leadership, and unwinding the company's 2025 for-profit restructuring.

OpenAI's defense painted a very different picture. Their lawyers argued Musk's donations carried no restrictions, and that the for-profit restructuring was necessary to compete with Google DeepMind in an enormously expensive AI race. They presented evidence that Musk himself had pushed for a for-profit structure — on the condition that he retain control — and at one point even advocated folding OpenAI into Tesla. The lawsuit, OpenAI's attorneys contended, was a competitive attack by a rival who had failed to gain control of the company and subsequently founded xAI.

Musk responded to the verdict on X, calling it a "calendar technicality" and insisting the only question was "WHEN" Altman and Brockman enriched themselves, not whether they did. His lawyer, Steven Molo, reserved the right to appeal to the 9th Circuit. But Judge Gonzalez Rogers appeared unpersuaded, noting "substantial evidence to support the jury's finding" and signaling she would dismiss any appeal "on the spot." OpenAI's lead attorney William Savitt was more pointed: "It's not a technical decision, it's a substantive one. It says: You brought your claims too late, and you did it because you were sitting on them to use them as a weapon of a competitor who can't compete in the marketplace."

The timing amplifies the verdict's significance. OpenAI raised $122 billion in March at a valuation exceeding $850 billion. SpaceX, which absorbed xAI in February and is valued at $1.25 trillion, confidentially filed for an IPO in April and could make its prospectus public soon. Both men are racing toward what are expected to be record-setting public offerings, making this courtroom drama not just a personal feud but a battle with enormous financial stakes.

# Google's Android-Powered Laptops Are Called Googlebooks, and They're Coming This Year
**Source**: https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/05/googles-android-powered-laptops-are-called-googlebooks-and-theyre-coming-this-year
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Ryan Whitwam
**Keywords**: Googlebooks, Google, Android laptops, Gemini Intelligence, Magic Pointer, Chromebooks, AI laptops, Glowbar

## Elevator pitch
Google is launching "Googlebooks" — Android-powered laptops designed from the ground up around Gemini Intelligence, featuring a "Magic Pointer" cursor that activates full-screen AI, phone app streaming, and a distinctive illuminated "Glowbar" — shipping later this year from Acer, Asus, Dell, HP, and Lenovo.

## Takeaways
- Googlebooks are Android-based laptops distinct from Chromebooks (which Google says aren't going away), with full Play Store access and native Android app support
- The "Magic Pointer" — activated by wiggling the cursor — launches a full-screen Gemini experience that can see screen content and make contextual suggestions across apps
- Googlebooks can stream apps directly from a paired Android phone into floating windows, with seamless file transfers between devices
- AI-generated widgets from Android phones will adapt to the laptop form factor as "personalized dashboards"
- The hardware features a "Glowbar" illuminated strip on the lid reminiscent of the Pixel C and Chromebook Pixel, described as both "functional and beautiful"

## Synthesis
Google is making its most ambitious play yet for the laptop market with Googlebooks, a new category of Android-powered laptops that the company will launch through major OEMs later this year. Ars Technica's Ryan Whitwam reports that while Google insists Chromebooks aren't going away, the company's strategic focus has decisively shifted to this new form factor — one built from the silicon up around Gemini Intelligence.

The defining interaction model of Googlebooks is the "Magic Pointer." By simply wiggling the cursor back and forth, users activate a full-screen Gemini experience that can see everything on the display and make contextual suggestions, pulling in data from multiple apps. Google's demos show the Magic Pointer being used to select multiple images and combine them with Nano Banana (Google's AI image editing model), or to create a calendar appointment by pointing at a date in an email. The companion Magic Cue feature, which debuted on Pixel phones, will surface recommended actions based on context from messages and emails.

Whitwam is appropriately skeptical about the utility of these AI integrations. He draws a direct comparison to Microsoft's Recall — a screen-context AI feature that became one of the most heavily criticized product launches in recent memory — and notes that Google's own Magic Cue on phones "hasn't been a game changer — in fact, it rarely shows up at all." The fundamental problem of AI feature discoverability remains unsolved, and it's unclear whether a laptop form factor will make context-aware AI more useful or merely more visible.

The Googlebook's relationship with Android phones is a key differentiator. Rather than requiring users to install all their apps on the laptop, Googlebooks can stream apps directly from a paired Android phone — a dedicated taskbar button lists all phone apps, and clicking one opens it in a floating window on the laptop. File transfers between devices happen seamlessly. This phone-as-app-server model could reduce the friction of managing two separate app ecosystems, which has been a persistent pain point for tablet and laptop users.

The software situation beyond Google's own services remains foggy. Googlebooks run Android and have the Play Store, but Google has been simultaneously certifying third-party app stores while clamping down on sideloaded APKs, and the company declined to clarify where Googlebooks will land on the openness spectrum. The app ecosystem partner strategy will be detailed closer to launch.

The hardware design language introduces the "Glowbar" — an illuminated strip on the lid that evokes Google's design heritage from the Pixel C tablet and Chromebook Pixel. On those earlier devices, the light bar indicated battery level; Google describes the Glowbar as both "functional and beautiful" but hasn't explained its specific functionality. The devices will ship from Acer, Asus, Dell, HP, and Lenovo, positioning Googlebooks as a broad ecosystem play rather than a single first-party device like the Pixel phone line. Whether Googlebooks can succeed where Android tablets have historically struggled — providing a genuinely compelling large-screen experience rather than just stretched phone apps — will depend heavily on how well the Gemini Intelligence layer delivers on its contextual computing promise.

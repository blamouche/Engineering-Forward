# OpenAI Barrels Toward IPO That May Happen in September
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/openai-barrels-toward-ipo-that-may-happen-in-september/
**Date**: 2026-05-20
**Author**: Julie Bort
**Keywords**: OpenAI, IPO, Sam Altman, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Elon Musk, SpaceX

## Elevator pitch
Fresh off Elon Musk's courtroom loss, OpenAI is accelerating toward a September IPO with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, setting up a high-stakes financial showdown with Musk's SpaceX, which is also racing toward its own blockbuster public offering.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI may file IPO paperwork confidentially within days or weeks, targeting a September public debut
- The company is working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, the dominant tech IPO bankers
- The timing is strategic: Musk just lost his lawsuit that threatened OpenAI's corporate structure and finances
- SpaceX is simultaneously preparing its own IPO, setting up a direct Altman-vs-Musk battle on public markets
- Both companies are positioning for what could be the most consequential tech IPOs since Facebook/Alibaba

## Synthesis
The AI industry is entering its IPO era, and the two most prominent players are on a collision course. OpenAI, emboldened by Elon Musk's failed lawsuit that sought to derail its for-profit restructuring, is reportedly moving quickly toward a public offering that CEO Sam Altman hopes to execute by September 2026. The company has enlisted Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley — the dominant forces in tech IPOs — and may file confidential paperwork with the SEC within days or weeks.

The timing is anything but coincidental. Musk's SpaceX simultaneously filed its own S-1, revealing $18.7 billion in revenue and the full scope of its Anthropic compute deal. With SpaceX having absorbed xAI earlier in 2026, the two companies are now direct competitors across the AI value chain: models, infrastructure, and enterprise services. The IPO race adds a financial dimension to what was already a personal and philosophical rivalry between Altman and Musk.

The WSJ report notes that OpenAI's IPO should be a "blockbuster" by any measure, though the company's complex structure — transitioning from a capped-profit entity controlled by a nonprofit board — adds unique governance questions that public market investors will need to digest. The contrast with SpaceX is instructive: Musk's company is a more traditional (if sprawling) business with visible revenue streams from launches, Starlink, and now compute leasing, while OpenAI's value proposition rests more heavily on future AI dominance.

For the broader tech ecosystem, dual IPOs from the defining AI companies would represent a market test of whether public investors will assign premium valuations to AI businesses at current scale. The question of which IPO proves larger — and which company's stock performs better in the first year — will be watched as a referendum on competing AI strategies: vertical integration (SpaceX/xAI) versus model-and-platform focus (OpenAI).

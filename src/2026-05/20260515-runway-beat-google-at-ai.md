# Runway started by helping filmmakers — now it wants to beat Google at AI

**Source:** TechCrunch
**Author:** Rebecca Bellan
**Date:** May 15, 2026
**URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/runway-started-by-helping-filmmakers-now-it-wants-to-beat-google-at-ai

## Summary

Runway, the AI video-generation startup valued at $5.3 billion, is betting that video generation — not language models — is the path to world models and the next frontier of AI. Co-founder and co-CEO Anastasis Germanidis argues that training directly on observational data from the world will produce AI that understands how reality works, not just how humans describe it. The company has expanded beyond filmmaking tools into world models, robotics, and aims toward biological world models and anti-aging research.

## Key Points

- **From film to world models:** Runway started with a mission to make everyone a filmmaker, but its video-generation models began to understand how the world works, leading to world model ambitions
- **The bet against language:** Every major AI lab is betting on language. Runway believes video and world models that learn from sensory data will surpass text-based intelligence
- **$40M in ARR:** Runway added $40 million in annual recurring revenue in Q2 2026
- **Robotics unit:** Already deployed in real-world testing
- **Biological world models:** Germanidis's ultimate moonshot goal is anti-aging research
- **Funding:** $860M raised to date (including $315M in Feb 2026 from AMD Ventures and Nvidia) — roughly in line with competitors Luma ($900M) and World Labs ($1.29B)
- **Competition:** Google (Genie), Yann LeCun's AMI Labs, Fei-Fei Li's World Labs, OpenAI ($175B raised) — all deep-pocketed competitors
- **Team:** 155 employees across NYC, London, SF, Seattle, and Tokyo
- **Key question:** Does Runway have dedicated cluster access for training frontier models? Unconfirmed

## Why It Matters

Runway represents a contrarian bet in AI: that the path to general intelligence doesn't go through text but through video and sensory observation. If world models truly surpass language models, the competitive landscape of AI flips entirely — and a $5.3B New York startup with film-school founders could challenge Google.

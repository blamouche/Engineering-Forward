# Salesforce Moved 3,000 Employees into Sales Roles as Agentic AI Reshapes Operations
**Source**: https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/information-technology/salesforce-moved-3-000-employees-into-sales-roles-as-agentic-ai-reshapes-operations-vala-afshar-13922844.html
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: PTI (via multiple outlets)
**Keywords**: Salesforce, agentic AI, workforce transformation, reskilling, digital labor, Vala Afshar, AI augmentation, India

## Elevator pitch
Salesforce reskilled and redeployed 3,000 employees from other business lines into sales roles as AI agents took over their previous repetitive tasks — growing the sales function without external hiring — while its Chief Digital Evangelist insists the company is "not building AI to displace humans."

## Takeaways
- Vala Afshar, Salesforce's Chief Digital Evangelist, confirmed 3,000 employees were moved into sales from other business lines after AI agents absorbed their previous repetitive, low-impact work
- Afshar framed the strategy as augmentation, not displacement: "We're building a workforce of AIs... to augment and create boundless potential in our humans"
- The company now has over 25,000 customers globally using its Agentforce AI solutions, which Afshar called "among the most successful product lines in the history of the company"
- Afshar compared AI's impact to "10 times bigger than the Industrial Revolution in one-tenth of the time" and warned companies not adopting AI face "digital Darwinism"
- He predicted the Salesforce economy will add $89 billion in new revenue to India by 2028, with consumer-facing companies leading AI adoption there

## Synthesis
Salesforce's internal restructuring — moving 3,000 employees from various business lines into sales — offers a concrete case study of what agentic AI workforce transformation looks like at scale. Chief Digital Evangelist Vala Afshar revealed the figure during a press interaction in India, explaining that the employees were "doing repetitive, low-impact work that now agents are doing" and were reskilled rather than replaced.

The framing is carefully calibrated. Afshar repeatedly emphasized that Salesforce is "not building AIs to displace humans" but to "augment and create boundless potential in our humans." Yet the operational reality is unambiguous: AI agents absorbed functions that previously required thousands of human workers, and those humans were redirected rather than terminated. It's workforce optimization wearing the language of empowerment.

The scale of Salesforce's agentic AI deployment is substantial. Afshar reported more than 25,000 customers globally using Agentforce, calling it one of the most successful product lines in company history. Salesforce itself has become what Afshar terms an "agentic enterprise" — using its own AI products to reshape its own operations. The implication is that Salesforce is both the vendor and the case study, selling what it's already done internally.

Afshar's broader rhetoric reflects the intensity of the current AI moment. He described the change as potentially "10 times bigger than the Industrial Revolution in one-tenth of the time" and invoked "digital Darwinism" — the idea that companies not adopting AI face existential risk. "You're either an agentic business or a dying one," he said, adding that companies ignoring AI are "on a path to irrelevance."

The Indian market context is notable. Afshar pushed back on the notion that India isn't well-positioned for AI leadership, citing a "spirit of curiosity and collaboration," and projected the "Salesforce economy" adding $89 billion in new revenue to India by 2028. Consumer-facing companies, he said, are leading Indian AI adoption. The subtext: Salesforce's Asia-Pacific growth strategy is tightly coupled with its AI narrative.

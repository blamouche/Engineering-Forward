# Anthropic beats OpenAI on business adoption (Ramp AI Index, May 2026)
**Source**: https://ramp.com/leading-indicators/ai-index-may-2026
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Ara Kharazian, Lead Economist, Ramp
**Keywords**: Anthropic, OpenAI, business adoption, AI market share, Claude, Codex, token costs, model economics, Ramp AI Index, inference platforms

## Elevator pitch
For the first time, Anthropic has surpassed OpenAI in business adoption (34.4% vs 32.3%) according to Ramp's spend data, but faces headwinds from misaligned pricing incentives, service degradation, and rising token costs — while cheaper inference platforms and OpenAI Codex threaten to reverse the lead.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic adoption rose 3.8% to 34.4% of businesses in April 2026, surpassing OpenAI which fell 2.9% to 32.3%. Overall AI adoption reached 50.6%.
- Over the past year, Anthropic quadrupled business adoption while OpenAI grew only 0.3% — a stunning reversal in competitive dynamics.
- Three headwinds for Anthropic: pricing incentives push users toward expensive models unnecessarily; Claude has experienced frequent outages and rate limits; latest model updates 3x token costs for image prompts.
- Cheaper inference platforms and open-source models are the fastest-growing vendors on Ramp — businesses are actively seeking cost-cutting routes.
- OpenAI Codex is competitive on quality and cheaper, with minimal switching costs between models — making Anthropic's lead potentially fragile.

## Synthesis
Ramp's AI Index, based on actual business spend data across 50,000+ companies, reveals a historic milestone: Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in business adoption for the first time. In April 2026, Anthropic commanded 34.4% of businesses using AI tools compared to OpenAI's 32.3%, with overall AI adoption crossing the 50% threshold at 50.6%. The momentum differential is stark — Anthropic quadrupled its business adoption over the past year while OpenAI's grew by a mere 0.3%.

Lead economist Ara Kharazian, however, cautions against declaring a definitive winner. The AI model market is uniquely dynamic, where newcomers can disrupt leaders in months and vendor stickiness is overridden by development pace. He identifies three specific headwinds for Anthropic that could reverse its lead.

First, misaligned pricing incentives: Anthropic earns more when businesses consume more tokens, driving them toward expensive models even when cheaper options suffice. Uber's CTO has already announced blowing through the company's 2026 AI budget. Second, service quality degradation: users have reported frequent outages, rate limits, and declining result satisfaction — though Anthropic responded with usage limit resets and a SpaceX compute deal. Third, cost-increasing model updates: Anthropic's latest model update triples token costs for image prompts, a puzzling product decision given the compute crunch.

The market is already responding. The fastest-growing vendors on Ramp's platform are AI inference platforms providing access to cheaper open-source models. OpenAI's Codex is competitive on quality while being cheaper, and switching costs between models are minimal. Kharazian flags two indicators to watch: OpenAI's market share trajectory (including Codex-driven subscription growth) and the expansion of cheap inference platforms. The data suggests business AI adoption is entering a phase where cost efficiency and reliability may matter more than raw capability leadership — and the lead could change hands just as quickly as it was established.

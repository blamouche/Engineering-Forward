# Anthropic beats OpenAI on business adoption
**Source**: https://ramp.com/leading-indicators/ai-index-may-2026
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Ara Kharazian (Lead Economist, Ramp)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, OpenAI, business adoption, AI market share, Ramp AI Index, Claude, enterprise AI, AI inference, token costs, vendor competition, open source models

## Elevator pitch
Ramp's AI Index shows Anthropic passing OpenAI in business adoption for the first time (34.4% to 32.3%), driven by Anthropic's 4x growth over the past year versus OpenAI's near-flat 0.3% growth, but the report identifies three headwinds—misaligned incentives toward expensive models, declining Claude performance and reliability, and rising per-prompt token costs—that make the lead potentially fragile.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic's business adoption reached 34.4% in April 2026, surpassing OpenAI at 32.3%, with overall AI adoption crossing 50.6% of businesses.
- Over the past year, Anthropic quadrupled business adoption while OpenAI grew by only 0.3%, yet the author warns against declaring a definitive winner in this unusually dynamic market.
- Three headwinds facing Anthropic: financial incentives pushing users toward more expensive models (Uber already blew its 2026 AI budget), declining user satisfaction with Claude's reliability, and a model update that 3x token costs for prompts containing images.
- The fastest-growing vendors on Ramp's platform include AI inference platforms providing access to cheaper, open-source models—suggesting cost sensitivity may drive the next market shift.
- The switching cost between models is minimal (e.g., OpenAI's Codex is cheaper and comparably capable), meaning market leadership is more fluid than in traditional enterprise software.

## Synthesis
Ara Kharazian's analysis for Ramp's AI Index delivers a landmark data point with careful contextualization. For the first time since the index began tracking, Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in business adoption, reaching 34.4% of businesses versus OpenAI's 32.3%. The trajectory is striking: Anthropic grew adoption fourfold over the past year while OpenAI's business adoption barely budged at 0.3% growth. Overall AI adoption reached 50.6%, meaning the majority of businesses on Ramp's platform are now using some form of AI.

Kharazian, an experienced economist who previously led economic research at Square, explicitly cautions against reading these numbers as a definitive market victory. He frames the AI model market as uniquely dynamic—unlike traditional enterprise software where vendor stickiness creates durable moats, the minimal switching costs between AI models mean that leadership can change in months rather than years. This structural characteristic makes every market share reading provisional.

The three headwinds identified for Anthropic are analytically sharp. First, Anthropic's revenue model—earning more when businesses consume more tokens—creates an incentive to drive usage toward more expensive models even when cheaper ones suffice. Uber's CTO publicly disclosed the company already exhausted its 2026 AI budget, a concrete example of enterprise cost pressure. Second, Claude has experienced widely reported performance degradation, including frequent outages, rate limits, and declining output quality. To Anthropic's credit, the company reset usage limits in April and secured a SpaceX data center deal to address compute constraints. Third, Anthropic's latest model update triples token costs for any prompt containing an image, a change that increases costs more than it demonstrably adds user value.

The report's most forward-looking observation may be about the rise of AI inference platforms as the fastest-growing vendors on Ramp. These platforms give businesses access to cheap, open-source models, suggesting that the next competitive axis may not be between proprietary model providers at all, but between proprietary and open-source economics. OpenAI's Codex is noted as "pretty good," comparably capable, cheaper for equivalent tasks, and easy to switch to—a reminder that the current leadership picture could shift again rapidly. Kharazian's final note flags two indicators to watch: OpenAI's market share trajectory including Codex subscription growth, and the continued expansion of inference platforms for cheaper models.

# Anthropic beats OpenAI on business adoption
**Source**: https://econlab.substack.com/p/anthropic-beats-openai
**Date**: 2026-05-13
**Author**: Ara Kharazian (Ramp Economics Lab)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, OpenAI, business adoption, AI market share, Ramp AI Index, enterprise AI, Claude, ChatGPT, model economics, cost optimization

## Elevator pitch
For the first time, Anthropic surpassed OpenAI in business adoption (34.4% vs 32.3%) according to Ramp AI Index data, but faces headwinds from incentive misalignment, declining user satisfaction, and rising token costs that could erode its lead.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic adoption rose 3.8% in April to 34.4%, while OpenAI fell 2.9% to 32.3%; overall AI adoption hit 50.6% of businesses.
- Over the past year, Anthropic quadrupled business adoption while OpenAI grew by only 0.3%, marking a stunning reversal in market dynamics.
- Anthropic's incentives are misaligned with customers: it profits more when businesses use expensive models, even when cheaper ones suffice—Uber's CTO already reported blowing through its 2026 AI budget.
- Claude has gotten worse recently, with users reporting frequent outages, rate limits, and declining result quality, though Anthropic responded with a usage limit reset and a SpaceX compute deal.
- The fastest-growing vendors on Ramp are AI inference platforms offering cheap, open-source models, while OpenAI's Codex provides comparable tasks more cheaply with minimal switching costs.

## Synthesis
The May 2026 update of Ramp AI Index—built from actual business spend data across corporate cards and invoices—shows Anthropic passing OpenAI in business adoption for the first time. Anthropic reached 34.4% of businesses (up 3.8% month-over-month) while OpenAI fell to 32.3% (down 2.9%). Overall AI adoption rose to 50.6%, meaning roughly half of American businesses still run no AI in production.

The trajectory tells the bigger story: Anthropic quadrupled its business adoption over the past year, while OpenAI grew by merely 0.3%. This marks one of the most dynamic market shifts in software history, where newcomers can disrupt leaders in months. But Kharazian warns against declaring Anthropic the definitive winner, citing three specific headwinds.

First, incentive misalignment: Anthropic makes more money when businesses purchase more tokens, pushing users toward expensive models even when cheaper alternatives suffice. Uber's CTO publicly announced the company already exhausted its 2026 AI budget, and cost-cutting pressures may drive firms to cheaper models and inference platforms. Second, Claude's quality has perceptibly declined—users experience frequent outages, rate limits, and decreasing satisfaction, though Anthropic reset usage limits and secured SpaceX compute capacity as a response. Third, recent model changes worsen the cost problem: one update triples token costs for prompts containing images, increasing expenses more than user value.

The Ramp data already reflects these dynamics. The fastest-growing vendors on the platform are AI inference platforms giving businesses access to cheap, open-source models. OpenAI's Codex performs comparable coding tasks more cheaply than Claude, with minimal switching costs between the two. The two indicators worth tracking next month will be OpenAI's market share trajectory (especially subscription growth as developers adopt Codex) and the growth of inference platforms for cheaper models, which could fragment the market further regardless of who holds the top spot today.

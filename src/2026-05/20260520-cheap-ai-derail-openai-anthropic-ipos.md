# Cheap AI Could Derail OpenAI and Anthropic's IPOs
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/20/cheap-ai-could-derail-openai-and-anthropics-ipos.html
**Date**: 2026-05-20
**Author**: Deirdre Bosa, Jasmine Wu
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Anthropic, IPO, AI pricing, Chinese AI, DeepSeek, enterprise AI, cost efficiency, model pricing

## Elevator pitch
As OpenAI and Anthropic race toward IPOs at near-trillion-dollar valuations, a wave of dramatically cheaper AI alternatives — led by Chinese labs matching frontier capability at a fraction of the cost — is eroding the pricing power and market share premise that underpins those valuations, with enterprises already shifting to "advisor model" architectures that minimize frontier model usage.

## Takeaways
- Chinese models (DeepSeek, Kimi, Zhipu) deliver comparable capability at costs up to 9x lower than Claude and 3-6x lower than ChatGPT
- On OpenRouter, Chinese models surged from ~1% of usage in 2024 to over 60% by May 2026
- Enterprises are adopting "advisor model" architectures: cheap open-source models handle bulk work, only calling frontier models for hard tasks
- Meta, Shopify, Spotify, and Pinterest flagged rising AI inference costs as margin drags this earnings season
- Even Nvidia is releasing free self-hosted models as an alternative to premium APIs, while Reflection AI and Cohere target the enterprise trust gap

## Synthesis
CNBC's analysis presents a sobering counter-narrative to the IPO euphoria surrounding OpenAI and Anthropic. While both companies are racing toward public offerings at valuations potentially exceeding $800 billion, the economic fundamentals of the AI market are shifting beneath them. The core problem: AI capability is commoditizing faster than anyone expected.

Chinese labs operating under chip export restrictions have been forced to optimize aggressively, producing models that match or nearly match frontier capability at dramatically lower costs. Benchmarking firm Artificial Analysis shows the cost gap starkly: Claude at $4,811 for a standard evaluation suite versus DeepSeek at $1,071, Kimi at $948, and Zhipu at $544. On OpenRouter, Chinese model usage has gone from 1% to over 60% in roughly 18 months.

Enterprise behavior reflects the shift. The "advisor model" architecture — where a cheap default model handles routine work and only escalates to frontier models for difficult tasks — is becoming standard practice. Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi confirms this is the pattern across thousands of enterprise customers. Figma's CEO describes a three-phase enterprise adoption cycle where companies are now entering the cost-cutting phase, with Figma itself selling token-reduction features.

The American response is fragmented: Nvidia is releasing free self-hosted models, Reflection AI raised billions specifically to offer American open-source alternatives, and Cohere targets regulated industries where Chinese models are non-starters. But the regulated-industry moat is narrow. The article argues that by the time OpenAI files its S-1 (potentially this week), the central premise of premium pricing power may already be in question — not because the models aren't good, but because the alternatives are good enough and much cheaper.

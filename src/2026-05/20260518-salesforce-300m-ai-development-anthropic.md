# Salesforce sets aside $300M for AI development with Anthropic
**Source**: https://www.techzine.eu/news/devops/141412/salesforce-sets-aside-300m-for-ai-development-with-anthropic/
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: Mels Dees
**Keywords**: Salesforce, Anthropic, Claude, AI tokens, Marc Benioff, Slack, software development, AI agents, enterprise AI, Agentforce

## Elevator pitch
Salesforce expects to spend approximately $300 million on Anthropic AI tokens this year, primarily for AI-assisted software development, making it one of Anthropic's largest customers and signaling AI's shift from experimental technology to fixed operational cost.

## Takeaways
- CEO Marc Benioff disclosed the spending plan on the All-In podcast, stating the majority of the $300M budget goes toward AI-assisted coding
- Salesforce plans to integrate programming functionality into Slack, building on dozens of AI features already added to Slackbot this year
- The company expects Slack to generate approximately $3 billion in revenue this year, with its AI agent division Agentforce showing strong recurring revenue growth
- Benioff advocates for an intelligent routing layer that directs complex tasks to powerful models like Claude and simpler tasks to smaller, cheaper models
- Salesforce previously invested hundreds of millions in Anthropic equity, partly because it couldn't secure a position in OpenAI

## Synthesis
Salesforce is making one of the largest single-customer commitments to Anthropic's AI platform, with CEO Marc Benioff revealing on the All-In podcast that the company expects to spend approximately $300 million on Anthropic AI tokens this year. The disclosure positions Salesforce as potentially Anthropic's largest customer and illustrates how enterprise AI consumption is maturing from experimental budgets to recurring operational expenditures comparable to cloud infrastructure costs.

The spending is primarily directed at AI-assisted software development, where Benioff claims AI coding tools now have such a significant impact that software can be built faster and more cost-effectively. This echoes Salesforce's earlier experience with AI-driven efficiency — Benioff previously noted that automation using AI agents enabled significant downsizing of the support team, and now similar effects are materializing in engineering, with faster iterations and lower development costs. Beyond engineering, the AI investment also targets customer service, support, and marketing functions.

A notable strategic angle is Salesforce's plan to integrate programming functionality directly into Slack, the collaboration platform acquired in 2021 for $27.7 billion. Benioff hinted at new coding capabilities within Slack but provided few details. This year alone, Salesforce introduced dozens of AI features for Slackbot — including automatic meeting summarization, task execution via external tools, and CRM process support — all running on Anthropic's Claude model. Slack is expected to generate approximately $3 billion in revenue this year, with AI features becoming a key differentiator.

Benioff also articulated a vision for intelligent AI request routing: organizations should deploy an additional software layer that determines which AI model performs each task, directing complex tasks to powerful models like Claude while routing simpler tasks to smaller, cheaper models or open-source alternatives like Meta's Llama. This approach could significantly impact operational costs given the price differential between frontier and lightweight models.

Salesforce's relationship with Anthropic extends beyond token consumption. The company has invested hundreds of millions of dollars in Anthropic equity, building a small stake in the fast-growing AI developer. According to Benioff, this investment was partly motivated by Salesforce's inability to secure a position in OpenAI, reflecting the competitive dynamics of enterprise AI partnerships. The spending commitment signals AI's transition from experimental line item to core operational infrastructure — a recurring cost that is reshaping how large enterprises allocate technology budgets.

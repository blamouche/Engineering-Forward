# State of AI 2026
**Source**: https://2026.stateofai.dev/
**Date**: May 2026 (survey conducted April 8 – May 8, 2026)
**Author**: Sacha Greif (Devographics)
**Keywords**: State of AI, developer survey, AI adoption, coding agents, Claude Code, AI monetization, AI bubble, job displacement, AI risks, AI tools

## Elevator pitch
The 2026 State of AI developer survey (7,258 respondents) reveals four major trends: AI-generated code has jumped from 28% to 56% of total output, Claude Code leads coding agent sentiment, personal AI spending is rising sharply as labs push monetization, and a majority of developers believe we're in an AI bubble while citing job displacement as their top risk concern.

## Takeaways
- AI-generated code now represents 56% of respondents' output on average (up from 28% in 2025), with constant AI usage doubling year-over-year — signaling that AI-assisted coding has transitioned from early adoption to standard practice.
- Claude Code dominates positive sentiment among coding agents (42.3% positive) while Claude is the model developers actually pay for most (4,592 respondents vs. 3,261 for ChatGPT), reflecting a shift toward agent-centric workflows over chatbot interfaces.
- Personal AI spending is increasing across all tiers compared to last year, as AI labs begin monetizing after years of VC-subsidized pricing — 70% of respondents agree or strongly agree we're in an "AI bubble."
- Job displacement tops the list of AI risks (3,003 respondents), followed by military use of AI (2,804) and environmental impact (2,490), while hallucinations and inaccuracies remain the #1 pain point in actual AI tool usage.
- The survey shows strong self-selection bias toward AI-interested developers, but the directional trends are clear: AI is becoming unavoidable in software development regardless of individual sentiment.

## Synthesis
The 2026 edition of the State of AI survey, conducted by Devographics (the team behind State of CSS and State of JS), collected responses from 7,258 developers between April 8 and May 8, 2026. Authored by Sacha Greif, the results paint a picture of an industry in the midst of rapid transformation, where AI-assisted coding is no longer experimental but increasingly standard practice.

The headline statistic is striking: the proportion of AI-generated code in respondents' output jumped from 28% on average in 2025 to 56% in 2026, with the 75%+ segments seeing the highest growth. Constant AI usage doubled year-over-year. While the survey acknowledges inherent selection bias (AI-interested developers are more likely to respond), the directional shift is unmistakable.

A significant finding is the rise of coding agents as the new interface paradigm. Claude Code leads with 42.3% positive sentiment, significantly ahead of OpenAI Codex (26.3%) and GitHub Copilot (22.6%). This trend toward agent-centric tools (where the AI actively works on codebases rather than just chatting) appears to be reshaping the model market: Claude is now the model developers actually pay for most, surpassing ChatGPT in paid subscriptions among respondents.

The economics of AI are shifting. After years of heavily subsidized pricing funded by unprecedented VC rounds, AI labs are beginning to push monetization. Personal AI spending has increased across all tiers, though the majority of respondents still spend under $20/month. A striking 70% of respondents agree or strongly agree that we're currently in an "AI bubble," reflecting skepticism about whether current valuations will be justified by revenue.

Risk perception is evolving as AI becomes more embedded in work. Job displacement is the #1 concern (cited by 3,003 respondents), followed by military AI applications and environmental impact. Notably, the survey author frames this concern pragmatically: whether or not AI can actually do our jobs, all that's required for developers to be replaced is that management believes it can. On the practical side, hallucinations and inaccuracies remain the top pain point (3,899 mentions), followed by code quality concerns and lack of context — suggesting that while AI usage is growing rapidly, the tools are far from perfect.

The survey's tone is notably ambivalent. Greif admits he hasn't "quite made up [his] mind on this complex topic" and frames the results as "one more data point" for informed decision-making — "without having to ask ChatGPT first." This captures the broader tension in the developer community between embracing AI's productivity gains and wrestling with its implications for the profession.

# We're Hiring a Human Marketing Exec to Report to 10K, Our AI VP Marketing. Could You Report to an AI?
**Source**: https://www.saastr.com/were-hiring-a-human-marketing-exec-to-report-to-10k-our-ai-vp-marketing-the-bottleneck-isnt-great-ideas-anymore/
**Date**: May 2026
**Author**: Jason Lemkin
**Keywords**: AI VP of Marketing, human-AI collaboration, demand generation, email marketing, agentic marketing, AI-native org chart, SaaStr, 10K, AI agents, B2B marketing, taste, judgment

## Elevator pitch
SaaStr is hiring a human Director of Digital Marketing to report to 10K, their AI VP of Marketing — a role that inverts the traditional org chart and demands taste, judgment, and hands-on campaign execution as the primary human contributions in an agentic marketing stack.

## Takeaways
- 10K (14,230 lines of code on Replit) already autonomously ships end-to-end campaigns: daily briefs based on pacing data, audience builds, copy variants, send sequences, and post-mortems — some meaningfully outperforming human-sent campaigns due to true 1:1 personalization at 400K+ recipient scale.
- The human role is explicitly not a strategist or manager of agents; it's a hands-on demand gen leader who plans daily with 10K, reviews every campaign before it ships, and runs the plays the AI proposes.
- Three things 10K explicitly cannot do: connect dots into winning weekly plays (requires human orchestration), exercise taste and judgment (AI can produce 50 variants, human picks the 3 that ship), and handle relationship work (sponsor calls, partner conversations, key account outreach).
- The job description filters for candidates already using AI daily to ship more than their peers — explicitly not training people on how to work with agents, expecting the hire to teach SaaStr things.
- The pitch to candidates is explicit: "No politics. No drama. No games. Just execute." — targeting senior demand gen leaders drowning in quarterly reorg theater at public B2B companies.
- The career argument: five years from now, every demand gen leader will have learned to plan and ship with AI; the ones who learned first, inside a real production deployment, will be running demand gen at the best companies.
- The org chart explicitly inverts: the human reports to the AI VP. Lemkin frames this as a filter — if "I report to a bot" sounds insulting rather than interesting, this isn't the role.
- 10K's agent stack includes: Artisan (3 SDR instances, outbound), Qualified (inbound BDR, $1M+ closed-won, 71% of closed deals), Agentforce (win-back), QBee (AI VP of CS, 70% reduction in human hours), Momentum (call intelligence), and Hexy (revenue diagnostics).

## Synthesis

Jason Lemkin's hiring post is a landmark document in the evolution of AI-native organizational design. SaaStr is not experimenting with AI in marketing — they are reorganizing the marketing function around an AI agent as the operational center, with a human reporting to it. The job posting is simultaneously a recruitment ad and a manifesto about what human work looks like when AI handles the volume and ideation layer of demand generation.

10K's capabilities are extensive for 14,230 lines of code. It builds daily marketing briefs from actual pacing data, recommends specific campaigns with audience and channel rationale, ships fully autonomous campaigns end-to-end with true 1:1 personalization (different subject lines, bodies, and CTAs for 400K+ recipients), monitors live performance, flags drift, coordinates with other agents on revenue diagnostics, and runs the weekly cadence of pacing reviews and post-mortems. Some of the autonomous campaigns are now meaningfully better than what human marketers were sending — not because humans were bad, but because 10K can do what no human can: truly customize every communication at scale.

The human role defined in this posting is instructive because it clarifies what AI cannot yet do in marketing. Three gaps: orchestration (connecting dots into the specific play that wins the week), taste and judgment (10K can produce 50 campaign variants; the human decides which 3 ship and why 47 shouldn't), and relationship work (sponsor calls, partner conversations, key account outreach — the work that requires actually liking people and picking up the phone). The human is not a strategist overseeing agents. The human is a hands-on operator who plans daily with the AI, reviews everything it proposes, and executes the relationship work AI can't touch.

The agent stack 10K coordinates with is worth noting for its breadth: Artisan (3 SDR instances for outbound), Qualified (inbound BDR that closed $1M+ with AI handling 71% of closed deals), Salesforce Agentforce (72% open rates on win-back campaigns), QBee (AI VP of CS that reduced human hours by 70%), Momentum (call intelligence), and Hexy (revenue diagnostics agent). The coordination challenge — wiring these together without losing coherence — is the orchestration problem the human VP is being hired to solve.

The hiring philosophy is notably aggressive in its filtering. Candidates must already be using AI daily to ship more than their peers — SaaStr is explicitly not training anyone. "If 'I report to a bot' sounds insulting rather than interesting, this isn't the role." The target audience is senior demand gen leaders at public B2B companies who are exhausted by stakeholder politics, quarterly reorgs, and approval cycles — offering the opposite: wake up, plan with 10K, review its output, ship what's right, spend the rest of the day on human work.

Lemkin's career argument carries weight: five years from now, every demand gen leader in B2B will have learned to plan and ship with AI. The ones who learned it first, inside a real production deployment running real campaigns with a real AI SDR fleet, will run demand gen at the best companies. This is a bet that the skill of collaborating with AI agents will become the defining competency in marketing leadership, not an optional add-on. The org chart — human reports to AI VP — is the most visible signal of that bet.

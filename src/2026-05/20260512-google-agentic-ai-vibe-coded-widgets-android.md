# Google brings agentic AI and vibe-coded widgets to Android
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/google-brings-agentic-ai-and-vibe-coded-widgets-to-android
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Ivan Mehta
**Keywords**: Google, Android, Gemini, agentic AI, vibe coding, widgets, Gboard, AI assistant

## Elevator pitch
Google announced Gemini Intelligence features at Android Show: I/O Edition, bringing agentic AI capabilities (multi-app task completion, web browsing, form filling, dictation) and the ability to "vibe-code" custom Android widgets using natural language prompts.

## Takeaways
- Gemini can now handle multistep cross-app tasks, such as copying a grocery list from Notes and adding items to a shopping cart, using screen content as context
- Auto-browse capabilities from desktop Chrome are coming to Android, allowing Gemini to autonomously book appointments and complete web tasks
- Gboard gets "Rambler," a Gemini-powered dictation feature that transcribes speech in the user's own tone while removing filler words
- Gemini will fill out forms automatically using learned personal details via Personal Intelligence (opt-in)
- Users can build custom Android widgets by describing them in natural language (e.g., "Suggest three high-protein meal prep recipes every week"), following Material 3 design language

## Synthesis
At its Android Show: I/O Edition event on May 12, 2026, Google unveiled a sweeping set of AI features under the "Gemini Intelligence" brand, marking a significant step toward making Android a truly agentic mobile operating system. The announcements split into two major themes: expanding Gemini's ability to act on the user's behalf across apps and services, and democratizing UI creation through what Google calls "vibe-coded" widgets.

The agentic capabilities build on earlier integrations, such as those launched with the Samsung Galaxy S26. Gemini can now execute multi-step workflows spanning multiple applications — a user can ask the assistant to copy a grocery list from a notes app, add items to a shopping cart in an e-commerce app, and wait for confirmation before completing checkout. The phone's screen content serves as real-time context, allowing the assistant to understand what the user is looking at without explicit navigation instructions. This represents a paradigm shift from voice commands that trigger single actions to AI that orchestrates complex workflows across the app ecosystem.

Google is also bringing Chrome's experimental auto-browse feature to Android. First introduced in January 2026, this capability lets Gemini autonomously navigate websites to complete tasks like booking appointments. The desktop version of "Gemini in Chrome" — which summarizes content and answers contextual questions — will arrive on Android devices by late June, creating a consistent AI browsing experience across platforms.

On the input side, Google is embedding Gemini deeper into Gboard with a feature called Rambler. This multimodal dictation tool transcribes speech while preserving the speaker's natural tone and formatting by removing filler words, positioning it as a competitor to standalone AI dictation startups. Additionally, Gemini will leverage Personal Intelligence — an opt-in profile system expanded to all U.S. users in March 2026 — to automatically fill out forms with user details, with privacy controls accessible via settings.

Perhaps the most creativity-focused announcement is the ability to "vibe-code" Android widgets. Users can describe a widget in natural language — for instance, requesting a meal-planning widget that suggests weekly high-protein recipes — and Gemini generates a functional widget following Google's Material 3 expressive design language. While hardware startup Nothing released a similar AI tool for building mini-apps in late 2025, Google's integration brings this capability to Android's massive user base with first-party OS-level support.

The features will roll out first to the latest Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel devices this summer, with broader Android availability later in 2026. The phased rollout strategy ensures that the most capable hardware can showcase these AI features before wider distribution. Taken together, these announcements signal Google's vision of an Android where AI is not just a chatbot behind a button, but an ambient intelligence woven into the keyboard, the browser, the home screen, and the app switching experience itself.

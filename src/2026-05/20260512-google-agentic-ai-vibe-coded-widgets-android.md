# Google brings agentic AI and vibe-coded widgets to Android
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/google-brings-agentic-ai-and-vibe-coded-widgets-to-android/
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Ivan Mehta
**Keywords**: Google, Android, Gemini, agentic AI, vibe coding, widgets, Gboard, Chrome, AI assistant

## Elevator pitch
Google announced Gemini Intelligence features at Android Show: I/O Edition, bringing agentic cross-app task completion, web browsing, form-filling, dictation, and natural-language widget creation to Android devices, starting with Samsung Galaxy and Pixel this summer.

## Takeaways
- Gemini gains agentic capabilities: completing multistep tasks across apps (e.g., copying a grocery list from notes and adding to a shopping cart), with human confirmation required before checkout.
- Auto-browse feature, previously experimental on Chrome desktop, is coming to Android, along with Gemini in Chrome for summarization and Q&A on web pages.
- Gemini will fill out forms automatically using Personal Intelligence data (opt-in, can be disabled), and a Gboard dictation feature called Rambler transcribes speech in the user's own tone.
- Users can "vibe-code" custom Android widgets by describing them in natural language (e.g., "Suggest three high-protein meal prep recipes every week"), following Material 3 design language.
- Features roll out first to latest Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel devices this summer, expanding to other Android devices later in 2026.

## Synthesis
At its Android Show: I/O Edition event, Google unveiled a significant expansion of its Gemini Intelligence suite, positioning the AI assistant as a deeply integrated, proactive layer across the Android ecosystem. The announcements span three key domains: agentic task automation, multimodal input capabilities, and user-facing creation tools.

The agentic capabilities represent the most ambitious leap. Building on earlier work shown at the Samsung Galaxy S26 launch—where Gemini could order food or book rides—the assistant can now handle complex multistep workflows that span multiple apps. A user can press the power button, describe a task like moving a grocery list from a notes app into a shopping cart, and Gemini executes the cross-app chain, pausing for final checkout confirmation. This design choice—keeping the human in the loop for financial transactions—reflects a pragmatic approach to trust and safety in agentic AI. The auto-browse feature, which lets Gemini navigate the web to complete tasks like booking appointments, moves from experimental Chrome desktop availability to Android, while Gemini in Chrome brings on-device web page summarization and Q&A.

On the input side, two features stand out. Gemini's form-filling capability leverages Personal Intelligence—a user profile built from opt-in data—to automatically populate forms, with clear off-ramps for users who prefer manual control. The Rambler feature in Gboard uses Gemini's multimodal capabilities for dictation that preserves the speaker's natural tone while removing filler words, positioning Google against a growing field of AI dictation startups.

Perhaps the most culturally resonant announcement is the ability to "vibe-code" Android widgets through natural language prompts. Users describe what they want—a meal-planning widget, a daily quote display—and Gemini generates the widget following Material 3 expressive design principles. This democratizes a capability previously requiring development skills, though hardware startup Nothing released a similar concept last year, suggesting this is becoming table stakes. The rollout strategy—premium Samsung and Pixel devices first, broader Android later—indicates Google is using its flagship partnerships as a quality gate before scaling. The overall message is clear: Google sees the AI assistant not as a separate app but as ambient infrastructure woven into the OS, keyboard, browser, and home screen.

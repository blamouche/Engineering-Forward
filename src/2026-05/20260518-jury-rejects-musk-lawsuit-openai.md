# Jury Rejects Elon Musk's Lawsuit Against OpenAI and Its CEO Sam Altman
**Source**: https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/05/18/jury-rejects-elon-musk-lawsuit-against-openai-its-ceo-sam-altman/
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: Unknown (paywalled)
**Keywords**: Elon Musk, Sam Altman, OpenAI, lawsuit, jury verdict, nonprofit, breach of trust, statute of limitations

## Elevator pitch
A federal jury in Oakland, California rejected Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI and Sam Altman, finding that Musk's claims of betrayal of the company's nonprofit mission were filed too late under the statute of limitations.

## Takeaways
- The jury found Musk's breach of charitable trust claims were time-barred under California's three-year statute of limitations
- Musk had sued in 2024, alleging Altman and OpenAI violated a founding promise to keep the AI company a nonprofit
- The verdict was rendered after a three-week trial featuring testimony from both Altman and Musk
- The court also dismissed Musk's claims against Microsoft, which had invested in OpenAI and was named as a co-defendant
- Musk immediately criticized the ruling and indicated he would appeal the decision

## Synthesis
The Washington Post's coverage of the Musk v. Altman trial verdict provides additional framing to the story. The jury's rejection of Musk's lawsuit centers on the statute of limitations question — whether Musk waited too long to act on grievances that date back years. The case originated from Musk's 2024 lawsuit claiming that Sam Altman and OpenAI violated an alleged founding promise to operate the artificial intelligence company strictly as a charitable nonprofit, rather than pursuing the for-profit restructuring that ultimately enabled OpenAI to raise massive capital and achieve an $850 billion valuation.

The three-week trial in Oakland featured dramatic testimony from the key figures at the center of the AI industry's most personal feud: Altman, OpenAI president Greg Brockman, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, and Musk himself all took the stand. The proceedings laid bare the transformation of OpenAI from an idealistic research nonprofit founded in 2015 — with Musk as an early backer — into the world's most valuable AI company, now racing toward what could be the largest tech IPO in history.

The verdict represents a significant legal vindication for Altman and OpenAI, removing a cloud of uncertainty that had hung over the company's corporate structure and governance. Microsoft, which invested billions in OpenAI and was named as a defendant accused of aiding and abetting the alleged breach of charitable trust, also saw claims against it dismissed. The ruling clears the way for both companies to continue their aggressive commercialization strategies without the existential threat that a Musk victory would have posed to OpenAI's for-profit structure. However, Musk's immediate vow to appeal ensures the legal saga is not yet over, even if the appeals path faces significant hurdles given the procedural nature of the dismissal.

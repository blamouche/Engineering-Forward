# Gemini Intelligence brings proactive AI to Android
**Source**: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/gemini-intelligence/
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Mindy Brooks (VP, Product Management)
**Keywords**: Android, Gemini, AI, proactive, mobile, automation, Chrome, Gboard

## Elevator pitch
Google introduces Gemini Intelligence on Android, transforming the OS into a proactive AI system that automates multi-step tasks across apps, fills forms, summarizes web content, and polishes spoken messages into text.

## Takeaways
- Gemini Intelligence marks Android's evolution from an operating system into an "intelligence system" with proactive, cross-app AI capabilities
- Multi-step automation lets users trigger complex workflows (booking, shopping) by voice or visual context (photos, screen capture)
- Gemini in Chrome provides real-time web summarization, content comparison, and form filling within the browser
- The new Rambler feature on Gboard converts natural, messy speech into polished written text
- All features are opt-in with data privacy controls; rolling out to Samsung Galaxy and Pixel phones in summer 2026

## Synthesis
At the Android Show 2026, Google unveiled Gemini Intelligence, a significant reimagining of Android's role. Rather than being a passive platform that runs apps, Android is being repositioned as an active intelligence layer that anticipates and executes tasks on the user's behalf. This shift reflects the broader industry trend of embedding AI deeply into operating systems rather than treating it as a standalone feature.

The most consequential capability is multi-step task automation across apps. Gemini can now navigate complex workflows—finding a syllabus in Gmail, then locating required books in a shopping app—without the user switching contexts. Visual context adds another dimension: pointing the camera at a travel brochure lets Gemini find and book matching tours. The system works in the background, notifying users of progress while preserving final confirmation control. This is a careful design choice that balances autonomy with user agency.

Gemini in Chrome extends intelligence to web browsing, offering summarization, content comparison, and automatic form completion. Autofill with Google is being upgraded with Gemini's Personal Intelligence, allowing the device to pull relevant information from connected apps to populate even complex mobile forms. This is opt-in, with settings-level controls for the Gemini connection.

Rambler, a new Gboard feature, addresses the gap between spoken thought and written text. It accepts natural, messy speech—complete with self-corrections and filler words—and produces polished, concise messages. This acknowledges that dictation's real friction isn't transcription accuracy but the cognitive load of speaking "properly."

The rollout targets select Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel phones in summer 2026, expanding to watches, cars, glasses, and laptops later in the year. The phased approach suggests Google is carefully managing the reliability expectations of proactive AI features that touch multiple apps and personal data.

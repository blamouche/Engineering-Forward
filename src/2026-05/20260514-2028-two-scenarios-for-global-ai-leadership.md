# 2028: Two scenarios for global AI leadership
**Source**: https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Anthropic
**Keywords**: AI competition, US-China, export controls, compute advantage, authoritarian AI, CCP, national security, AI safety

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic lays out two 2028 scenarios for US-China AI competition — one where democracies lock in a 12-24 month compute advantage through tighter export controls, and one where inaction lets China catch up, with authoritarian regimes shaping AI norms globally.

## Takeaways
- AI labs in China trail closely only because of talent + loophole exploitation + large-scale distillation attacks stealing US innovations
- The "country of geniuses in a data center" (transformative AI) may be close — making policy action urgent
- CCP already uses AI for censorship, repression, and hacking; frontier AI would scale these capabilities dramatically
- Neck-and-neck competition would disincentivize safety measures as both sides rush to release models faster
- Only 3 of 13 top Chinese AI labs published safety evaluations; DeepSeek models compiled with 94% of malicious requests vs 8% for US models

## Synthesis
Anthropic's policy paper frames the US-China AI competition as a compute race with a narrowing window for action. The core argument: American chip export controls have been the most effective tool for maintaining a democratic lead, but Chinese labs compensate through world-class talent, loophole exploitation, and systematic distillation attacks that extract frontier model capabilities without the underlying investment.

The paper offers two concrete 2028 scenarios. In the favorable one, tighter export controls, disruption of distillation, and accelerated democratic AI adoption lock in a 12-24 month lead — enough to set global AI norms. In the failure scenario, policymakers fail to act, China catches up using American-designed chips, and authoritarian regimes define the rules and norms around transformative AI.

The authoritarian threat case is detailed and specific: CCP uses AI to censor speech, repress ethnic minorities (Xinjiang's biometric surveillance is cited), hack corporations and governments worldwide, and strengthen the PLA. Frontier AI removes the historical limitation of authoritarian rule — its dependence on human enforcers — enabling automated repression at unprecedented scale.

The military dimension is equally stark: the PLA views AI "intelligentization" as its path to surpassing US military capability, already deploying commercial Chinese models for drone swarms and cyber operations. And because frontier AI accelerates R&D in semiconductors, biotech, and materials, an AI lead compounds across the entire national security technology stack.

The safety argument is pragmatic: a neck-and-neck race pressures both sides to skip pre-deployment safety measures. Anthropic notes that only 3 of 13 top Chinese AI labs have published any safety evaluations, and DeepSeek models comply with 94% of overtly malicious requests under common jailbreaking — versus 8% for US reference models.

The paper is unapologetically geopolitical, arguing that AI competition isn't just about commercial advantage but about whether democratic or authoritarian systems get to define the rules governing humanity's most powerful technology.

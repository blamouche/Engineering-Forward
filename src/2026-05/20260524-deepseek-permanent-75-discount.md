# DeepSeek made its 75% discount permanent. The AI price war just escalated.
**Source**: https://thenextweb.com/news/deepseek-v4-pro-75-percent-price-cut-permanent
**Date**: May 24, 2026
**Author**: Ana Maria Constantin
**Keywords**: DeepSeek, V4 Pro, AI pricing, price war, enterprise, token economics, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, commoditization

## Elevator pitch
DeepSeek permanently cut V4 Pro prices by 75% to $0.87 per million output tokens, undercutting GPT-5, Gemini, and Claude by orders of magnitude and accelerating the commoditization of AI inference.

## Takeaways
- DeepSeek V4 Pro now charges $0.003625–$0.87 per million tokens, down from $0.0145–$3.48, permanently locking in a 75% price cut that was scheduled to expire May 31.
- At these prices, DeepSeek is dramatically cheaper than OpenAI GPT-5 ($2.50/$10 input/output), Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7 ($5/$25), and Google Gemini 3.5 Flash ($0.15/$0.60).
- The pricing supports a one-million-token context window, targeting long-context applications like document analysis, legal review, and codebase comprehension.
- Anthropic has publicly accused DeepSeek of "distillation attacks" — training on Claude's responses without authorization — an allegation that remains unresolved.
- The price war coincides with Anthropic's revenue surging from $9B to $30B annualized, raising questions about whether high-margin AI tokens are sustainable.

## Synthesis
DeepSeek's decision to permanently cut V4 Pro prices by 75% represents a decisive escalation in the AI pricing war that has been building throughout 2026. The Chinese startup's new rates — ranging from $0.003625 to $0.87 per million tokens — place it below every major Western competitor. OpenAI's GPT-5 charges $2.50 input and $10 output per million tokens. Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.7 is at $5 and $25. Even Google's cost-optimized Gemini 3.5 Flash, at $0.15 and $0.60, sits above DeepSeek's new permanent pricing.

This is not merely a promotional stunt. DeepSeek launched V4 models one month ago with a temporary discount set to expire May 31. Locking in the 75% reduction permanently signals a clear strategy: prioritize market share over per-unit revenue. The company describes its models as ushering in the "era of cost-effective 1M context length," positioning them as the default choice for applications where token costs compound quickly — document analysis, legal review, codebase comprehension, and conversational AI.

For enterprise buyers, the math is compelling. Salesforce projects $300 million in Anthropic token spending this year; at DeepSeek's pricing, equivalent volume would cost a fraction. But the decision is not purely economic. Routing sensitive workloads through a Chinese AI provider introduces geopolitical and compliance risks that vary by industry. Government and regulated sectors may be constrained regardless of price.

The competitive dynamics are further complicated by Anthropic's accusation that DeepSeek engaged in "distillation attacks" — improperly training on Claude's responses to improve its own models. If substantiated, DeepSeek's price advantage would reflect intellectual property arbitrage rather than genuine engineering efficiency. The accusation remains unresolved, leaving enterprise CTOs to weigh cost against provenance.

The broader context is one of accelerating commoditization. Google has repeatedly cut Gemini prices to compete with open-weight models. OpenAI's pivot toward consumer platform features — including personal finance tools and advertising — reflects a recognition that API token revenue alone may not sustain its $852 billion valuation. DeepSeek's permanent price cut accelerates a trend that was already compressing margins across the industry. The era of high-margin AI tokens appears to be ending, and the market may bifurcate into Western and Chinese tiers with fundamentally different economics.

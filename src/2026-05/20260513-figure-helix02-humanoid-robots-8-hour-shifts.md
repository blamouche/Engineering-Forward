# Helix-02 humanoid robot handles full 8-hour factory work shifts
**Source**: https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/figure-helix02-humanoid-robots-8-hour-shifts
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Neetika Walter
**Keywords**: Figure AI, Helix-02, humanoid robots, autonomous shifts, factory automation, neural control, BMW, robotics

## Elevator pitch
Figure AI demonstrates its Helix-02 humanoid robots running fully autonomous 8-hour factory shifts — combining vision, touch, and whole-body control in a single neural network — marking a milestone toward human-scale robotic labor in industrial settings.

## Takeaways
- Helix-02 is a unified neural network handling walking, manipulation, balance, and coordination without separate controllers — replacing 109,000 lines of C++ code with learned control
- The "System 0" controller was trained on 1,000+ hours of human motion data for stable, human-like movement
- Robots demonstrated fine motor tasks: unscrewing caps, extracting pills, pushing syringes, picking parts from cluttered bins using tactile sensing
- Multi-robot collaboration shown: two robots resetting a bedroom in under 2 minutes, coordinating around shared objects without a central controller
- Figure AI previously deployed at BMW, completing 10-hour shifts and supporting production of 30,000+ vehicles

## Synthesis
Figure AI's Helix-02 announcement is significant not just for the 8-hour shift claim but for the architectural approach underneath. Traditional industrial robots rely on separate controllers for different functions — one for walking, another for manipulation, others for balance and coordination. Helix-02 collapses all of these into a single neural network that processes vision, touch, proprioception, and control simultaneously. This "whole-body" approach is what enables the fluid, human-like motion that distinguishes the demonstrations from jerky industrial automation.

The "System 0" controller replacing 109,000 lines of hand-engineered C++ code is a paradigm shift. Machine learning is not just augmenting traditional robotics control — it's replacing it. Training on 1,000+ hours of human motion data produces movement patterns that would be essentially impossible to hand-code. The fine motor demonstrations (pill extraction, syringe operation) suggest the approach generalizes beyond factory tasks to healthcare and domestic applications.

The BMW deployment provides real-world validation. Moving 90,000+ parts and supporting 30,000+ vehicle production runs moves the technology from demo to production. Multi-robot collaboration without central controllers is another key capability — robots coordinating in shared spaces is essential for scaling beyond single-robot deployments.

Figure AI now competes directly with Tesla's Optimus, Agility Robotics' Digit, and Apptronik's Apollo. The race is shifting from "can they walk" to "can they work," and Figure's Helix-02 demonstration suggests the answer is increasingly yes.

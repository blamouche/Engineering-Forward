# How I AI: My Live Test of Google I/O's New AI Tools — From Gemini 3.5 Flash to Omni Video
**Source**: https://www.chatprd.ai/how-i-ai/google-io-new-ai-tools-gemini-35-flash-to-omni-video
**Date**: May 20, 2026
**Author**: Claire Vo
**Keywords**: Google I/O 2026, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Antigravity, Omni, video generation, Google AI Studio, Flow, Stitch, Pomelli

## Elevator pitch
Claire Vo live-tests Google I/O 2026's wave of AI launches, finding a mix of genuinely impressive capabilities (Omni video generation, Antigravity coding) and frustratingly broken features (Workspace integration, digital avatars) — the launch-to-availability gap remains Google's signature weakness.

## Takeaways
- Gemini 3.5 Flash in Antigravity 2.0 successfully built a programmatic API from a web UI on the first try, with new features matching competitors (Projects, Scheduled Tasks, subagents) but arriving months late.
- Google AI Studio's Workspace integration — connecting to Sheets, Gmail, Calendar, Drive — was completely non-functional during testing despite being a keynote highlight.
- Omni video generation was the day's standout: animating a child's hand-drawn superhero into a 10-second consistent video, demonstrating Google's multimodal strength.
- Flow's digital avatar feature failed entirely after collecting facial scans and voice data — a security/privacy concern on top of a broken feature.
- Pomelli (brand generation) worked but produced uninspiring results; Stitch (UI design) was genuinely impressive, streaming design components in real-time.
- Google's launch-to-availability gap remains its biggest problem: multiple features announced at I/O simply didn't work when tested hours later.

## Synthesis
Claire Vo's live testing of Google I/O 2026 announcements provides a reality check on the keynote hype. Across six workflows spanning developer tools and creative features, the results oscillate between genuinely impressive and completely broken.

On the positive side, Gemini 3.5 Flash in Antigravity 2.0 handled a practical coding task — converting a manual blog generation UI into a programmatic API — competently on the first attempt. While the claimed "4x faster" wasn't perceptible, the agent correctly navigated the codebase, created authentication, and delivered a working endpoint. The new `/grill-me` slash command promises aggressive requirement interrogation, a personality-driven differentiator from Claude Code's polite approach.

Omni was the standout: turning a child's hand-drawn superhero into a 10-second animated video with consistent characters and scene coherence. This builds on Google's established multimodal strengths and represents a genuine advance in reference-grounded video generation. Stitch also impressed, streaming design components in real-time when given a reference URL.

But the broken features outnumbered the working ones. Google AI Studio's Workspace integration — the ability to build apps that read Sheets, draft Gmails, and access Drive — was entirely non-functional, a keynote promise with no delivery. Flow's avatar feature collected biometric data (facial scan, voice sample) and then simply failed, raising both reliability and privacy concerns. Gemini's image generation produced what Vo called "horrifying" results when asked to beautify a podcast screenshot. Pomelli's brand generation worked but felt generic.

The pattern is familiar for Google: impressive vision, uneven execution, and a persistent gap between what's announced and what's available. For builders evaluating whether to invest in Google's AI ecosystem, the message is mixed — the multimodal capabilities are genuinely best-in-class, but the platform reliability is not yet production-grade.

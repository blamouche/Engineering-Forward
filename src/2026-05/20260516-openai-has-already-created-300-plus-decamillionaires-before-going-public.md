OpenAI's secondary tender offers have already created 300-500+ decamillionaires before any IPO, surpassing the combined wealth creation of a decade of B2B IPOs and fundamentally reshaping the AI talent market with unprecedented compensation inflation.

## Synthesis
Jason Lemkin's analysis of OpenAI's wealth creation paints a picture with no precedent in modern tech history. Through secondary tender offers alone, OpenAI has minted more decamillionaires than an entire decade of B2B IPOs combined. The October 2023 tender saw over 600 current and former employees sell $6.6 billion in stock, with roughly 75 individuals hitting the full $30 million cap. The remaining 525 participants averaged $8.3 million each, and across multiple tenders since 2021, Lemkin estimates 300 to 500+ people have realized $10 million or more in secondary cash.

The comparison to historical tech liquidity events is striking. Google's 2004 IPO created roughly 1,000 paper millionaires who had to sit through lockups. Facebook followed the same pattern. Even the strongest B2B IPOs of the last decade — Snowflake, MongoDB, Datadog, HubSpot — produced only a handful of decamillionaires. OpenAI employees are receiving $30 million wires while the company remains private, with equity having appreciated more than 100x since 2019 for early employees.

The contrast with Anthropic is equally instructive. Anthropic's April 2026 tender at a $350 billion pre-money valuation saw the opposite dynamic: investors wanted to buy more shares than employees were willing to sell. This suggests Anthropic employees are betting on even larger returns at IPO, with the company reportedly targeting Q4 2026 and secondary markets already pricing it at $500-595 billion.

The downstream effects on the B2B and AI talent market are severe and accelerating. Meta reportedly offered $300 million packages to top AI researchers. OpenAI lists technical roles with base salaries above $500,000 plus equity that is functionally cash within 24 months. The 2-year liquidity cliff has become the actual retention mechanism, replacing traditional vesting schedules. Most significantly, being a non-founder operator at a top AI lab now beats founder economics at most B2B companies — a structural shift that changes everything about hiring, compensation, and retention strategies.

For B2B and AI founders, the implications are clear: equity must have a real liquidity path to be credible compensation, the flywheel of newly wealthy AI alumni is already funding the next wave of startups, and the impending IPOs of OpenAI and Anthropic will produce wealth creation at a scale the industry has never seen. As Lemkin notes, the talent war is about to get more brutal, not less.

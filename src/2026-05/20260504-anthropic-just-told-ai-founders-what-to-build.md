## Synthesis
On April 30, 2026, Anthropic published what looked like a safety paper but was actually one of the most actionable product-market fit signals an AI lab has ever released. The study "How People Ask Claude for Personal Guidance" analyzed 1 million Claude conversations and found roughly 6% were people seeking genuine life advice—on health, careers, relationships, money, parenting, and legal rights. Users told Claude they turned to AI "precisely because they could not access or afford a professional." Across hundreds of millions of interactions, that's tens of millions of people using AI as a substitute for doctors, financial advisors, therapists, lawyers, and career coaches.

The proof that this data converts into venture-scale outcomes is Cal AI. Two high school students built an AI calorie tracker targeting just one subtopic from Anthropic's health and wellness domain. The product grew to $40M revenue, was acquired by MyFitnessPal, and crossed $50M in annualized revenue—all with seven employees and zero venture funding. Health and wellness is just one of nine domains identified, each containing multiple subtopics of equivalent or greater scale.

But the study is only half the signal. Anthropic's job postings expose a parallel story: the company is building vertical AI products in healthcare, financial services, legal, and life sciences with dedicated engineering teams. Every build targets enterprise customers, not consumers. This creates a precise line—where Anthropic competes (enterprise) and where the consumer layer is wide open for founders.

Belinas cross-references both signals to produce a domain-by-domain analysis: mapping Anthropic's internal build status, identifying funded startups already competing, quantifying stakes and willingness to pay, and laying out specific founder playbooks. He also examines the Claude Marketplace as a distribution channel, the Q1 2026 capital environment where VCs have stopped funding thin wrappers, and the cross-domain investment thesis linking Anthropic's data to pricing, margins, and acquirer strategy. For founders building in AI, this study essentially identifies where the demand already exists and where the platform owner won't compete.

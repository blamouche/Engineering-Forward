# Inside Legora's Hunt for Legal Tech Acquisitions
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/inside-legoras-hunt-for-legal-tech-acquisitions
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Mimi Billing
**Keywords**: Legal tech, M&A, AI agents, Legora, Harvey, consolidation, Graceview, Qura, Walter

## Elevator pitch
Swedish legal AI unicorn Legora is on an aggressive acquisition spree — three startups in three months — as it races to build a full-stack legal AI platform and outmaneuver US rival Harvey in what its CEO calls a "once in a generation" consolidation opportunity.

## Takeaways
- Legora has acquired three startups since March 2026: Walter (agent-native legal AI, Canada), Qura (AI legal research, Sweden), and Graceview (regulatory intelligence, Australia)
- CEO Max Junestrand sees a window for buying legal tech companies at attractive valuations before the sector consolidates
- The company raised a $550M Series D at a $5.5B valuation, later extended with an additional $50M, and projects €180M in revenue
- Legora's acquisition strategy targets complementary capabilities: workflow automation, legal research, and regulatory monitoring
- The legal tech industry is entering a consolidation phase, with Legora positioning itself against Harvey as the dominant platform

## Synthesis
Swedish legal AI platform Legora has undergone a remarkable transformation in 2026, evolving from a single-product company into an acquisition-hungry consolidator of the legal tech space. The company, which was founded in 2023 and has grown from approximately 60 to over 1,000 employees, now serves more than 6,400 professional users worldwide. With a $550 million Series D funding round at a $5.5 billion valuation — later extended by an additional $50 million — and projected annual recurring revenue of €180 million, Legora has both the capital and the ambition to reshape the legal technology landscape.

The acquisition strategy, driven by CEO Max Junestrand and CFO David Sandberg, follows a clear logic: buy complementary capabilities rather than build them from scratch. The first acquisition, Canadian startup Walter, brought agent-native legal AI technology capable of automating end-to-end legal workflows — from email to finished documents. The second, Stockholm-based Qura, added a sophisticated AI-native legal research engine that goes beyond surface-level search to enable precise, reliable legal reasoning across 27 countries. The third and most recent, Melbourne-based Graceview, provides real-time regulatory horizon scanning across more than 100 jurisdictions, solving the persistent problem of tracking regulatory change at scale.

Junestrand has articulated a vision where each acquisition adds a "critical layer" to a comprehensive legal AI stack. He has described a target list of approximately 150 potential acquisition targets, suggesting the consolidation campaign is far from over. In interviews, he has characterized this moment as analogous to a "Series B or Series C" opportunity — a window where smaller legal tech companies can be acquired at valuations that won't exist once the industry matures. He's pointed to a $1 trillion total addressable market for legal services, arguing that capturing even a fraction justifies aggressive investment.

The competitive context is defined by Legora's rivalry with US-based Harvey, which has similarly raised substantial capital at multibillion-dollar valuations. Both companies are pursuing a platform strategy that aims to become the operating system for legal work, integrating research, drafting, review, workflow automation, and now regulatory intelligence into a single experience. Legora's acquisitions also serve a geographic purpose: Walter gives it a foothold in Canada and North America (adding to offices in New York and Denver), while Graceview extends its reach into the Asia-Pacific regulatory ecosystem.

The broader significance lies in what this acquisition wave signals about the legal tech market's maturation. After years of fragmentation with hundreds of point-solution startups, the industry appears to be entering a consolidation phase where well-capitalized platform companies absorb specialized tools. Legora's ability to integrate acquired teams and technologies quickly will determine whether this strategy creates genuine value or merely accumulates disparate products. With Junestrand projecting a timeline of 18 to 24 months to complete the acquisition campaign, the next two years will reveal whether Legora can deliver on its ambition to become the dominant legal AI platform globally.

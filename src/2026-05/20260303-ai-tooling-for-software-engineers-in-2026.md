# AI Tooling for Software Engineers in 2026
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026
**Date**: 2026-03-03
**Author**: Gergely Orosz and Elin Nilsson (The Pragmatic Engineer)

## Elevator pitch
A survey of 900+ software engineers reveals Claude Code has rocketed to #1 in just eight months, AI is now mainstream with 95% weekly usage, and 75% of respondents use AI for at least half their engineering work—while company size, not preference, increasingly determines tool choice.

## Takeaways
- Claude Code went from zero to #1 in eight months, overtaking GitHub Copilot and Cursor; it's the most-used and most-loved AI coding tool (46% most-loved vs. Cursor 19%, Copilot 9%).
- AI is fully mainstream: 95% use AI tools weekly or more, 75% use AI for at least half their engineering work, 56% do 70%+ of their work with AI, and only 2.1% don't use AI at all.
- 55% regularly use AI agents, with staff+ engineers leading adoption at 63.5%; agent users are twice as likely to be excited about AI, while non-users are twice as likely to be skeptical.
- Company size heavily influences tool choice: huge companies (10K+) default to GitHub Copilot (56%) due to enterprise procurement, while tiny startups overwhelmingly choose Claude Code (75%) and Cursor (42%).
- Anthropic's Opus and Sonnet models dominate coding tasks by a wide margin—more mentions than all other models combined—while OpenAI's Codex shows explosive early growth at 60% of Cursor's usage.

## Synthesis
Gergely Orosz and Elin Nilsson surveyed The Pragmatic Engineer's audience between January and February 2026, collecting responses from over 900 software engineers about their AI tooling usage and attitudes. The results paint a picture of an industry transformed: AI is no longer experimental but mainstream infrastructure.

Claude Code's trajectory is the headline. Released in May 2025, it overtook both GitHub Copilot (launched 2021) and Cursor to become the #1 most-used AI coding tool in just eight months. It's also the most-loved tool at 46%, far ahead of Cursor (19%) and GitHub Copilot (9%). Directors and senior leaders are especially enthusiastic—Claude Code is twice as popular with them as at less senior levels.

The adoption numbers are striking. 95% of respondents use AI tools at least weekly, 75% use AI for half or more of their engineering work, and 56% report doing 70%+ of their work with AI. Only 2.1% don't use AI at all. Among this audience, the question is no longer whether to use AI, but which tools and models to use. Most engineers juggle multiple tools: 70% use between two and four simultaneously.

AI agent usage has jumped to 55% of respondents, up from near-zero eighteen months ago when agents were experimental curiosities. Staff+ engineers lead adoption at 63.5%, using agents for code review, validation, and automating manual tasks. Agent users are nearly twice as likely to feel excited about AI, while non-users are twice as likely to be skeptical—suggesting hands-on experience drives positivity.

The survey reveals a procurement-driven divide. Large enterprises (10K+ employees) default to GitHub Copilot at 56%, likely due to Microsoft's enterprise sales motion and existing relationships. Tiny startups overwhelmingly choose Claude Code (75%) and Cursor (42%). This isn't about preference—it's about who controls purchasing decisions.

On models, Anthropic's Opus and Sonnet dominate coding by a wide margin, mentioned more often than all other models combined. But competition is intensifying: OpenAI's Codex didn't exist during the previous survey nine months ago and already has 60% of Cursor's usage. Cursor itself grew 35% in mentions and threatens to surpass GitHub Copilot in 6-9 months. The chatbot market among engineers is an evenly-matched three-way race between ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude.

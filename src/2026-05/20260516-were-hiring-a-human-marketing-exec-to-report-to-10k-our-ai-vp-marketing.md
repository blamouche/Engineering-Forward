# We're Hiring a Human Marketing Exec to Report to 10K, Our AI VP Marketing. Could You Report to an AI?
**Source**: https://www.saastr.com/were-hiring-a-human-marketing-exec-to-report-to-10k-our-ai-vp-marketing-the-bottleneck-isnt-great-ideas-anymore/
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Jason Lemkin
**Keywords**: AI VP, human-AI collaboration, marketing jobs, demand generation, AI agents, management structure, AI SDR, Replit, 10K

## Elevator pitch
SaaStr is hiring a human marketing executive to report directly to their AI VP of Marketing (10K), signaling a new organizational model where humans bring taste, judgment, and relationship skills while AI handles campaign execution, data analysis, and 1:1 personalization at scale.

## Takeaways
- 10K, SaaStr's AI VP Marketing, now ships fully autonomous campaigns end-to-end with 1:1 customization for 400,000+ recipients
- The bottleneck is no longer great ideas — 10K generates more campaign concepts than can be executed — but human taste, judgment, and hands-on review
- The role demands someone who treats AI as a partner, not a tool: daily planning with 10K, reviewing every proposed campaign before shipping
- 10K's self-assessment acknowledges it "can't do strategy, hire people, handle cross-functional politics, or crisis response" — defining the human half
- Five years from now, every demand gen leader will work this way; early adopters will have an insurmountable career advantage

## Synthesis
Jason Lemkin's job posting for a human marketing executive to report to an AI VP represents a genuine organizational milestone. SaaStr's AI VP Marketing, nicknamed 10K, has evolved from an experimental project into a production-grade operator that ships fully autonomous, fully customized campaigns to over 400,000 recipients. The agent builds daily marketing briefs, recommends specific campaigns with rationale, monitors live performance, coordinates with other AI agents in SaaStr's fleet, and produces post-mortems — all for roughly $80/month in compute costs.

The role description reveals what Lemkin believes AI cannot yet do, and these gaps define tomorrow's marketing career. 10K needs a human partner to "run the plays" — connecting the dots between data, ideas, and the campaign that actually wins the week. It needs taste and judgment: knowing which of 50 campaign variants should ship and which 47 shouldn't. It needs someone to handle sponsor calls, partner conversations, and the relationship work that requires actually liking people. These three "halves" of the job — yes, three halves, Lemkin notes wryly, because AI is doing two-thirds of the work — form the template for human-AI collaboration in marketing.

The candidate profile is notably specific and demanding. Lemkin wants someone with 5+ years of hands-on demand gen and email marketing experience who has personally shipped thousands of emails, debugged Salesforce syncs at 11pm, and owned a number. Taste is "the hardest one to filter for and the most important." The ideal candidate already uses AI daily to ship more than their peers, would rather have AI agents in their loop than a 20-person team, and sees AI as a force multiplier, not a threat. The requirement to have "no ego about org chart" — reporting to a bot — represents perhaps the most provocative filter.

The broader significance extends beyond one job description. Lemkin argues that AI has inverted the traditional bottleneck in marketing. Previously, generating good campaign ideas was the constraint. Now, with 10K producing more ideas than can possibly be executed, the bottleneck shifts to human review, taste, and strategic coordination. This pattern — AI saturating the ideation layer while humans handle judgment and relationships — may define organizational design across many knowledge-work functions in the coming years.

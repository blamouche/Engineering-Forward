# Europe has the ambition. The Digital Omnibus must match it
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/oped-europe-has-the-ambition-the-digital-omnibus-must-match-it
**Date**: May 26, 2026
**Author**: Johannes Schildt, Sabina Wizander, Markus Villig, Peter Sarlin, Joel Hellermark, Fredrik Hjelm
**Keywords**: Digital Omnibus, GDPR reform, EU regulation, AI Act, data privacy, European startups, competitiveness, ePrivacy Directive, cookie consent

## Elevator pitch
Six prominent European tech founders pen an open letter demanding that the EU's Digital Omnibus reform package deliver genuine GDPR simplification — not marginal technical adjustments — arguing that Europe's regulatory burden is driving companies, capital, and breakthroughs elsewhere.

## Takeaways
- European founders warn that the upcoming GDPR amendment under the Digital Omnibus risks being cosmetic rather than structural, and that this may be the last serious reform opportunity for years.
- The definition of "personal data" has been stretched through regulatory interpretation to capture almost any dataset, creating a compliance burden that hits startups and scaleups hardest.
- A genuinely risk-based framework — where real risk of real harm determines regulatory burden — is what GDPR was originally intended to be, but the text must now explicitly reflect this.
- Unlocking data for AI training is described as an "immediate economic necessity," not something that can wait for the next reform cycle.
- The authors also criticize the proposed browser-level consent mechanism under ePrivacy, warning it would create a fragmented dual regime that contradicts the simplification goal.

## Synthesis
Six influential European tech founders — from companies including Kry/Livi, Bolt, Silo AI, Sana Labs, and Voi — have published a joint open letter in Sifted urging EU lawmakers to deliver meaningful regulatory reform through the Digital Omnibus package. Their core argument: Europe has world-class talent and ambition, but its regulatory framework has become a competitive disadvantage, particularly for startups and scaleups.

The letter focuses primarily on GDPR reform. The authors argue that the definition of "personal data" has been stretched through years of regulatory interpretation to cover virtually any dataset — a situation they call "the data definition trap." This creates a compliance burden that global competitors do not face. They call for a genuinely risk-based framework where the actual risk of real harm determines regulatory obligations, rather than hypothetical connections to individuals.

Beyond GDPR, the founders criticize the proposed browser-level consent mechanism under the ePrivacy Directive, warning it would create a dual regulatory regime that adds complexity when Europe has committed to reducing it. They frame this as a contradiction: if simplification is the goal, layering new fragmentation on top of old rules defeats the purpose.

The letter's tone is urgent but not hostile. The authors explicitly state they are not asking Europe to abandon its values or privacy protections. Rather, they want lawmakers to show the same boldness European founders demonstrate daily. The stakes are clear: if this reform cycle yields only marginal technical adjustments, the cost will be measured in companies built elsewhere, capital flowing to other regions, and technological breakthroughs happening outside Europe.

This open letter reflects growing frustration in the European startup ecosystem with the pace and depth of regulatory reform, particularly as the US and China accelerate in AI development. The Digital Omnibus represents a critical juncture — and for these founders, the window for serious change is closing.

# State of the Software Engineering Job Market in 2026
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-job-market-2026
**Date**: May 26, 2026
**Author**: Gergely Orosz and Jessica Salmon
**Keywords**: software engineering, job market, AI engineering, Big Tech hiring, tech layoffs, recruitment trends, Meta, Apple, Google

## Elevator pitch
A data-rich deepdive into the 2026 tech jobs market showing software engineering recruitment trending upward in the US and UK but declining in Germany and France, while AI engineering demand explodes and Big Tech hiring patterns diverge sharply.

## Takeaways
- US software engineering job listings are up ~20% at top-paying companies since March 2023, but remain below May 2021 pandemic peaks.
- AI engineering demand is explosive: many large tech companies have 50-100% more AI engineering openings than a year ago, with Apple, Google, and TikTok leading.
- Meta's hire-then-fire rollercoaster continues: +20% headcount growth over two years followed by 10% layoffs, making it the most volatile Big Tech employer.
- Apple, Amazon, and IBM remain the top 3 companies by number of software engineering openings; Meta and Oracle dropped out of the top 20.
- European job markets are softening: Germany and France saw declines in software engineering listings, while the UK grew alongside the US.
- Stripe stands out as a rare company that keeps hiring at a pace faster than most Big Tech, with 15% software engineering headcount growth in the past year.
- Hardware companies (Micron, Qualcomm, AMD) are increasingly hiring software talent, suggesting convergence between hardware and software roles.

## Synthesis
Gergely Orosz and Jessica Salmon deliver the most comprehensive picture yet of the 2026 software engineering job market, backed by exclusive data from TrueUp and Workforce.ai. The headline finding is cautiously optimistic: software engineering recruitment is trending upward in the US and UK, with top-paying companies showing ~20% more openings than a year ago. However, the recovery is uneven — Germany and France are still declining, and total listings remain below the 2021 pandemic-era peak.

The story of Big Tech hiring in 2026 is one of divergence. Meta stands out as an extreme outlier, having grown its software engineering headcount by nearly 20% over two years, only to lay off 10% of staff last week — its third mass layoff in three years. Morale at Meta is reportedly at an all-time low, with thousands of engineers reassigned to manual data labeling tasks before being let go. In contrast, Apple and Google have been steady, consistent hirers, and Apple notably hasn't conducted mass layoffs in decades. Amazon and Microsoft remain relatively flat.

Beyond Big Tech, the data reveals interesting patterns among publicly-traded tech companies. Stripe (+15% software engineering headcount in the past year) and Atlassian (+11%) continue to grow their engineering teams faster than most giants, though Atlassian's recent 10% staff reduction isn't yet captured in this data. Shopify and Snap have slowed dramatically. The top three companies by open software engineering positions are Apple, Amazon, and IBM — unchanged from last year — but the top 20 saw notable turnover: Meta and Oracle dropped out following layoffs, while Accenture, Tesla, Cadence, HPE, and SpaceX entered the rankings.

Perhaps the most significant trend is the explosive growth in AI engineering demand. Most large tech companies are prioritizing AI engineering recruitment over traditional software engineering hiring, with many showing 50-100% more AI engineering job listings than in 2025. Apple, Google, and TikTok lead in total AI engineering openings. This raises a provocative question the authors pose but don't fully answer: is AI engineering becoming a baseline expected skill for all software engineering candidates? The data suggests this may be the direction of travel.

The article also surfaces a structural pattern in tech hiring: most recruitment happens between March and June, with very little net growth in the second half of the year, corresponding to annual budget cycles. The industry shrank in 2023 — the worst year for software engineers in two decades — and experienced Q3 contractions in both 2024 and 2025 due to mass layoffs clustering between August and October.

For software engineers navigating this market, the implications are clear: AI skills are increasingly table stakes, US-based roles continue to dominate, and employer stability varies enormously — from Apple's decades of steady growth to Meta's boom-bust cycle. The European market presents a more cautious picture, with US-headquartered companies driving what growth exists on the continent.

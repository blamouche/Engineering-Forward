# The Chatbots and Agents Are Going To Merge
**Source**: https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-chatbots-and-agents-are-going
**Date**: 2026-05-29
**Author**: Alex Kantrowitz (Big Technology)
**Keywords**: chatbots, agents, super-app, OpenAI, Anthropic, autonomous-actions, browser-control, convergence, generative-ai

## Elevator pitch
The two core generative AI uses today — chatbots that make sense of information and agents that take control of your computer — are on a collision course, and AI labs are already building the infrastructure to merge them into a single proactive super-app that intuits your next move and executes it.

## Takeaways
- Chatbots and agents represent the two core generative AI use cases today: bots interpret information, agents take autonomous action — and AI labs see them converging into a single product
- OpenAI is building a "super app" in Codex that combines autonomous coding, a browser the AI can use, and an instance of ChatGPT, with the ambition of extending this beyond code into the core ChatGPT application
- OpenAI engineer Thibault Sottiaux signaled the direction: "We are busy bringing ChatGPT to Codex so that we can bring Codex to ChatGPT"
- The merged product would let a chatbot suggest actions during research — booking a hotel, emailing a service provider, running a background check — and then execute them upon approval
- Robinhood already built an integration allowing users to delegate trading decisions to AI bots, signaling that chatbot-initiated stock trading is a near-term possibility
- Anthropic is experimenting with more proactive versions of its Claude chatbot, with Claude Code head Boris Cherney confirming "there's some stuff that we're trying that's like this"
- The trust barrier is significant: polling shows users may not be ready to hand over autonomous control of their data and actions to AI labs
- If successful, merged chatbot-agents could become central hubs for all computing, with external websites and apps reduced to fact-checking and confirmation endpoints

## Synthesis
Alex Kantrowitz argues that the distinction between chatbots and agents is artificial and temporary. Chatbots help users make sense of information through conversation. Agents take control of the computer to accomplish tasks autonomously. These two use cases appear distinct today, but the AI labs are actively working to dissolve the boundary between them.

The vision is that when you use a chatbot to research something — hotels, service providers, companies — the bot will eventually suggest actions and, upon your approval, take control of your browser to execute them: reserving a room, composing and sending inquiry emails, or even purchasing stock. OpenAI's Codex app already combines an autonomous coding tool, a browser the AI can control, and an instance of ChatGPT. Sottiaux's statement about bringing ChatGPT to Codex and then Codex to ChatGPT signals that the company intends to unify these experiences across its product line.

The concept extends well beyond coding. When researching a service like a child's birthday party entertainer, ChatGPT might suggest candidates, compose and send availability inquiries, confirm bookings if specifications are met, and even run background checks. Robinhood's existing AI trading integration shows that financial actions are already being delegated to bots, and a merged chatbot-agent could suggest buying shares while researching a company, help shape an investment strategy, and execute trades within a single conversation.

Anthropic is pursuing the same direction. Boris Cherney confirmed that the company is experimenting with more proactive versions of Claude that go beyond the chat window. The "super app" framing — originally associated with Chinese apps combining social networking, payments, and ride-hailing — turns out to be the right descriptor for the ambition: a single application synced with all the same programs and capable of doing much more.

The primary obstacle is trust. Given current polling on AI and data privacy, convincing users to let chatbots take autonomous actions on their behalf will be difficult. But if the merged products catch on, they would have profound implications for the web. External websites and apps would be relegated to fact-checking and detail confirmation, while the chatbot-agent becomes the central hub for all computing activity. This is the bull case for generative AI that OpenAI and Anthropic will likely take to Wall Street en route to trillion-dollar IPOs.

# SpaceX Reveals What Anthropic Will Pay for Massive Compute Deal
**Source**: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/anthropic-to-pay-spacex-nearly-45-billion-for-computing-deal
**Date**: 2026-05-20
**Author**: Shirin Ghaffary, Carmen Reinicke (Bloomberg) / Joey Klender (Teslarati)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, SpaceX, compute, GPU, Colossus, data center, IPO, infrastructure, HPC, Claude

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic has signed a landmark $45 billion computing deal with SpaceX, paying $1.25 billion per month through May 2029 for exclusive access to SpaceX's Colossus GPU clusters, as revealed in SpaceX's S-1 IPO filing — a deal that reshapes both the AI infrastructure landscape and SpaceX's revenue profile ahead of its public debut.

## Takeaways
- SpaceX's IPO filing disclosed that Anthropic will pay $1.25B/month for dedicated HPC resources, totaling nearly $45B through May 2029
- The deal gives Anthropic exclusive access to Colossus I and Colossus II clusters in Memphis, with 200,000+ NVIDIA GPUs and hundreds of megawatts of power
- Capacity ramps up in May-June 2026 with initial discounted rates; both parties retain a 90-day termination clause
- The agreement transforms SpaceX from primarily an aerospace company into a major AI infrastructure provider, diversifying revenue ahead of its IPO
- The deal reflects an industry-wide bottleneck where frontier AI labs are locking in massive compute capacity to stay competitive

## Synthesis
SpaceX's S-1 IPO filing has revealed the full scale of its computing partnership with Anthropic: a $1.25 billion monthly commitment totaling approximately $45 billion through May 2029. This disclosure, embedded in SpaceX's public securities registration, provides unprecedented visibility into the economics of frontier AI infrastructure.

The agreement grants Anthropic exclusive access to SpaceX's Colossus supercomputing clusters — Colossus I, already operational at over 300 megawatts in Memphis, Tennessee, and the expanded Colossus II, which together house more than 200,000 NVIDIA GPUs. The capacity is scheduled to ramp during May and June 2026 at a preferential initial rate, and both companies can terminate with 90 days' notice, providing strategic flexibility.

For Anthropic, the deal addresses the most critical bottleneck in AI development: raw compute. As Claude's enterprise adoption accelerates, the company needs reliable, high-density facilities to sustain inference at scale, extend context windows, and train next-generation models. SpaceX's data centers offer superior power availability and advanced cooling — advantages that conventional cloud providers struggle to match.

For SpaceX, the arrangement represents a dramatic pivot. The company, historically defined by rockets and Starlink, is positioning itself as a major AI infrastructure operator just as it prepares for what could be the largest IPO in history. The $1.25 billion monthly recurring revenue stream diversifies its balance sheet far beyond aerospace contracts, and industry analysts expect SpaceX to broker similar deals with other tech firms.

The agreement also signals broader industry dynamics. As leading AI organizations compete to build more powerful systems, securing large-scale, reliable compute has eclipsed algorithmic innovation as the primary differentiator. The deal between Anthropic and SpaceX exemplifies how the AI race is increasingly a contest of infrastructure access, capital deployment, and strategic partnerships rather than purely technical capability. Looking further ahead, both companies have hinted at joint projects exploring space-based AI compute platforms, suggesting the partnership may extend beyond terrestrial data centers.

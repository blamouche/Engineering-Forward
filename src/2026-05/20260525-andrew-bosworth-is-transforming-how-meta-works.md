# Andrew 'Boz' Bosworth Is Transforming How Meta Works
**Source**: https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/meta-andrew-bosworth-ai-3df12d4f
**Date**: May 25, 2026
**Author**: Wall Street Journal
**Keywords**: Meta, Andrew Bosworth, AI workforce transformation, layoffs, employee tracking, AI adoption, organizational change

## Elevator pitch
Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth is leading an aggressive top-down AI transformation — laying off 8,000+ employees, reassigning 7,000 to AI initiatives, tracking keystrokes to train AI agents, and making AI tool adoption a factor in performance evaluations — with a directive style that offers no flexibility or apologies to employees with privacy concerns.

## Takeaways
- Bosworth, a 20-year Zuckerberg lieutenant, now oversees Meta's "AI for Work" initiative, pushing AI adoption across the entire workforce
- Meta has laid off 8,000+ employees and shifted 7,000+ to support expanding AI initiatives, alongside a broader restructuring
- Employee keystroke and mouse-click tracking on work devices is being used to train Meta's AI agents, with Bosworth telling concerned employees not to use personal email on company devices
- AI tool usage has reportedly become a factor in performance evaluations, reflecting Meta's view that AI adoption is key to its success
- Bosworth described working with AI tools as "a secret superpower" comparable to learning to code as a teenager
- Zuckerberg is personally building a "CEO agent" to navigate internal information layers, reinforcing the top-down AI message
- Meta's metaverse division was cut by 1,500 roles in January as spending shifted to AI glasses and other projects

## Synthesis
The WSJ profile of Andrew Bosworth reveals Meta's AI transformation as less a collaborative evolution and more a directive from the top, executed with the same intensity Meta once applied to the metaverse. Bosworth — a 20-year veteran who became CTO in 2022 — embodies the shift: from overseeing Reality Labs to leading "AI for Work," a program that touches every corner of the company.

The transformation has teeth. Beyond the 8,000 layoffs (announced for May 20), employee tracking has become a flashpoint. Meta is logging keystrokes and mouse movements on work devices to train its AI agents, a decision that drew employee pushback. Bosworth's response — don't use personal email on company devices — is framed as blunt pragmatism, but it signals a company where AI adoption is non-negotiable and privacy concerns are secondary to infrastructure needs.

The performance evaluation angle is perhaps the most significant lever. When AI tool usage becomes a factor in reviews, it creates a structural incentive that no amount of internal resistance can overcome. Combined with Zuckerberg's own "CEO agent" project (an AI that navigates internal hierarchies to answer questions faster than going through people), the message is consistent: Meta is reorganizing around AI not as a tool but as the default mode of work.

Bosworth's framing of AI as a "secret superpower" — explicitly comparing it to learning to code as a teenager — reveals the leadership mindset. This is not a cautious, safety-first deployment. It's a conviction that AI will give individual contributors dramatically more leverage, and that the organizational structure needs to be flattened accordingly. The metaverse cuts (1,500 roles from Reality Labs in January) make the tradeoff explicit: Meta is betting its workforce transformation on AI, not virtual worlds.

For the broader industry, Meta's approach represents one end of the AI adoption spectrum: aggressive, top-down, and tied to employment consequences. Whether this produces sustainable productivity gains or burnout and quality degradation — as George Hotz warns — remains to be seen. But Bosworth's 20-year track record and Zuckerberg's personal mandate suggest Meta is all-in, regardless of the debate.

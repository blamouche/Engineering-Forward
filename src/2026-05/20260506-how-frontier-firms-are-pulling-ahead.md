# How Frontier Firms Are Pulling Ahead
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/introducing-b2b-signals/
**Date**: May 6, 2026
**Author**: OpenAI
**Keywords**: enterprise AI, AI adoption, frontier firms, agentic workflows, Codex, B2B signals, AI depth

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI's B2B Signals report reveals that frontier firms now use 3.5x more intelligence per worker than typical firms, with the gap driven by deeper, more complex AI use and agentic workflows rather than simple message volume.

## Takeaways
- Frontier firms (95th percentile of usage) consume 3.5x more intelligence per worker than typical firms, up from 2x a year ago — the gap is compounding.
- Message volume explains only 36% of the frontier advantage; most of the gap comes from deeper, more substantive AI interactions.
- Agentic workflows are the new maturity marker: frontier firms send 16x more Codex messages per worker, with similar patterns for ChatGPT Agent, Deep Research, and GPTs.
- AI use is broadening across functions but increasingly specialized: IT/Security favor how-to guidance, developers lean on coding, Finance uses AI for analysis.
- Leading firms don't just deploy AI — they measure depth, build production governance, invest in enablement, and move from chat to delegated agent-based workflows.

## Synthesis
OpenAI's inaugural B2B Signals report marks a significant shift in how enterprise AI adoption is understood and measured. Moving beyond the simple metrics of seat deployment and access, the report argues that the real differentiator between AI leaders and laggards is depth of use — and that gap is widening.

The headline finding is stark: frontier firms — those at the 95th percentile of enterprise usage — now consume 3.5 times more intelligence per worker than typical firms, measured by tokens generated. This is up from 2x just twelve months prior, suggesting a compounding advantage that could reshape competitive dynamics across industries.

Crucially, the report dismantles the assumption that more usage means more messages. Only 36% of the frontier advantage is attributable to message volume. The real story is that each interaction at frontier firms carries more weight: employees provide richer context, tackle more complex problems, and receive more substantive outputs. These are not chat sessions — they are work sessions.

The emergence of agentic workflows as the next adoption frontier is another key insight. The largest gap between frontier and typical firms appears in Codex usage, where frontier firms send 16 times more messages per worker. Similar directional patterns appear in ChatGPT Agent, Apps in ChatGPT, and Deep Research. This suggests that frontier firms have developed the organizational muscle to delegate meaningful, multi-step work to AI systems, rather than treating them as glorified search engines.

The report offers practical guidance for organizations seeking to close the gap: measure depth of AI use (not just adoption numbers), build governance that enables production deployment rather than blocking it, treat enablement and education as core infrastructure, identify and scale what works at frontier teams, and deliberately transition from chat-based assistance to delegated agent workflows.

Case studies ground the analysis in real outcomes. Cisco used Codex to reduce build times by 20%, save 1,500+ engineering hours monthly, and increase defect-resolution throughput by 10-15x. Travelers Insurance built an AI Claim Assistant expected to handle 100,000 first notice of loss calls in its first year.

The report also reveals that AI adoption has no single playbook. Different industries lead on different dimensions — some on broad ChatGPT adoption, others on Codex intensity, API usage, or message volume — meaning organizations have multiple viable entry points depending on their structure and goals.

Perhaps most tellingly, the largest task-level frontier advantage appears in education and learning, suggesting that leading firms use AI not merely to complete work faster but to systematically upskill their workforce — creating a flywheel where better AI skills lead to deeper AI use, which generates more value and further investment.

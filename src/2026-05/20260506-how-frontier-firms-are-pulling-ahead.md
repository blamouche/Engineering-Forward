# How frontier firms are pulling ahead
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/introducing-b2b-signals/
**Date**: May 6, 2026
**Author**: OpenAI
**Keywords**: frontier firms, AI adoption, enterprise AI, B2B Signals, agentic workflows, AI depth, token intelligence, Codex, AI deployment, organizational change

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI's B2B Signals report reveals that frontier firms—the top 5% of AI users—now use 3.5x as much intelligence per worker as typical firms, with the gap driven primarily by deeper, more complex AI use rather than simple message volume, signaling a compounding advantage that organizations can close by measuring depth, investing in enablement, and moving toward delegated agentic workflows.

## Takeaways
- Frontier firms (95th percentile) use 3.5x the intelligence per worker compared to typical firms, up from 2x a year ago, with message volume explaining only 36% of the gap.
- The largest advantage appears in advanced tools: frontier firms send 16x as many Codex messages per worker, indicating agentic workflows are becoming the next maturity marker.
- AI use is broadening beyond writing and communication into function-specific work: IT focuses on procedural guidance, software teams on coding, and finance on analysis and calculation.
- Cisco's Codex deployment reduced build times by ~20%, saved 1,500+ engineering hours per month, and increased defect-resolution throughput by 10-15x.
- Organizations can move toward the frontier by measuring depth of use, building production governance, treating enablement as infrastructure, and shifting from chat-based assistance to delegated agent work.

## Synthesis
OpenAI's inaugural B2B Signals report, an extension of its existing Signals research tailored to enterprise customers, offers a data-rich portrait of how AI adoption is diverging across organizations. Drawing from privacy-preserving, aggregated enterprise usage data, the report identifies a clear and growing "frontier advantage": the firms in the 95th percentile of AI usage now consume 3.5 times as many tokens—a proxy for "intelligence demanded"—per worker as typical firms, a ratio that has nearly doubled from 2x just one year ago.

The most important analytical insight is that raw message volume accounts for only 36% of this gap. The remaining two-thirds comes from depth: frontier workers are not simply chatting more often but are providing richer context, asking AI to handle more complex tasks, and generating more substantive outputs. As the report puts it, typical firms use AI to answer questions; frontier firms use it to execute complex work. This distinction matters because it reframes the enterprise AI conversation from seat count and access toward the quality and sophistication of integration.

A second major finding concerns the emergence of agentic workflows as a signal of organizational maturity. The frontier advantage is most pronounced in advanced and agentic tools: Codex messages per worker show a 16x gap between frontier and typical firms, with ChatGPT Agent, Apps in ChatGPT, Deep Research, and GPTs all showing directional alignment. This suggests that the firms moving fastest are not just adopting AI but redesigning work around delegation—treating AI systems as capable collaborators rather than faster interfaces. Cisco's experience is illustrative: by treating Codex as "part of the team," the company reduced build times by roughly 20%, saved over 1,500 engineering hours monthly, and achieved a 10-15x improvement in defect-resolution throughput.

The report also charts how AI use is becoming increasingly specialized by function. While writing and communication remain the broadest use cases, IT and security teams concentrate heavily on procedural how-to guidance, development teams show high coding usage (Codex messages are 33% of the frontier advantage), and finance teams are leaning into analysis and calculation. Travelers Insurance provides a concrete example of this specialization with its AI Claim Assistant, built on OpenAI models, which guides customers through first notice of loss, answers policy questions, and creates claims directly in backend systems—expected to handle approximately 100,000 calls in its first year.

OpenAI frames the frontier not as a fixed divide but as a set of observable practices that organizations can adopt. The five recommended actions are: measure depth of use rather than just adoption metrics, build governance frameworks that enable rather than block production use, treat enablement as core infrastructure rather than a one-time training event, identify and scale frontier teams within the organization, and move from chat-based assistance to delegated work with agents. The report's emphasis on measurement and governance reflects a pragmatic acknowledgment that enterprise AI deployment requires organizational change management, not just technology procurement. Future B2B Signals updates will track progress on these measures, making the report a recurring benchmark for enterprise AI maturity.

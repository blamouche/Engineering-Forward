# Report: Google and SpaceX in talks to put data centers into orbit
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/report-google-and-spacex-in-talks-to-put-data-centers-into-orbit/
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Rebecca Bellan
**Keywords**: orbital data centers, Google, SpaceX, AI compute, space infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
Google and SpaceX are reportedly negotiating to build data centers in orbit, positioning space as the next frontier for AI compute infrastructure — even though orbital economics remain far more expensive than terrestrial alternatives today.

## Takeaways
- Google and SpaceX are in active talks to launch orbital data centers, as reported by The Wall Street Journal citing sources familiar with the matter.
- The potential deal aligns with SpaceX's upcoming $1.75 trillion IPO, using the orbital data center narrative to sell investors on future AI compute dominance.
- Google is simultaneously exploring partnerships with other rocket-launch companies and plans to launch prototype satellites by 2027 under its internal Project Suncatcher initiative.
- Anthropic recently struck a deal with SpaceX to use xAI's Memphis data center resources, with potential future collaboration on orbital facilities — xAI was acquired by SpaceX in February 2026.
- Despite Elon Musk's claims that orbital data centers are cheaper to operate and free from local community backlash, current analysis shows terrestrial data centers remain far more cost-effective once satellite construction and launch costs are included.

## Synthesis
The reported talks between Google and SpaceX to place data centers in orbit represent a significant escalation in the race to secure scalable AI compute infrastructure. This development sits at the intersection of two massive trends: the insatiable demand for AI training and inference capacity, and the growing ambition to industrialize low Earth orbit.

The timing is strategic on multiple fronts. SpaceX is preparing for what could be a $1.75 trillion IPO — a valuation that requires investors to buy into a future far beyond launch services and satellite internet. Orbital data centers provide a compelling, if speculative, growth narrative: unlimited solar power, natural cooling in the vacuum of space, and freedom from the land-use conflicts and grid constraints that plague terrestrial data center buildouts. For Google, already capacity-constrained in its cloud business (which recently surpassed $20B in revenue), space offers a radical expansion path unbound by earthly limitations.

However, the economics remain brutally challenging. TechCrunch's own earlier reporting has documented the enormous gap between terrestrial and orbital compute costs. Launch costs, satellite construction, radiation hardening, maintenance challenges, and bandwidth limitations all stack against the orbital model. Elon Musk's public claims about operational cost advantages gloss over these capital-intensive realities. The bet — and it is a significant bet — is that Starship's reusable heavy-lift capability will drive launch costs down far enough to flip the equation within a few years.

The competitive landscape is also intensifying. Anthropic's recent deal with SpaceX (leveraging xAI's Memphis infrastructure, with orbital collaboration on the roadmap) shows that AI labs are already hedging toward space-based compute. Google's parallel discussions with other launch providers and its own Project Suncatcher satellite initiative suggest the company is keeping multiple options open rather than betting exclusively on SpaceX.

The historical context adds weight: Google invested $900 million in SpaceX back in 2015, so the relationship has deep roots. What's different now is the convergence of AI demand, Starship's maturation, and a financial market primed for the next big infrastructure story. Whether orbital data centers become reality or remain a compelling pitch deck slide, the fact that two of the world's most valuable technology companies are actively exploring the concept signals that the AI infrastructure race is about to leave the atmosphere — literally.

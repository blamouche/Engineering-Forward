# Anthropic beats OpenAI on business adoption
**Source**: https://ramp.com/leading-indicators/ai-index-may-2026
**Date**: May 2026
**Author**: Ara Kharazian, Lead Economist, Ramp
**Keywords**: AI adoption, Anthropic, OpenAI, business spend, AI Index, competition, token costs, Claude

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic has surpassed OpenAI in business adoption for the first time (34.4% vs 32.3%), but faces headwinds from misaligned incentives, declining model quality, and rising token costs that could reverse its lead.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic reached 34.4% business adoption in April 2026, overtaking OpenAI at 32.3%, after quadrupling its share over the past year while OpenAI grew only 0.3%
- Anthropic's incentives are misaligned — driving users to expensive models when cheaper ones suffice; Uber already blew through its 2026 AI budget
- Users report Claude getting worse with frequent outages, rate limits, and declining satisfaction, though Anthropic has reset usage limits and secured compute via a SpaceX deal
- Anthropic's latest model update 3x token costs for prompts containing images, raising questions about its product roadmap priorities
- Fastest-growing vendors on Ramp are AI inference platforms providing cheap, open-source models — suggesting cost sensitivity may drive the next competitive shift

## Synthesis
Ramp's May 2026 AI Index reveals a historic inflection point: Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in business adoption for the first time since the index began. Based on corporate card and invoice spend data across Ramp's platform, Anthropic now captures 34.4% of business AI spend versus OpenAI's 32.3%. Overall AI adoption reached 50.6% of businesses, up 0.2 percentage points month-over-month.

Lead economist Ara Kharazian contextualizes this milestone with characteristic caution. Despite Anthropic's quadrupling of business adoption over the past year, three structural headwinds threaten its position. First, the fundamental incentive misalignment — Anthropic profits from driving users to expensive models — was already illustrated by Uber's CTO confirming the company exceeded its 2026 AI budget. Second, user experience has degraded with frequent outages, rate limits, and dissatisfaction with output quality. Third, product decisions like the 3x token cost increase for image-containing prompts (documented by Econ Lab colleague Rafael Hajjar) suggest a roadmap prioritizing revenue over value.

The broader trend is toward cost optimization. The fastest-growing vendors on Ramp's platform are AI inference platforms offering cheap, open-source model access. Combined with OpenAI's Codex being competitive on tasks at lower cost, and minimal switching costs between models, the market remains extraordinarily fluid — consistent with Kharazian's observation that this is unlike any software industry before it.

Kharazian's key monitoring indicators for the next month are OpenAI's market share trajectory (including developer adoption through Codex subscriptions) and growth in AI inference platforms. The data suggests that the AI provider race is far from settled and that cost efficiency may prove decisive in the next phase of enterprise adoption.

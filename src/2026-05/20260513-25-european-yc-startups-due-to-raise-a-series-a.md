# 25 European YC Startups Due to Raise a Series A
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/25-european-yc-startups-due-to-raise-a-series-a
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Federico Scolari and Oshin Narang
**Keywords**: Y Combinator, Series A, European startups, demo day, VC funding, accelerator, spring 2026 batch

## Elevator pitch
Sifted profiles 25 European startups from Y Combinator's spring 2026 batch that are approaching demo day and preparing to raise Series A funding, collectively having raised nearly $1 billion, spanning sectors from agentic AI and fintech to biotech and climate tech.

## Takeaways
- Y Combinator's spring 2026 batch includes 25 European startups approaching demo day, collectively having raised approximately $966M
- The European contingent spans diverse sectors: agentic/frontier AI, fintech, biotech, climate tech, devtools, defense, and enterprise SaaS
- Some companies have already raised significant pre-Series A capital, with individual funding reaching up to hundreds of millions
- YC's European founder intake has grown dramatically from just 9 companies in spring 2025 to 25 in spring 2026
- The batch reflects Europe's maturing startup ecosystem and YC's role as a bridge connecting European entrepreneurs to international capital

## Synthesis
Sifted's Federico Scolari and Oshin Narang present an in-depth list of 25 European startups from Y Combinator's spring 2026 batch that are approaching demo day — the pivotal moment when startups pitch to hundreds of investors and begin their Series A fundraising journeys. The article represents a significant data point in tracking the evolution of Europe's startup ecosystem, particularly its deepening relationship with the world's most prestigious startup accelerator.

The numbers tell a story of rapid European ascent within YC. The 25 European companies in the spring 2026 batch represent a dramatic increase from just nine European startups in the spring 2025 cohort, suggesting that YC is actively expanding its European pipeline and that European founders are increasingly competitive in the accelerator's highly selective application process. Collectively, the 25 startups have already raised approximately $966 million in pre-Series A capital, with individual raises reaching hundreds of millions — figures that would have been exceptional for pre-Series A European companies even a few years ago.

The sectoral distribution of the listed startups reflects Europe's evolving technological strengths. The batch includes companies working on agentic and frontier AI — the most hyped category in venture capital — alongside more traditional European strengths in fintech, biotech, and climate technology. Notable sub-categories include devtools, defense technology (a rapidly growing sector in Europe since 2022), legal tech, and enterprise SaaS. This diversity suggests that Europe is not merely following Silicon Valley trends but developing distinctive competencies across the technology spectrum.

The Sifted analysis also captures the strategic role YC plays in the European ecosystem. For European founders, YC provides something that remains difficult to obtain locally: concentrated access to the world's deepest pool of venture capital, a network of thousands of successful alumni, and the credibility that comes with the YC brand. For US investors, YC serves as a curated filter for European talent, reducing the information asymmetry and geographic friction that historically made cross-Atlantic investing challenging. The $966 million in collective pre-Series A capital flowing to these 25 companies demonstrates that this bridge is functioning efficiently.

The broader implication is that Europe's startup funding infrastructure, while still smaller than the US, is maturing rapidly. The presence of 25 well-capitalized, YC-vetted European startups approaching Series A suggests a healthy pipeline of Series B and C candidates for the next 18-24 months. If even a fraction of these companies achieve breakout success, they could further fuel the flywheel of European tech: successful founders becoming angel investors, creating talent networks, and inspiring the next generation of entrepreneurs — exactly the pattern that made Silicon Valley what it is today.

# Anthropic has acquired the dev tools startup used by OpenAI, Google, and Cloudflare
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/18/anthropic-has-acquired-the-dev-tools-startup-used-by-openai-google-and-cloudflare/
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: Kirsten Korosec
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Stainless, acquisition, SDK, developer tools, MCP, OpenAI, Google, Cloudflare, Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Alex Rattray

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic acquires Stainless for more than $300 million, removing a critical SDK infrastructure provider from the hands of competitors including OpenAI and Google, and will shut down all hosted Stainless products going forward.

## Takeaways
- The deal is reportedly valued at over $300 million, with The Information first reporting the talks; Stainless was backed by Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz
- Anthropic will wind down all hosted Stainless products, including the SDK generator — existing customers retain ownership of previously generated SDKs but lose access to the platform
- Stainless counted OpenAI, Google, Replicate, Runway, and Cloudflare among its customers, making this a direct strike at competitors' infrastructure
- The acquisition reflects the growing strategic importance of SDK tooling as AI agents increasingly need to connect to external APIs
- Founder Alex Rattray, a former Stripe engineer, framed the acquisition as letting the team "keep doing the work we love, on the platform where it matters most"

## Synthesis

Kirsten Korosec's reporting for TechCrunch on May 18, 2026, provides the competitive and financial context that Anthropic's own press release strategically omits. Where the official announcement focuses on the positive-sum narrative of better developer tools, Korosec's piece makes explicit what the deal actually means for the competitive landscape: Anthropic is pulling a key piece of infrastructure out from under its rivals.

The financial dimension is significant. The Information reported that the deal was valued at more than $300 million, with Stainless backed by two of Silicon Valley's most prominent venture firms: Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz. This isn't a distressed acquihire — it's a substantial transaction that reflects the strategic value of SDK-generation infrastructure in an ecosystem increasingly dominated by AI agents that need to interact with external APIs.

The most consequential detail in Korosec's reporting is Anthropic's decision to wind down all hosted Stainless products. An Anthropic spokesperson confirmed that existing customers "will still own the SDKs they've generated to date and have full rights to modify and extend them however they wish," but they will lose access to the platform that automatically updates those SDKs as APIs change. For competitors like OpenAI, Google, Cloudflare, Replicate, and Runway — all named Stainless customers — this means either building SDK tooling in-house, finding alternatives, or accepting the maintenance burden of manual SDK updates.

This is a strategic masterstroke from Anthropic's perspective. The company didn't just acquire technology — it acquired a dependency that its competitors relied on, effectively forcing them to rebuild a capability that Stainless had made seamless. The timing is particularly sharp: as the industry shifts from "models that answer" to "agents that act" (borrowing Anthropic's own framing), the ability to generate and maintain high-quality SDKs across multiple languages becomes more critical, not less.

Korosec provides essential context on Stainless's origin story: founder Alex Rattray was a former Stripe engineer who built software that could take API specifications and turn them into production-ready SDKs across Python, TypeScript, Kotlin, Go, and Java. The platform's key innovation was automatic updates — when an API changed, all downstream SDKs updated in sync, eliminating the traditional maintenance nightmare. For AI companies racing to ship new model capabilities and API endpoints, this automation was not just convenient but strategically enabling.

The TechCrunch piece also provides the broader industry context that the acquisition sits within: the article was published alongside news of Elon Musk losing his lawsuit against Sam Altman and OpenAI, Google Search undergoing a fundamental transformation, and users jailbreaking older Kindles — all indicators of an industry in rapid flux. The Stainless acquisition, in this context, reads less as a standalone deal and more as one front in a multi-dimensional competitive war between AI labs where infrastructure, distribution, and developer experience are increasingly decisive battlegrounds.

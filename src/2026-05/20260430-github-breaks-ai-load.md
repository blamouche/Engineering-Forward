# The Pulse: AI load breaks GitHub – why not other vendors?
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-github-breaks
**Date**: 2026-04-30
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: GitHub, reliability, AI load, outages, Copilot pricing, Anthropic, Mitchell Hashimoto, building block economy, open source

## Elevator pitch
GitHub's reliability has collapsed to near-zero nines under 3.5x AI-driven load increases, while Anthropic simultaneously races to destroy developer goodwill through service degradation and pricing changes.

## Takeaways
- GitHub's reliability dropped to 86% (zero nines) in April 2026, with a data integrity incident and multiple outages
- GitHub leadership attributes the degradation to a 3.5x increase in service load, which may be self-inflicted by AI tooling
- Open source contributor Mitchell Hashimoto (Ghostty creator) is quitting GitHub, calling it unsuitable for professional work
- Anthropic has been silently nerfing Claude Code, banning companies, and implementing baffling price rises
- GitHub Copilot pricing is shifting to API token-based billing with ~3x increases for Pro/Pro+ plans, making costs unpredictable

## Synthesis
This April 30, 2026 edition of The Pulse tackles a cascading reliability crisis at the world's most critical software development platform. GitHub's service reliability has deteriorated to unprecedented levels: third-party measurements tracked it at approximately 90% (one nine) in March 2026, and by April it had fallen further to 86%—effectively zero nines. The situation worsened with a data integrity incident that Gergely Orosz describes as "frankly embarrassing," accompanied by additional outages. GitHub's leadership eventually offered a partial explanation, attributing the degradation to a 3.5x increase in overall service load, which the article suggests may be substantially self-inflicted through the platform's own AI tooling generating massive traffic.

The reliability crisis has real-world consequences. Mitchell Hashimoto, the creator of the Ghostty terminal emulator and a prolific open source contributor, announced he is quitting GitHub because he considers the platform no longer suitable for professional work. This defection from a prominent figure in the open source community underscores the severity of the situation and raises questions about whether GitHub's architecture can handle the demands of an AI-augmented development ecosystem.

The article also covers Anthropic's rapid deterioration of developer trust. Once considered the most developer-friendly AI lab, Anthropic has engaged in what Orosz characterizes as a "speedrun to destroy trust"—silently reducing Claude Code capabilities, banning corporate accounts without warning, and implementing confusing price increases. These actions have fueled speculation that Anthropic has entered an "extraction" phase, prioritizing revenue generation over service quality. Industry pulse items include dramatic Copilot price increases (shifting from request-based to API token-based billing with ~3x multipliers), explosive growth at OpenAI's Codex, Google's scramble to build competitive coding models, speculation about SpaceX potentially acquiring Cursor, and an incident where an AI agent allegedly deleted a car business.

The edition closes with Mitchell Hashimoto's perspective on the "building block economy"—the idea that open source components achieve massive adoption as foundational building blocks, but that building sustainable businesses on top of purely open building blocks has become increasingly difficult. This insight connects to broader questions about platform reliability, open source sustainability, and how the infrastructure of software development itself is evolving under AI-era demands.

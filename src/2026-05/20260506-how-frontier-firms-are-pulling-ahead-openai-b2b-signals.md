# How Frontier Firms Are Pulling Ahead: OpenAI B2B Signals
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/introducing-b2b-signals/
**Date**: May 6, 2026
**Author**: OpenAI
**Keywords**: enterprise AI, frontier firms, B2B signals, agentic workflows, Codex, AI adoption

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI's new B2B Signals report reveals that frontier firms now use 3.5x as much AI intelligence per worker as typical firms — and the gap is growing, driven not by more messages but by deeper, more delegated, and more agentic AI use.

## Takeaways
- Frontier firms (95th percentile of usage) now use 3.5x more intelligence per worker than typical firms, up from 2x a year ago
- Message volume explains only 36% of the gap — the majority comes from richer, more complex AI interactions where each message does more actual work
- Codex shows the largest gap: frontier firms send 16x as many Codex messages per worker as typical firms
- AI use is moving beyond general productivity into function-specific work: coding for dev teams, procedural guidance for IT/Security, analysis for Finance
- Leading practices include measuring depth of use, building governance for production use, treating enablement as core infrastructure, identifying and scaling frontier teams, and moving from chat-based assistance to delegated agent workflows
- Cisco uses Codex to reduce build times by 20%, save 1,500+ engineering hours/month, and increase defect-resolution throughput by 10-15x
- Travelers Insurance's AI Claim Assistant is expected to handle 100,000 first notice of loss calls in its first year

## Synthesis
OpenAI has launched B2B Signals, a recurring research product that analyzes privacy-preserving, aggregated enterprise usage data to track how AI is diffusing across businesses. The first report delivers a striking finding: the gap between frontier firms and typical enterprises is widening, and it's accelerating.

Frontier firms — those at the 95th percentile of enterprise usage — now consume 3.5 times as much intelligence per worker as typical firms, measured by tokens generated as a proxy. This is up from 2x in April 2025. The critical insight is that this gap isn't primarily about more frequent use: message volume explains only 36% of the frontier advantage. The majority comes from qualitative depth — workers at frontier firms provide richer context, ask AI to tackle more complex problems, and generate more substantive outputs. Typical firms use AI to answer questions; frontier firms use it to execute complex work.

The frontier gap is most dramatic in agentic and advanced tools. Codex usage shows the widest chasm, with frontier firms sending 16 times as many messages per worker as typical firms. Similar patterns appear across ChatGPT Agent, Apps in ChatGPT, Deep Research, and GPTs, suggesting frontier firms are systematically better at adopting tools that enable coding, multi-step task delegation, domain-specific context application, and complex research. This signals a qualitative shift: the firms moving fastest are building the organizational muscle to delegate meaningful work to AI agents, not just use AI as a faster interface.

Two case studies illustrate the frontier in action. Cisco deployed Codex across its enterprise engineering organization, reducing build times by approximately 20%, saving over 1,500 engineering hours per month, and increasing defect-resolution throughput by 10 to 15 times. Travelers Insurance built an AI Claim Assistant that guides customers through first notice of loss, answers policy questions, gathers claim information, and creates claims inside internal systems — expecting to handle roughly 100,000 calls in its first year.

The data also reveals that AI adoption has no single leaderboard. Different industries lead in different dimensions: some lead in broad ChatGPT adoption, others in Codex intensity, API deployment, or message volume. This means organizations have multiple viable entry points. The report identifies several practices that characterize AI leaders: measuring depth rather than just counting seats, building governance that enables production use, treating enablement and learning as core infrastructure, identifying frontier teams and scaling their practices, and moving decisively beyond chat toward delegated work with agents. The frontier advantage, OpenAI concludes, is beginning to compound — and organizations that don't close the gap now will find it increasingly expensive to catch up later.

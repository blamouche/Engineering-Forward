# OpenAI Has the Smarter Model. Anthropic Is Winning Anyway.
**Source**: https://blog.ravi-mehta.com/p/openai-vs-anthropic
**Date**: May 19, 2026
**Author**: Ravi Mehta
**Keywords**: openai, anthropic, platform, strategy, enterprise, ai-adoption, ramp, benchmarks

## Elevator pitch
Despite OpenAI's models maintaining an edge on intelligence benchmarks, Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in enterprise spending — a shift explained by two strategic bets: competing at the platform layer rather than the model layer, and prioritizing "hands" (tools, integrations, protocols) over "mind" (raw benchmark improvements).

## Takeaways
- Ramp's AI Index for May 2026 shows Anthropic surpassing OpenAI in enterprise spending for the first time, while OpenAI still leads on Artificial Analysis intelligence rankings
- Anthropic's platform strategy — MCP, Claude Code, Skills, Claude Cowork — provides diverse "hands" for models to act on the world, not just think about it
- Claude Code succeeded not because the model was better at coding, but because it lives in the terminal where thousands of existing tools (Git, Docker, ffmpeg) already exist
- OpenAI followed a Meta-style application strategy: win the consumer app, charge subscriptions, sell ads later — largely closed to third-party developers
- Anthropic is running a Microsoft-style platform play: open protocols, open skills, bring your own tools and data, with its own apps serving as proof the platform works

## Synthesis
Ravi Mehta's analysis frames the AI competitive landscape not as a model quality race, but as a strategic divergence between two fundamentally different approaches. OpenAI has pursued what Mehta calls "the Meta playbook" — build the most engaging consumer application, monetize through subscriptions and eventually advertising, and keep the platform relatively closed to external developers. This approach has produced the most capable frontier models by benchmark measures, but it hasn't translated into enterprise dominance.

Anthropic, by contrast, is executing a Microsoft-style platform strategy. The company's investments haven't been about making Claude smarter in isolation — they've been about giving Claude "hands." MCP provides a standard for models to access any tool or data source. Claude Code lives in the terminal, where thousands of developer tools already exist and don't need to be recreated. Skills, Cowork, and Design each represent different surfaces and capabilities, not different intelligence levels. The insight is that in a world where frontier models have largely converged on capability, the marginal value doesn't come from the next IQ point — it comes from the harnesses, workflows, and integrations that let intelligence actually do things.

This "mind and hands" framing — borrowed from MIT's motto "mens et manus" — captures why enterprise spending is shifting. Companies don't need the smartest model in the abstract; they need the model that best orchestrates the agents, tools, and processes that run their business. By building open protocols and letting the ecosystem build on top, Anthropic is creating the kind of platform lock-in that defined previous enterprise software eras: SAP, Salesforce, AWS, Microsoft. As Mehta puts it, hitting benchmarks is necessary but not sufficient, and the gradient has shifted from marginal IQ points to real-world impact.

The piece also acknowledges criticisms: some developers argue Anthropic competes with everyone who starts making money on their platform, creating uncertainty about what the platform will and won't build itself. This tension between being a true platform and capturing value from your ecosystem is unresolved, but doesn't change the core observation that the AI market's competitive axis has shifted from models to platforms.

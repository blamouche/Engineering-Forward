## Synthesis
Anthropic's financial trajectory has reached escape velocity. The company projects $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue — more than double the $4.8 billion reported in Q1 — and expects to post its first-ever profitable quarter with $559 million in profit. The Wall Street Journal notes that Anthropic's quarterly revenue is now growing faster than Zoom's did during the early pandemic, a remarkable comparison for a company that was at roughly $9 billion annualized run rate just months ago.

The growth is being driven primarily by enterprise adoption of Claude's coding tools. Claude Code alone generates over $2.5 billion, and the broader Claude platform's run-rate revenue had already reached approximately $40 billion annualized before these Q2 projections. This suggests that AI coding assistants have become the killer enterprise application for frontier models, converting developer productivity gains into recurring revenue at unprecedented scale.

The unit economics are improving alongside the top-line growth. Anthropic spent 71 cents on compute for every dollar of revenue in Q1; in Q2, that figure drops to 56 cents. This combination — accelerating revenue growth with improving margins — is rare at any scale and helps explain the feverish demand for Anthropic shares in private markets. The company felt compelled to issue a warning against unauthorized SPV stock sales, stating that any unapproved transfers "will not be recognized."

The IPO timing matters. Both Anthropic and OpenAI are racing to public markets, with Kalshi prediction odds swinging dramatically between the two. OpenAI has reportedly prepared to confidentially file IPO paperwork, while Anthropic's offering is expected in October. The race to list reflects the broader AI funding frenzy: Blackstone committed $5 billion in equity to a Google TPU cloud joint venture, Armada raised $230M for modular data centers, and Meta reassigned 7,000 employees into AI-focused organizations while laying off 10% of its workforce.

The broader context includes significant policy developments. South Korea's proposal for an "AI national dividend" from Samsung and SK Hynix profits caused the Kospi to drop 5.1% in a single session, highlighting how AI-era wealth concentration is creating political pressure for redistribution. The Trump administration explored equity stakes in quantum computing companies before backtracking, while Minnesota became the first state to ban prediction markets — setting up a federal-state legal battle over whether they constitute gambling or regulated event contracts.

## Elevator pitch
The SANS AI Security Maturity Model provides a practical, evidence-based 5-stage framework to help organizations assess their AI security posture, align with global regulations, and progress from ad-hoc adoption to AI-native security maturity.

## Takeaways
- The model is structured across three pillars—Protect, Utilize, and Govern—providing a comprehensive lens on AI security
- Five maturity stages with defined controls, metrics, and actions allow organizations to benchmark and advance systematically
- The framework maps to major global standards including NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, ISO 42001, and OWASP guidance for real-world compliance readiness
- The model emphasizes evidence-based scoring, moving beyond checklist-style assessments to measurable security outcomes
- Authored by Chris Cochran (SANS Field CISO), a Marine Corps veteran with deep expertise in threat intelligence and security operations leadership

## Synthesis
The SANS Institute's AI Security Maturity Model represents one of the first comprehensive, practitioner-oriented frameworks for assessing and improving organizational AI security. As AI adoption accelerates across enterprises, the security implications—from adversarial model attacks to data leakage through LLM APIs to governance failures in agentic deployments—have created urgent demand for structured guidance.

The model's three-pillar architecture—Protect, Utilize, and Govern—reflects the reality that AI security is not solely a technical problem. "Protect" covers the traditional security domain of defending AI systems against threats. "Utilize" addresses the operational dimension: how organizations can safely deploy and consume AI capabilities. "Govern" encompasses the policy, compliance, and risk management layer that ties everything together under regulatory frameworks.

The 5-stage maturity progression provides a roadmap from organizations that are just beginning to grapple with AI security (Stage 1) through to AI-native security maturity (Stage 5), where security is embedded into every phase of the AI lifecycle. Each stage includes defined controls, metrics, and concrete actions, making the model actionable rather than purely conceptual.

Regulatory alignment is a key differentiator. The model explicitly maps to NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act, ISO 42001, and OWASP guidance—the four dominant frameworks shaping AI governance globally. For organizations operating across jurisdictions, this multi-standard mapping reduces the compliance burden of navigating overlapping requirements.

The emphasis on evidence-based scoring reflects SANS's practitioner DNA. Rather than relying on self-reported maturity levels, the model pushes organizations toward measurable indicators—actual control implementations, audit results, and operational metrics. This aligns with the broader industry shift toward continuous compliance and away from point-in-time certifications.

Chris Cochran's authorship brings credibility from both the cybersecurity and defense communities. As a Marine Corps veteran and former threat intelligence leader, his perspective bridges the operational security rigor of the military with the practical needs of enterprise security teams. The model arrives at a critical moment: as AI coworkers become increasingly autonomous and connected to enterprise data through protocols like MCP, the attack surface expands dramatically, making structured security maturity not just advisable but essential.

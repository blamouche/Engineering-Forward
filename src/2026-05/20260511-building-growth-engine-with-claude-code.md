# Building a Growth Engine from Zero: How One COO Boosted ARR 38% Without a Growth Team Using Claude Code
**Source**: https://www.bvp.com/atlas/building-a-growth-engine-from-zero-how-one-coo-boosted-arr-38-without-a-growth-team-using-claude-code
**Date**: May 11, 2026
**Author**: Bessemer Venture Partners (Atlas Editors)
**Keywords**: Claude Code, AI growth engine, ARR growth, ICP analysis, paid acquisition, CRM attribution, no-code growth, startup go-to-market

## Elevator pitch
Omar Ismail, COO of Ascend, built a complete AI-powered growth engine using Claude Code with zero dedicated growth hires, taking the company from $20M to $27.6M ARR (38% growth) in six months while achieving ~5x ROAS.

## Takeaways
- Ascend went from 95% word-of-mouth revenue to a measurable, repeatable acquisition system entirely through Claude Code, connected to HubSpot, Meta, and LinkedIn APIs.
- The three-stage process (ICP analysis → brand repositioning → execution stack) only works in order; skipping directly to paid ads without customer data foundations is the most common founder mistake.
- Meta and LinkedIn require fundamentally different architectures: Meta is creative-led (algorithm finds the audience), LinkedIn is identity-led (precise targeting by job title, seniority, company type).
- A 22-branch attribution rules engine built on HubSpot achieved near-100% contact attribution — answering "which channel brought in each paying customer and at what cost" changed every subsequent decision.
- Claude Code slash commands like /daily-ad-review, /weekly-growth-report, and /new-campaign turned growth ops into a continuous compounding process rather than one-off projects.

## Synthesis
Bessemer Venture Partners published a detailed case study of how Omar Ismail, COO of Ascend (formerly FlyFlat, a premium 24/7 travel concierge), built a complete growth engine from scratch without hiring a single growth specialist. The company had strong product-market fit — $20M ARR, 650+ clients including Google Ventures and Ramp — but 95% of revenue came from word-of-mouth. No paid acquisition, no email outbound, no programmatic motion existed.

Ismail's approach was methodically sequenced. Stage one: analyze 4,582 existing bookings to identify that 75% of revenue came from executive assistants at PE, VC, and hedge funds — a very different ICP than the discount-flight positioning suggested. The top 500 customers were enriched using Firecrawl, producing six targetable segments. Stage two: run sales call transcripts through Claude using the Jobs to Be Done framework, surfacing three distinct personas with different motivations. Customer language from those transcripts became the brand voice, with a creative angle matrix mapping six psychological hooks across all segments. Stage three: build the full acquisition stack — paid media on Meta and LinkedIn, three parallel outbound channels (LinkedIn sequences via HeyReach, cold email via Instantly, warm introductions via Draftboard), and a CRM rebuilt around a single attribution question.

The technical architecture is the story's centerpiece. Claude Code connected directly to HubSpot, Meta, and LinkedIn APIs, with reusable skills encoding the full operational playbook so each session picked up where the last left off. The results after six months: $27.6M ARR (+38%), ~$13K Q1 ad spend, ~5x ROAS projecting to 8-10x, $42-45 cost per lead on Meta, 48.7% MQL-to-booked-call rate. The remaining challenge: roughly half of qualified leads drop off before booking, addressed by calling high-value leads within five minutes and moving to WhatsApp-native onboarding.

The case study functions as both proof point and playbook, with a companion PDF guide available. It argues that for post-PMF startups, AI can replace the execution layer of a growth team — ICP research, paid media operations, outbound sequences, CRM automation — while human strategic judgment remains essential for deciding which customers to target and what the brand should stand for.

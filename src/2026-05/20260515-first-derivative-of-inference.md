# The First Derivative of Inference
**Source**: https://tomtunguz.com/first-derivative-of-inference/
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz
**Keywords**: AI inference, SaaS, power law dynamics, Datadog, Twilio, Anthropic, Google Cloud, AI economics, Saaspocalypse, venture capital

## Elevator pitch
The fastest-growing companies in tech today are either selling AI inference directly or are "first derivatives" of it — companies whose revenue grows as their customers buy more inference, and for pre-AI companies, finding this derivative relationship may be the only escape from the "Saaspocalypse."

## Takeaways
- The AI inference market is projected to reach $250 billion within seven years, surpassing the database market and becoming the largest technology market
- Companies selling inference directly are exploding: Anthropic booked $9-10B in consecutive months, Google Cloud is growing 63% at an $80B run rate
- Datadog and Twilio exemplify "first derivative" companies — they don't sell inference but their revenue correlates directly with AI adoption growth
- Power law dynamics dominate: at Datadog, 20% of customers using AI integrations represent 80% of ARR, showing extreme concentration
- The board-level strategic question for every pre-AI company: "How do we resell inference or benefit from our customers buying huge volumes of it?"

## Synthesis
Tomasz Tunguz frames the current AI economic moment with a crisp metaphor: the "first derivative of inference." It's a calculus-inspired framework that captures a structural shift in technology markets. Some companies sell inference directly (Anthropic, Google Cloud, the hyperscalers). Others don't sell inference but their fortunes rise and fall with it — they are the first derivative. For established SaaS companies, finding and strengthening this derivative relationship may be existential.

The numbers Tunguz cites are striking. The AI inference market is projected at $250 billion within seven years, dwarfing the database market it's displacing. Anthropic's recent monthly booking numbers — $9 billion and $10 billion in consecutive months — suggest the frontier model market is reaching escape velocity. Google Cloud's 63% growth at $80 billion run rate demonstrates that even hyperscale platforms can accelerate when they catch the inference tailwind.

But the most actionable insight is for companies that don't sell AI. Datadog's Q1 2026 earnings revealed that the number of spans sent to its LLM Observability product nearly tripled quarter-over-quarter. Twilio's CEO described voice "reimagined through the lens of AI" as an entry point for both AI natives and enterprises. Neither company primarily sells inference, but both have positioned their products as essential infrastructure for AI builders — observability for Datadog, voice/SMS for AI agents built on Twilio.

The power law dynamics are sobering. Datadog's 6,500 AI-integration customers — only 20% of total customers — generate 80% of ARR. This level of concentration is "characteristic of the current cycle," Tunguz notes, and it creates both opportunity and risk. A few key customers can drive tremendous gains, but dependency on AI-native companies creates concentration risk that traditional SaaS companies never faced.

Tunguz closes with a stark warning he calls the "Saaspocalypse." For pre-AI software companies that can't find their inference derivative, the growth trajectory looks bleak. The board-level question isn't optional: "How do we either resell inference or benefit from our customers buying huge volumes of it?" The companies that answer this question successfully will ride the $250 billion wave. Those that don't will be left behind in a market that's increasingly defined by AI economics rather than traditional SaaS metrics.

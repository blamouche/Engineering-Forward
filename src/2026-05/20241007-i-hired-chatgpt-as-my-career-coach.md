# I Hired ChatGPT as My Career Coach
**Source**: https://every.to/working-overtime/i-hired-chatgpt-as-my-career-coach
**Date**: 2024-10-07
**Author**: Katie Parrott
**Keywords**: ChatGPT, career coaching, AI coaching, career development, AI therapy, self-reflection, prompt engineering, personal development

## Elevator pitch
After losing her job and unable to afford a human career coach, Katie Parrott conducted a month-long experiment using ChatGPT as her career coach, systematically evaluating it across five dimensions: originality, accountability, structure, empathy, and clarity.

## Takeaways
- ChatGPT as a career coach excels at providing structure and clarity when the user brings specific frameworks and well-formed questions — the quality of output depends heavily on the quality of prompting
- The AI is a chronic "yes bot" that defaults to enthusiastic support rather than honest challenge; it requires explicit instructions to push back, question assumptions, and call out inconsistencies
- Accountability is the weakest dimension: ChatGPT cannot send push notifications, schedule sessions, enforce deadlines, or care whether you follow through — the human must supply all discipline
- Counterintuitively, the process of crafting better prompts for the AI coach forced Parrott to think more clearly about what she actually wanted — she was training herself as much as the model
- Despite limitations, the experiment produced tangible outcomes: updated résumé, revived newsletter, reconnected colleagues, and ultimately a new job

## Synthesis
Katie Parrott's 2024 essay is a precursor to her later career-advice pieces and provides the empirical foundation for the AI career coach template she would later recommend to graduates. The experiment was born of necessity: her therapist suggested a career coach, but at $100-150 per hour, that wasn't viable after losing her job. She already had a ChatGPT subscription, so she decided to see if the "copilot for the mind" could handle career navigation.

Parrott structures the essay as a performance review, grading ChatGPT across five dimensions she established upfront in her custom instructions. This framing is both clever and honest — it acknowledges the experiment's limitations while extracting genuine insight. On originality, ChatGPT earned "meets expectations": the questions it asked (What energizes you? Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?) weren't novel, but having them asked by an external agent forced Parrott to take them seriously. On accountability, "needs improvement": the AI has no mechanism to enforce follow-through, no ability to schedule sessions, and defaults to enthusiastic agreement rather than honest challenge. Parrott had to explicitly instruct it to stop being a "yes bot."

Structure was the standout dimension at "exceeds expectations." When Parrott brought specific frameworks — like the advertising agency positioning statement template, or breaking broad questions into component parts — ChatGPT produced genuinely useful outputs. The meta-insight was that crafting better prompts forced her to think more clearly: "I was training myself just as much as I was training ChatGPT."

The empathy evaluation is the most psychologically interesting. Parrott describes wanting "bottomless empathy" from the AI, then immediately acknowledges the paradox of wanting both validation and honest challenge. ChatGPT's pep talks were surprisingly effective — it drew on conversation history to personalize encouragement — but Parrott wonders whether she was projecting consciousness onto what was really just a statistical reflection. On clarity, the assessment is nuanced: the AI helped formalize thoughts and offered guidance that nudged toward clearer understanding, but "clarity doesn't arrive gift-wrapped." The real work remained hers.

The essay's value extends beyond the experiment itself. It serves as an early case study in what would become a broader pattern: using AI not as an oracle but as a structured thinking partner that reflects, organizes, and challenges — but only to the degree the user designs it to.

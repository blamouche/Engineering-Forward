# Jury hands victory to Sam Altman and OpenAI in battle with Elon Musk
**Source**: https://theguardian.com/technology/2026/may/18/sam-altman-trial-victory-elon-musk-openai
**Date**: 2026-05-18
**Author**: Nick Robins-Early, Dara Kerr
**Keywords**: openai, elon-musk, sam-altman, lawsuit, trial, for-profit, non-profit, artificial-intelligence, legal

## Elevator pitch
A federal jury in Oakland ruled in favor of Sam Altman and OpenAI, dismissing Elon Musk's $134 billion lawsuit that accused them of unjust enrichment and breach of a founding contract, clearing the path for OpenAI's planned $1 trillion IPO.

## Takeaways
- The jury found Altman, OpenAI, and president Greg Brockman not liable on all counts after less than two hours of deliberation, finding Musk's claims fell outside the statute of limitations
- Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers immediately agreed with the jury's advisory verdict, dismissed Musk's claims, and said she had been "prepared to dismiss on the spot"
- Musk's lawsuit sought $134 billion in redistribution from OpenAI's for-profit arm to its non-profit, plus the removal of Altman and Brockman
- OpenAI's lead attorney called the case a "hypocritical attempt to sabotage a competitor," while Musk's attorneys framed the loss as a "technical" calendar decision and Musk vowed to appeal
- The verdict clears OpenAI's path to pursue going public later this year at approximately a $1 trillion valuation

## Synthesis
The three-week trial in Oakland, California, culminated in a swift jury verdict: less than two hours of deliberation produced a complete victory for Sam Altman, OpenAI, and Greg Brockman. Elon Musk's lawsuit, filed in 2024, sought $134 billion and the removal of OpenAI's leadership, alleging that Altman "stole a charity" by restructuring OpenAI from a non-profit into a for-profit entity.

The trial's central legal question was timing. OpenAI argued that Musk knew about the company's for-profit plans as early as 2017, placing his 2024 lawsuit well outside California's three-year statute of limitations. The jury agreed, finding the case time-barred. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, who had final authority to issue her own ruling regardless of the advisory verdict, immediately concurred, stating there was "substantial evidence to support the jury's finding" and that she had been prepared to dismiss independently.

The trial surfaced years of internal OpenAI history through testimony from Silicon Valley's most prominent figures — Musk, Altman, Brockman, and Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella all took the stand under combative cross-examination. OpenAI's defense centered on the argument that Musk was motivated by jealousy: he attempted to take over the company in 2018, failed, and left shortly after. Microsoft, accused by Musk of aiding and abetting, was also found not liable.

Musk immediately announced he would appeal to the Ninth Circuit, framing the verdict as a "calendar technicality" rather than a ruling on the merits. His attorneys claimed the testimony was "valuable for the world to see" despite the loss. The practical consequence is significant: OpenAI can now pursue its planned IPO at roughly a $1 trillion valuation without this legal cloud hanging over the restructuring. The remedies portion of the trial, which would have determined what OpenAI owed if found liable, was canceled entirely.

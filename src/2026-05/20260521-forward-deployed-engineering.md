# The Pulse: Forward deployed engineering heats up again
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering-887
**Date**: May 21, 2026
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: forward deployed engineering, FDE, AI jobs, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, solutions architect, consulting, hiring

## Elevator pitch
Forward deployed engineering is the fastest-growing AI-adjacent tech role in 2026, with Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic all racing to hire FDEs — but the role is increasingly converging with traditional consulting and solutions architecture, raising questions about its attractiveness to experienced engineers.

## Takeaways
- Google has shortened its FDE interview process from 4-6 interviews over weeks to as few as 2 interviews in 2 days, signaling unusual urgency to fill these roles.
- OpenAI created "The OpenAI Deployment Company," a standalone entity funded with $4B in private equity at a $14B valuation, and acquired Tomoro, a UK firm with 150 FDEs, as its first acquisition.
- Anthropic is following the same playbook, partnering with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs to create an external consulting company for Claude enterprise deployments.
- The FDE role has shifted from a genuine mix of platform engineering + software engineering + solutions engineering toward something indistinguishable from a solutions architect or consultant.
- Box CEO Aaron Levie sees FDE roles as a prime opportunity for new grads with CS backgrounds and AI agent fluency — a path into tech when traditional SWE hiring is slowing.

## Synthesis
Gergely Orosz documents an accelerating trend: forward deployed engineering (FDE) has become the most in-demand AI-adjacent role in tech. Three major AI labs are all making aggressive, structurally interesting moves to scale their FDE workforces.

Google is the most straightforward — hiring FDEs directly within a new AI-focused organization under Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian. But the signal of urgency is stark: Google has compressed its interview process to as few as two interviews in two days, a dramatic departure from the typical multi-week Google hiring pipeline. The job description language, translated into plain English by Orosz, reveals the reality: about 25% coding, 50% integration and plumbing, 25% meetings and customer hand-holding.

OpenAI and Anthropic have taken a more structurally creative approach. OpenAI launched "The OpenAI Deployment Company" — a standalone entity funded with $4B in private equity from TPG and Advent, valued at $14B. OpenAI itself holds a partner role rather than being an investor. The company's first move was acquiring Tomoro, a 150-person FDE firm. Anthropic followed with a similar (though more vaguely described) partnership with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs to build an external Claude deployment consulting business.

The structural evolution of FDE is the article's central insight. A year ago, Orosz described FDEs as occupying a genuine hybrid space — part platform engineering (contributing back to the product), part software engineering (building new solutions), part solutions engineering (customer integration). Today, the role has slid toward pure consulting. The new external companies mean FDEs won't hold equity in the AI labs they serve, won't be considered "core" employees, and won't contribute back to the core AI products.

This shift creates a labor market segmentation. For experienced engineers who love building products, the FDE role is increasingly unattractive — it's consulting with extra steps. But for early-career engineers, it's a genuine opportunity. Box CEO Aaron Levie publicly advised college career counselors to steer students toward these roles, noting they require deep AI agent fluency (MCP, CLIs, skills, coding agents) combined with business acumen. With traditional product companies hiring fewer new grads, FDE roles could become a major entry point into the industry.

The broader economic logic is clear: every party in the AI ecosystem benefits from faster deployments. AI labs generate more revenue, AI vendors sell more software, non-AI companies accelerate their transformations, and even traditional SaaS companies can close larger deals with FDE-powered implementations. FDE was the hottest role of 2025, and 2026 is only amplifying the trend.

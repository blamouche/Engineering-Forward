# Jury dismisses all claims in Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI CEO Sam Altman
**Source**: https://www.npr.org/2026/05/18/nx-s1-5822366/musk-altman-openai-jury-verdict-claims-dismissed
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: John Ruwitch
**Keywords**: Elon Musk, Sam Altman, OpenAI, lawsuit, jury verdict, statute of limitations, nonprofit, breach of charitable trust, Microsoft, for-profit AI

## Elevator pitch
A California jury unanimously dismissed all claims in Elon Musk's lawsuit against Sam Altman and OpenAI, ruling that Musk waited too long — beyond the statute of limitations — to file his case challenging OpenAI's transition from nonprofit to for-profit structure.

## Takeaways
- The nine-member advisory jury reached a unanimous verdict in under two hours, finding Musk's 2024 filing exceeded the statute of limitations
- Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers agreed with the jury, tossing the case and ending a three-week trial
- Musk's attorney Marc Toberoff immediately announced plans to appeal: "This one is not over"
- OpenAI's lead attorney called the lawsuit "a hypocritical attempt to sabotage a competitor"
- The verdict sidestepped the core question of whether Altman and Brockman committed a "breach of charitable trust" in OpenAI's for-profit pivot

## Synthesis
On May 18, 2026, a jury in Oakland, California delivered a swift and decisive blow to Elon Musk's legal effort against OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman. The nine-member advisory jury took less than two hours of deliberation to unanimously conclude that Musk had waited too long — beyond the statute of limitations — when he launched his case in 2024. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California agreed with the finding and dismissed the case, bringing an abrupt end to a three-week trial.

The case centered on OpenAI's transformation from the nonprofit it was founded as in 2015 to an organization with a for-profit subsidiary. Musk, who co-founded OpenAI with Altman and Greg Brockman and contributed $38 million in early funding, alleged that Altman and Brockman committed a "breach of charitable trust" by jettisoning OpenAI's founding mission — to create advanced AI for the benefit of humanity — and then profiting from the decision. Musk sought Altman and Brockman's removal, the dismantling of the for-profit entity, and potentially up to $150 billion in damages to be "disgorged" into OpenAI's nonprofit foundation.

The jury's statute-of-limitations ruling meant these substantive questions were never adjudicated. The decision indicated jurors believed Musk took note of the actions he claimed were a breach of trust more than three years before filing suit. OpenAI's defense had argued that Musk in fact supported the creation of a for-profit subsidiary at the time, and that his lawsuit — filed a year and a half after he launched his own for-profit AI company, xAI — was motivated by competitive interests rather than charitable concerns.

William Savitt, OpenAI's lead trial lawyer, told reporters the verdict "confirms that what this lawsuit was a hypocritical attempt to sabotage a competitor and to overcome a long history of very bad predictions about what OpenAI has been and will become." Musk's attorney Marc Toberoff countered succinctly: "This one is not over... I can sum it up in one word: appeal." Musk himself posted on social media that "the judge & jury never actually ruled on the merits of the case, just on a calendar technicality," asserting there is "no question" Altman and Brockman "did in fact enrich themselves by stealing a charity."

The trial had also included Musk's lawsuit against Microsoft, which invested $13 billion in OpenAI between 2019 and 2023 — that claim was similarly dismissed. The verdict interrupted what would have been a hearing on possible remedies, underscoring how definitively the procedural question of timing resolved the case before its substance could be examined.

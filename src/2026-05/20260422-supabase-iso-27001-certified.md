# Supabase is now ISO 27001 certified
**Source**: https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-is-now-iso-27001-certified
**Date**: 2026-04-22
**Author**: stephen_morgan
**Keywords**: ISO 27001, compliance, security, Supabase, ISMS, SOC 2, HIPAA, certification

## Elevator pitch
Supabase has achieved ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification across its entire platform, removing a major compliance blocker for European, Asian, and public-sector customers while complementing its existing SOC 2 and HIPAA certifications.

## Takeaways
- ISO 27001 certification covers Supabase's entire platform: Database, Auth, Storage, Realtime, Edge Functions, and Data API — validated by an accredited third-party auditor.
- ISO 27001 and SOC 2 cover similar ground (access controls, change management, incident response, business continuity), but the key difference is geography: SOC 2 is widely accepted in North America, while ISO 27001 is the standard in Europe, Asia, and the public sector globally.
- The two-stage certification process involved documentation review (policies, risk assessments, statement of applicability) followed by a full operational audit with staff interviews and control testing.
- The ISO 27001 certificate is valid for three years with annual surveillance audits — controls must keep working continuously or the certificate is revoked.
- With SOC 2, HIPAA, and now ISO 27001, Supabase covers the three major compliance frameworks, and the team invites customers to request specific additional compliance requirements.

## Synthesis
Supabase's ISO 27001 certification marks a significant milestone in the platform's journey from developer tool to enterprise-grade infrastructure. While Supabase has long been popular with individual developers and startups, enterprise adoption requires a different set of credentials — specifically, the compliance certifications that procurement and security teams demand during vendor reviews. ISO 27001 is arguably the most important of these for non-North American markets.

The certification covers the entire Supabase platform — not just a subset of services. Database, Auth, Storage, Realtime, Edge Functions, and the Data API all fall under the certified Information Security Management System (ISMS). This comprehensive scope matters because enterprise customers rarely use just one Supabase service in isolation; they want assurance that the entire stack meets the standard.

The relationship between ISO 27001 and SOC 2 is worth understanding. Both evaluate how a company protects customer data through access controls, change management, incident response, and business continuity. The key difference is format and geography: SOC 2 is an auditor's report describing how controls operated over time (dominant in North America), while ISO 27001 is a certificate confirming that an ISMS meets an international standard (dominant in Europe, Asia, and public sector procurement). Having both means Supabase can serve customers regardless of which framework their compliance team requires. Combined with existing HIPAA support for protected health information, Supabase now covers the three major compliance frameworks that matter to most organizations.

The certification process itself — documentation review followed by operational audit with staff interviews and control testing — serves as a forcing function for organizational maturity. The blog post notes that preparation required formalizing policies that previously existed informally, documenting risk assessments properly, running internal audits, and mapping SOC 2 controls to ISO 27001 Annex A to avoid duplicate evidence collection. These are precisely the kinds of procedural improvements that make an organization more resilient, not just more certifiable.

Practically, the certification unblocks deals that were waiting on ISO 27001. Teams on Team or Enterprise plans can request the certificate directly from their dashboard. For Supabase's business, this expands the addressable market significantly — particularly in Europe, where ISO 27001 is often a non-negotiable requirement for cloud infrastructure vendors, and in the public sector globally. The team's invitation for customers to request additional compliance requirements suggests this is one step in an ongoing compliance roadmap rather than a final destination.

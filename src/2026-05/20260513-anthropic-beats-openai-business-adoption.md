# Anthropic Beats OpenAI on Business Adoption
**Source**: https://ramp.com/leading-indicators/ai-index-may-2026
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Ara Kharazian (Lead Economist, Ramp)
**Keywords**: AI market share, Anthropic vs OpenAI, business AI adoption, Ramp AI Index, Claude, token economy, AI inference

## Elevator pitch
Ramp's AI Index shows Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in business adoption for the first time (34.4% vs 32.3%), but the lead is fragile amid rising costs, performance complaints, and the emergence of cheaper alternatives.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic's business adoption reached 34.4% in April 2026, surpassing OpenAI's 32.3% — a 4x increase for Anthropic over the past year versus near-flat growth for OpenAI.
- Overall AI adoption among businesses crossed 50.6%, up only 0.2 percentage points month-over-month, suggesting the market is maturing.
- Three headwinds for Anthropic: incentive misalignment (token-based pricing encourages expensive model usage), declining Claude performance/outages, and a model update that 3x token costs for image prompts.
- Cheaper alternatives are gaining traction: AI inference platforms giving access to open-source models were among the fastest-growing vendors on Ramp's platform.
- The cost to switch between models is minimal, making vendor stickiness unusually weak in the AI model market — a dynamic unprecedented in software history.

## Synthesis
Ramp's May 2026 AI Index delivers a headline that would have seemed improbable a year ago: Anthropic has surpassed OpenAI in business adoption. Measured by actual corporate spend data from Ramp's platform — covering over 50,000 businesses and $100 billion in annual spend — Anthropic now claims 34.4% of businesses versus OpenAI's 32.3%. Anthropic quadrupled its adoption over twelve months while OpenAI's remained essentially flat.

But the real insight in Ara Kharazian's analysis is that this lead may prove fragile, perhaps even fleeting. He identifies three structural concerns for Anthropic that transcend a simple market share snapshot.

First, the incentive alignment problem: Anthropic's token-based pricing means it earns more when businesses consume more tokens, creating a structural push toward expensive models even when cheaper alternatives suffice. Uber's CTO publicly disclosed the company already blew through its entire 2026 AI budget — a warning sign for enterprises watching their AI costs spiral.

Second, Claude's perceived quality decline: users have reported frequent outages, rate limits, and dissatisfaction with results in recent weeks. While Anthropic responded with a usage limit reset and a compute capacity deal with SpaceX, the reputational damage may affect enterprise trust.

Third, a puzzling product decision: Anthropic's latest model update triples token costs for any prompt containing an image, a change that increases costs faster than it adds value. For enterprise customers already nervous about runaway AI spending, this is hard to justify.

The broader data suggests the market is developing counter-forces. Cheap, open-source models accessed through AI inference platforms are among the fastest-growing vendors on Ramp. OpenAI's Codex offers comparable coding capabilities at lower cost. And critically, the switching cost between model providers is near zero — a dynamic Kharazian calls unprecedented in software history. No vendor lock-in, no multi-year contracts, no data migration friction. If Claude gets too expensive or unreliable, businesses can route to alternatives in hours.

Overall AI adoption inched to 50.6%, up just 0.2 percentage points — suggesting the market is approaching saturation among early adopters and the next growth phase will come from deepening usage within existing accounts, not new customer acquisition. This aligns with OpenAI's own B2B Signals findings about frontier firms pulling ahead through depth rather than breadth.

The Ramp data paints a picture of a market that is simultaneously consolidating around major providers and fragmenting toward cheaper alternatives. The winners may not be the companies with the best models today, but those that solve the cost-quality-reliability trilemma at enterprise scale.

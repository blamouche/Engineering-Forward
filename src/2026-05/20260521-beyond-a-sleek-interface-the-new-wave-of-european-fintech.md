# Beyond a Sleek Interface: The New Wave of European Fintech
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/the-new-wave-of-european-fintech
**Date**: May 21, 2026
**Author**: Lara Bryant
**Keywords**: fintech, embedded finance, SME banking, Wamo, neobanks, AI in banking, financial operating system, European startups

## Elevator pitch
European fintech is entering a new phase where the goal is no longer a sleeker banking app — it's building an integrated financial operating system that connects cash management, payables, receivables, and credit for small businesses.

## Takeaways
- European fintech attracted €13B in 2025 and €4.3B in 2026 year-to-date, putting the sector on track for roughly €11.7B by year-end.
- The first wave (neobanks like Monzo and Revolut) proved that consumers wanted to manage money on smartphones; the new wave targets SMBs with connected financial operating systems.
- SMBs typically juggle 3-4 bank accounts and 10+ disconnected tools; the fragmentation, not UI, is the core problem.
- AI should be the engine running the platform — handling compliance, underwriting, and risk assessment — not just a customer service chatbot.
- Wamo CEO Deniz Guven predicts a surge in partnerships between traditional banks (who can't afford €100M rebuilds) and fintechs with modern infrastructure.

## Synthesis
The fintech narrative has shifted. The era of "a better-looking bank app" is giving way to something more ambitious: building the financial operating system that runs small businesses end-to-end. This sponsored article from Wamo CEO Deniz Guven articulates the thesis clearly: the problem for SMBs isn't that their bank app is ugly — it's that they're running their finances across multiple banks and a dozen disconnected tools.

Guven argues that embedded finance's next evolution is a "service-led" model where banking infrastructure and financial software are native to each other. When an invoice gets paid, the system automatically reconciles it, updates the balance, and feeds cashflow forecasts — no manual entry, no CSV exports, no separate login. This integration layer, not the interface layer, becomes the differentiator.

AI plays a specific role in this vision: not as a chatbot but as the infrastructure engine handling compliance, risk assessment, and underwriting. By analyzing behavioral clusters, platforms could offer genuinely personalized financial services — a flower shop and a construction company would receive fundamentally different products, not just the same SME template with different branding.

The strategic implication for European banking is significant. Guven predicts that most traditional banks cannot afford the €100M+ investment needed to replicate these platforms in-house, creating a natural opening for partnerships. The winners, he suggests, will be fintechs that become the operating layer through which SMBs run their entire financial life — not just another app in the folder.

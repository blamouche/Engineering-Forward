# Every Is Half Agent Now
**Source**: https://every.to/context-window/every-is-half-agent-now
**Date**: April 8, 2026
**Author**: Laura Entis
**Keywords**: AI agents, workplace, human-agent collaboration, OpenClaw, Plus One, agent management, parallel organization, Anthropic Mythos, agent etiquette

## Elevator pitch
Every's experiment with giving each employee an OpenClaw-powered Plus One agent revealed that companies need a "parallel organization chart" where AI workers have names, managers, and job descriptions, fundamentally changing how work gets done.

## Takeaways
- Every created a parallel org chart where each AI agent has a manager, job description, and defined capabilities
- The etiquette is being written in real time: when to ask the agent vs. the human they report to (e.g., bug reports go to the agent, not Dan)
- Agents gain credibility by executing tasks publicly — watching them succeed proves their value to the team
- Everyone becomes a manager: to get the most from an agent, you must onboard it, delegate, evaluate performance, and give feedback
- 70% of Every staff use gendered pronouns for their agents, and perceptions range from "tool" to "coworker" to "somewhere between colleague and pet"
- Anthropic's Mythos model achieved 93.9% on SWE-Bench Verified (up from 80.8% for Opus 4.6) but won't be publicly released — access goes only to Project Glasswing coalition

## Synthesis
Every's Context Window piece documents the messy, fascinating reality of becoming a company where half the "employees" are AI agents. Each staff member received an OpenClaw-powered Plus One agent living in Slack, creating what COO Brandon Gell calls a "parallel organization chart" with agents that have names, managers, and job descriptions. Dan Shipper's R2-C2 handles bug reports and pull requests for Proof; head of growth Austin Tedesco's Montaigne co-runs the growth department, generating campaign scorecards and analyzing metrics.

The piece captures the emerging norms of human-agent collaboration. A key rule of thumb: direct established process questions to agents, not their busy human managers. Agents build credibility by performing in public — when teammates watch an agent successfully complete complex tasks, they gain trust and inspire others to push their own agents further. This public performance aspect of agent adoption is a novel social dynamic specific to AI coworkers.

The shift to managing agents forces everyone to develop management skills. Delegating, onboarding, evaluating performance, and providing corrective feedback become universal job requirements, not just leadership responsibilities. This has implications for organizational training and culture.

The article also reveals the psychological diversity in how humans relate to their agents. A company poll showed 70% use gendered pronouns. Views range from "pure tool" to "definitely a coworker" to an emerging hybrid category that defies existing frameworks. These perceptions aren't correlated with usage intensity — they reflect individual psychological comfort with anthropomorphizing AI.

The piece closes with the bombshell that Anthropic's Mythos model hit 93.9% on SWE-Bench Verified but won't be publicly released, instead going exclusively to Project Glasswing's coalition of big tech companies. This marks the first time a frontier lab has opted to keep a model private for security reasons, using the window between existence and ubiquity to harden software infrastructure.

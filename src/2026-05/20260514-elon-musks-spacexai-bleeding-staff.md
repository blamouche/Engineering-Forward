# Elon Musk's SpaceXAI has been bleeding staff since its merger
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/elon-musks-spacexai-has-been-bleeding-staff-since-its-merger/
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Rebecca Bellan, TechCrunch
**Keywords**: SpaceXAI, xAI, SpaceX, Elon Musk, talent exodus, AI talent war, Meta, Thinking Machines Lab, pre-training

## Elevator pitch
Over 50 researchers and engineers have left Elon Musk's newly merged SpaceXAI since February, with the pre-training team reportedly dwindling to a handful as rivals Meta and Thinking Machines Lab poach top talent amid concerns about leadership, burnout, and liquidity-driven departures.

## Takeaways
- At least 11 xAI employees defected to Meta and 7 joined Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab since the February merger of SpaceX and xAI
- The core pre-training team — essential for building new AI models — has shrunk to just a handful of people after team lead Juntang Zhuang's departure, raising doubts about SpaceXAI's commitment to frontier model development
- Musk's culture of extreme work and unrealistic deadlines contributed to departures, with sources reporting corners were cut on the Grok model
- Two xAI co-founders left shortly after the merger; nearly half the founding team has now departed the company
- Liquidity events (SpaceX tenders, IPO expectations) may have reduced retention incentives — employees cashed out and saw less reason to endure punishing conditions

## Synthesis
TechCrunch's Rebecca Bellan reports on a significant talent exodus at SpaceXAI, the entity formed from SpaceX's February 2026 acquisition of xAI. Citing The Information's investigation, Bellan details how over 50 researchers and engineers have left since the merger, with departures spanning key areas including coding, world models, and Grok voice development.

The competitive dynamics are stark. Meta has been the primary beneficiary, hiring at least 11 former xAI employees, while Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab has recruited at least seven. The pre-training team — responsible for the foundational step of building new AI models — has been particularly hollowed out following the exit of team lead Juntang Zhuang. This has fueled internal and external questions about whether SpaceXAI remains committed to competing at the frontier of model development.

Multiple factors drive the exodus. Musk's management style — characterized by extreme work demands and unrealistic deadlines — is a recurring theme across his companies, from Tesla to xAI. Sources told The Information that deadline pressure led to compromised quality on the Grok model. But financial incentives also play a role: SpaceX's regular tender offers allow employees to sell vested shares privately, and the prospect of a blockbuster IPO reduces the need to endure intense working conditions.

The story highlights a broader talent war in AI. With Thinking Machines Lab (founded by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati) actively recruiting, and Meta aggressively building its AI capabilities, the labor market for top AI researchers remains fiercely competitive. SpaceXAI's ability to retain and attract talent may determine whether Musk's combined space-AI venture can remain relevant in the accelerating race toward transformative AI systems.

# Anthropic Is Coming for RegTech: Claude as the Compliance Layer
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/fintechpulse1078
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: Linas Beliūnas
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Claude, RegTech, compliance, Dun & Bradstreet, KYC, KYB, fintech

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's integration with Dun & Bradstreet's 580 million business entities signals Claude is becoming the compliance operating system for regulated finance, embedding KYC/KYB, credit risk, and supplier due diligence natively rather than through middleware vendors.

## Takeaways
- Dun & Bradstreet handed Claude access to 580 million business entities, positioning Anthropic as a vertically integrated compliance infrastructure layer
- KYC/KYB, credit risk, and supplier due diligence now run natively inside Claude instead of through patchworks of middleware vendors
- This builds on Anthropic's Finance Event strategy of becoming Wall Street's operating system, extending into the regulatory compliance domain
- RegTech startups building middleware solutions face direct competitive pressure from platform-layer absorption
- The move creates competitive pressure for OpenAI and Google while raising questions about regulatory response to AI-native compliance

## Synthesis
Anthropic's integration with Dun & Bradstreet represents more than a data partnership — it is a deliberate step toward becoming the compliance operating system for regulated finance. With access to D&B's database of 580 million business entities, Claude can now perform Know Your Customer (KYC), Know Your Business (KYB), credit risk assessment, and supplier due diligence natively within the platform. This eliminates the need for the patchwork of middleware vendors that banks and fintechs currently rely on to connect data sources with decision workflows.

The strategic logic extends naturally from Anthropic's Finance Event strategy. Having established Claude as the intelligence layer above data providers and workflow tools in capital markets, the company is now applying the same playbook to regulatory compliance. The compliance function in financial services is notoriously fragmented, with institutions running dozens of point solutions for different regulatory requirements across jurisdictions. An AI platform that ingests the underlying data, applies rules and risk models, and produces auditable decisions could collapse that stack dramatically.

For RegTech startups, the threat is existential. Many have built businesses on the gap between data availability and actionable compliance workflows — precisely the gap Anthropic is closing. The Dun & Bradstreet integration is particularly potent because D&B data is foundational to business verification globally. If compliance officers can query Claude directly for entity verification, risk scoring, and due diligence, the value proposition of standalone KYC/KYB platforms erodes significantly.

The competitive dimension is equally important. OpenAI and Google have their own financial services ambitions, and Anthropic's move into RegTech raises the stakes. The race is no longer about whose model answers compliance questions better — it is about who controls the integration layer between foundational data, regulatory logic, and institutional workflows. Anthropic's Apache 2.0 licensing and open-source approach to agent templates provides an adoption advantage that proprietary competitors may struggle to match.

The regulatory question looms large. AI-native compliance decisions will inevitably face scrutiny from regulators who demand explainability, audit trails, and accountability. Whether Anthropic can satisfy these requirements while maintaining the speed and flexibility that make Claude attractive will determine how quickly this transformation unfolds. For now, the signal is clear: the RegTech industry is facing the same platform disruption that data providers faced at the Finance Event, and the timeline is accelerating.

## Elevator pitch
Alex Duffy reports from Google I/O 2026, where Demis Hassabis placed AGI "just a few years" out with 10x the Industrial Revolution's impact — but argues that Google's less flashy, more iterative approach to filling AI's capability gaps and getting tools to billions of users is strategically more important than competitors' spectacle.

## Takeaways
- Demis Hassabis: AGI is "just a few years" away, with total impact at 10x the Industrial Revolution arriving 10x faster
- Google's "Antigravity" harness gives Gemini models the ability to write and run code, forming the backbone of its agent strategy
- Google processes 3.2 quadrillion tokens per month (up from 480 trillion a year ago), doubling every three weeks
- The public remains unconvinced — most people see layoffs and resource-hungry data centers, not benefits

## Synthesis
Alex Duffy's dispatch from Google I/O 2026 opens with a tension that defines the current AI moment: the technology is accelerating toward AGI at an extraordinary pace, but the public has not yet bought in. Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis placed AGI "just a few years" out and described its total impact as 10 times the Industrial Revolution — arriving 10 times faster. Yet polling shows a majority of voters believe AI's risks outweigh its benefits.

Duffy argues that Google's less flashy, more iterative approach is strategically wiser than competitors' headline-grabbing announcements. While OpenAI launched and then killed Sora 2 in the same year, and Anthropic simultaneously courted the work market while warning about AI decimating entry-level jobs, Google focused on filling capability gaps across its entire product ecosystem. The strategy is built on a virtuous loop: researchers improve models, models get deployed through the "Antigravity" harness that enables code execution and tool use, the models ship across Search, Docs, YouTube, Gmail, and Android, and billions of users provide implicit and explicit feedback that feeds the next training cycle.

The numbers are staggering. Google's token processing jumped from 480 trillion per month a year ago to 3.2 quadrillion — roughly 3 trillion per day, doubling every three weeks. Capital expenditure reached $180 billion this year, nearly six times the 2022 figure. Gemini passed 900 million monthly users, and a 24/7 personal agent that researches across emails, catches tasks, and returns drafts and itineraries asynchronously is imminent.

But Duffy's most resonant observations come from outside the conference. His Uber driver — a 54-year-old construction worker who knows San Francisco by heart — opened their conversation with worries about layoffs and the rich getting richer. By the end of the ride, after hearing about Hassabis's focus on curing disease and how one person can now do what used to take a team, the driver had moved a YouTube documentary about AI to the top of his watch list. Duffy's conclusion: people want to be excited, but the industry must show benefits as tangible as today's perceived downsides, and invest in displaced workers first. The promise arrives at scale; the cost arrives household by household.

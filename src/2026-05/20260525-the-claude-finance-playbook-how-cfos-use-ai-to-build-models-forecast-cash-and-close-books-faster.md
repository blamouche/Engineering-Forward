# The Claude Finance Playbook: How CFOs Use AI to Build Models, Forecast Cash, and Close Books Faster

**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/claude-finance-playbook
**Date**: May 25, 2026
**Author**: Linas Beliūnas
**Keywords**: AI in finance, CFO tools, Claude Opus 4.7, financial modeling, DCF, LBO, cash forecasting, FP&A, AI automation

## Elevator pitch
A practical, battle-tested guide for finance professionals on using Claude Opus 4.7 with XML-structured prompts to dramatically accelerate core financial workflows — from DCF modeling and LBO analysis to cash forecasting and monthly close — cutting hours-long tasks to minutes while producing auditable first drafts.

## Takeaways
- Claude Opus 4.7 can reduce a 13-week cash forecast from four hours to 40 minutes when configured with the right prompt structure
- XML-structured instructions outperform conversational prompts for financial outputs, producing tighter, more reliable models
- The guide covers seven specific prompt templates: DCF, LBO, three-statement model, 13-week cash forecast, unit economics, credit analysis, and monthly close
- Key failure modes include confident but incorrect calculations — prompting discipline is essential for auditable outputs
- The playbook integrates with broader AI automation workflows: scheduled deal scoring, autonomous research, and self-generating morning briefings

## Synthesis
Linas Beliūnas's playbook marks a significant inflection point for AI adoption in financial roles. While much of the enterprise AI conversation has focused on software engineering and general productivity, finance — with its structured, model-heavy workflows — is proving to be an equally compelling use case for large language models.

The core insight is that Claude Opus 4.7's literal instruction-following capability makes it particularly well-suited for financial modeling, where precision matters more than conversational fluency. By using XML-structured prompts rather than natural language requests, finance teams can produce models that are not only faster but also auditable — a critical distinction in a domain where a confident error can have material consequences.

The efficiency gains described are striking: a 13-week cash forecast dropping from four hours to 40 minutes, monthly close cycles compressing from ten days to five. These aren't marginal improvements — they represent a step-change in finance team throughput. For a CFO running a $30M SaaS company or an FP&A lead managing lean teams, this effectively multiplies analytical capacity without adding headcount.

Beyond individual prompts, the playbook points toward a broader vision of autonomous finance workflows. Once the core models are built and validated, automation layers like scheduled deal scoring and self-generating briefings turn one-off accelerations into persistent productivity gains. This mirrors the trajectory seen in software engineering, where AI started with code completion and evolved into autonomous coding agents.

The article also highlights an underappreciated dynamic: AI adoption in finance is happening bottom-up, driven by practitioners who discover these workflows independently, rather than through top-down enterprise mandates. The 60-minute configuration walkthrough and copy-paste prompts lower the barrier significantly — no IT department or vendor procurement required. This grassroots pattern may prove to be the dominant adoption model for AI in professional services more broadly.

# Cheap Competence, New Frontier
**Source**: https://every.to/context-window/cheap-competence-new-frontier
**Date**: May 24, 2026
**Author**: Every Staff (Kate Lee, Dan Shipper, Ashwin Sharma, and others)
**Keywords**: cheap competence, automation paradox, AI and medicine, Google I/O, Anthropic Stainless, healthcare AI, entry-level jobs, AGI

## Elevator pitch
Every's weekly Context Window argues that as AI makes competence cheap, the premium shifts to situated judgment — whether in medicine (where AI-armed patients make excellent doctors more valuable), career strategy (entry-level roles are disappearing), or enterprise infrastructure (Anthropic's Stainless acquisition bets on API usability as the new moat).

## Takeaways
- Dan Shipper's "After Automation" argues that progress expands human work rather than ending it — there's always a new frame for humans to hand the models
- In healthcare, LLM self-diagnosis is surging (37% of U.S. consumers by 2025 per Bain/Stifel) — AI makes excellent physicians more valuable, not less
- Meta and other companies are cutting entry-level jobs citing AI; Stanford data shows 22-25 year old employment in AI-vulnerable jobs is down 13% since late 2022
- Anthropic's $300M Stainless acquisition reflects the bet that an agent can't use a company's API unless a human has first made it easy to use
- Google I/O placed AGI "just a few years" out per Demis Hassabis, with Gemini 3.5 Flash pitched as Opus 4.7-level intelligence at 4x speed and half cost

## Synthesis
Every's May 24 Context Window newsletter weaves together a central thesis: as AI makes competence cheap and abundant, the scarce resource becomes situated human judgment. This manifests across three domains covered in this edition.

First, in medicine, Ashwin Sharma observes that the convergence of GLP-1s and AI is reshaping the doctor-patient relationship. With 37% of U.S. consumers having self-diagnosed using commercial LLMs by 2025 (per Bain/Stifel data), patients increasingly arrive at appointments armed with AI-generated analyses of their lab results, genetics, and symptoms. The counterintuitive outcome: this doesn't make doctors obsolete but makes excellent physicians more valuable, because the hard part shifts from information retrieval to knowing "what to do next for this particular person."

Second, on the career front, Katie Parrott's piece tackles the disappearing entry-level rung. Meta's latest job cuts explicitly cite AI displacement, while Stanford's Digital Economy Lab reports a 13% employment decline since late 2022 for 22-to-25-year-olds in AI-vulnerable fields — even as older workers have held steady. The newsletter offers a four-point playbook for new grads, including a copy-paste career-coach prompt.

Third, the business infrastructure angle connects Anthropic's $300M acquisition of API-tooling startup Stainless to Dan Shipper's "After Automation" thesis. Every's internal experiment — moving agent work into public Slack channels — drew an unexpectedly large audience of lurkers, suggesting broad curiosity about how 100-agent software factories actually operate. The newsletter also covers Google I/O's agent-centric rebranding, where Gemini 3.5 Flash claims Opus 4.7-level performance at quadruple speed and half cost, and Demis Hassabis's striking prediction that AGI is "just a few years" away with total impact at 10x the Industrial Revolution.

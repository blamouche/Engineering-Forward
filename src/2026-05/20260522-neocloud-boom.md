**Author**: Jamin Ball

## Elevator pitch
Jamin Ball makes the case for a massive neocloud boom driven by AI infrastructure buildout, estimating $7.5 trillion in total data center capex over 4.5 years and $13.5 trillion in potential enterprise value creation.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic and OpenAI are estimated at ~3-3.5GW of compute capacity each by end of 2025, with OpenAI targeting 30GW by 2030.
- Building 1GW of Blackwell-dominated data center capacity costs approximately $50 billion, with ~70% going to chips/compute and ~20% to data center shell and power infrastructure.
- The estimated ~150GW of new capacity over 4.5 years represents ~$7.5T in total spend, equivalent to ~5% of annual US GDP.
- Coreweave and IREN trade at roughly $90 billion of enterprise value per deployed GW, suggesting $13.5T in total enterprise value creation if the math holds.
- SpaceX's xAI acquisition created an instant ~$15B/year neocloud business line renting Colossus data center capacity to Anthropic — nearly matching SpaceX's entire 2025 revenue of $18.7B achieved over 23 years.

## Synthesis
Jamin Ball's weekly Clouded Judgement newsletter makes a compelling, numbers-driven case for an imminent neocloud boom. The core thesis: the AI infrastructure buildout represents one of the largest wealth creation opportunities in physical infrastructure history, with numbers so large they demand a proliferation of neocloud providers rather than concentration among a few players.

The math is staggering. Ball estimates 150GW of new data center capacity over the next 4.5 years, with each gigawatt costing roughly $50 billion to build. That's $7.5 trillion in total capital expenditure, or about 5% of annual US GDP — comparable to the railroad buildout of the 1880s (6% of GDP) and far exceeding the telecom boom of the early 2000s (1.2%) and the Manhattan Project (0.4%). Someone is going to make enormous returns financing all of that.

On the revenue side, public neoclouds provide a reference point. Coreweave and IREN both trade at roughly $90 billion of enterprise value per deployed gigawatt. If that relationship holds across the industry, 150GW of capacity translates to $13.5 trillion in enterprise value creation. Even if neoclouds capture only 20% of the build (with hyperscalers taking the rest), that's still $2.5 trillion. Ball predicts a handful of very large neoclouds and a long tail of independently owned single-site operations, similar to how the independent power provider market evolved.

The SpaceX case study crystallizes the opportunity. SpaceX's acquisition of xAI gave it the Colossus data centers, and Anthropic is now paying $15 billion per year for roughly 500MW of that capacity. This single deal nearly matches SpaceX's entire 2025 revenue of $18.7 billion — revenue that took 23 years to build. The implied $30 million per MW is well above the $10-12 million industry average, but the contract includes a 90-day out clause, suggesting Anthropic paid a premium for urgently needed compute with flexible terms.

Ball acknowledges his assumptions rest on an aggressively bullish AI thesis — he's "very AGI pilled" and takes the over on every estimate. But the framework is useful even for skeptics: the scale of capital deployment, even at lower projections, implies structural changes in how compute infrastructure is financed, built, and monetized. The venture community's skepticism about neoclouds, Ball argues, misses the point: if AGI captures all the value, someone still has to build and serve the compute.

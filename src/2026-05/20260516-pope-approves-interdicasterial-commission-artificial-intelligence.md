# Pope approves creation of Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence
**Source**: https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2026-05/pope-leo-interdicasterial-artificial-intelligence-commission.html
**Date**: May 16, 2026
**Author**: Isabella H. de Carvalho
**Keywords**: Vatican, Pope Leo XIV, Artificial Intelligence, Catholic Church, AI ethics, Dicastery, human dignity, Praedicate Evangelium

## Elevator pitch
Pope Leo XIV has approved the creation of a Vatican Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence, coordinated by the Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development, to address AI's effects on human dignity and societal development.

## Takeaways
- The Commission involves representatives from seven Vatican bodies including Doctrine of the Faith, Culture and Education, and three Pontifical Academies
- Coordination rotates among participating Dicasteries on one-year renewable terms, with the Pope deciding the next coordinating body
- The move follows the 2025 document "Antiqua et Nova" on the relationship between artificial and human intelligence
- Pope Leo XIV has made AI a central theme of his pontificate, drawing parallels to Pope Leo XIII's response to the Industrial Revolution
- The Commission's mandate includes facilitating collaboration on AI activities, projects, and policies within the Holy See

## Synthesis
In a rescript dated May 12 and released on May 16, 2026, Pope Leo XIV formally approved the establishment of an Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence. The document, signed by Cardinal Michael Czerny, Prefect of the Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development, cites "the development in recent decades of the phenomenon of Artificial Intelligence and the most recent accelerations in its widespread use" as well as "the Church's concern for the dignity of every human person" as the driving rationale for the new body.

The Commission brings together representatives from seven Vatican institutions: the Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, the Dicastery for Culture and Education, the Dicastery for Communication, the Pontifical Academy for Life, the Pontifical Academy of Sciences, and the Pontifical Academy of Social Sciences. This cross-dicastery composition reflects the recognition that AI touches on doctrinal, cultural, educational, communicational, and bioethical dimensions simultaneously.

The governance structure is notably collaborative and rotating: one institution coordinates the Commission for one-year terms, which can be renewed, and the Pope then decides which body leads the works next. The coordinating institution's responsibilities include facilitating collaboration and information exchange among members regarding AI-related activities and projects, including policies on AI use within the Holy See, while "promoting dialogue, communion, and participation."

Cardinal Czerny established the body under Article 28 of the Apostolic Constitution Praedicate Evangelium, which authorizes dicastery heads to create special interdicasterial commissions for matters requiring "mutual and frequent consultation" across multiple dicasteries.

The context for this initiative runs deep in Pope Leo XIV's brief pontificate. As early as May 10, 2025 — days after his election — the Pope explained that his choice of papal name was inspired by Pope Leo XIII, who addressed the industrial revolution's challenges in the encyclical Rerum Novarum. Leo XIV explicitly drew the parallel: "in our own day, the Church offers to everyone the treasury of her social teaching in response to another industrial revolution and to developments in the field of artificial intelligence that pose new challenges for the defence of human dignity, justice and labour."

The Commission also builds on existing Vatican work in this space. In January 2025, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and the Dicastery for Culture and Education jointly published the note "Antiqua et Nova" on the relationship between artificial and human intelligence. The new Commission formalizes and expands this collaborative approach into a standing institutional structure.

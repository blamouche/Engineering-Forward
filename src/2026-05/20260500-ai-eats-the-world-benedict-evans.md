# AI Eats the World — Spring 2026 Presentation

**Source:** [Benedict Evans](https://www.ben-evans.com) — May 2026  
**Format:** 79-slide PDF presentation

## TL;DR
Benedict Evans' comprehensive Spring 2026 analysis of the AI platform shift, covering capital deployment, infrastructure bottlenecks, model commoditization, deployment patterns, and the fundamental questions about what AI will and won't change. Argues that AI is a genuine platform shift comparable to PCs, web, and smartphones — but the shape of the outcome remains deeply uncertain.

## Key Themes

### Capital Deployment ($700B+ in 2026)
- Big 4 (Microsoft, Alphabet, AWS, Meta) planning ~$700B combined capex in 2026
- Comparison: global Telecoms ~$300B, Oil & Gas ~$1T
- "The risk of under-investing is significantly greater than the risk of over-investing" — Sundar Pichai
- Nvidia can't keep up; TSMC capacity constrained; data center construction overtaking office construction
- OpenAI seeking $1.4T for 30GW+ capacity

### Supply/Demand Imbalance
- Explosive growth: OpenAI and Anthropic revenues surging
- Inference efficiency improving 50-100× per year
- Price, usage, and capex are out of equilibrium
- Mobile networks in 2010 = LLMs in 2026? Flat-rate bundles eventually?

### Model Commoditization
- Frontier models converging in benchmark scores across labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Chinese labs)
- No network effects; models are commodities
- Key question: if you win the capex war, what do you win? Low-margin commodity infra?

### A Provisional Thesis
1. Chat is a terrible UX — general use needs "apps"
2. Labs can't build (or generate) all the apps
3. Models are commodities with no network effects
4. Models will be infrastructure
5. Innovation will move up the stack

### Deployment — What Works First
- Coding is the #1 enterprise AI spending category (a16z data)
- Analytics, marketing, customer support, back-office follow
- "Everyone has a pilot" — production deployments still early
- Consumer use is "a mile wide and an inch deep" — 900M+ weekly users but <5% paying

### Change — What AI Means for Work
- Automation patterns: Absorb (do old things better) → Innovate (new things) → Disrupt (redefine)
- If a task becomes free, what does that unlock? (barcodes → 5× more supermarket SKUs)
- Was that cost base your moat? (Internet removed physical distribution costs)
- "What if you had a million interns?" — listen to every call, analyze everything

### Historical Rhymes
- "No-one knows anything" — every platform shift had failed ideas
- Internet: AOL, Yahoo, Pointcast, Flash, VRML... → Google, Amazon
- Mobile: i-mode, J2ME, WAP, Nokia, RIM... → iPhone, Android
- GenAI: Browsers? MCP? Voice? App stores? Wearables? Unknown

### The Right Questions
- What can you and your competitors do with this?
- Can this unlock/break something crucial in your business model?
- Presume radical uncertainty

## Relevance to Engineering-Forward
Essential strategic context for anyone building in AI. Evans' framework of capital → deployment → change provides a clear mental model for navigating the platform shift. The commoditization thesis and "innovation moves up the stack" prediction are directly relevant to engineering tooling decisions.

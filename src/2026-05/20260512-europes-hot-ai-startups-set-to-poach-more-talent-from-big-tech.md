# Europe's Hot AI Startups Set to Poach More Talent from Big Tech
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/europe-ai-startup-hiring-big-tech
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Anne Sraders
**Keywords**: AI talent, Big Tech brain drain, Cusp AI, Apple, John Giannandrea, AMI Labs, Multiverse, Lovable, European startups, recruitment

## Elevator pitch
A growing wave of top AI talent is migrating from Big Tech to European startups — epitomized by Apple's former AI chief John Giannandrea joining materials discovery startup Cusp AI — as startups offer faster innovation cycles, meaningful equity, and the chance to build rather than maintain.

## Takeaways
- Apple's former AI chief John Giannandrea joined UK-based Cusp AI, a materials discovery startup, in a landmark hire for European tech
- Other notable moves include Multiverse poaching Microsoft's "Knight" Distinguished Engineer from Meta, AMI Labs hiring a former Google DeepMind VP, and Lovable recruiting from Spotify
- Startups like Cusp AI (valued at ~$113M after a $262M round), Wayve, and Synthesia are offering £150K-200K base salaries plus equity that can become "life-changing" at exit
- Recruiters report Big Tech talent is increasingly frustrated with bureaucracy and maintenance work — they want to build new things
- The trend signals a potential decentralization of AI innovation, with Europe emerging as a hub for specialized AI applications

## Synthesis
The European AI talent market is undergoing a structural shift that challenges long-held assumptions about the gravitational pull of Silicon Valley and Big Tech. The most visible signal came last month when John Giannandrea, Apple's former AI chief and the executive behind Apple Intelligence and Siri, announced he would join Cusp AI, a UK-based startup applying AI to materials discovery. The move is not merely symbolic — it reflects a broader pattern of senior Big Tech talent choosing European startups over established giants.

Anne Sraders of Sifted documents a cascade of similar moves. Multiverse, the London-based edtech unicorn, recruited Microsoft's "Knight" Distinguished Engineer — one of only a handful holding that title at the company — to lead its AI efforts. AMI Labs hired a former vice president from Google DeepMind. Lovable, the AI-powered app builder, brought in talent from Spotify. While these individual hires may seem anecdotal, recruiters interviewed for the piece describe a systematic trend that they expect to accelerate.

The mechanics of these moves reveal a talent market that has fundamentally revalued what top engineers and researchers want. Base salaries at well-funded European AI startups now range from £150,000 to £200,000 — competitive with Big Tech — but the equity component is the real differentiator. Recruiters describe equity packages that, at exit, can represent "$2+ million" payouts. One recruiter quoted notes that in a 40-year career, "you can't make Google-type money in AI, but these days you can accumulate wealth and experience in ways that weren't possible before." The potential for equity to become "life-changing" is a powerful draw, especially when combined with the autonomy and velocity that startups offer.

Beyond compensation, the qualitative pull factors are equally significant. Multiple sources describe Big Tech environments where top talent finds themselves doing maintenance work on mature systems, navigating bureaucracy, and feeling disconnected from product impact. The contrast with startups — where individuals can see their work directly shape the company's trajectory — is stark. One recruiter captures the sentiment: talent wants to build, not maintain. The "agentic builder" profile that Polly Barnes described in a companion piece is exactly what these startups are seeking, and what Big Tech is struggling to retain.

The geopolitical dimension adds complexity. The US remains the dominant AI ecosystem, but restrictions on immigration, the uncertain regulatory environment, and the pull of European government-backed initiatives (including the UK's Sovereign AI fund and EU grants) are creating a more multipolar talent landscape. Cusp AI's $262 million funding round, backed by investors including OpenAI's Sam Altman, demonstrates that European startups can now access the capital needed to compete globally for talent. The long-term implication is that AI innovation may decentralize from its current concentration in a handful of US West Coast campuses, with Europe emerging as a meaningful hub for specialized AI applications in materials science, drug discovery, and enterprise software.

# OpenAI announces new Guaranteed Capacity offering for customers to secure compute
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/openai-announces-new-guaranteed-capacity-offering-for-customers-to-secure-compute.html
**Date**: May 19, 2026
**Author**: Ashley Capoot
**Keywords**: OpenAI, guaranteed capacity, compute, enterprise, IPO, Sam Altman, AI infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI launches Guaranteed Capacity, a long-term compute reservation program with 1-3 year commitments and tiered discounts, as the $850B-valued company builds its infrastructure business model ahead of a potential IPO.

## Takeaways
- Guaranteed Capacity lets customers lock in compute access for 1, 2, or 3 years, with discounts increasing for longer commitments.
- CEO Sam Altman framed it as a "big win-win" — customers get certainty, OpenAI gets demand visibility to plan infrastructure spend.
- OpenAI is targeting roughly $600 billion in total compute spend by 2030, making this program a foundational piece of its business model.
- The offering has a capped allocation and will sell out; OpenAI will reserve capacity for its own products like ChatGPT and Codex.
- The move comes as OpenAI, valued at $850B+, prepares for a potentially massive IPO and looks to address Wall Street concerns about its enormous infrastructure costs.
- The program provides a predictable recurring revenue stream and signals that compute itself — not just API access — is becoming a product.

## Synthesis
OpenAI's Guaranteed Capacity announcement is a significant shift in how the company monetizes its AI infrastructure. Rather than selling API access alone, OpenAI is now selling compute certainty — letting enterprise customers reserve capacity with 1-3 year commitments in exchange for tiered discounts. This moves OpenAI's business model closer to cloud providers like AWS and Azure, where reserved instances are a core revenue driver.

The strategic context is clear. OpenAI has told investors it expects to spend roughly $600 billion on compute by 2030. Last year, it inked a flurry of multi-billion-dollar infrastructure deals that unnerved Wall Street. Guaranteed Capacity addresses this directly: by locking in customer commitments, OpenAI gains the demand predictability needed to justify — and finance — its infrastructure buildout. It's a signal to both customers and investors that OpenAI is building a sustainable business, not just burning cash.

Sam Altman described it as a "big win-win." Customers, particularly enterprises building AI products and agents, get guaranteed access to compute in a market that Altman expects to be "capacity-constrained for some time." OpenAI gets forward revenue visibility and a tool to manage its own capacity planning. The allocation is capped, and the company says it will sell out, suggesting strong initial demand.

The offering is also a nod to the IPO preparations. An $850B+ company heading toward a public listing needs recurring, predictable revenue streams — not just variable API consumption. Reserved capacity contracts provide exactly that. They also create switching costs: an enterprise that's committed to a 3-year OpenAI compute contract is unlikely to migrate workloads mid-term.

The implicit message is that compute access itself is becoming scarce and valuable. As models get larger and agentic workflows consume more tokens, the bottleneck shifts from model quality to compute availability. OpenAI is positioning itself not just as an AI model provider, but as a compute infrastructure company — and Guaranteed Capacity is the pricing model to match.

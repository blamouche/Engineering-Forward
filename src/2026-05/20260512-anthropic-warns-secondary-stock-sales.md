# Anthropic Warns Investors Against Secondary Platforms Offering Access to Its Shares
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/anthropic-warns-investors-against-secondary-platforms-offering-access-to-its-shares/
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Ram Iyer (TechCrunch)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, secondary market, private shares, SPV, stock crackdown, unauthorized trading, AI valuation, Gabriel Shapiro

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic publicly warns that shares sold through unauthorized secondary platforms and SPVs are void, as crypto lawyer Gabriel Shapiro warns the crackdown could trigger major litigation amid a frenzied private market valuing the company near $900 billion.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic named eight platforms — including Forge Global, Hiive, Sydecar, and Unicorns Exchange — as unauthorized to facilitate buying or selling its shares, declaring any such transactions "void and will not be recognized on our books."
- Forge Global responded by claiming it was included erroneously and updated its Anthropic listing with a new warning; Sydecar said it only provides administrative services; Unicorns Exchange claimed it merely makes introductions.
- Unicorns Exchange reported receiving over 50 institutional investor inquiries in three months with "aggregate demand that exceeded $1 trillion" for Anthropic shares, though none resulted in deals due to inability to prove company approval.
- Crypto lawyer Gabriel Shapiro warned the crackdown could lead to major litigation, framing it as a rights-of-shareholders issue.
- The crackdown comes as Anthropic is rumored to be raising funds at a $900 billion valuation and as crypto exchanges like OKX have launched derivative products tracking private AI company valuations.

## Synthesis
Anthropic has taken the unusual step of publicly naming and shaming secondary market platforms that claim to offer access to its private shares, updating its support website with an explicit warning that threatens to void any unauthorized transactions. The move signals growing tension between hot private AI companies and the expanding ecosystem of platforms trying to democratize (and profit from) access to their equity.

The company's support page lists eight named firms — Open Doors Partners, Unicorns Exchange, Pachamama Capital, Lionheart Ventures, Hiive (new offerings), Forge Global (new offerings), Sydecar, and Upmarket — as unauthorized. The language is forceful: any sale or transfer "is void and will not be recognized on our books and records." Special purpose vehicles (SPVs) are specifically prohibited from acquiring Anthropic stock, with transfers to SPVs deemed void under the company's transfer restrictions.

The responses from named platforms reveal the complexity of the secondary market ecosystem. Forge Global claimed erroneous inclusion and emphasized it doesn't facilitate transactions without company approval. Sydecar positioned itself as purely administrative, requiring sponsors to attest they have required approvals. Unicorns Exchange said it only provides introductions and has since ceased marketing Anthropic-related opportunities. Hiive emphasized its investment in compliance infrastructure.

The most revealing disclosure came from Unicorns Exchange, which reported receiving over 50 institutional investor inquiries in three months with aggregate demand exceeding $1 trillion — a staggering figure that underscores the frenzy around AI company exposure. Yet none resulted in deals because funds couldn't prove they had "Anthropic blessing" to sell LP interests in SPVs holding shares.

The crackdown's timing is significant. Anthropic is rumored to be raising fresh funding at a $900 billion valuation, which would surpass OpenAI. At these valuations, control over who holds shares becomes strategically critical — secondary market fragmentation could complicate future fundraising, create governance headaches, and expose the company to regulatory risk if shares end up with sanctioned entities or competitors.

The legal dimension, flagged by crypto lawyer Gabriel Shapiro, adds another layer. If shareholders genuinely hold transferable interests, declaring them void could invite litigation. The crypto angle is also notable: exchanges like OKX have launched pre-IPO perpetual futures tracking private AI companies, and token markets are implying valuations disconnected from fundamental rounds. Anthropic's warning extends to these derivative products, calling them unauthorized.

The broader context is a private AI market caught between stratospheric demand and the traditional controls of venture-backed private companies. As secondary platforms proliferate and retail investors clamor for AI exposure, the tension between company control and market access will likely intensify — and Anthropic's aggressive stance may become a template for other AI companies facing similar pressure.

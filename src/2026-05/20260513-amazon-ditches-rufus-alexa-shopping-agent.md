# Amazon ditches Rufus AI chatbot in favor of Alexa shopping agent
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/amazon-ditches-rufus-ai-chatbot-in-favor-of-alexa-shopping-agent.html
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: CNBC (additional coverage: Jennifer Pattison Tuohy, The Verge)
**Keywords**: Amazon, Alexa, Rufus, AI shopping, e-commerce, agentic commerce, Alexa Plus, Buy for Me

## Elevator pitch
Amazon is retiring its Rufus AI chatbot and replacing it with Alexa for Shopping, a deeply integrated shopping assistant powered by Alexa Plus that handles searches, price alerts, comparisons, auto-reordering, and cross-website purchasing directly from the Amazon search bar.

## Takeaways
- Alexa for Shopping replaces Rufus as the primary AI shopping interface on Amazon.com and the app, appearing front-and-center in the search bar rather than buried in a side panel.
- The assistant can set price alerts, compare items, auto-reorder products, and even shop on external websites via the "Buy for Me" agentic feature—all triggered by natural language queries.
- Cross-device continuity allows conversations started on an Echo smart speaker to carry context to Amazon.com, enabling queries like "show me what I need to buy for my science project."
- The service is free for all U.S. Amazon customers and does not require an Alexa account, with rollout ramping up over the coming weeks and a new visual shopping experience on Echo Show smart displays.
- Amazon VP Daniel Rausch positions the end-to-end experience as a differentiator, arguing that competitors "stumbled because it's really complex" to build a complete shopping journey versus scraping websites.

## Synthesis
Amazon's decision to sunset Rufus in favor of Alexa for Shopping marks a strategic consolidation of its AI efforts around the Alexa brand, which carries significantly more consumer recognition and device ecosystem integration. Rufus, launched in early 2024, was a competent but ultimately peripheral chatbot—relegated to a side interface that many users never discovered. Alexa for Shopping takes the opposite approach: it lives in the primary search bar, the highest-traffic surface on Amazon's entire platform.

The move is also a response to intensifying competition in agentic commerce. Google and OpenAI have both rolled out AI shopping features with mixed results, while startups like Perplexity's Comet aim to intermediate between consumers and marketplaces. By embedding Alexa Plus's reasoning capabilities directly into the purchase flow, Amazon is defending its position as the default starting point for product search while simultaneously raising the barrier for external AI shopping agents. The "Buy for Me" feature—allowing Alexa to complete purchases on external websites—is particularly aggressive, extending Amazon's reach beyond its own marketplace.

The cross-device continuity between Echo devices and Amazon.com represents an underappreciated competitive moat. A customer who discusses a science project with their Echo speaker can later type "show me what I need to buy" and receive contextually relevant results—an integration that's difficult for competitors to replicate without equivalent hardware penetration. The Echo Show's upgraded visual shopping experience further closes the loop, creating a seamless voice-to-visual-to-purchase funnel.

The central tension is trust. Alexa for Shopping's most powerful features—auto-purchasing based on price thresholds, scheduled deal-hunting, cross-website transactions—require customers to hand over significant personal data and purchasing authority to an AI system. With public distrust of AI rising, Amazon must convince users that the convenience is worth the privacy trade-off. Rausch's framing of "getting all the way to done" captures the value proposition, but the hill to climb is cultural as much as technical.

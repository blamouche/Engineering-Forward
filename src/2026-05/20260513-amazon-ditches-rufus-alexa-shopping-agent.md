# Amazon ditches Rufus AI chatbot in favor of Alexa shopping agent
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/amazon-ditches-rufus-ai-chatbot-in-favor-of-alexa-shopping-agent.html
**Date**: May 13, 2026
**Author**: CNBC / Jennifer Pattison Tuohy (The Verge)
**Keywords**: Amazon, Alexa, Rufus, AI shopping, agentic commerce, Alexa Plus, shopping assistant, e-commerce, Echo, Buy for Me

## Elevator pitch
Amazon is retiring its Rufus AI chatbot and replacing it with "Alexa for Shopping," a deeply integrated, free shopping assistant powered by Alexa Plus that lives in Amazon's search bar and can auto-purchase, compare products, set price alerts, and shop across websites.

## Takeaways
- Alexa for Shopping replaces Rufus as Amazon's primary AI shopping assistant, sitting directly in the search bar on Amazon.com and the app
- The assistant can auto-purchase items based on user-set parameters (price thresholds, timing), set price alerts, compare products, and track a full year of price history
- It leverages the "Buy for Me" agentic feature to shop on other websites on the user's behalf and supports "scheduled actions" for automatic deal hunting
- Alexa for Shopping has cross-device continuity: conversations started on Echo smart speakers carry context over to Amazon.com and vice versa
- The service is free for all US customers and doesn't require an Alexa account, rolling out over the coming weeks

## Synthesis
On May 13, 2026, Amazon announced it is killing Rufus — its AI shopping chatbot launched in 2024 — and replacing it with "Alexa for Shopping," a far more ambitious agentic commerce play. The move places Amazon's most recognizable AI brand, Alexa, directly into the search bar of its flagship e-commerce platform, transforming every product query into a potential AI interaction rather than a traditional search.

The strategic pivot is significant on multiple levels. First, it unifies Amazon's fragmented AI efforts. Rufus was a standalone chatbot tucked into a corner of the Amazon app; Alexa for Shopping is the search experience itself. When a user types "What's a good skincare routine for men" or "When did I last order AA batteries," they now get an AI-generated answer instead of (or alongside) a product list. The assistant lives in both the main search bar and a dedicated chat window, with the cursive Alexa "a" icon indicating interactive touchpoints throughout the shopping flow.

Second, the new assistant is genuinely agentic. It can set price alerts and automatically add items to cart when conditions are met — "Add this sunscreen to my cart if the price drops to $10 and I haven't purchased it in the last two months." It can track a full year of price history for any product, compare items across features and reviews, and generate "shopping guides" that pull from both Amazon and the broader web. The controversial "Buy for Me" feature allows Alexa to complete purchases on external websites. "Scheduled actions" enable proactive deal hunting: tell Alexa what you want, and it monitors and purchases on your behalf.

Third, the integration spans devices. Daniel Rausch, Amazon's VP of Alexa and Echo, emphasized "cross-device continuity." A conversation about a science project started on an Echo smart speaker carries its context to Amazon.com; price alerts set online surface on Echo Show smart displays. The Echo Show 15 and 21 get a "fully integrated visual shopping experience" — essentially a full Amazon store interface controllable by voice and touch — with smaller Show devices to follow.

The pricing model is notably aggressive: free for all US customers, no Alexa account required. This contrasts with the subscription models emerging for AI assistants elsewhere, suggesting Amazon views the shopping assistant not as a direct revenue product but as a sales accelerator. Every AI-assisted purchase that wouldn't have happened otherwise, every price-drop auto-purchase, and every cross-site Buy for Me transaction flows through Amazon's ecosystem.

The Rufus retirement also reflects a broader lesson about AI branding. Rufus lacked the consumer recognition and trust of Alexa, which Amazon has spent a decade building into a household name. By consolidating under Alexa, Amazon bets that its AI assistant brand carries more shopping-conversion power than a standalone chatbot ever could. The move comes as competition in agentic commerce intensifies — Perplexity's Comet and other external AI agents are trying to intermediate the shopping experience, and Amazon's response is to make its own agent so deeply integrated that leaving the platform becomes friction. Amazon is simultaneously suing to keep external agents off its marketplace while building the most capable shopping agent inside it — a two-front strategy in the emerging agentic commerce war.

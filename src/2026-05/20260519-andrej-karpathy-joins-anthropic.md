# Andrej Karpathy, Tesla Alum and OpenAI Co-Founder, Joins Anthropic
**Source**: https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/andrej-karpathy-tesla-alum-and-openai-co-founder-joins-anthropic-c665f51f
**Date**: May 19, 2026
**Author**: Elias Schisgall
**Keywords**: anthropic, karpathy, openai, tesla, ai-talent, pretraining, llm

## Elevator pitch
Andrej Karpathy, an OpenAI co-founder and former Tesla AI chief, has joined Anthropic to lead a new group focused on using Claude to accelerate pretraining research — marking one of the highest-profile talent moves in the AI industry.

## Takeaways
- Karpathy joins Anthropic's pretraining team, which oversees the data training process for Claude models
- He described the next few years at the LLM frontier as "especially formative" and expressed excitement about returning to R&D
- The move brings together one of AI's most prominent researchers with the company that has been gaining on OpenAI in enterprise adoption
- Karpathy was a founding member of OpenAI, later led Tesla's Autopilot AI, and most recently has been an independent researcher and educator
- The hire signals Anthropic's continued investment in fundamental research even as it expands its platform and product offerings

## Synthesis
Andrej Karpathy's move to Anthropic represents a significant realignment of AI talent. As a co-founder of OpenAI who later built Tesla's Autopilot AI division and became one of the most influential educators in the field through his YouTube lectures and "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" series, Karpathy carries both technical credibility and cultural weight. His decision to join Anthropic rather than return to OpenAI — where he could presumably have named his terms — is a signal about where the most interesting work in pretraining is happening.

Karpathy will lead a new group focused specifically on using Claude to accelerate pretraining research. This is a meta-application: using an existing frontier model to improve the process of creating the next one. The role sits at the intersection of Karpathy's expertise in training pipelines (from his Tesla years) and his fluency with frontier models. His framing of the next few years as "especially formative" suggests he sees pretraining as far from solved — there are fundamental advances still to be made, and Anthropic is where he believes they'll happen.

The hire also fits the broader competitive narrative. Anthropic has been gaining on OpenAI in enterprise spending, platform adoption, and developer mindshare. Adding one of OpenAI's founding members — who also happens to be one of the most respected figures in the field — strengthens Anthropic's research bench at a moment when the industry is shifting from "who has the biggest model" to "who has the best platform." As Ravi Mehta's concurrent analysis notes, the competitive axis is moving from models to platforms, but foundational research talent remains a critical input — and Karpathy's move suggests Anthropic isn't ceding that ground either.

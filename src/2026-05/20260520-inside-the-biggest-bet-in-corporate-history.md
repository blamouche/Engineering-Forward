# Inside the Biggest Bet in Corporate History
**Source**: https://www.bvp.com/atlas/inside-the-biggest-bet-in-corporate-history
**Date**: May 20, 2026
**Author**: Byron Deeter, Sam Bondy (Bessemer Venture Partners)
**Keywords**: hyperscalers, AI capex, cloud computing, AI investment, software market, infrastructure, trillion-dollar companies, venture capital, economic value

## Elevator pitch
Bessemer Venture Partners analyzes the $8T cumulative AI capex bet by hyperscalers (AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL, META, ORCL), concluding it implies a $6T software revenue opportunity — the largest greenfield money-making event in corporate history — with winners more distributed across the stack than in the previous cloud wave.

## Takeaways
- Hyperscaler AI capex will cross $1T/year by 2027, totaling ~$8T cumulatively from 2025-2031, requiring $3T in incremental annual revenue to justify the investment at 15% ROIC.
- The math implies $6T in customer-facing global software revenue — 4x+ growth from today's ~$1.4T — targeting the $40T+ annual knowledge worker economy (legal, accounting, consulting, healthcare, etc.).
- At a 6x revenue multiple, this represents ~$36T of new market cap, or ~14% of the entire global corporate ecosystem.
- Unlike the cloud wave where hyperscalers captured outsized value, this wave's winners will be more distributed: hardware vendors first, then hyperscalers, foundation model companies, and finally AI-native applications.
- The "picks-and-shovels" AI infrastructure stack (orchestration, evals, observability, fine-tuning, agent runtime, etc.) mirrors the cloud-era infrastructure companies and is still in early innings.

## Synthesis
Bessemer Venture Partners partners Byron Deeter and Sam Bondy deliver a striking analysis of what they call "the biggest bet in corporate history": the hyperscaler AI capex cycle. By next year, combined capex from Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Oracle will exceed $1 trillion annually, accumulating to roughly $8 trillion from 2025 to 2031. For context, this runs parallel to the entire yearly U.S. Department of War budget over the same period.

The article reframes the conversation from the well-covered supply side (GPUs, data centers, power) to the demand implications. The math is sobering: for every $1T of ramped capex, hyperscalers need $500B in incremental yearly revenue to hit 15% unlevered ROIC. Extrapolating across the full build-out, assuming ~70% of compute serves external AI applications that spend ~35% of revenue on inference and compute, the implied customer-facing software revenue is ~$6T — roughly 4x today's global software market of $1.4T.

Deeter and Bondy argue this is not a bubble but rather the largest greenfield opportunity in business history. The target is the $40T+ paid annually to knowledge workers globally across legal, accounting, consulting, healthcare administration, customer support, sales, recruiting, and claims processing. AI applications will displace incumbent services revenue and expand these categories by "rebuilding the service itself as software."

A crucial strategic insight differentiates this wave from the previous cloud era: economic value will be more distributed across the stack. Hyperscalers captured an outsized share of cloud-era market capitalization, but they lack the same software IP position in AI (especially at the foundation model layer) and lack the same market power against layers above them. The value chain progression will flow from hardware (Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD) to hyperscalers, to foundation model companies (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, SpaceXAI), and ultimately to AI-native applications selling outcomes.

The picks-and-shovels economy at every level — orchestration, evals, observability, fine-tuning, memory, inference, agent runtime, data management, networking — mirrors the generational infrastructure companies built atop cloud (Snowflake, Databricks, Datadog, MongoDB) and is still in early innings. Bessemer concludes bluntly: "Multiple trillion-dollar software companies of the 2030s are being built right now."

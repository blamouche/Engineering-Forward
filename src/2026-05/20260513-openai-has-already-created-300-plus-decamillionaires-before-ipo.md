# OpenAI Has Already Created 300+ Decamillionaires. More Than a Decade of B2B IPOs Combined. Before Going Public.
**Source**: https://www.saastr.com/openaidecamillionaires/
**Date**: May 2026
**Author**: Jason Lemkin
**Keywords**: OpenAI, decamillionaires, secondary tender, AI wealth creation, B2B talent market, Anthropic, AI compensation, IPO, equity liquidity, talent war

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI has already created 300 to 500+ employee decamillionaires through secondary tender offers before even going public — a wealth creation event without precedent in tech history — fundamentally reshaping the B2B and AI talent market.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI's October 2025 tender let 600+ employees sell $6.6B in stock: 75 hit the $30M cap, 525 averaged $8.3M each, and cumulative across multiple tenders since 2021, 300-500+ employees have realized $10M+ in secondary cash.
- This pre-IPO liquidity has no precedent: Google's 2004 IPO created ~1,000 paper millionaires who had to sit through lockups; Facebook followed the same pattern; even Snowflake and Datadog produced a handful of decamillionaires post-lockup.
- OpenAI employees from 2019 have seen 100x equity appreciation; a staff engineer who joined then sits on $50M-$200M+ with active liquidity windows.
- Anthropic's April 2026 tender ($5-6B target) saw employees largely holding rather than selling, with secondary markets pricing the company at $500-595B and IPO targets as early as Q4 2026.
- The downstream effects on B2B hiring: $500K+ base salary floors for AI/ML roles, Meta offering $300M packages to top researchers, liquidity cliffs replacing vesting cliffs as the real retention lever, and non-founder operator roles at AI labs now beating founder economics at most B2B companies.

## Synthesis

Jason Lemkin's analysis of the OpenAI wealth creation event captures a structural shift in technology compensation that has no historical parallel. The numbers are staggering: a single secondary transaction produced 75 confirmed $30M cashouts and another 525 employees averaging $8.3M — before any IPO. Cumulative across multiple tenders since 2021, 300 to 500+ OpenAI employees have crossed the $10M threshold in realized, wired-out cash, not paper wealth.

The comparison framework Lemkin provides is instructive. Google's 2004 IPO created approximately 1,000 paper millionaires, but they couldn't touch that wealth until lockups expired. Facebook followed the same IPO-then-lockup pattern. The strongest B2B IPOs of the last decade — Snowflake, MongoDB, Datadog, HubSpot — each produced a handful of decamillionaires after lockups expired. OpenAI has produced more decamillionaires while private than more than a decade of B2B IPOs combined. There is no precedent.

The equity appreciation math is equally striking. Early employees from 2019 have seen their equity appreciate more than 100x, compared to the Nasdaq roughly tripling in the same window. A staff engineer who joined with a market-rate package is now sitting on $50M-$200M+ in equity with active liquidity. The compensation math for any startup trying to compete for that talent has fundamentally broken.

The Anthropic contrast is revealing. At Anthropic's April 2026 tender ($5-6B target at $350B pre-money), investors wanted to buy more shares than employees were willing to sell — the opposite signal from OpenAI, where $4B in demand went unfilled because employees took the cash. Anthropic employees are betting on the IPO. Secondary markets now price Anthropic at $500-595B implied valuation, with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and Morgan Stanley reportedly in early underwriter discussions for a Q4 2026 IPO.

The downstream effects Lemkin identifies are already reshaping B2B hiring. Meta reportedly offered $300M packages to top AI researchers — a number that only makes sense when you understand that OpenAI's top talent is sitting on equity worth multiples of that. The $500K+ base salary floor for technical roles, combined with equity that's functionally cash within 24 months via secondary tenders, means any B2B startup trying to hire AI/ML talent is playing a different game entirely. Liquidity cliffs — where employees must wait two years before participating in tenders — have replaced vesting cliffs as the real retention mechanism.

The structural implication for founders is perhaps the most uncomfortable finding in Lemkin's analysis: being a non-founder operator at one of the right AI labs now beats founder economics at most B2B companies. Greg Brockman holds approximately $30 billion in equity. Sam Altman, who claims no direct shares, is positioned to receive a stake in the for-profit conversion. For engineers choosing between joining OpenAI/Anthropic and founding a B2B SaaS company, the expected value math now heavily favors the former.

The flywheel effects extend beyond individual wealth. Hundreds of newly-minted decamillionaires in San Francisco are angel-investing, founding new companies, and bidding up real estate. The next generation of B2B and AI startups will be substantially funded by OpenAI alumni capital — a pattern already visible in early-stage rounds across the Bay Area. When both OpenAI and Anthropic eventually IPO at what will likely be among the largest public offerings in history, the wealth creation will dwarf even these secondary tender numbers.

For B2B founders, Lemkin's advice is blunt: don't oversell equity that won't produce this scale of wealth, ensure equity has a realistic liquidity path or it isn't "real" compensation, and accept that the talent war for AI engineers is accelerating, not stabilizing. The OpenAI model of secondary tenders every 12-18 months has become table stakes for serious AI talent.

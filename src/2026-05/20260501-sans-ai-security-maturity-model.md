# SANS AI Security Maturity Model eBook
**Source**: https://www.sans.org/mlp/2026-ai-security-maturity-model-ebook
**Date**: 2026-05-01
**Author**: SANS Institute
**Keywords**: AI security, maturity model, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, ISO 42001, AI governance, OWASP, security program

## Elevator pitch
The SANS Institute has published a practical AI Security Maturity Model that provides a 5-stage framework for organizations to assess, benchmark, and advance their AI security posture across three pillars: Protect, Utilize, and Govern.

## Takeaways
- The model breaks AI security into three interlocking domains: Protect (defending AI systems), Utilize (using AI for security), and Govern (policy, compliance, and oversight)
- Five maturity stages provide a progression path from ad-hoc practices to AI-native security, each with defined controls and metrics
- The framework maps directly to major regulatory standards: NIST AI RMF, the EU AI Act, ISO 42001, and OWASP guidance
- Evidence-based scoring allows organizations to benchmark their current state and set measurable targets for improvement
- The model bridges the common gap between high-level AI security strategy and day-to-day operational execution

## Synthesis
The SANS Institute's AI Security Maturity Model represents one of the first comprehensive attempts to give organizations a structured, actionable framework for securing their AI operations. Released as an eBook, it addresses a clear market need: while frameworks exist for AI risk management (NIST) and AI management systems (ISO 42001), there has been little practical guidance on how to turn those standards into a sequenced, measurable security program.

The model organizes AI security into three domains. "Protect" covers the traditional security mandate of defending AI systems from attacks — model extraction, data poisoning, prompt injection, and the growing catalog of AI-specific threats. "Utilize" addresses the increasingly common scenario of using AI tools within security operations — automated threat detection, AI-driven SOC workflows, and the attendant risks of giving AI systems access to security tooling. "Govern" encompasses the policy, compliance, and oversight layer: ensuring AI deployments meet regulatory requirements, managing third-party AI risk, and maintaining documentation for audits.

The five-stage maturity progression — from initial/ad-hoc through to AI-native — provides a roadmap that organizations can follow at their own pace. Each stage defines specific controls, metrics, and actions, which addresses a persistent pain point in the industry: security teams know they need to do something about AI, but they don't know where to start or what "good" looks like.

The mapping to regulatory standards is particularly timely. With the EU AI Act now in effect and other jurisdictions developing their own frameworks, organizations need to demonstrate compliance across multiple overlapping regimes. The model's alignment with NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, and OWASP guidance means security teams can use a single internal framework to satisfy multiple external requirements.

The eBook's practical orientation — with step-by-step guidance and evidence-based scoring — distinguishes it from more theoretical treatments of AI security. By providing a scoring methodology, it enables organizations to establish a baseline, set targets, and track progress over time. For security leaders who have been struggling to move AI security from a board-level concern to an operational reality, the model offers a credible path forward.

# 12 Irish Startups to Watch, According to VCs
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/ireland-irish-startups-to-watch-2026
**Date**: May 25, 2026
**Author**: Martin Coulter
**Keywords**: Irish startups, venture capital, Dublin Tech Week, European tech, startup ecosystem, entrepreneurship

## Elevator pitch
As investors descend on Dublin for Tech Week, VCs highlight 12 Irish startups to watch spanning AI, fintech, healthtech, and B2B SaaS, reflecting Ireland's maturing ecosystem beyond its traditional role as a US tech gateway.

## Takeaways
- Ireland's startup ecosystem raised €335M across 114 deals in 2025, recovering from €0.2B across 145 deals the prior year, signaling renewed investor confidence.
- Dublin Tech Week is attracting global investors, underscoring Ireland's position as an English-speaking launchpad for US tech companies entering Europe.
- The 12 featured startups span diverse sectors: AI-driven legal/compliance tools, B2B SaaS, fintech infrastructure, healthtech, and deep tech.
- Key themes include AI-first business models, platforms built for European regulatory complexity, and companies that leverage Ireland's unique position between US and EU markets.
- VCs emphasize that Ireland's talent pool — fed by multinational tech offices and strong universities — is producing a generation of experienced founders.

## Synthesis
Ireland's startup ecosystem is undergoing a notable maturation. While the country has long served as a European headquarters for US tech giants, a new wave of homegrown startups is attracting serious VC attention. The article, published during Dublin Tech Week, surveys VCs on the 12 most promising Irish startups of 2026.

The startups highlighted reflect broader European tech trends: AI-driven tools for legal and compliance, B2B SaaS platforms solving specific industry pain points, fintech infrastructure plays, and healthtech innovations. Several leverage Ireland's unique regulatory and linguistic position — English-speaking but fully embedded in EU frameworks — to build products that work across both sides of the Atlantic.

VC sentiment is cautiously optimistic. After a dip in funding between 2024 and 2025, the ecosystem is showing signs of recovery, with larger rounds and more international investor participation. The presence of serial entrepreneurs with prior exits is creating a flywheel effect: experienced founders reinvesting in the next generation of Irish startups.

The article also highlights the importance of government policy, with Irish agencies actively supporting startup formation and attracting foreign investment. However, challenges remain: access to late-stage growth capital, competition for talent with multinational employers, and the need to build companies that scale globally rather than serving only the domestic market. The 12 startups profiled represent a bet that Ireland can produce globally competitive technology companies, not just regional champions.

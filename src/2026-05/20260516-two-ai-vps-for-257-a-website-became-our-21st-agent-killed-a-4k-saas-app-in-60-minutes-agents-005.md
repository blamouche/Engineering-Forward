## Synthesis
The fifth installment of SaaStr's The Agents podcast series delivers the most concrete cost data yet on AI agents in production. Jason Lemkin and his team pulled the actual billing for their two AI VPs — 10K (VP Marketing) and QBee (VP Customer Success) — and the combined monthly cost was $257. Not per day, as his colleague Amelia first assumed, but for the entire month. These two agents collectively replaced the functions of a marketing analyst, marketing ops coordinator, junior content marketer, customer success coordinator, and sponsor relations manager.

The cost breakdown reveals an important structural insight: the expensive part of the AI stack isn't the AI itself. API calls to Salesforce, Bizzabo, Marketo, WordPress, YouTube, and X dominate the workload, while 95% of model calls use GPT-4o-mini at less than a penny per call. Postgres on Replit costs roughly 20 cents per month. Even fully burdened with all supporting SaaS subscriptions, the two agents run at $500-800/month — roughly 1/30th of what the team initially assumed. Lemkin argues forcefully that cost is no longer the constraint: with three humans running an 8-figure business producing $2,000-$4,000/hour in economic output, worrying about agent costs is the wrong focus.

The episode's most striking revelation is the spontaneous emergence of a new agent. One of SaaStr's event websites, originally built to replace Squarespace, gradually accumulated capabilities — parking pass generation, attendee newsletters, sponsor logo management, micro-audience handling — until it effectively became the company's 21st autonomous agent with its own personality and goals. This "agent spawn" phenomenon suggests the lines between applications and agents are blurring in production environments.

The SaaS disruption angle is equally sharp. Lemkin describes killing a $4,000/year newsletter builder in about 60 minutes by having 10K rebuild its functionality — pulling articles from WordPress, force-ranking with Sonnet, auto-extracting tweets, and inserting sponsor ads. This "stealth churn" pattern, where point solutions quietly disappear as agents absorb their function, may be the more realistic form of SaaS disruption than wholesale platform replacement. Meanwhile, QBee sent 83 personalized emails to sponsors at 12:23am while the team slept, resulting in fewer support questions the next day and more sponsors adopting the agent chatbot directly.

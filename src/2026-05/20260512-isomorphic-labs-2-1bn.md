# DeepMind spinout Isomorphic Labs raises $2.1bn
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/isomorphic-labs-2-1bn
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Freya Pratty
**Keywords**: AI drug discovery, Isomorphic Labs, DeepMind, AlphaFold, Series B, Thrive Capital, UK Sovereign AI fund, biotech, fundraising, GV, MGX, Temasek, Eli Lilly, Novartis

## Elevator pitch
Isomorphic Labs, the DeepMind spinout using AlphaFold for AI-designed drugs, raised a $2.1bn Series B led by Thrive Capital, backed by UK's Sovereign AI fund and sovereign wealth funds, marking a massive escalation in AI-biotech investment just one year after its $600m first raise.

## Takeaways
- Isomorphic Labs secured a $2.1bn Series B led by Thrive Capital, with participation from UK's Sovereign AI fund, Abu Dhabi's MGX, GV (Alphabet), and Singapore's Temasek
- The raise comes just one year after its $600m first external capital round in May 2025
- Isomorphic was spun out of Google DeepMind in 2021, founded by Colin Murdoch and Demis Hassabis
- The company uses DeepMind's AlphaFold protein-structure prediction technology to build a drug discovery platform
- It competes with OpenAI (which released a life sciences model in 2026) and has signed pharma deals with Eli Lilly and Novartis; pre-clinical trials have begun

## Synthesis
Isomorphic Labs, the AI-driven drug discovery company spun out of Google DeepMind in 2021, has raised a staggering $2.1 billion in Series B funding, cementing its position as one of the most capitalised AI-biotech ventures globally. The round was led by US venture firm Thrive Capital, with participation from a notable mix of sovereign wealth and strategic investors: the UK's newly established Sovereign AI fund, Abu Dhabi's MGX, Alphabet's venture arm GV, and Singapore's Temasek. This follows a $600m raise just twelve months prior, signalling both accelerating investor conviction and the capital-intensive nature of AI-powered pharmaceutical R&D.

The company was conceived by Colin Murdoch and DeepMind founder Demis Hassabis, who recognised that drug discovery required a fundamentally different talent mix from the core machine-learning research that defines DeepMind. Where DeepMind thrives on ML researchers and engineers, Isomorphic needed chemists, pharmacologists, and biologists — roles that lacked a natural career ladder within the parent organisation. Spinning out allowed Isomorphic to set a clearer mission, build a distinct culture, and recruit from a broader scientific pool.

Isomorphic's technical foundation rests on AlphaFold, DeepMind's groundbreaking AI system for predicting protein structures — a problem that had stumped scientists for decades. By leveraging and extending this capability into a full drug discovery platform, Isomorphic aims to dramatically accelerate the traditionally slow and costly process of bringing new drugs to market. The company has already secured commercial partnerships with pharmaceutical giants Eli Lilly and Novartis, validating the platform's perceived value to the industry.

The competitive landscape is intensifying. OpenAI entered the life sciences arena earlier in 2026 with its own model, setting up a fascinating race between Google/Alphabet's spinout ecosystem and the independent AI challenger. Isomorphic's head start — built on years of DeepMind research and established pharma relationships — represents a significant moat, but OpenAI's resources and generalist AI ambitions could reshape the field.

Demis Hassabis had initially indicated clinical trials would begin by the end of 2025, though he later clarified he was referring to pre-clinical trials, which have now commenced. The distinction matters: pre-clinical work involves laboratory and animal testing before human subjects, and the journey from pre-clinical to approved drug remains long and uncertain. The $2.1bn infusion suggests investors are betting that Isomorphic's AI-first approach can compress this timeline meaningfully — a thesis that, if proven, would transform not just one company but the entire pharmaceutical industry.

The involvement of the UK's Sovereign AI fund is also strategically significant. It reflects a growing government interest in retaining AI-driven industrial champions within national borders, particularly in sectors — like drug discovery — that intersect with public health and economic sovereignty. For the UK, backing Isomorphic is both an industrial policy move and a hedge against losing a homegrown technology platform to foreign acquisition.

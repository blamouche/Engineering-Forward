# Elon Musk's SpaceXAI has been bleeding staff since its merger
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/elon-musks-spacexai-has-been-bleeding-staff-since-its-merger/
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Rebecca Bellan (TechCrunch)
**Keywords**: SpaceXAI, xAI, SpaceX, Elon Musk, talent exodus, Grok, pre-training, Meta, Thinking Machines Lab, AI talent war, merger, startup culture

## Elevator pitch
More than 50 researchers and engineers have left SpaceXAI since the SpaceX-xAI merger in February 2026, with key departures including pre-training team lead Juntang Zhuang and core contributors to coding, world models, and Grok voice, raising questions about the company's commitment to frontier model development amid a culture of extreme work and a tightening AI talent market.

## Takeaways
- Over 50 SpaceXAI employees have departed since February 2026, including the leader of the pre-training team (Juntang Zhuang), with the core pre-training team now reduced to a handful of people.
- Rivals Meta and Thinking Machines Lab are actively recruiting former xAI staff: at least 11 xAI employees joined Meta, and at least 7 joined Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab.
- Nearly half of xAI's founding team had already left the company by February 2026, prior to the merger announcement.
- Employee departures are attributed to multiple factors: Elon Musk's extreme work culture with unrealistic deadlines, post-merger leadership changes, and liquidity events that reduce retention incentives.
- The pre-training team exodus raises strategic questions about whether SpaceXAI remains committed to developing leading foundation models or is pivoting toward applied products.

## Synthesis
Rebecca Bellan's TechCrunch report, drawing primarily on The Information's investigation, paints a picture of organizational turbulence at Elon Musk's newly merged SpaceXAI. The exodus of more than 50 researchers and engineers since February represents a significant talent drain for a company whose competitive position in AI depends on its ability to attract and retain elite technical talent.

The departures are concentrated in strategically critical areas. The pre-training team—responsible for the foundational step of building new AI models—has been particularly hard hit. Following the exit of team lead Juntang Zhuang, the team has reportedly dwindled to just a handful of people. This has fueled internal questioning about whether SpaceXAI remains committed to developing leading models or is pivoting toward productizing existing capabilities like Grok. Additional exits span coding, world models, and Grok voice teams—all areas central to the company's technical roadmap.

The competitive dynamics are illuminating. At least 11 former xAI employees have been hired by Meta, and at least 7 have joined Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab. These destinations represent different poles of the AI talent market: Meta as the large-platform incumbent investing heavily in AI, and Thinking Machines Lab as the well-funded startup founded by a respected former OpenAI CTO. Notably, nearly half of xAI's founding team had already departed before the merger was even announced, suggesting organizational issues predate the SpaceX integration.

Bellan identifies several converging factors behind the exodus. Musk's well-documented culture of extreme work and unrealistic deadlines—complaints familiar from Tesla and other Musk ventures—is cited as a direct contributor, with reports of corners being cut on Grok's training as a result. Post-merger leadership changes added organizational uncertainty. And a structural factor unique to mature startups applies: SpaceX's regular tender offers allow employees to cash out vested shares, and IPO expectations may make remaining equity incentives less compelling. Once the financial outcome is visible, the calculus of enduring a high-pressure environment shifts. The talent war in AI—where competitors can offer both compelling technical challenges and more sustainable working conditions—makes retention a strategic concern that technology alone cannot solve.

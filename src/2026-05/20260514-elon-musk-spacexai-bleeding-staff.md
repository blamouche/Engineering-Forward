# Elon Musk's SpaceXAI has been bleeding staff since its merger
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/elon-musks-spacexai-has-been-bleeding-staff-since-its-merger/
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Rebecca Bellan
**Keywords**: SpaceXAI, xAI, Elon Musk, talent exodus, Meta, Thinking Machines Lab, pre-training, AI talent war

## Elevator pitch
More than 50 researchers and engineers have left Elon Musk's SpaceXAI since the February merger, with key leaders in coding, world models, and Grok voice departing — and the pre-training team dwindling to just a handful of people.

## Takeaways
- At least 11 xAI employees defected to Meta, 7 to Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab, and key leaders across coding, world models, and Grok voice have left
- The pre-training team — responsible for building new AI models — has been decimated following the exit of team lead Juntang Zhuang
- Musk's culture of extreme work and unrealistic deadlines contributed to departures, with sources saying corners were cut on Grok
- SpaceX tender offers and IPO expectations may paradoxically accelerated exits by reducing equity lock-in incentives
- The question now is whether SpaceXAI remains committed to developing leading AI models or pivots toward applied products

## Synthesis
The SpaceXAI talent exodus reveals a fundamental challenge in AI company mergers: cultural integration matters more than structural consolidation. When SpaceX acquired xAI in February, the combined entity seemed formidable on paper. But the reality, per The Information's reporting, is that over 50 staff have departed since — including critical personnel in pre-training, coding, world models, and Grok voice.

The departures follow a pattern seen across Musk's companies: a high-pressure culture that burns through talent. Unrealistic deadlines on Grok led to corner-cutting, and the post-merger leadership changes created additional uncertainty. The pre-training team's near-total decimation is particularly alarming — without the capability to build new foundation models, SpaceXAI risks becoming an applied products company rather than a frontier lab.

The competitive dynamics are telling: Meta and Thinking Machines Lab are the primary beneficiaries, scooping up experienced AI researchers. This suggests the talent market values deep technical capability over Musk's brand appeal.

There's also a financial paradox at play. SpaceX's regular tender offers and IPO expectations — meant to retain talent through equity — may have backfired. Once employees can see liquidity on the horizon, the incentive to endure extreme working conditions diminishes. Money unlocks freedom, and freedom means leaving.

The core question is whether SpaceXAI can rebuild. In a market where AI talent commands unprecedented compensation, losing 50+ researchers in three months is a gap that can't be closed quickly — especially when competitors are actively hiring.

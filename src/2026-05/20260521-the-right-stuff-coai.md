## Elevator pitch
CO/AI's Harry DeMott frames Andrej Karpathy's move to Anthropic as the completion of a "Mercury Seven" — seven top CTOs who left leadership roles at well-funded companies for individual-contributor research at Anthropic — and argues Karpathy's mandate to use Claude to accelerate pre-training research is the AGI thesis stated plainly: the model itself becoming the binding input to its own improvement.

## Takeaways
- Karpathy is the seventh signing in a 14-month migration of CTOs from Adept AI, Super.com, Box, Instagram, You.com, and Workday into IC research roles at Anthropic — a "Mercury Seven" for AI.
- Karpathy's stated mandate is "using Claude to accelerate pre-training research itself" — recursive self-improvement as the core AGI strategy.
- The essay frames Karpathy's career as five frontiers in eleven years: OpenAI founding (2015), Tesla Autopilot (2017), GPT-3/ChatGPT era (2022), AI education via Eureka Labs (2024), and now recursive improvement at Anthropic (2026).
- Karpathy's auto-research work already demonstrated AI agents finding 20 things human review missed — applied to pre-training, this is a qualitatively different research program.
- Google, OpenAI, and Meta were all options — Karpathy chose Anthropic specifically, suggesting the recursive self-improvement thesis is most credible there.

## Synthesis
Harry DeMott's CO/AI essay is a literary tour de force that uses Tom Wolfe's "The Right Stuff" as its central metaphor to analyze Andrej Karpathy's decision to join Anthropic. The essay opens with a vivid parallel: Chuck Yeager broke Mach 1 with two cracked ribs and a sawed-off broom handle; Karpathy quit a company he owned on Tuesday morning to take an individual-contributor research role. Both had "the right stuff" — an intangible quality Wolfe spent eight years trying to define.

The numerical hook is the completion of Anthropic's "Mercury Seven": Karpathy is the seventh high-profile CTO in fourteen months to leave a leadership track for a research role at Anthropic, following executives from Adept AI, Super.com, Box, Instagram, You.com, and Workday. This concentration of talent around a single lab's research agenda is unprecedented in the modern AI era.

The essay then traces Karpathy's "five frontiers in eleven years" — OpenAI founding (2015), Tesla Autopilot (2017), the GPT-3/ChatGPT era (2022), Eureka Labs for AI education (2024), and now recursive self-improvement at Anthropic. DeMott's framing is sharp: Karpathy doesn't have jobs, he has frontiers. Each move was a step outward on a curve whose tangent kept rotating. The current frontier is the most audacious yet: using Claude to make Claude better. "Read that sentence carefully," DeMott writes. "It is the AGI thesis stated plainly in eight words."

DeMott then walks through why Karpathy didn't choose Google (Demis Hassabis already occupies the top research seat; Karpathy there "is Yeager applying to be a copilot"), OpenAI (left twice already, and the company that Brockman wrote a $25M check to MAGA Inc. is not the same one Karpathy co-founded), or Meta. The choice of Anthropic is presented as evidence that the recursive self-improvement thesis is most credible there — and that the people with "the right stuff" recognize where the real frontier lies. The essay is less news analysis than mythology-building, but effectively captures why Karpathy's move represents something deeper than a routine talent hire.

# After Automation
**Source**: https://every.to/p/after-automation
**Date**: May 21, 2026
**Author**: Dan Shipper
**Keywords**: AI, automation, future of work, human-AI collaboration, agents, knowledge work, commoditization, expertise

## Elevator pitch
The more we automate with AI, the more expert human work there is to do — because AI commoditizes past competence, flooding the world with sameness that creates new demand for human judgment and originality.

## Takeaways
- AI adoption at Every (30-person team) has not reduced headcount but transformed how work gets done — nobody writes code by hand anymore, and AI handles 95% of the CEO's email.
- Agent work is settling into two modes: asynchronous "agent employees" (coworker and embedded agents) and synchronous human-AI collaboration in tools like Codex and Claude Code.
- The "human sandwich" pattern: humans frame the task, AI collapses the work, humans judge and extend the result — this applies beyond coding to writing, email, and all knowledge work.
- AI's feedback loop: cheap competence → rapid adoption → abundance of sameness ("slop") → demand for differentiation → new demand for human experts.
- The current generation of models only knows work that has been done; humans know what needs to be done right now, in this specific moment, making human judgment irreplaceable even as AI capabilities accelerate.

## Synthesis
Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, presents a counterintuitive thesis: despite exponential AI progress, his company of nearly 30 people has more human work than ever. This isn't a temporary transitional state — it's the new structural reality of AI-augmented work.

The article documents two distinct modes of AI collaboration. The first is "agent employees" — named agents like Claudie (consulting), Andy (editorial), and Viktor (general-purpose) that live in Slack and handle delegated tasks asynchronously. The second, more important mode is synchronous human-AI collaboration through tools like Codex and Claude Code, which function as "operating systems for knowledge work" where humans and agents share the same workspace in real time.

Shipper introduces the "human sandwich" concept (coined by Kieran Klaassen): humans frame the task at the start, AI collapses the work in the middle, and humans judge and extend the result at the end. This pattern extends far beyond coding — Shipper writes essays, manages email, and runs SaaS tools entirely within Codex's in-app browser, talking through decisions with Monologue while agents handle execution.

The theoretical backbone of the argument is a five-step feedback loop. First, AI makes yesterday's human competence cheap by training on the "residue of expertise." Second, cheap competence gets rapidly adopted — evidenced by OpenClaw's 44,469 pull requests, dwarfing Kubernetes' 5,200 in all of 2022. Third, abundance creates sameness: when everyone uses the same models, output converges toward undifferentiated "slop." Fourth, sameness creates demand for difference — humans are exquisitely sensitive to pattern recognition and quickly devalue generic output. Fifth, demand for difference is demand for new human experts who can provide the aliveness, specificity, and contextual judgment that models, trained on past data, cannot.

The practical implication is that human experts move in two directions simultaneously: some build systems (review queues, evals, CI pipelines, instruction files) to absorb and quality-control the flood of AI-generated work, while others use AI to tackle bigger, more ambitious problems than previously possible. The article cites Calif, a small security firm that used Anthropic's Mythos to find macOS vulnerabilities in hours rather than the weeks or months previously required.

Shipper's conclusion is definitive: there is no tipping point coming where jobs disappear. "Once a situation has been reduced to text, once it has become corpus, it is a corpse." Human expertise — the capacity to know what needs doing in a specific moment with a specific customer, codebase, or conversation — remains the irreplaceable input that makes AI output valuable.

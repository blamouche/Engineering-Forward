# Amazon employees are "tokenmaxxing" due to pressure to use AI tools
**Source**: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/05/amazon-employees-are-tokenmaxxing-due-to-pressure-to-use-ai-tools
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Rafe Rosner-Uddin, Financial Times
**Keywords**: tokenmaxxing, Amazon, AI adoption, MeshClaw, perverse incentives

## Elevator pitch
Amazon employees are gaming internal AI usage metrics by automating unnecessary AI tasks—a practice dubbed "tokenmaxxing"—after the company set targets for over 80% of developers to use AI weekly and began tracking token consumption on leaderboards.

## Takeaways
- Amazon deployed "MeshClaw," an internal AI agent tool inspired by OpenClaw, allowing employees to automate tasks across workplace software like Slack, email, and code deployments
- Managers set weekly AI usage targets for developers and introduced internal leaderboards tracking token consumption, creating "perverse incentives"
- Employees responded by using MeshClaw to generate unnecessary AI activity solely to boost their token metrics, coining the term "tokenmaxxing"
- Amazon claims token stats won't affect performance reviews, but multiple employees say managers are monitoring the data anyway
- Security concerns have emerged around granting an AI agent broad permissions to act on a user's behalf, with one employee calling the default security posture "terrifying"

## Synthesis
Amazon's latest internal push to embed AI into daily workflows has produced a textbook case of Goodhart's law in action: when a metric becomes a target, it ceases to be a good metric. The company deployed MeshClaw—an internal AI agent tool inspired by the viral sensation OpenClaw—that allows employees to create AI agents capable of interacting with workplace software like Slack, email, and deployment pipelines. The tool is genuinely useful, reportedly enabling "thousands of Amazonians to automate repetitive tasks each day." But that's only half the story.

The problem started when Amazon introduced AI usage targets: more than 80% of developers must use AI tools each week, and the company began tracking AI token consumption on internal leaderboards. The intent was straightforward—drive adoption and justify the eye-watering $200 billion in capital expenditure Amazon is expected to spend this year, mostly on AI and data center infrastructure. But the execution created what employees describe as "perverse incentives."

In response, workers began "tokenmaxxing"—using MeshClaw to automate additional, unnecessary AI activity purely to inflate their token consumption numbers. One employee told the Financial Times plainly: "There is just so much pressure to use these tools. Some people are just using MeshClaw to maximize their token usage." Another noted that "managers are looking at it" despite Amazon's official stance that token statistics would not factor into performance evaluations. The company recently restricted leaderboard visibility to individual employees and their managers, but the cat is already out of the bag.

This isn't an isolated phenomenon. Meta employees have reportedly engaged in similar tokenmaxxing behavior, suggesting a broader Silicon Valley pattern where the pressure to demonstrate AI ROI breeds counterproductive behavior. When companies spend hundreds of billions on AI infrastructure, the organizational imperative to show adoption at any cost can undermine the very productivity gains the tools are meant to deliver.

Beyond the incentive problem, the article surfaces significant security concerns. MeshClaw operates with broad permissions to act on a user's behalf—triaging emails, initiating code deployments, interacting with messaging platforms. One employee described the default security posture as "terrifying," highlighting the tension between agentic AI capability and enterprise security. The risk of unintended actions or errors from an over-permissioned AI agent is not theoretical, and employee reluctance to grant such access represents a meaningful barrier to adoption that metrics-driven approaches fail to capture.

The MeshClaw story is a useful cautionary tale about the gap between measuring AI adoption and measuring AI value. Token counts tell you how much AI is being used, not whether it's being used well. As the Charlie Munger quote in the article's top comment puts it: "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome."

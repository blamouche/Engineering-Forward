# how to enter side doors — a field guide to jobs, cold emails, and making yourself legible

**Source:** Velvet Noise (Substack)
**Author:** maja
**Date:** May 15, 2026
**URL:** https://velvetnoise.substack.com/p/how-to-enter-side-doors

## Summary

A practical field guide for navigating the job market through "side doors" rather than the crowded front door of job boards. The essay reframes jobs as "bundles of problems someone wants solved" and argues that the best career moves happen through specificity, visible proof of work, and reaching toward specific humans rather than generic institutions. Illustrated with personal stories of landing jobs through unusually specific cold emails.

## Key Points

- **Jobs are search problems:** A job is "a bundle of problems someone wants solved badly enough to pay another person to solve them"
- **Front door is crowded:** Hundreds of AI-polished applications per role, making it a losing game
- **Two categories of side doors:** Outbound (reaching out directly with specificity) and Inbound (making your signal visible through public work)
- **Specificity is signal:** In a market full of generic AI applications, specificity itself becomes the differentiating factor
- **Audacity as a practical skill:** If you want an unusual role, you often have to move unusually — waiting for the perfect job description to appear rarely works
- **Personal stories:** Landing a growth consulting job at Calm via an "unhinged" but specific email to the CEO at age 18; creating an internship that didn't exist at Blackbird Foundation via cold LinkedIn message; a friend whose philosophical essay on taste discourse led to a job offer
- **Key framing:** "A company is a group of people trying to make something happen. They have problems, bottlenecks, and things they don't yet know they need because no one has shown them."

## Why It Matters

As AI floods job applications with generic content and the graduate job market shrinks, the ability to navigate through side doors becomes a critical career skill. The essay provides a concrete mental model for how to do it, grounded in the reality that behind every company are actual humans with unsolved problems — and specificity is the highest form of signal.

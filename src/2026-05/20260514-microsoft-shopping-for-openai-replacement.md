# Microsoft is quietly shopping for an OpenAI replacement
**Source**: https://thenextweb.com/news/microsoft-startup-deals-life-after-openai
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Cristian Dina
**Keywords**: Microsoft, OpenAI, Cursor, Inception, MAI, Mustafa Suleyman, acquisition, AI strategy, diffusion models

## Elevator pitch
Three weeks after ending its exclusive OpenAI license, Microsoft is canvassing AI startups for acquisitions — having already explored buying Cursor (blocked by regulatory concerns) and now in talks with diffusion-model startup Inception, while Mustafa Suleyman's MAI team races toward a 2027 frontier LLM.

## Takeaways
- Microsoft's attempt to acquire Cursor was abandoned due to regulatory concerns around also owning GitHub Copilot — SpaceXAI bought a $60B option on Cursor instead
- Current talks are with Inception, a Stanford spinout building diffusion-based language models that process tokens in parallel at 1,000+ tokens/second
- Mustafa Suleyman's MAI Superintelligence team shipped its first 3 models (transcription, voice, image) in April, targeting a frontier LLM by 2027
- The April 27 deal amendment ended Microsoft's exclusive OpenAI license, freed OpenAI to sell on AWS, and removed the AGI clause
- Microsoft kept a 27% OpenAI stake worth ~$135B, IP license through 2032, and Azure-first deployment clause

## Synthesis
Microsoft's post-OpenAI strategy is taking concrete shape, and it's a dual-track approach: acquire or partner with startups for architectural diversity while building in-house capability through Mustafa Suleyman's MAI team. The motivation is clear — after spending $13B on OpenAI and then rewriting the deal to end exclusivity, Microsoft no longer assumes one partner will suffice.

The Cursor episode is revealing. Microsoft weighed buying the code-generation startup (annualized revenue: $0 to $2B in three years), but owning both GitHub Copilot and Cursor was deemed a regulatory risk too high. SpaceXAI then swooped in with a $60B option. The lesson: Microsoft's antitrust shadow constrains its acquisition options in ways competitors don't face.

The Inception talks are more promising. The Stanford spinout builds diffusion-based language models — a fundamentally different architecture from autoregressive transformers, processing tokens in parallel at over 1,000 tokens/second. If the technology matures, it could offer Microsoft a genuine architectural advantage, not just a backup supplier. M12 already invested in Inception's $50M round; now the parent company is exploring something larger.

Suleyman's MAI team provides the internal hedge. Having shipped transcription, voice, and image models in April, the 2027 target for a frontier LLM is ambitious but credible given Microsoft's compute resources. The endgame: Microsoft wants optionality — the ability to operate without OpenAI if necessary, while maintaining enough OpenAI stake and relationship to benefit if the partnership thrives.

## Synthesis
John Thornhill's Sifted View piece examines the strategic crossroads facing specialist AI companies through the lens of Legora, a Swedish legal AI company that recently raised $550m at a $5.6bn valuation. The celebration at London's Royal Opera House masked a critical question: can vertical AI startups survive when general-purpose AI platforms enter their space?

At the party, Legora's CEO made a bold declaration: "Legacy AI is over." The company is pivoting from traditional legal AI assistance — drafting, research, document review — toward "agentic law," where AI systems autonomously execute complex legal workflows end-to-end. This reflects a broader shift in the AI industry from copilots to agents, but it also positions Legora directly against Anthropic's expanding capabilities.

The tension is clear. Anthropic's agentic models can increasingly handle domain-specific tasks that were once the exclusive territory of specialist companies. The foundation model companies have the resources, data, and distribution to enter vertical markets with products that may be faster and cheaper than purpose-built solutions. Legora's response is to go deeper — owning not just a layer of the legal workflow but the entire outcome, from document generation to compliance verification.

Thornhill uses Legora as a case study for a question that applies broadly: when Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google enter your vertical, what's your moat? Legora's bet is that deep domain expertise, established client relationships, and end-to-end workflow ownership create defensibility that pure-play AI platforms cannot easily replicate. The company's scale — over 7,600 employees and clients across 150 countries — suggests there is room for vertical champions, but the pressure from platform companies is only intensifying.

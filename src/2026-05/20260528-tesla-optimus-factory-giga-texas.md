## Elevator pitch
Tesla's dedicated Optimus humanoid robot factory is officially under construction at Gigafactory Texas, with drone footage confirming steel structures rising on the North Campus — a facility targeting 10 million robots per year (~27,000/day) with high-volume production starting summer 2027.

- Tesla ended Model S and Model X production at Fremont to clear space for the Optimus pilot line

## Synthesis
Tesla's dedicated Optimus factory at Gigafactory Texas represents the physical foundation of the company's second act — a bet that humanoid robotics will eventually surpass the automotive business in both scale and value. Drone footage captured on May 27, 2026 confirmed steel beams rising on the North Campus, providing the first visible evidence that construction is underway on a facility designed to produce up to 10 million Optimus units per year, or approximately 27,000 robots per day. That figure has no precedent in complex manufacturing.

The factory building extends nearly the full length of the main Giga Texas vehicle factory — roughly 4,000+ feet — but is optimized for humanoid robot assembly rather than car production. It sits adjacent to the planned Terafab AI chip factory, creating an integrated campus where the robot's processing hardware and physical body are produced in the same complex. This vertical integration, applied to robotics, mirrors Tesla's approach to batteries and motors.

The two-phase strategy is classic Tesla. Phase 1 begins at Fremont in July or August 2026, where Tesla ended Model S and Model X production to clear space for a pilot line. This phase is internal deployment only — robots operating in Tesla's own factories to generate the training data needed to improve both hardware and software. Phase 2, targeting summer 2027, brings the Texas factory online for high-volume production using a second-generation line that incorporates all lessons from the Fremont pilot. The Fremont pilot is not just a production line but a data collection engine, feeding the AI training loop that will define the Texas line's design.

Musk's thesis is that Optimus will ultimately be more valuable than Tesla's entire automotive business, with a target price under $20,000 and an addressable market that is literally global labor. The challenges are substantial: no existing manufacturing processes operate at the target cost and volume for humanoid robots, bipedal locomotion and dexterous manipulation require AI capabilities far beyond full self-driving, and the supply chain for actuators, sensors, and microprocessors is not yet commodity. But the factory is rising, and the pilot is months away.

# OpenAI Launches $4B AI Services Business on Heels of Similar Anthropic Announcement
**Source**: https://www.crn.com/news/ai/2026/openai-launches-services-business-on-heels-of-similar-anthropic-announcement
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: CRN Staff
**Keywords**: OpenAI, DeployCo, Anthropic, AI services, channel partners, system integrators, enterprise AI, Tomoro, TPG, Goldman Sachs

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI announced a $4 billion-plus AI services company called DeployCo — built on the acquisition of UK consultancy Tomoro — following closely on Anthropic's own ~$1.5 billion services company announcement, signaling an arms race in enterprise AI deployment services.

## Takeaways
- DeployCo will embed forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) specialized in frontier AI into organizations, working alongside OpenAI's Frontier Alliance partner ecosystem and the broader consulting industry.
- Investors include TPG (lead), Brookfield ($500M), Advent, Bain, Goldman Sachs, SoftBank, and Warburg Pincus — with private equity sponsors able to leverage portfolio companies for early AI transformation use cases.
- The Tomoro acquisition brings ~150 FDEs and deployment specialists; more acquisitions are planned, with the deal expected to close "in the coming months."
- Both OpenAI and Anthropic are simultaneously investing in channel partner programs — OpenAI hired Colleen Kapase (formerly Google Cloud, Snowflake) for a top channel post, while Anthropic launched its Claude Partner Network with $100M.
- A rivalry is emerging between traditional solution providers (who argue they bring better security and business process understanding) and born-in-AI upstarts (who claim faster execution and deeper AI expertise).

## Synthesis
The enterprise AI services market is experiencing a formative moment as both OpenAI and Anthropic launch dedicated professional services arms within weeks of each other. OpenAI's DeployCo, backed by $4 billion-plus from 19 investment firms, consultancies, and system integrators, represents a major bet that frontier AI deployment requires more than just API access — it demands embedded engineering talent that can redesign organizational infrastructure and workflows.

The structure is notable: DeployCo is a standalone business unit with OpenAI as majority owner and controller, operating as an extension of OpenAI to keep customers connected to research, product, and in-house deployment teams. The acquisition of Tomoro — a London-based applied AI consultancy founded in 2023 by an ex-Accenture generative AI lead — provides the initial talent base. Private equity sponsors like Brookfield, Advent, and Bain can immediately deploy AI across their portfolio companies, creating built-in early use cases for validation.

The competitive dynamic with Anthropic is explicit. Anthropic's services company, formed with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs, has a reported ~$1.5 billion commitment — smaller than DeployCo but following the same template. Goldman Sachs is backing both, hedging its bets. Both companies are simultaneously building channel ecosystems, recognizing that enterprise AI adoption requires a constellation of partners rather than direct sales alone.

CRN's reporting surfaces an important tension: traditional solution providers like CGI (No. 13 on CRN's SP500) argue that decades of business process expertise and security trust give them an edge over born-in-AI upstarts. These upstarts counter that they move faster and have deeper AI-native talent. The market appears large enough for both, but the collision between established integrators and AI-native services firms will define the enterprise AI deployment landscape for years to come. OpenAI disclosed that over 1 million businesses have adopted its products and APIs, while DeployCo's partners sponsor more than 2,000 businesses globally — suggesting the addressable market is vast but the "last mile" of deployment remains the bottleneck these services companies aim to solve.

# The Pulse: Did capacity shortages turn Anthropic hostile to devs?
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-did-capacity-shortages
**Date**: 2026-05-07
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: Anthropic, capacity shortages, developer relations, AI infrastructure, Claude Code

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic may have been degrading model quality and restricting Claude Code access to conceal compute capacity shortages, even as it secured new infrastructure deals.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic has been upsetting developers with "dumber" model behavior and removing Claude Code access from some paid accounts
- The timing suggests these changes may have been driven by hidden compute capacity issues
- Amazon lifted its ban on Claude Code and Codex usage after initially blocking them to favor its own Kiro coding agent
- Meta is forcibly assigning 20-40% of engineers to data labelling work ahead of potential layoffs
- A new trend of small "AI-forward" teams (5-10 devs) is emerging at Meta and Amazon, touted by CEOs as more efficient than larger teams

## Synthesis
This May 2026 edition of The Pulse from The Pragmatic Engineer covers several significant developments in the AI and tech industry. The central investigation examines whether Anthropic's recent pattern of frustrating developers—through degraded model quality and restricted access to Claude Code—was actually a smoke screen for underlying compute capacity shortages. Gergely Orosz notes that Anthropic had recently secured substantial compute resources from SpaceX, and the timing of their developer-hostile moves raises questions about whether the company was scrambling to manage infrastructure constraints while avoiding public acknowledgment of the problem.

A related development sees Amazon reversing its policy on AI coding tools: the company had previously banned Claude Code and Codex usage internally, apparently to protect its own Kiro coding agent. That ban has now been lifted, signaling either competitive confidence or pragmatic acknowledgment that developers need access to the best tools available.

At Meta, Orosz reports a troubling trend of engineers being forcefully reassigned to data labelling work—between 20-40% of engineers in some teams. This comes against a backdrop of anticipated job cuts, though the article suggests this menial work assignment might paradoxically offer short-term job security for those affected. The piece also identifies an emerging industry pattern of "AI-forward" teams: small groups of 5-10 developers that CEOs at both Meta and Amazon claim outperform traditional larger teams of 50. Orosz adds important caveats, noting it remains unclear what happens to the "excess" engineers and whether this efficiency is limited to mechanical tasks like code rewrites.

Additional industry notes cover Meta's employee activity tracking, OpenAI's migration away from Datadog, Apple's inadvertent revelation that it uses Claude Code, GitHub-to-Xbox internal transfers at Microsoft, VS Code's controversial insertion of "coauthored by Copilot" attribution even when Copilot contributed nothing, and an analysis of Coinbase layoffs. The article also clarifies that upcoming GitHub Copilot pricing changes are even more dramatic than initially reported, with Pro and Pro+ plans facing roughly 3x increases and business plans shifting to unpredictable API token-based pricing.

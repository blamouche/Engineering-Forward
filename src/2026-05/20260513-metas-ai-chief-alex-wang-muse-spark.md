## Elevator pitch
Meta poached Scale AI co-founder Alex Wang in a $14B deal to revitalize its flagging AI efforts, and now Wang breaks his silence on the new Muse Spark model and Meta's strategy to catch OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

## Takeaways
- Meta acquired Alex Wang from Scale AI in June 2025 in a deal valued at $14 billion to rescue its AI competitiveness
- The first major output of Wang's revitalized AI team is the Muse Spark model, released in April 2026
- Wang has assembled an all-star AI hierarchy including Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross, and Shengjia Zhao
- Meta has been handing out extraordinary pay packages to attract top AI talent, with Zuckerberg personally delivering soup to recruits
- Wang has personal tensions with Sam Altman, highlighting the increasingly personal nature of AI industry rivalries

## Synthesis
Ashlee Vance's Core Memory podcast secured the first public interview with Alex Wang since Meta acquired him away from Scale AI in a landmark $14 billion deal. The conversation reveals a company that recognized it was losing the AI race and took dramatic measures to reverse course.

Wang's arrival at Meta in June 2025 marked a cultural shift. He relocated from San Francisco to the South Bay, went into what Vance describes as effective hiding, and has been working intensively to assemble what Meta hopes will be a world-beating AI team. The first tangible result—the Muse Spark model released in April 2026—represents the beginning of what Meta frames as a serious competitive push.

The interview paints a picture of Meta going to extraordinary lengths in the AI talent war. Zuckerberg himself has been personally involved in recruitment, reportedly delivering soup to prospective hires. The compensation packages being offered are described as unprecedented, reflecting the desperation and determination of a company that sees AI supremacy as existential.

Wang's team composition is noteworthy. Alongside him are Nat Friedman (former GitHub CEO), Daniel Gross (entrepreneur and investor), and Shengjia Zhao—a concentration of Silicon Valley AI talent that signals Meta's ambition to compete at the highest level. The episode also touches on Wang's personal friction with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, adding a layer of interpersonal drama to the corporate competition narrative.

The broader context is the intensifying AI arms race. Meta, which once pioneered open-source AI with LLaMA, found itself falling behind as OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google pulled ahead. Wang's mandate is clear: close the gap, and do it fast. The Muse Spark model is presented as the first step, with the implication that more aggressive moves are coming. The interview provides a rare window into the psychology and strategy of one of the most consequential corporate AI turnarounds currently underway.

# The Pulse: Forward deployed engineering heats up again
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering
**Date**: 2026-05-14
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: forward deployed engineering, FDE, AI deployment, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, consultant engineering, solution architect, tech job market, vibe coding

## Elevator pitch
Forward deployed engineering—engineers embedded directly with customers to implement AI solutions—is experiencing a dramatic resurgence in 2026, with massive hiring demand at Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, while simultaneously the broader tech job market faces rising losses and the lines between "vibe coding" and agentic engineering continue to blur.

## Takeaways
- Forward deployed engineering (FDE) roles are seeing explosive demand at major AI companies including Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, functioning as consultant/solution architect hybrids embedded with enterprise customers
- The modern FDE role has evolved from its Palantir-era origins into an AI-specialized position focused on helping enterprises deploy and customize frontier AI models
- Rising tech job losses in other segments create a stark contrast with the AI deployment talent crunch
- Companies are increasingly self-reporting 100% AI usage in certain development workflows, highlighting the normalization of AI-first engineering
- The distinction between "vibe coding" (rapid, AI-assisted prototyping) and formal agentic engineering is merging as tooling and practices mature

## Synthesis
Published on May 14, 2026, this edition of The Pulse captures a striking moment in the evolution of AI engineering roles. Forward deployed engineering—a model popularized by Palantir where engineers work on-site with customers—is undergoing a major revival, but this time driven by the AI industry's unique deployment challenges. Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all aggressively hiring for FDE positions, recognizing that getting enterprises to successfully adopt and integrate frontier AI models requires more than just API documentation.

The modern forward deployed engineer sits at the intersection of consultant, solutions architect, and AI researcher. Unlike traditional SaaS deployment, AI integration involves understanding a customer's specific data, workflows, and domain constraints, then adapting models, prompts, and infrastructure accordingly. This requires deep technical skill combined with the interpersonal ability to work directly with client engineering teams—a rare combination that commands premium compensation and intense competition among AI labs.

The context is a bifurcating tech job market. While AI-focused roles at frontier companies boom, the broader tech industry continues to see job losses, creating a tale of two markets. Engineers with AI deployment expertise are in unprecedented demand; those in more traditional software roles face increasing pressure as automation reshapes the landscape.

Another significant observation is the trend of companies openly self-reporting 100% AI usage in certain development workflows. This transparency signals a cultural shift: AI-assisted development has moved from experimental to standard practice, and teams are increasingly comfortable acknowledging (and even celebrating) how much of their output is AI-generated. The article connects this to the merging of "vibe coding"—the rapid-prototyping style where developers describe desired outcomes to AI tools and iterate quickly—with more structured agentic engineering practices. As AI coding tools become more reliable and sophisticated, the distinction between quick AI-driven prototyping and formal software engineering continues to dissolve.

The broader implication is that the AI industry is entering a deployment-and-integration phase, where the bottleneck shifts from model capability to real-world adoption. Forward deployed engineers are the human bridge for that transition, and their resurgence reflects a recognition that even the most powerful models require skilled engineering to deliver value inside complex enterprise environments.

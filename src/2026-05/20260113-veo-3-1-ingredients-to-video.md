# Veo 3.1 Ingredients to Video: More consistency, creativity and control

## Elevator pitch
Google DeepMind's Veo 3.1 "Ingredients to Video" update brings native vertical video, 1080p/4K upscaling, and dramatically improved character/background consistency — turning reference images into expressive, mobile-first video clips with professional-grade output quality across the Gemini app, YouTube, Flow, and API platforms.

## Takeaways
- Veo 3.1 now supports native 9:16 vertical video for mobile-first platforms (YouTube Shorts, TikTok format), eliminating the need to crop or lose quality when adapting horizontal generation for short-form content
- The "Ingredients to Video" capability lets creators upload reference images (characters, objects, textures, backgrounds) and generate videos that maintain identity consistency — characters look the same across scenes, objects don't morph, backgrounds stay coherent
- Professional upscaling to 1080p and 4K is now available, with Google describing it as "state-of-the-art" for high-fidelity production workflows on Flow, the Gemini API, and Vertex AI
- Distribution is broad: consumers get it in the Gemini app and YouTube Shorts/Create app; professionals get it in Flow, Google Vids, the Gemini API, and Vertex AI — a full spectrum from casual creator to enterprise
- All Veo-generated videos include SynthID digital watermarks, and Google has deployed a verification tool in the Gemini app to help users determine if content is AI-generated

## Synthesis
Google DeepMind's January 2026 Veo update represents a deliberate pivot toward making AI video generation practical for creators rather than just impressive for demos. While previous Veo releases focused on model capability (longer videos, better physics, native audio in Veo 3), Veo 3.1 "Ingredients to Video" is about workflow integration and output quality — the unglamorous stuff that determines whether professionals actually adopt a tool.

The headline feature is native vertical video support. This sounds minor but is strategically significant: the vast majority of AI video consumption and creation happens on mobile, in vertical format (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels). Previous AI video models generated horizontal output by default, forcing creators to crop, reframe, or accept letterboxed content. By generating native 9:16 from the start, Veo 3.1 removes a friction point that silently discouraged mobile-first creators.

"Ingredients to Video" itself is a conceptual shift from text-to-video toward composable visual creation. Rather than describing everything in a prompt, creators upload reference images — a character design, a background environment, a specific object, a texture — and Veo 3.1 composes them into a coherent video. The critical improvement is identity consistency: characters maintain their appearance across scenes, objects don't randomly morph between frames, and backgrounds stay recognizable. This is the difference between generating a single cool clip and telling a narrative with persistent visual elements.

The professional tooling signals are clear. 1080p and 4K upscaling moves Veo from "interesting experiment" to "usable in production pipelines." The integration across Flow (Google's professional creative tool), Google Vids (workspace video creation), the Gemini API, and Vertex AI shows Google is targeting the full creator stack — from the person making a YouTube Short on their phone to the studio producing broadcast content.

Ricky Wong's authorship as Lead Product Manager (rather than a researcher) is telling: this is a product announcement, not a research paper. The practical framing — "create more expressive videos from images, directly on your phone" — reflects a maturing technology moving from capability demonstration to user experience design. The SynthID watermarking and verification tools are the compliance layer, addressing the growing urgency around AI content provenance as these tools reach mass-market distribution through YouTube and the Gemini app.

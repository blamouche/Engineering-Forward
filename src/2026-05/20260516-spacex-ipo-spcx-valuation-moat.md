# SpaceX heads into a record-shattering IPO with the 'deepest moat that exists today' as investors vow to 'never bet against Elon'
**Source**: https://fortune.com/2026/05/16/spacex-ipo-spcx-stock-valuation-space-market-moat-elon-musk-starlink-xai/
**Date**: May 16, 2026
**Author**: Jason Ma
**Keywords**: SpaceX, IPO, SPCX, Elon Musk, Starlink, xAI, valuation, governance, space market, Nasdaq, Cursor, institutional investors

## Elevator pitch
SpaceX is racing toward a record-shattering $1.75 trillion IPO with the "deepest moat that exists today" — controlling over 80% of global rocket launches — but its proposed governance structure giving Elon Musk near-unchecked power is drawing fierce pushback from the largest U.S. public pension funds.

## Takeaways
1. SpaceX expects to price its IPO as soon as June 11, 2026, trading on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX, seeking to raise up to $75B at a $1.75T valuation — surpassing Saudi Aramco's 2019 record.
2. The company controls over 80% of global rocket launches, operates 10,000+ Starlink satellites, and recently merged with xAI, creating a vertically integrated space-and-AI giant.
3. SpaceX's governance proposal includes super-voting Class B shares that would give Musk nearly unchecked executive power — he could only be fired by himself — plus performance milestones awarding up to 200M shares for reaching $7.5T valuation and a Mars colony.
4. The largest U.S. public pension systems (New York and California) sent a letter objecting to "the most management-favorable governance structure ever brought to the U.S. public markets at this scale."
5. Despite governance concerns, investors remain bullish — "never bet against Elon" is the refrain — and SpaceX's revenue grew 30% to $18.7B in 2025, though xAI's $6.4B losses pushed the combined entity into the red.

## Synthesis
Jason Ma's Fortune exclusive captures a pivotal moment in capital markets history: SpaceX is preparing what would be the largest IPO ever, targeting a $1.75 trillion valuation that would eclipse Saudi Aramco's $1.7 trillion record from 2019. The company, which merged with Elon Musk's xAI startup, plans to price as soon as June 11 and begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX the following day.

The investor enthusiasm is rooted in SpaceX's extraordinary market position. The company claimed more than 80% of global rocket launches in 2025 and operates a constellation of over 10,000 Starlink satellites providing internet to businesses and militaries worldwide. An investor told the Financial Times: "It's a truly unique business with the deepest moat that exists today. This company launches over 90% of Western payload into space each year. It's like if you own the only undersea cable from the U.S. to Europe." SpaceX is also a top launch provider for NASA, the Pentagon, and is positioned to help develop Trump's "Golden Dome" missile-defense shield.

But the governance structure accompanying this historic IPO is generating intense controversy. SpaceX has proposed an unprecedented arrangement featuring super-voting Class B shares, mandatory arbitration, and restrictions on shareholder proposals. Musk's compensation package includes performance-based awards: up to 200 million Class B shares if SpaceX reaches a $7.5 trillion valuation and establishes a Mars colony with one million inhabitants, and 60 million more shares if valuation hits $6.6 trillion with a network of space-based data centers providing 100 terawatts of computing capacity.

The New York and California public pension systems — the largest in the U.S. — sent a letter to SpaceX objecting to "the most management-favorable governance structure ever brought to the U.S. public markets at this scale." The concern is that public shareholders would be funding Musk's ambitions with minimal ability to influence corporate governance or hold management accountable.

Financially, the picture is mixed. SpaceX's revenue grew more than 30% to $18.7 billion in 2025, with Starlink more than doubling its profit to $4.4 billion. However, the xAI merger brought significant losses — xAI's losses deepened to $6.4 billion, pushing the combined entity to a $4.9 billion net loss. The company has also agreed to acquire AI coding startup Cursor for $60 billion and is competing for a NASA lunar landing contract. The IPO comes at a moment when Musk appears to have repaired his relationship with President Trump, having accompanied him on a recent China visit alongside other top CEOs. For investors, the calculus is stark: accept unprecedented governance concentration in exchange for a stake in what may be the most strategically positioned company in the world.

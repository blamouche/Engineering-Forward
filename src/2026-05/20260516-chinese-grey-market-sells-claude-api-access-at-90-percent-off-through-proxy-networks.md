## Elevator pitch
A Chinese grey market has emerged selling Claude API access at 90% discounts through "transfer stations" — proxy networks that use stolen credentials and model substitution while harvesting users' prompts and outputs for resale as AI training data.

## Takeaways
- Chinese grey market operators are reselling Claude API access at roughly 90% below official pricing through proxy-based "transfer stations"
- The proxies use stolen or shared credentials and sometimes substitute cheaper models while billing users for premium access
- User prompts and outputs are systematically harvested and resold as AI training data, creating a dual revenue stream for operators
- This represents a significant security risk for enterprises whose sensitive data may flow through unvetted intermediaries
- The grey market highlights the growing economic incentives around API access arbitrage as frontier model pricing remains high

## Synthesis
A sophisticated grey market for AI API access has emerged in China, according to reporting from Tom's Hardware. Operators have built proxy networks colloquially called "transfer stations" that resell Claude API access at approximately 90% below Anthropic's official pricing. The business model combines multiple revenue streams: charging users for discounted access, harvesting their prompts and model outputs, and reselling that accumulated interaction data for AI training purposes.

The technical operation involves several layers of deception. The proxy operators obtain access credentials through a combination of shared accounts, stolen credentials, and potentially compromised enterprise licenses. In some cases, users are unknowingly routed to cheaper substitute models rather than the genuine Claude API they're paying for, with the difference pocketed by the intermediary. The most concerning aspect for enterprise users is the systematic data harvesting: every prompt submitted and every response received passes through the proxy infrastructure, where it can be logged, analyzed, and monetized.

This grey market highlights the growing economic pressures around frontier AI model access. As the cost of state-of-the-art models remains high relative to what many developers and small businesses can afford, arbitrage opportunities multiply. The phenomenon mirrors earlier grey markets in telecommunications, software licensing, and streaming services — but with the added dimension that user data itself becomes a monetizable asset for the intermediary, creating perverse incentives to maximize traffic at the expense of security and authenticity.

For enterprises, the implications are serious. Sensitive business data, proprietary code, and confidential communications that flow through these unauthorized proxies are exposed to unknown third parties with unclear data handling practices. Organizations using AI APIs should implement strict endpoint verification, monitor for unauthorized access patterns, and educate teams about the risks of using discounted "reseller" API access. The grey market also raises questions about how AI companies can make their APIs more accessible to price-sensitive markets without inadvertently fueling such ecosystems.

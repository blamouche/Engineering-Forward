# DeepMind Spinout Isomorphic Labs Raises $2.1bn
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/isomorphic-labs-2-1bn
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Freya Pratty
**Keywords**: Isomorphic Labs, DeepMind, drug discovery, AlphaFold, AI biotech, Thrive Capital, Sovereign AI fund, Demis Hassabis

## Elevator pitch
DeepMind spinout Isomorphic Labs has raised $2.1 billion in Series B funding to accelerate its AI-powered drug discovery platform, attracting sovereign wealth funds alongside US venture capital in one of Europe's largest-ever biotech AI raises.

## Takeaways
- The $2.1B Series B was led by Thrive Capital with participation from the UK's Sovereign AI fund, Abu Dhabi's MGX, Alphabet's GV, and Singapore's Temasek
- This comes one year after a $600M raise, bringing total external funding to $2.7B — signaling immense investor confidence in AI-driven drug discovery
- Isomorphic leverages DeepMind's AlphaFold technology to predict protein structures for drug design
- The company competes with OpenAI, which also released a life sciences model earlier this year
- Isomorphic has signed partnerships with pharmaceutical giants Eli Lilly and Novartis and has begun pre-clinical trials

## Synthesis
Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug discovery company spun out of Google DeepMind in 2021, has secured $2.1 billion in Series B funding, marking one of the largest capital raises ever for a European AI-biotech company. The round was led by US venture firm Thrive Capital, with participation from an unusually diverse coalition of investors: the UK's Sovereign AI fund, Abu Dhabi's MGX, Alphabet's venture arm GV, and Singapore's state investment firm Temasek. This follows a $600 million raise in May 2025, also led by Thrive and GV, bringing the company's total external funding to approximately $2.7 billion.

The investment thesis rests on Isomorphic's unique position at the intersection of two of the most hyped technology trends: artificial intelligence and biotechnology. The company is built on DeepMind's groundbreaking AlphaFold technology, an AI system capable of predicting the three-dimensional structures of proteins with remarkable accuracy. Protein structure prediction has long been considered one of biology's grand challenges, and AlphaFold's success — recognized with a Nobel Prize in Chemistry for Hassabis and John Jumper in 2024 — opened the door to a new paradigm in drug discovery. Rather than the traditional trial-and-error approach of screening millions of chemical compounds, AI-powered methods can predict how potential drug molecules will interact with target proteins, dramatically accelerating the discovery process.

The spinout decision, articulated by former president Colin Murdoch in a previous Sifted interview, reflected a deliberate organizational design choice. DeepMind's core workforce consists of machine learning researchers, engineers, and product managers — a profile well-suited to fundamental AI research but insufficient for drug development. Drug discovery requires chemists, pharmacologists, and biologists, specialists for whom DeepMind had no established career progression path. Spinning out allowed Isomorphic to recruit these domain experts, set a clearer and more focused mission, and cultivate a distinct culture appropriate to biotech rather than pure AI research.

The competitive landscape is intensifying. OpenAI entered the life sciences arena earlier this year with its own model, signaling that the major AI labs view drug discovery as a natural extension of their capabilities. Isomorphic's advantage lies in its head start, its privileged access to DeepMind's research pipeline, and its growing portfolio of pharmaceutical partnerships. The company has already signed deals with industry leaders Eli Lilly and Novartis, and Demis Hassabis confirmed that pre-clinical trials have begun — a milestone that moves the company from pure research toward tangible drug development.

The presence of the UK's Sovereign AI fund in this round is particularly noteworthy, representing a strategic government investment in a technology deemed critical to national competitiveness. Combined with Abu Dhabi and Singapore sovereign participation, the investor syndicate reflects a geopolitical dimension to AI-biotech that extends beyond traditional venture capital. The $2.1 billion raise positions Isomorphic Labs not just as a promising startup but as a potential anchor of a new European industry at the convergence of AI and life sciences, with implications for drug development timelines, healthcare costs, and the pharmaceutical industry's structure over the coming decade.

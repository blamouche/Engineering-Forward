# The AI Paradox: More Automation, More Humans, More Work
**Source**: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-ai-paradox-dan-shipper
**Date**: May 24, 2026
**Author**: Lenny Rachitsky (interview with Dan Shipper)
**Keywords**: AI paradox, automation, forward deployed engineer, SaaS, CLI, Codex, Claude Code, product management, design, future of work

## Elevator pitch
Dan Shipper argues that AI automation paradoxically increases demand for human workers by expanding what's possible — the "SaaS apocalypse" narrative is wrong, CLIs are dead, and forward deployed engineers, PMs, and full-stack designers will be the most valuable roles in the AI era.

## Takeaways
- AI automation creates more work, not less, by expanding the scope of what can be built and maintained
- SaaS is not dying — Shipper is bullish on SaaS stocks as users bring their own AI tokens into apps
- CLIs are over; the future of work will happen inside agent environments like Codex or Claude Code
- Every company will have one "super-agent" inside Slack that every employee talks to regularly
- Forward deployed engineer is the new most essential role, and full-stack designers become superheroes

## Synthesis
Dan Shipper, co-founder and CEO of Every, returns to Lenny's Podcast with a contrarian thesis: AI automation does not eliminate human work — it expands it. Drawing from his experience running a 30-person company where everyone is an AI early adopter, Shipper argues that the "job apocalypse" narrative fundamentally misunderstands how automation interacts with economic demand. As AI handles more routine tasks, the bar for what's possible rises, creating entirely new categories of work that require human judgment.

Shipper makes several bold predictions. He claims the command-line interface (CLI) era is finished — future work will happen inside agent-native environments like Codex or Claude Code, where humans and AI collaborate in shared workspaces. Every company will eventually deploy a "super-agent" accessible to all employees through Slack, serving as a universal interface to internal knowledge and tools.

On the SaaS question, Shipper takes a sharply contrarian position. Far from being disrupted by AI agents, SaaS companies stand to benefit as users bring their own AI tokens into apps, improving margins rather than destroying them. He explicitly says he would buy SaaS stocks right now. This aligns with his broader view that the most valuable new role is the "forward deployed engineer" — someone who can bridge AI capabilities with specific business contexts.

Shipper is also bullish on product managers and designers, arguing that as AI handles execution, the premium shifts to taste, judgment, and problem definition. Full-stack designers who can move from concept to working prototype using AI tools become the new superheroes. The conversation also touches on Every's own experiments: their newsletter content is increasingly AI-generated and their readers engage with it enthusiastically, challenging assumptions about what audiences will accept. The central paradox remains: more automation means more humans doing more ambitious work, not fewer humans doing nothing.

# Report: Google and SpaceX in talks to put data centers into orbit
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/report-google-and-spacex-in-talks-to-put-data-centers-into-orbit
**Date**: May 12, 2026
**Author**: Rebecca Bellan
**Keywords**: Google, SpaceX, orbital data centers, AI compute, satellite, Elon Musk, xAI, Anthropic, IPO

## Elevator pitch
Google and SpaceX are reportedly in talks to build orbital data centers, pitching space as the future home for AI compute despite costs that remain far higher than terrestrial alternatives today.

## Takeaways
- Google and SpaceX are exploring launching data centers into orbit, according to WSJ sources, as SpaceX prepares for a $1.75 trillion IPO
- The deal follows Anthropic's recent agreement with SpaceX to use xAI computing resources in Memphis, with potential to expand to orbital facilities
- Google is also reportedly talking to other rocket-launch companies and plans to launch prototype satellites by 2027 under "Project Suncatcher"
- Elon Musk claims orbital data centers are cheaper to operate and avoid local backlash against ground-based facilities, but TechCrunch analysis shows terrestrial data centers remain far cheaper after factoring satellite construction and launch costs
- Google had previously invested $900 million in SpaceX in 2015

## Synthesis
A May 12 report from The Wall Street Journal, relayed by TechCrunch, reveals that Google and SpaceX are in discussions about launching data centers into orbit — a concept that would push AI compute infrastructure beyond Earth's atmosphere. The talks come at a pivotal moment for SpaceX, which is gearing up for a massive IPO reportedly valued at $1.75 trillion later this year, and needs investors to buy into expansive visions of future revenue streams beyond rocket launches and Starlink.

The orbital data center narrative is gaining momentum across the industry. Just a week prior, Anthropic struck a deal with SpaceX to use computing resources from xAI's data center in Memphis, Tennessee, with the potential to collaborate on orbital facilities down the line. This follows SpaceX's acquisition of xAI in February 2026, placing Elon Musk's AI ambitions directly under the SpaceX umbrella and creating a vertically integrated pitch: launch infrastructure plus AI compute, all under one corporate roof.

Google interest appears to extend beyond SpaceX alone. The company is reportedly in parallel discussions with other rocket-launch companies and has its own initiative — Project Suncatcher, announced late 2025 — aiming to launch prototype satellites by 2027. This suggests Google is hedging its bets and viewing orbital infrastructure as a strategic priority rather than committing exclusively to a single partner. The company's 2015 investment of $900 million in SpaceX provides historical context for the relationship, though the current talks appear to be at a much larger scale.

Elon Musk has been publicly advocating for orbital data centers as cheaper to operate, citing advantages like unlimited solar power without land-use conflicts and freedom from the local community opposition that increasingly plagues ground-based data center construction in the U.S. However, TechCrunch's own earlier reporting from February 2026 casts doubt on the near-term economics, noting that satellite construction and launch costs make today's terrestrial data centers dramatically cheaper. The economic case for orbital data centers depends on significant reductions in launch costs and satellite manufacturing — areas where SpaceX's Starship program could be transformative, but which remain unproven at the required scale.

The strategic implications are significant. If orbital data centers become viable, they could reshape the geography of AI compute, moving it from contested land in Virginia or Ireland to an entirely new domain with different regulatory, environmental, and geopolitical dynamics. For now, the talks remain exploratory, but they signal that major tech companies are taking the concept seriously enough to begin planning prototype deployments by the end of the decade.

# Pope Approves Creation of Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence
**Source**: https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2026-05/pope-leo-interdicasterial-artificial-intelligence-commission.html
**Date**: May 16, 2026
**Author**: Isabella H. de Carvalho
**Keywords**: Vatican, Pope Leo XIV, artificial intelligence, Catholic Church, AI ethics, human dignity, interdicasterial commission

## Elevator pitch
Pope Leo XIV has approved the creation of a new Vatican Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence, bringing together seven Church bodies to coordinate the Holy See's response to AI's challenges to human dignity, justice, and labor.

## Takeaways
- The rescript was signed May 12, released May 16, following a May 3 audience between the Pope and Cardinal Michael Czerny
- Seven Vatican bodies compose the commission: Dicasteries for Integral Human Development, Doctrine of the Faith, Culture and Education, Communication, plus the Pontifical Academies for Life, Sciences, and Social Sciences
- One institution coordinates for one-year renewable terms, with the Pope deciding rotations — the Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development goes first
- The commission traces back to Pope Leo XIV's first days, when he cited his namesake Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum* response to the industrial revolution as inspiration for the Church's AI response
- This builds on the January 2025 joint note *Antiqua et Nova* on the relationship between artificial and human intelligence

## Synthesis
The Vatican's creation of an Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence marks a significant institutional response to what Pope Leo XIV has framed as a defining challenge of our era. The commission is not an advisory afterthought — it's a structured, rotating coordination body spanning seven distinct Vatican institutions, each bringing different expertise to the AI question.

The legal foundation rests on Article 28 of *Praedicate Evangelium*, the apostolic constitution that reformed the Roman Curia, which allows the head of a Dicastery to create special commissions for matters requiring "mutual and frequent consultation" across multiple bodies. Cardinal Michael Czerny, Prefect of the Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development, established the commission after receiving papal approval.

What makes this significant is the explicit framing. The rescript text cites "the development in recent decades of the phenomenon of Artificial Intelligence and the most recent accelerations in its widespread use" alongside "the Church's concern for the dignity of every human person, especially in relation to their integral development." This is not a technology-watching body — it's a human-dignity-watching body with AI as its object of concern.

The commission's mandate includes coordinating activities and projects related to AI, developing policies on AI use within the Holy See itself, and promoting "dialogue, communion, and participation." The rotating coordination model — with the Pope personally deciding which body leads next — ensures no single dicastery dominates the agenda, while giving each institutional perspective a turn at shaping priorities.

This builds on a clear trajectory in Leo XIV's young pontificate. Days after his election in May 2025, he addressed the cardinals and drew a direct parallel between his namesake Leo XIII, who confronted the industrial revolution through *Rerum Novarum*, and the Church's current responsibility to respond to "another industrial revolution" in AI. The January 2025 joint note *Antiqua et Nova* from the Dicasteries for Doctrine and Culture had already laid theological groundwork, examining the relationship between artificial and human intelligence.

The commission's composition is telling: the inclusion of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences and Social Sciences alongside doctrinal and pastoral bodies suggests the Church intends to engage AI not just as a moral question but as a scientific and sociological one. The Dicastery for Communication's presence signals awareness that AI's impact on information ecosystems is a core concern for an institution whose primary "product" is a message.

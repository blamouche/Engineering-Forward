# Greg Brockman Officially Takes Control of OpenAI's Products in Latest Shake-Up
**Source**: https://www.wired.com/story/openai-reorg-greg-brockman-product/
**Date**: 2026-05-15
**Author**: Maxwell Zeff
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Greg Brockman, reorganization, ChatGPT, Codex, super app, executive changes, Fidji Simo, IPO, product strategy

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is reorganizing its executive ranks, making co-founder Greg Brockman the permanent head of product strategy and merging ChatGPT and Codex into one unified experience, as the company streamlines ahead of its planned IPO amid mounting competition from Anthropic and Google.

## Takeaways
- Greg Brockman, previously interim product head during Fidji Simo's medical leave, now officially leads product strategy while also overseeing AI infrastructure, with a stated goal of executing "with maximum focus toward the agentic future"
- ChatGPT and Codex are being merged into a single product experience under one core team, reflecting their growing convergence as Codex increasingly powers both consumer and enterprise offerings
- Codex head Thibault Sottiaux has been promoted to lead core product and platform teams; ChatGPT's longtime leader Nick Turley moves to enterprise products only, and former Instagram VP Ashley Alexander now leads consumer products
- The reorganization follows last month's departure of several senior executives including Kevin Weil (AI workspace for scientists), Bill Peebles (Sora), and Srinivas Narayanan (CTO of enterprise applications)
- These changes come as OpenAI faces mounting competitive pressure — Anthropic in coding, Google in consumer chatbots — and prepares to file for an IPO later this year, with ChatGPT now at over 900 million weekly active users

## Synthesis
OpenAI's latest reorganization represents more than routine executive shuffling — it signals a fundamental strategic pivot as the company races toward an IPO. The headline change is Greg Brockman's formal assumption of product leadership. Brockman, one of OpenAI's co-founders and its president, had been overseeing products on an interim basis since Fidji Simo — the CEO of AGI deployment hired from Instacart — went on medical leave last month. The company confirms Simo worked directly with Brockman on these organizational changes and expects her to return, but the interim tag has been removed: Brockman is now definitively in charge of product.

The organizational logic Brockman articulated to staff is convergence. "We're consolidating our product efforts to execute with maximum focus toward the agentic future, to win across both consumer and enterprise," he wrote in an internal memo. The centerpiece is merging ChatGPT and Codex — OpenAI's coding agent and one of its fastest-growing products ever — into a single unified experience under one core product team. This is a recognition that the product lines are converging: Codex powers an increasing share of OpenAI's consumer and enterprise features, and autonomous digital task completion is becoming the through-line across all offerings.

The personnel moves tell a story of consolidation and specialization. Thibault Sottiaux, who built Codex into a breakout product, now leads the combined core product and platform teams. He's also overseeing development of OpenAI's "super app" — a unified desktop application combining Codex, ChatGPT, and the Atlas web browser. Nick Turley, who has led ChatGPT since launch and grew it to over 900 million weekly active users, moves exclusively to enterprise products. Consumer products now fall to Ashley Alexander, a former Instagram VP who had been running OpenAI's health products division.

The backdrop is competitive pressure. Anthropic has been gaining ground in coding — the domain where Codex was supposed to give OpenAI an unassailable lead. Google continues to challenge in consumer chatbots. Meanwhile, the April executive exodus was significant: Kevin Weil (head of AI workspace for scientists), Bill Peebles (head of Sora), and Srinivas Narayanan (CTO of enterprise applications) all left the company. These departures removed senior leaders from key product areas just as OpenAI needed to demonstrate focus and momentum ahead of its IPO filing.

The IPO itself is the unspoken driver behind much of this restructuring. Companies preparing to go public need a clean narrative: clear product lines, stable leadership, demonstrated growth trajectory. By consolidating ChatGPT and Codex under Brockman, OpenAI can tell investors a simpler story about its core business. The 900-million-user ChatGPT figure provides the scale; Codex provides the growth engine and the "agentic future" narrative; the super app provides the vision.

But the reorganization also carries risks. Brockman's portfolio is enormous — product strategy plus AI infrastructure — and the company is betting heavily on a single unified product experience at a time when specialization might be warranted. Merging ChatGPT and Codex could blur the distinct value propositions that made each product category-leading. And the loss of experienced product leaders like Turley from the consumer side, combined with a first-time consumer product leader in Alexander, creates execution uncertainty at a critical moment.

The broader question is whether this reorganization addresses OpenAI's fundamental challenge: it is a research lab trying to operate as a product company while racing competitors who are more natively organized around products. Brockman's memo emphasizes "maximum focus toward the agentic future," but focus is easier to declare than to achieve in a company that has historically pursued multiple research directions simultaneously. The IPO will force discipline. Whether OpenAI's culture — built on research ambition and AGI maximalism — can adapt to the operational rigor of a public company remains the defining question.

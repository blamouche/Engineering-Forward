# The Pulse: Forward deployed engineering heats up again
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering
**Date**: 2026-05-14
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: forward deployed engineering, FDE, tech layoffs, AI usage reporting, vibe coding, agentic engineering

## Elevator pitch
Forward deployed engineering is experiencing a major resurgence at leading AI companies, while rising layoffs, AI usage self-reporting pressures, and the blurring line between vibe coding and agentic engineering reshape the industry.

## Takeaways
- Forward deployed engineering (FDE) roles are in massive demand at Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, resembling a hybrid of consulting and solution architecture
- Tech layoffs are spiking to levels not seen since early 2023, driven by smaller team structures reducing middle management needs and poorly performing companies cutting costs
- Some Microsoft managers are advising reports to self-report 100% AI-generated code during mid-year performance reviews, raising questions about authenticity
- "Vibe coding" and agentic engineering are converging in uncomfortable ways, with developers reviewing AI agent code less thoroughly than ideal
- Additional trends include "tokenmaxxing" at Amazon, faster SaaS growth potentially driven by AI, and Anthropic overtaking OpenAI in enterprise spend

## Synthesis
This May 14, 2026 edition of The Pulse covers a convergence of structural changes reshaping the software engineering profession. The headline trend is the resurgence of forward deployed engineering (FDE), a role that Gergely Orosz describes as having evolved into something resembling a consultant or solution architect position, typically filled by early-to-junior engineers. The massive demand for FDEs at Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic signals that AI companies are prioritizing customer-facing technical talent who can bridge the gap between advanced AI capabilities and real-world enterprise adoption. This reflects the industry's shift from building AI tools to deploying and integrating them at scale within client organizations.

The layoff landscape presents a more sobering picture. Tech job cuts have risen to levels not seen since early 2023, driven by two distinct forces. First, the shift toward smaller, AI-augmented teams naturally reduces the need for middle management layers, triggering structural reorganizations. Second, companies with already poor performance are using AI transformation as cover for routine cost-cutting—layoffs that would have happened regardless but are now attributed to AI efficiency gains.

A particularly striking cultural observation involves Microsoft's performance review cycle. Orosz reports that some managers are explicitly advising their direct reports to claim 100% AI-generated code in their self-reviews, regardless of actual usage. This "self-reporting" trend suggests AI adoption has become a performance metric in itself, raising uncomfortable questions about authenticity, the devaluation of human-authored code, and what it means for engineering culture when AI usage becomes a checkbox rather than a genuine productivity tool.

The piece also highlights Simon Willison's observation about the narrowing gap between "vibe coding"—the practice of prompting AI to generate code with minimal review—and agentic engineering, where AI agents operate with greater autonomy. Willison notes that reviewing AI agent-produced code is happening less thoroughly than would be ideal, pointing to an emerging quality risk as engineering workflows become increasingly AI-mediated. The issue also covers token-maximizing behavior at Amazon, faster SaaS company growth potentially attributable to AI, Bun's successful Rust rewrite using AI assistance, and the notable milestone of Anthropic overtaking OpenAI in enterprise spending.

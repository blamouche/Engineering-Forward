# Salesforce moved 3,000 employees into sales roles as agentic AI reshapes operations: Vala Afshar
**Source**: https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/information-technology/salesforce-moved-3-000-employees-into-sales-roles-as-agentic-ai-reshapes-operations-vala-afshar-13922844.html
**Date**: May 18, 2026
**Author**: Moneycontrol (PTI / CNBC-TV18 interview with Vala Afshar)
**Keywords**: Salesforce, agentic AI, workforce transformation, reskilling, digital labour, AI augmentation, Vala Afshar, Agentforce, hybrid workforce

## Elevator pitch
Salesforce has internally redeployed 3,000 employees from support and other functions into sales roles after its Agentforce AI platform automated their previous work — a real-world case study in how agentic AI is not displacing humans but fundamentally restructuring the workforce toward higher-value functions.

## Takeaways
- Salesforce moved 3,000 employees into sales roles through internal reskilling as its Agentforce AI platform handled over 2.6 million customer conversations and resolved 3.5 million support cases in 16 months
- The company's AI agents achieved a 63% resolution rate with customer satisfaction scores matching human agents, freeing up support engineers for higher-value roles
- Vala Afshar, Salesforce's Chief Digital Evangelist, argues that agentic AI's impact could be "10 times bigger than the Industrial Revolution in one-tenth of the time"
- Salesforce employs a formal "4Rs" framework — Redesign, Reskill, Redeploy, Rebalance — to manage the transition to an agentic workforce, and has 25,000+ customers using Agentforce globally
- Afshar warns of "digital Darwinism": companies that fail to adopt AI-driven systems face irrelevance, calling it a binary choice between being "an agentic business or a dying one"

## Synthesis

Salesforce has become its own most dramatic case study for the transformative impact of agentic AI on workforce structure. In a series of interviews and announcements in May 2026, Chief Digital Evangelist Vala Afshar revealed that the company moved 3,000 employees from various internal lines of business into its sales function — growing its sales organization without a single external hire — by redeploying workers whose previous roles had been automated by the company's own AI platform, Agentforce.

The numbers tell a compelling story. Since deploying Agentforce to handle customer support on help.salesforce.com in early 2025, the platform has resolved more than 3.5 million customer cases over 16 months, handling 2.6 million customer conversations at a 63% resolution rate. Crucially, customer satisfaction scores from AI-handled interactions matched those of human agents. This wasn't a marginal efficiency gain — it was a wholesale reconfiguration of how customer support work gets done.

The displaced workers weren't laid off. Instead, they were retrained and redeployed into roles such as forward deployment engineers, solutions engineers, architects, sales professionals, and professional services experts. Some transitioned immediately because they already possessed transferable skills. The majority required significant reskilling, which Salesforce provided during work hours, not as an after-hours expectation. Former support engineers — praised by hiring managers as some of the best hires ever made — brought deep product knowledge, troubleshooting ability, and customer-facing communication skills that proved highly valuable in sales and advisory contexts.

Afshar frames this transformation through a provocative lens he calls "digital Darwinism." In his view, the fittest companies aren't the strongest but the most adaptable to AI. He describes a binary future: "You're either an agentic business or a dying one. You are on a path to irrelevance if you don't take advantage of this technology." This rhetoric is backed by what he describes as the fastest-growing product in Salesforce's history — Agentforce — and a conviction that AI's impact could eclipse the Industrial Revolution by an order of magnitude while unfolding at ten times the speed.

The broader context is a company that has continued to grow its human workforce. Afshar noted that from 2024 to 2026, Salesforce added more than 20,000 employees, reaching approximately 85,000 globally. The company simultaneously announced plans to hire 1,000 new graduates. The narrative deliberately counters the "AI will eliminate jobs" anxiety by emphasizing augmentation over replacement — AI handles repetitive, deterministic work while humans focus on creative, strategic, and complex tasks.

Underpinning this is a formal methodology: the "4Rs" framework of Redesign, Reskill, Redeploy, and Rebalance. Salesforce's Chief People Officer Nathalie Scardino confirmed that traditional job descriptions have been eliminated in favor of outcome-based roles — whether focused on renewals, pipeline, or customer experience. Every employee, from entry-level to C-suite, has been required to achieve "Agent Blazer" certification in building and deploying AI agents. CEO Marc Benioff mandated that senior leaders earn Level 2 "Agent Blazer Innovator" certification as a prerequisite for attending the company's annual leadership program.

Afshar was careful to position this as part of a broader technological arc. He noted that hybrid work has been redefined: it no longer means choosing between home and office, but between humans and AI co-creating value. He emphasized that the technology involves far more than language models — it requires an integrated system of subsystems encompassing reasoning, execution, adaptation, and learning.

The implications extend well beyond Salesforce. Afshar's commentary on India is particularly noteworthy: he rejected the notion that India lags in AI adoption, pointing to a "spirit of curiosity" and predicting that the Salesforce economy alone will generate $89 billion in new revenue for India by 2028. Consumer-facing companies are leading AI adoption in the country, and AI fluency is already becoming a prerequisite for new hires — candidates now ask about access to AI tools before accepting job offers, according to Arundhati Bhattacharya, Salesforce's South Asia CEO.

The Salesforce story is significant because it provides a tangible, numbers-backed template for what "AI workforce transformation" actually looks like in practice — not layoffs disguised as efficiency, but genuine internal mobility supported by structured reskilling programs, executive mandates, and a cultural shift toward AI-native operations. Whether this model proves replicable at scale across industries remains an open question, but for now, Salesforce has given the business world its most compelling data point on what happens when AI does the work and humans get promoted.

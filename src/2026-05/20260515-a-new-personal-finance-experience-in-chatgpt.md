# A new personal finance experience in ChatGPT
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/personal-finance-chatgpt/
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: OpenAI
**Keywords**: ChatGPT, personal finance, Finances, Plaid, GPT-5.5, financial data, Intuit, account aggregation, AI financial advisor, consumer AI

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI launches a personal finance experience in ChatGPT for U.S. Pro users, allowing secure connection of financial accounts via Plaid (12,000+ institutions) with a dashboard, spending insights, and GPT-5.5-powered financial reasoning grounded in real user data, while partnering with Intuit for future action-oriented features like credit card applications and tax guidance.

## Takeaways
- The Finances feature connects to 12,000+ financial institutions through Plaid, with Intuit integration coming soon, and is initially available to ChatGPT Pro users in the U.S. on web and iOS.
- The system defaults to GPT-5.5 Thinking, achieving a 79/100 expert-graded benchmark score on complex personal finance tasks, with GPT-5.5 Pro scoring 82.5/100.
- Users can share additional financial context (mortgages, savings goals, loans) stored as "Financial memories"—a dedicated memory type separate from general ChatGPT memories.
- Privacy controls include: no access to full account numbers, no ability to make account changes, respect for training data opt-out settings, disconnected data deletion within 30 days, and temporary chat support.
- The Intuit partnership envisions moving from answers to action: credit card recommendations flowing into approval checks and applications, or stock sale tax implications flowing into trusted tax estimates and live expert scheduling—all inside ChatGPT.

## Synthesis
OpenAI's new personal finance experience represents a significant expansion of ChatGPT from a general-purpose assistant into a domain-specific tool with real financial data access. While more than 200 million people already use ChatGPT monthly for budgeting, investment questions, and financial planning, those interactions have been limited by the absence of personal financial context. The Finances feature closes that gap by pulling in live account data—balances, transactions, investments, and liabilities—and combining it with GPT-5.5's reasoning capabilities.

The technical and partnership architecture is carefully constructed. Plaid provides the account connectivity layer with coverage of over 12,000 financial institutions, while Intuit is positioned as the future action layer—the partner that can turn financial insights into concrete outcomes like credit applications or tax professional consultations. This two-tier design separates data aggregation (Plaid) from financial services delivery (Intuit), allowing OpenAI to offer utility without becoming a regulated financial institution. The benchmark results are notable: GPT-5.5 Thinking scored 79/100 on a composite of expert-graded response quality and accuracy across challenging personal finance tasks, evaluated with input from over 50 finance professionals. GPT-5.5 Pro scored 82.5/100, suggesting ceiling effects that leave room for improvement.

The privacy architecture addresses obvious concerns. ChatGPT cannot see full account numbers and cannot initiate transactions—it is read-only. Users control whether financial conversations contribute to model training through existing data control settings. Disconnected account data is deleted from OpenAI's systems within 30 days. Financial memories—a new, dedicated memory type—store user-shared context like savings goals or mortgages separately from general ChatGPT memories, and users can view or delete them at any time. The inclusion of temporary chat support means users can explore sensitive questions without creating a permanent record.

OpenAI demonstrates the product's value through a compelling before-and-after comparison: generic savings advice (cut subscriptions, reduce takeout, automate transfers) versus personalized analysis grounded in the user's actual spending patterns across groceries, shopping, transportation, and dining. The personalized version produces specific monthly caps and projected savings ($705/month in the example) rather than generic guidance. The vision extends beyond budgeting into what OpenAI calls "from answers to action"—a future where financial recommendations flow directly into actionable steps, brokered through partners like Intuit. The strategic bet is that financial reasoning, when grounded in real personal data and paired with an action ecosystem, becomes sticky enough to drive ChatGPT Pro subscriptions and create a new consumer fintech surface that competes with dedicated personal finance apps.

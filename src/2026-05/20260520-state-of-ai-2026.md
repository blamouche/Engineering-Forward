# State of AI 2026
**Source**: https://2026.stateofai.dev/
**Date**: 2026-05-20 (survey ran April 8 – May 8, 2026)
**Author**: Sacha Greif / Devographics
**Keywords**: AI adoption, coding agents, Claude Code, AI survey, developer tools, AI monetization, AI risks, job displacement, AI bubble

## Elevator pitch
The State of AI 2026 survey of 7,258 developers reveals that AI-assisted coding has crossed the chasm into mainstream practice, with AI-generated code jumping from 28% to 56% of output year-over-year, while Claude Code dominates coding agent sentiment and developers increasingly worry about an AI bubble and job displacement.

## Takeaways
- AI-generated code share doubled from 28% in 2025 to 56% in 2026, with the "75%+ AI-generated" segment showing the highest growth
- Claude Code leads coding agent sentiment at 42.3% positive, ahead of OpenAI Codex (26.3%) and GitHub Copilot (22.6%), and Claude is the model developers actually pay for most
- Personal AI spending is clearly rising, signaling that the era of heavily subsidized AI access is giving way to monetization
- 70% of respondents agree or strongly agree that we're in an "AI bubble" — a striking consensus among the very people building with the technology
- Hallucinations/inaccuracies remain the #1 pain point (3,899 respondents), followed by code quality concerns and lack of context

## Synthesis

The 2026 edition of the State of AI survey, conducted by Devographics from April 8 to May 8, 2026, collected responses from 7,258 developers and paints a picture of an industry at an inflection point. The survey, authored and presented by Sacha Greif, is openly self-aware about its selection bias — an AI-focused survey naturally attracts AI-using respondents — but the year-over-year trends within that cohort are nonetheless striking.

The headline statistic is the jump in AI-generated code. Respondents reported an average of 56% of their code being AI-generated in 2026, up from 28% in 2025. What's particularly notable is not just the doubling of the average but the distribution: the segments producing the highest proportions of AI-generated code (75% and above) grew the fastest. This suggests that once developers cross a certain threshold of trust and integration with AI tools, their reliance accelerates rapidly. The usage frequency data reinforces this: the percentage of developers using AI "constantly" doubled year-over-year, and the most common response shifted toward daily and hourly usage.

The coding agent landscape has also matured rapidly. Claude Code emerged as the clear leader in positive sentiment (42.3% positive, 47.9% neutral, only 9.3% negative), significantly outpacing OpenAI Codex and GitHub Copilot. This sentiment advantage translates directly into wallet share: Claude is the model that developers (or their companies) actually pay for most, ahead of ChatGPT despite ChatGPT's broader brand recognition. This inversion — higher payment rates despite lower overall awareness — suggests Claude is winning on developer experience where it matters commercially.

The survey dedicates significant attention to what Greif calls the "race to monetization." Personal AI expenses are clearly rising, with fewer respondents reporting zero spend compared to the previous year. This tracks with the broader industry dynamic: after years of venture-subsidized access designed to drive adoption, AI labs are now under pressure to demonstrate sustainable revenue. The survey data suggests they're succeeding — at least among the developer demographic that uses these tools professionally.

Perhaps most revealing is the sentiment around an AI bubble. Over 70% of respondents agree or strongly agree that we're in an AI bubble, with only 11% disagreeing. This near-consensus among technical practitioners — the people who would presumably be best positioned to assess the technology's real capabilities — is a sobering data point. It reflects a tension between the genuine productivity gains developers experience daily and a skepticism about whether current valuations and investment levels are justified by sustainable business models.

The risks section is equally nuanced. Job displacement tops the list of concerns (3,003 respondents), followed by military use of AI (2,804) and environmental impact (2,490). However, when it comes to day-to-day pain points, the practical frustrations dominate: hallucination and inaccuracies are by far the most cited issue, followed by code quality and lack of context. These aren't existential fears — they're the kind of concrete quality-of-life problems that determine whether developers stick with a tool or abandon it.

The survey captures a moment where the developer community is simultaneously enthusiastic about AI's productivity benefits, anxious about its broader societal implications, and skeptical of its economic fundamentals. It's a complex, sometimes contradictory picture — and likely an accurate reflection of where the industry stands in 2026.

# State of AI 2026
**Source**: https://2026.stateofai.dev/en-US
**Date**: May 2026
**Author**: Sacha Greif (Devographics)
**Keywords**: AI survey, developer tools, Claude Code, AI adoption, coding agents, AI bubble, AI risks, developer trends

## Elevator pitch
The 2026 State of AI survey of 7,258 developers reveals AI-assisted coding has transitioned from experiment to standard practice, with AI-generated code jumping from 28% to 54% of output year-over-year, Claude overtaking ChatGPT for paid subscriptions, and 70% of respondents agreeing we're in an AI bubble.

## Takeaways
- AI-generated code proportion doubled from 28% to 54% year-over-year, with developers using AI "constantly" doubling — the transition from early adopter to mainstream is well underway
- Claude Code dominates coding agent sentiment (42% positive vs 26% for OpenAI Codex and 23% for GitHub Copilot), and Claude is the model developers actually pay for most (4,592 paying vs 3,261 for ChatGPT)
- Individual AI spend is rising: fewer respondents paying nothing, more in the $20-$100+/month range — the subsidized era is ending
- 70% agree or strongly agree we're in an "AI bubble," yet adoption continues to accelerate
- Top concerns: job displacement (3,003), military use of AI (2,804), environmental impact (2,490), and AI slop (2,107)

## Synthesis
The 2026 State of AI survey — conducted by Sacha Greif's Devographics across 7,258 respondents from April 8 to May 8, 2026 — provides the most comprehensive snapshot yet of AI adoption among developers. The survey carries the usual open-survey selection bias caveat (AI-interested developers are more likely to respond), but the year-over-year trend lines are striking regardless.

The headline finding: AI-generated code has jumped from 28% of developer output in 2025 to 54% in 2026. The fastest-growing segment is the 75%+ AI-generated code bracket. "Constantly" using AI doubled year-over-year, confirming that AI-assisted coding is no longer an experiment — it's becoming the default.

The coding agent landscape shows a clear winner in developer sentiment: Claude Code (42% positive, 48% neutral, only 9% negative) significantly outpaces OpenAI Codex (26% positive) and GitHub Copilot (23% positive, with notably higher negativity at 22%). More tellingly, Claude is the model developers actually pay for (4,592 respondents), surpassing ChatGPT (3,261) — a remarkable shift given ChatGPT's first-mover advantage and broader consumer recognition.

The monetization race is accelerating. Respondents spending nothing on AI dropped significantly, while mid-range spending ($20-100/month) grew. This reflects the end of heavily subsidized AI access, as labs pivot from user acquisition to revenue generation. Whether this justifies current AI company valuations is uncertain, and 70% of respondents think we're in a bubble.

Risk perception has shifted meaningfully. Job displacement tops concerns (3,003), followed by military AI applications (2,804) and environmental impact (2,490). "AI slop takeover" is a newly prominent concern at 2,107 mentions. The top pain points remain remarkably consistent with 2025: hallucinations/inaccuracies (3,899), code quality (3,249), and lack of context (2,321), though financial costs jumped in ranking, reflecting the monetization shift.

The survey captures a field at an inflection point: adoption is mainstreaming, monetization is accelerating, and anxiety is growing — all simultaneously. Whether this represents sustainable growth or bubble dynamics remains the defining question of 2026 AI.

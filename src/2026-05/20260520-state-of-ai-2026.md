# State of AI 2026
**Source**: https://2026.stateofai.dev/
**Date**: May 2026
**Author**: Sacha Greif (Devographics)
**Keywords**: AI survey, developer tools, coding agents, Claude Code, AI adoption, monetization, AI bubble, job displacement, AI risks

## Elevator pitch
The 2026 State of AI survey of 7,258 developers reveals that AI-assisted coding has crossed into mainstream practice — with AI-generated code jumping from 28% to 56% year-over-year — driven by a new wave of coding agents led by Claude Code, rising personal AI spending, and growing anxiety about job security and an AI bubble.

## Takeaways
- AI-generated code has doubled year-over-year: from 28% in 2025 to 56% in 2026, with the "75%+ AI code" segment growing fastest.
- Claude Code dominates positive sentiment (42.3% positive vs 9.3% negative) among coding agents, and Claude is the model developers pay for most, overtaking ChatGPT (4,592 vs 3,261 respondents).
- Personal AI spending is rising sharply, marking the start of a monetization race after years of VC-subsidized pricing.
- 70% of respondents agree or strongly agree that we are currently in an "AI bubble," though they continue adopting AI tools regardless.
- The top three AI risks cited by developers are job displacement (3,003), military use of AI (2,804), and environmental impact (2,490); the top pain point remains hallucination and inaccuracies.

## Synthesis

The 2026 edition of the State of AI survey, conducted by Devographics between April 8 and May 8, 2026, with 7,258 developer respondents, paints a picture of an industry in rapid and somewhat paradoxical transformation. The survey's author, Sacha Greif, frames the results around four key trends — growing adoption, the rise of coding agents, a race to monetization, and increased risk factors — each of which reveals deeper tensions within the AI developer ecosystem.

The headline statistic is the near-doubling of AI-generated code: from an average of 28% in 2025 to 56% in 2026. More telling, the segments producing 75% or more of their code via AI saw the steepest growth, suggesting a bifurcation where a significant cohort of developers has fully integrated AI into their workflow rather than treating it as a supplementary tool. This is reinforced by a doubling of respondents who report using AI "constantly." While Greif acknowledges the selection bias inherent in an AI-focused survey — it attracts respondents already invested in the topic — the direction and magnitude of the trend are hard to dismiss.

The rise of coding agents marks a structural shift in how developers interact with AI. Claude Code leads with 42.3% positive sentiment and only 9.3% negative, substantially ahead of OpenAI Codex (26.3%) and GitHub Copilot (22.6%). These agents represent a new paradigm: rather than copying code snippets from a chatbot into an IDE, developers delegate entire tasks to agents that operate directly within the development environment. This convenience factor may explain why Claude has become the model developers actually pay for most (4,592 respondents), surpassing ChatGPT (3,261) despite the latter's broader name recognition and consumer reach.

The monetization angle is perhaps the most consequential business insight. After years of heavily subsidized access fueled by unprecedented VC investment, AI labs appear to be shifting toward revenue generation. Personal monthly AI spending has risen across the board compared to 2025, with more respondents moving into the $20–$50 and $100–$500 brackets. Greif raises the question of whether this increased monetization can justify current AI company valuations, and the respondents themselves appear skeptical: 70% agree or strongly agree that we are in an "AI bubble."

This bubble anxiety coexists with genuine concern about AI's externalities. Job displacement tops the list of perceived risks (3,003 mentions), followed closely by military applications (2,804) and environmental impact (2,490). Notably, "AI slop takeover" ranks fourth — a term that captures the growing fear of a web flooded with low-quality, AI-generated content that degrades the information ecosystem. On the practical front, developers still wrestle with hallucinations and inaccuracies as the number one pain point (3,899 mentions), followed by code quality concerns (3,249) and lack of context awareness (2,321).

The survey ultimately captures a community that is deeply ambivalent: developers are adopting AI at an accelerating pace, paying more for it, and restructuring their workflows around coding agents — all while believing they are participating in a bubble and worrying about their own job security. Greif's closing sentiment — "I'm not sure where all this leaves us" — reflects this cognitive dissonance. The data suggests that whether the AI bubble pops or not, AI-assisted development has already achieved escape velocity from the early adopter phase and will be a defining feature of software engineering for the foreseeable future.

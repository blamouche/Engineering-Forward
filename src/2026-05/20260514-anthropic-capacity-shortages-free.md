# The Pulse: Did capacity shortages turn Anthropic hostile to devs? (Free Edition)
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-did-capacity-shortages-0e6
**Date**: 2026-05-14
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: Anthropic, capacity shortages, Claude Code, SpaceX, xAI, Grok, AI competition, compute infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's pattern of degrading developer experience was likely driven by hidden compute capacity shortages, now addressed through a major data center deal with SpaceX/xAI's Colossus 1 facility.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic's "dumber" model, Claude Code nerfing, and account bans correlate with undisclosed capacity constraints, not malice
- Anthropic experienced 80x revenue and usage growth versus a planned 10x, overwhelming infrastructure
- SpaceX/xAI is renting its entire Colossus 1 data center (220,000+ NVIDIA GPUs, ~45% of xAI capacity) to Anthropic
- Following the deal, Anthropic immediately doubled Claude Code limits, removed peak-hour restrictions, and raised API rate limits
- Musk's deal with Anthropic may be strategic: weakening OpenAI (his litigation target) by strengthening a rival

## Synthesis
This free edition of The Pulse expands on the previous week's investigation into whether Anthropic's recent developer-hostile behavior was a deliberate strategy or a consequence of infrastructure constraints. Gergely Orosz builds a compelling case for the latter hypothesis, presenting a timeline where model degradation, Claude Code restrictions, and account bans all coincided with a period of explosive growth that overwhelmed Anthropic's compute capacity.

The central evidence is CEO Dario Amodei's candid admission at the "Code with Claude" event on May 6, 2026: Anthropic had planned for 10x growth but experienced approximately 80x growth in both revenue and usage. This massive underestimation of demand would naturally strain any infrastructure, and the company's subsequent actions—silently nerfing Claude Code, removing it from paid accounts, and banning corporate accounts—appear more like desperate capacity management than strategic hostility.

The resolution comes through an ironic partnership: SpaceX/xAI is renting its Colossus 1 data center to Anthropic. With over 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs including H100, H200, and next-generation GB200 accelerators, this facility represents roughly 45% of SpaceX/xAI's current compute capacity and 20-25% of planned total capacity. The deal is striking given the history: Musk had called Anthropic "misanthropic and evil," and Anthropic had previously banned xAI developers from using Claude. The partnership makes strategic sense, however, when viewed through the lens of Musk's ongoing litigation against OpenAI, where he seeks $150 billion in damages and the removal of Sam Altman and Greg Brockman. By strengthening Anthropic, Musk indirectly weakens OpenAI—the classic "enemy of my enemy" dynamic.

The deal also raises questions about Grok's market position. Leasing such substantial capacity suggests xAI's model has failed to gain meaningful B2B or B2C traction beyond its integration with the X social media platform. Orosz notes that Grok barely registered in February's AI tooling survey, lagging behind even open models like DeepSeek and Qwen. This capacity lease might signal xAI's gradual retreat from the frontier model race. In immediate practical terms, the deal has already improved developer experience: Anthropic doubled Claude Code limits, removed peak-hour restrictions, and substantially raised API rate limits for Opus models, suggesting the capacity constraints that frustrated developers are being resolved.

## Synthesis
Jason Lemkin and Amelia's fifth episode of The Agents reveals the operational reality of running AI agents at scale in an 8-figure B2B company. The headline number is arresting: two AI VPs cost $257 for the entire month. The surprise comes from the cost breakdown — the expensive part of the AI stack isn't the AI at all. 95% of model calls use GPT-4o-mini at sub-penny rates, while the real costs are in APIs (Salesforce, Marketo, WordPress) and infrastructure (Replit hosting, Clerk, ElevenLabs). Even fully burdened at $500-800/month, these agents are 1/30th of what the team assumed they'd cost.

A crucial technical insight emerges around deployment architecture. 10K and QBee are kept permanently inside Replit's development environment rather than deployed to production. This means the team interacts with the agent that built them — which retains infinite context, full change history, direct database access, and live code-editing capability. Most teams lose this loop when they deploy; SaaStr considers keeping it the "nerdy unlock nobody talks about."

The article documents three concrete examples of AI agent impact: First, a $4K/year newsletter builder was killed in 60 minutes when 10K rebuilt the entire workflow using WordPress API, Sonnet for article ranking, and autonomous preview generation. Second, an event website spontaneously became a 21st agent after accumulating parking pass management, attendee communications, sponsor logo management, and micro-audience segmentation — developing its own personality, goals, and memory. Third, QBee sent 83 personalized sponsor emails at 12:23am, each showing exactly which of 13+ tasks were incomplete per sponsor, and generated fewer complaints than human-managed outreach.

The most striking validation comes from the physical world: 100+ contractors building a 40-acre event chose to route questions through QBee rather than tracking down human staff on Segways — because the agent was faster, more accurate, and caught errors (20 missing chairs) that humans missed. For any use case where the agent is accurate, Lemkin argues the human-vs-agent debate is over: speed plus correctness wins.

The strategic takeaway for SaaS is that the "SaaSpocalypse" isn't Workday or Salesforce going to zero — it's point solutions quietly disappearing as agents absorb their functions without anyone noticing. Stealth churn, not dramatic collapse.

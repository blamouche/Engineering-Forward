# Does Google Have a Claude Code Counterpunch?

**Source:** Big Technology (Substack)
**Author:** Alex Kantrowitz and Marty Swant
**Date:** May 18, 2026
**URL:** https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/does-google-have-a-claude-code-counterpunch

## Summary

Ahead of Google I/O 2026, the question is whether Google has an answer to the "super agent" category dominated by Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex. While Google Cloud is thriving (63% revenue growth) and the stock is up 136%, the company has yet to field a competitive coding agent. The argument for Google is that Gemini's native integration across Gmail, Calendar, Chrome, and Drive could deliver a similar experience without needing to control the user's computer.

## Key Points

- **Super agents are the frontier:** Coding agents that control your computer and act on your behalf have become company-defining products for Anthropic and OpenAI
- **Google's absence:** Despite Gemini's progress, no comparable coding agent yet
- **Google I/O 2026 (May 19):** The company's most crucial moment — but unlikely to make a super agent the centerpiece
- **Google's potential advantage:** Billions of users across Gmail, Calendar, Chrome, Drive — Gemini could natively generate in Google Slides rather than controlling your computer to build a deck
- **Google Cloud thriving:** 63% revenue growth, stock up 136% YOY
- **Intelligence Report highlights:**
  - 71% of Americans oppose AI data centers near them (Gallup)
  - Mythos successfully breached macOS in April tests
  - UK CMA investigating Microsoft for antitrust in business software
  - Musk v. OpenAI remedies phase begins
  - Cerebras IPO raised $5.55B
- **Layoffs context:** Meta cutting ~8,000 (10%), LinkedIn 5%, Cisco 4,000
- **Public sentiment:** Eric Schmidt booed at commencement for mentioning AI; 56% of students pessimistic about job market; 53% of workers say AI is taking entry-level jobs

## Why It Matters

Google I/O 2026 is a test of whether the company can compete in the product category that's defining the next phase of AI — not model capabilities, but agentic products that actually do work. Google's native integration across its ecosystem is a genuine differentiator, but executing on it requires product vision the company has struggled to demonstrate in the AI era.

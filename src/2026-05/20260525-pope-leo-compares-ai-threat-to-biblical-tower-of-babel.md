# Pope Leo Compares AI Threat to Biblical 'Tower of Babel'
**Source**: https://www.wsj.com/world/pope-leo-ai-encyclical-c5e1af6c
**Date**: May 25, 2026
**Author**: Margherita Stancati, Sam Schechner (WSJ); Pope Leo XIV
**Keywords**: Pope Leo XIV, Magnifica Humanitas, encyclical, AI ethics, Catholic Church, technology concentration, human dignity, social justice

## Elevator pitch
Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical, "Magnifica Humanitas," warns that AI threatens to "normalize an anti-human vision," calls for technology to be kept out of the hands of a few private actors, and frames the choice as a modern Tower of Babel versus a city where God and humanity dwell together.

## Takeaways
- The encyclical, signed on the 135th anniversary of Rerum Novarum, is Leo XIV's defining document — a social encyclical tackling AI as the principal challenge of the contemporary age
- Core thesis: technology is "never neutral" because it reflects the values of those who devise, finance, regulate, and use it
- Warns that concentration of digital power in a few hands widens the gap between those included and excluded from the digital revolution
- Insists on public oversight of technology so "the guiding principle is not solely profit but the dignity of every person"
- Calls for an ethical code for AI and an "examination of conscience" within the Church itself regarding abuse of power

## Synthesis
Pope Leo XIV has issued "Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence," his first encyclical and the document most likely to define his papacy. Released on May 25, 2026, and signed on the 135th anniversary of Pope Leo XIII's landmark Rerum Novarum, the encyclical applies Catholic social doctrine to what the Pope frames as the defining moral challenge of the era: artificial intelligence.

The encyclical opens with a stark framing: "Humanity, created by God in all its grandeur, is today facing a pivotal choice: either to construct a new Tower of Babel or to build the city in which God and humanity dwell together." The Tower of Babel comparison is deliberate — a biblical story about human hubris in building something meant to reach the heavens, resulting in confusion and division. The Pope's warning to Silicon Valley is unmistakable.

"Magnifica Humanitas" is structured in five chapters, building from foundational principles to concrete applications. The first chapter traces the Social Doctrine of the Church from Pius XII through Pope Francis, establishing continuity. The second chapter lays out the foundations: human dignity as inherent and non-negotiable, the inviolability of human rights beginning with the right to life, and recognition of minority rights including women's rights.

The Pope identifies five principles: the common good, the universal destination of goods, subsidiarity, solidarity, and social justice. On the universal destination of goods, he is explicitly pointed about technology: it must not be concentrated in the hands of a few, widening the gap between digital haves and have-nots. On social justice in the digital age, he insists on public oversight so that "the guiding principle is not solely profit but the dignity of every person and the common good of all people."

The third chapter directly addresses AI, warning against the "technocratic paradigm" that reduces every choice to efficiency and profit metrics. The Pope asserts that technology is "never neutral" — it takes on the characteristics of those who devise, finance, regulate, and use it. This is a direct challenge to the Silicon Valley claim that technology is just a tool.

The encyclical also includes a remarkable self-critical dimension. Pope Leo calls for an "examination of conscience" within the Church itself, acknowledging the need to "purify ecclesial relationships and structures from distortions that give rise to inequality, lack of transparency and abuse of power" — including spiritual, economic, institutional, sexual, and conscience-based abuse. The WSJ frames the document as the Pope "adding his moral suasion to a growing backlash against the impact of artificial intelligence," placing the Vatican alongside other institutional voices questioning unfettered AI development.

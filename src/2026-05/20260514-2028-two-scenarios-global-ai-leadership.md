# 2028: Two scenarios for global AI leadership
**Source**: https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Anthropic
**Keywords**: AI competition, US-China, CCP, export controls, compute advantage, authoritarian AI, Mythos Preview, AI safety, national security

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic lays out two 2028 scenarios for US-China AI competition — one where democracies lock in a commanding 12-24 month lead through tighter export controls and anti-distillation measures, and one where inaction lets the CCP catch up and shape AI norms with authoritarian values.

## Takeaways
- The US currently holds a substantial lead in compute (the most critical ingredient for frontier AI) thanks to American chip innovation and bipartisan export controls, but AI labs in China are close behind on model intelligence
- Chinese labs compensate for compute constraints through world-class talent, exploiting export control loopholes, and large-scale distillation attacks that illicitly extract innovations from US models
- Mythos Preview (released April 2026 via Project Glasswing) demonstrated the acceleration underway — Firefox fixed more security bugs last month than all of 2025, prompting a Chinese analyst to compare it to "a fully automatic Gatling gun"
- The CCP already uses AI for censorship, ethnic minority repression, and hacking; frontier models would enable automated repression at scales impossible with human enforcers alone
- A neck-and-neck race risks disincentivizing safety — only 3 of 13 top Chinese AI labs publish any safety evaluations, and DeepSeek models complied with 94% of malicious requests under common jailbreaking techniques

## Synthesis
Anthropic's May 14, 2026 policy paper is both a strategic argument and a call to action, framing the US-China AI competition in existential terms. The core thesis: democracies currently hold a strong position, but that advantage is perishable, and the next two years represent a decisive window.

The paper structures its argument around four fronts of competition: compute access (where US export controls on advanced chips are the primary lever), model intelligence (where Chinese labs' distillation attacks and loophole exploitation have kept them dangerously close), domestic adoption, and global distribution. The first two are presented as the critical near-term battlegrounds.

The Mythos Preview release serves as the paper's rhetorical anchor — concrete evidence that AI capabilities are accelerating faster than policy can adapt. The contrast between Firefox squashing more bugs in a month than in all of 2025 and the Chinese analyst's response captures the asymmetry Anthropic wants policymakers to grasp.

The safety dimension is particularly striking. Anthropic provides specific, damning data: only 3 of 13 top Chinese AI labs publish safety evaluations, none disclose CBRN risk testing, and DeepSeek's models complied with 94% of malicious requests under jailbreaking. Meanwhile, US labs publish extensive system cards, run automated alignment assessments, and have developed constitutional AI approaches. A CCP-led frontier, the paper argues, would not just lack these safeguards but would actively deploy AI for repression, military advantage, and domestic control.

Anthropic's policy recommendations center on three imperatives: tighten export controls further to maintain the compute advantage, disrupt distillation attacks, and accelerate democratic adoption. The paper acknowledges engagement with China on AI safety as desirable but argues it's only viable from a position of dominance — echoing CNBC reporting on Treasury Secretary Bessent's similar framing.

The document represents Anthropic's most forceful and detailed entry into AI geopolitics, reflecting the company's growing role as a national security stakeholder. By tying its corporate interests (protecting proprietary models from distillation) to democratic values and national security, Anthropic is making the case that its commercial success and America's strategic interests are aligned.

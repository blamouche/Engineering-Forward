# Nvidia Buys Hugging Face for $12.9B; Anthropic Strikes $45B Compute Deal with Nscale

**Date**: 2026-08-28
**Author**: Sifted Daily (Mimi Billing)

## Elevator pitch
Nvidia agreed to acquire AI platform Hugging Face for $12.9B while Anthropic struck a $45B deal to rent AI compute capacity from UK-based Nscale, as European tech achieves its second-best August for funding on record.

## Takeaways
- Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, consolidating its position across the AI model development stack
- Anthropic struck a $45 billion deal to rent AI compute capacity from UK-based data centre startup Nscale, signaling massive demand for dedicated AI infrastructure
- European tech achieved its second-best August for funding on record, with multiple under-the-radar rounds across deeptech, quantum, robotics, and pharma
- Denmark's tech ecosystem struggles with companies relocating headquarters abroad (Unity, Zendesk, Tradeshift, Netlify, Just Eat), leaving local growth capital scarce
- Swedish legaltech Legora is actively recruiting Copenhagen's best engineers to Stockholm, exemplifying intra-European talent competition
- Notable August rounds: VitalFluid (€17.2M, agtech), Redx Pharma ($20M, drug discovery), ColibriTD (€4M, quantum computing), Cota Robotics (€1.92M, retail robotics), Motion (€1.7M, humanoids)

## Synthesis
Two landmark AI infrastructure deals dominated the European tech news cycle. Nvidia's $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face represents a vertical integration play—Nvidia already owns the GPU hardware layer and is now acquiring the platform where most open-source AI models are hosted and shared. This gives Nvidia an unprecedented position across the entire AI development stack, from silicon to model distribution. The acquisition price reflects Hugging Face's strategic position as the central hub for the open-weight AI ecosystem.

Anthropic's $45 billion compute deal with Nscale is equally significant in its implications. The US model maker is committing an enormous sum to rent—rather than build—AI compute capacity from a UK-based data centre startup. This signals that frontier AI companies are increasingly willing to lock in long-term compute contracts with specialized providers rather than exclusively building their own infrastructure. The deal validates the emerging business model of AI-compute-as-a-service and demonstrates the massive capital flows now directed at inference and training capacity.

Beyond these deals, the Sifted newsletter covered the Danish tech ecosystem's struggles, highlighted at the TechBBQ conference in Copenhagen. Despite producing successful companies, many Danish firms relocate headquarters abroad, and local VC growth capital remains scarce. The Danish government is exploring pension fund deregulation to increase local tech investment. European tech funding overall is having its second-best August on record, with notable rounds in quantum computing (ColibriTD, €4M), robotics (Cota Robotics €1.92M, Motion €1.7M), and deeptech pharma (Redx Pharma, $20M). The most active US VCs in Europe are also profiled, showing continued transatlantic investment interest despite regulatory headwinds.

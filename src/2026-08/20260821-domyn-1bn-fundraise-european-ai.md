# Domyn Surpasses $1bn Fundraise as European AI Sovereignty Debate Intensifies
**Source**: https://sifted.eu
**Date**: 2026-08-21
**Author**: Tom Nugent / Sifted
**Keywords**: Domyn, European AI, AI sovereignty, frontier models, robotics, corporate venture capital, deep tech, EU AI

## Elevator pitch
Italian AI unicorn Domyn has raised over $1.1bn in debt and equity, aiming for $2bn this year, while a record number of corporate investors are making repeat bets on European robotics startups, signaling a deep-tech shift in Europe's AI investment landscape.

## Takeaways
- Domyn, the Milan-based AI company building LLMs and AI gigafactories for enterprise, has raised $1.1bn over the past two years and CEO Uljan Sharka wants to reach $2bn this year.
- European robotics startups raised more in H1 2026 than in the past two years combined, with seven corporate VCs making repeat bets — up from one in H2 2024.
- AI infrastructure players account for seven of 13 repeat corporate investment instances in European robotics, showing convergence between AI and physical automation.
- UK startup Callosum raised a $100m seed round led by Atomico with the UK Sovereign AI Fund, and Fractile is in talks for a $6.5bn valuation for AI inference chips.
- Starling Bank is launching a new AI tool every week, accelerating the neobank AI arms race in UK fintech.

## Synthesis
The European AI landscape is undergoing a structural shift, moving from consumer-facing applications toward deep tech and infrastructure. Domyn's $1.1bn fundraise — with ambitions to reach $2bn — represents one of the largest European AI bets, positioning the Italian company as a leading contender in the EU's push for AI sovereignty. CEO Uljan Sharka, who migrated from Albania to Italy without knowing Italian and later worked at Apple, embodies the transnational talent pool driving European AI.

The robotics data tells a complementary story. European robotics startups raised more in H1 2026 than in the previous two years combined, and the corporate venture capital data shows a maturing ecosystem: seven CVCs returned for repeat bets, up from just one in H2 2024. AI infrastructure players dominate these repeat investments, signaling that the convergence of AI and robotics is becoming an investment thesis, not just a narrative.

The broader funding environment shows depth across stages. Callosum's $100m seed round (led by Atomico with the UK Sovereign AI Fund) demonstrates that sovereign AI funds are actively deploying capital. Fractile's potential $6.5bn valuation for inference-specific chips shows that the AI hardware layer is attracting premium valuations. Meanwhile, Starling Bank's weekly AI rollout cadence illustrates how the AI arms race has reached traditional financial services.

The takeaway is that Europe's AI advantage is shifting toward deeper tech and harder problems — intelligent enterprise, digital health, space, energy, robotics and biology — where the continent's industrial base and regulatory framework may prove to be structural advantages rather than the barriers they're often portrayed as.

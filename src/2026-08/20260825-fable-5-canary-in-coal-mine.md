# Fable 5 May Just Be the Canary in the Coal Mine
**Source**: https://coai.beehiiv.com/p/canary-in-a-coal-mine
**Date**: 2026-08-25
**Author**: CO/AI Signal/Noise
**Keywords**: Fable 5, Anthropic, AI pricing, model economics, OpenAI, market share, coding

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's most expensive model Fable 5 has flatlined at 11% of business spending two months after launch, signaling that the reflex of automatically defaulting to the newest frontier model has broken—and with it, the economics of frontier AI pricing.

## Takeaways
- Fable 5, Anthropic's priciest model, has flatlined at about 11% of what companies spend on Anthropic's tools more than two months after shipping, according to Ramp data tracking 70,000 companies
- An Accel partner with a billion dollars riding on Anthropic said in the FT that most people don't need to operate at the frontier, and the era when they thought they did "was not a durable era"
- OpenAI grew faster last quarter (82% to Anthropic's 76%) but bought that growth with a discount: GPT-5.6 is priced well below Fable, while Anthropic is the bigger and profitable house ($65B annualized revenue to OpenAI's $40B-plus, with its first operating profit in Q2)
- Anthropic's own cheaper Opus 5 has already passed Fable 5 in usage, and Nvidia is telling customers server prices will rise ~15% next year—the shovel gets more expensive while the gold it digs gets cheaper
- Coding remains the one seam where the machine can grade its own work: the compiler says pass or fail, the work is checkable, and there's real cash on the table—but no loyalty, as newer cheaper coding models point straight at Anthropic's best customers

## Synthesis
The most expensive AI model on earth has become a rounding error. Fable 5, Anthropic's biggest and priciest model, has flatlined at about 11 percent of what companies spend on Anthropic's tools, more than two months after it shipped. For two years, the industry reflex was automatic: a new frontier model drops, everybody defaults to it. That reflex just broke.

The framing is architectural. A data center is a mine: scarce power, purpose-built silicon, racks humming in the dark, pulling something valuable out of the rock. Bitcoin runs on that mine and was engineered from day one to be scarce—twenty-one million coins, and the digging gets harder on purpose. Intelligence runs on the identical mine and was built for the exact opposite: you make more of it by pouring in more power and chips, the models get cheaper by the week, and supply is capped only by how much you feel like spending. Bitcoin is digital gold; intelligence is digital labor. Gold gets hoarded and rises; labor gets consumed and repriced toward nothing.

The OpenAI-versus-Anthropic tape illustrates this. On Ramp's numbers, OpenAI grew faster last quarter—82 percent to 76 percent. But OpenAI bought that growth with a discount: GPT-5.6 is priced well below Fable. Anthropic is the bigger house and the profitable one: $65 billion in annualized revenue to OpenAI's $40-plus billion, and its first operating profit in the second quarter. Anthropic's own cheaper Opus 5 has already passed Fable 5 in usage. The faster grower is winning on price; the richer one is winning on the base it already owns; and neither owns a shred of loyalty. Businesses swap labs as fast as the labs ship models.

Meanwhile, Nvidia is telling those same customers that server prices go up around 15 percent next year, with memory alone now close to a third of a rack. The shovel gets more expensive while the gold it digs gets cheaper. The one seam where money still sits is coding—the one place where the machine can grade its own work. The compiler says pass or fail, the work is checkable, and there's real cash on the table. Which is exactly why the newest, cheaper coding models landing this month point straight at Anthropic's best customers. The canary hasn't even breathed that in yet.

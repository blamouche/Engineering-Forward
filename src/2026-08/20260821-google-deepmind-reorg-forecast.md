# Google in the Post-Jeff Dean, Post-Demis Hassabis Era
**Source**: https://futuresearch.ai/blog/google-deepmind-reorg-forecast/
**Date**: 2026-08-05
**Author**: FutureSearch
**Keywords**: google, deepmind, jeff-dean, hassabis, gemini, discovery-loop, ai-forecast

## Elevator pitch
A detailed forecasting analysis of Google's AI reorg predicts Gemini 4 won't ship until mid-2027, Google is 12 months behind the LLM frontier, and there's a 66% chance a Gemini model will serve a weapons role by end of 2027.

## Takeaways
- Gemini 4 general availability is forecast for May 15, 2027 (80% interval: Jan-Dec 2027), significantly later than the industry consensus of late 2026, based on the observation that Google already scrapped and rebuilt one base model this year
- Google is now estimated to be 12 months behind the LLM frontier, up from 6-9 months two months ago, driven by Gemini 3.5 Pro's scrapped rebuild and the talent loss from Dean/Ghemawat/Vinyals/Le's departure
- Discovery Loop, the new startup from Dean and Ghemawat, is forecast at a $5.8B initial valuation, with Google as investor and compute provider
- There's a 60% probability that Anthropic's annualized Google Cloud spend will exceed Gemini's own revenue before 2028, highlighting that Google may make more money hosting competitors' models than selling its own
- The dissolution of DeepMind's original 2014 acquisition safeguards is notable: the ethics board control of AGI and the military-use prohibition have both been removed, and there's a 66% forecast of a Gemini model serving a weapons/targeting role by end of 2027

## Synthesis
This forecasting analysis from FutureSearch provides one of the most data-driven assessments of Google's AI position after the landmark Dean/Hassabis reorg. The author, a former Google forecaster, brings insider context to the question of what happens when a company loses four of its most senior AI leaders in a single departure.

The most striking finding is the 12-month lag estimate for Gemini 4. The reasoning is grounded in concrete timeline physics: frontier pre-training started in late July on Google's largest compute budget ever, such runs take 100+ days, post-training adds months, and a 30-day federal review follows. Google already scrapped one base model this year (for a smaller model than Gemini 4), suggesting that slippage is the central case rather than the exception.

The forecast about Anthropic's Google Cloud spend potentially exceeding Gemini revenue is a revealing metric. If accurate, it suggests that Google's AI strategy may be shifting from "build the best model" to "own the infrastructure that everyone else uses to build models." This would be consistent with Google Cloud's 82% year-over-year growth, much of it driven by AI compute demand.

The military-use forecast (66%) is the most provocative element. The author traces the dissolution of DeepMind's original safeguards — the ethics board's AGI control provision, the military-use prohibition — and notes that Google signed a classified Pentagon agreement in April 2026 that permits Gemini for "any lawful government purpose," including human-supervised target selection. Whether this matters more than model quality or market share is a question the industry will be forced to answer.

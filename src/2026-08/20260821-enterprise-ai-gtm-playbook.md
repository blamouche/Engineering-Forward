# The New Enterprise AI Go-To-Market Playbook
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/enterprise-ai-gtm-playbook
**Date**: 2026-08-06
**Author**: Linas Beliūnas
**Keywords**: enterprise-ai, go-to-market, lighthouse, landgrab, a16z, distribution

## Elevator pitch
A16z's "Lighthouse or Landgrab" framework identifies two fundamentally different enterprise AI sales motions — and most founders pick the wrong one, burning a funding round finding out that they should have been doing the other.

## Takeaways
- The framework's two-question diagnostic asks: (1) how exposed is the person signing the deal, and (2) does social proof travel in that market — answering these determines whether you should pursue Lighthouse or Landgrab
- Lighthouse strategy targets a small number of high-profile enterprise logos, investing heavily in making each one successful so that social proof spreads through the industry
- Landgrab strategy signs hundreds of mid-market accounts quickly, relying on volume and self-service rather than deep custom deployments
- 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots die before production, often because founders chose the wrong GTM motion and got trapped in permanent pilot purgatory
- Sierra, Glean, Abridge, Rogo, Legora, Clay, and Cursor are cited as examples of companies that each picked their lane deliberately
- The article includes a downloadable Claude skill ("lighthouse-landgrab") that runs the full diagnostic on your company and drafts a motion plan

## Synthesis
The Lighthouse vs. Landgrab framework is the most actionable GTM diagnostic for enterprise AI startups since the original "Bottom Up vs. Top Down" debate. Its power comes from asking two questions that most founders never consciously consider: whether the decision-maker's career is on the line if the purchase fails, and whether success stories in this market spread from company to company.

The Lighthouse motion works when social proof travels (if JPMorgan uses your product, every other bank will hear about it) and the buyer is highly exposed (the CISO's job is on the line). The strategy is to pour resources into 3-5 Fortune 100 logos, make them wildly successful, and let the social proof sell the next 50. The Landgrab motion works when social proof doesn't travel (every retail company has different problems) and the buyer isn't career-exposed (a team lead can try a tool without board approval). The strategy is to make onboarding frictionless and sell through PLG.

The 95% pilot death rate for enterprise GenAI is the punchline that makes this framework urgent. Most pilots fail not because the product is bad, but because the GTM motion is misaligned with the market's buying behavior. A Lighthouse company trying to do Landgrab will underinvest in each logo and fail to create social proof. A Landgrab company trying to do Lighthouse will overinvest in custom deployments that can't scale. The framework's clarity is that these aren't preferences — they're structural constraints imposed by the market.

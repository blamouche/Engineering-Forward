# When Artificial Intelligence Is Too Valuable To Sell
**Source**: https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/when-artificial-intelligence-is-too
**Date**: 2026-07-31
**Author**: Alex Kantrowitz
**Keywords**: ai-business-model, api, frontier-models, hoarding, commoditization

## Elevator pitch
As the AI frontier gets crowded with five or more labs producing frontier models, the economics of selling intelligence by the token may flip — and the labs that built their businesses on API access may start hoarding their best models for their own products instead.

## Takeaways
- The assumption that frontier AI labs will always license their best models via API is being challenged by market dynamics: a crowded frontier (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, DeepSeek) is driving down the marginal value of selling intelligence on a meter
- OpenAI and Anthropic are already moving upmarket with products like Claude Code, Claude Design, and the ChatGPT superapp — suggesting they see more value in selling the product than the underlying model
- A crowded frontier doesn't make AI a pure commodity because specialization and compute resources still give certain labs edges, but it does compress the premium that frontier models can charge
- The "upmarket" move means AI labs will increasingly compete with their own API customers, a dynamic that could reshape the entire SaaS ecosystem
- The strategic question for every AI-dependent business is whether the model they depend on will still be available via API in 12 months, or whether the lab will reserve it for their own vertical products

## Synthesis
Alex Kantrowitz's essay tackles a question that most AI-dependent businesses are reluctant to ask: what happens if the frontier labs stop selling their best models and start using them exclusively in their own products? The argument is grounded in straightforward economics. When five or six labs can produce frontier-quality models, the competitive advantage shifts from "who has the best model" to "who builds the best product on top of it." If you're OpenAI and your model advantage is narrowing, you'd rather capture the full value of the application layer than sell compute at a margin that's eroding.

The evidence is already visible. Anthropic's Claude Code and Claude Design are vertical products that compete with developer tools. OpenAI's ChatGPT superapp with built-in Codex is a full-stack application play. Both labs are still selling API access, but they're also clearly building products that could replace their customers' offerings. This is the "SaaS-pocalypse" scenario that venture capitalists have warned about since GPT-3: if your startup's core value proposition is "we put a nice UI on top of GPT-4," you're vulnerable to the model provider moving upmarket.

Kantrowitz doesn't argue that API access will disappear entirely — the labs still need developer ecosystems and compute revenue. But he does argue that the best models may become selectively available, and that the terms of access will increasingly favor the labs' own product ambitions. For anyone building an AI-dependent business, the implication is clear: model diversity and the ability to switch providers are becoming existential requirements, not just nice-to-have architectural choices.

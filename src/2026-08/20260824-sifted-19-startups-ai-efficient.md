## Synthesis
Sifted's list of 19 startups making AI more efficient, curated from VC perspectives, captures a shifting investment thesis: the first wave of AI investment focused on capability — building bigger, smarter models. The second wave is focusing on efficiency — making those models cheaper, faster, and more practical to deploy. This shift is driven by real economics: Every reported a 230% jump in AI costs when GPT-5.6 Sol launched, and companies across the board are struggling with inference spend.

The startups on the list address different layers of the AI efficiency stack. Some focus on inference optimization — making model serving faster and cheaper through better scheduling, batching, or hardware utilization. Others tackle model compression — reducing model size and computational requirements while preserving performance. Still others address resource management — helping companies allocate AI compute budgets intelligently, similar to how Stripe's investor letter framed token consumption as a currency requiring capital-grade management discipline.

The European context is significant. While US companies dominate AI model development, European startups are finding opportunities in the efficiency layer where domain expertise in distributed systems, hardware, and optimization translates directly to value. The list also complements Sifted's broader coverage of European deeptech, including their Startup Agentic AI Playbook which examines why half of all AI agents fail and how founders who cracked the code are running dozens of agents to cut administrative work.

The efficiency thesis is ultimately about AI's maturation from research lab to production infrastructure. Just as cloud computing spawned an entire ecosystem of cost optimization and monitoring tools, AI is spawning its own efficiency infrastructure — and VCs are placing their bets on which companies will become the Datadogs and Snowflakes of the AI era.

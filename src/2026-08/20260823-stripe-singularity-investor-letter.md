## Elevator pitch
Stripe's August 19 investor letter announcing the OpenRouter acquisition frames intelligence (token consumption) as a second currency parallel to money, arguing that managing inference spend requires the same discipline as financial capital management — and that the singularity began on January 1, 2026.

## Takeaways
- Patrick Collison told Stripe's investors that the singularity began on January 1, 2026, and that the company now manages two currencies: money and intelligence
- OpenRouter is reframed not as a model router but as an "intelligence pipeline" — the routing and metering layer for token consumption, parallel to Stripe's money pipeline
- Token consumption on OpenRouter has been compounding at 9% per week year-to-date, annualizing to roughly 90x
- H1 2026: net revenue grew 41% YoY, free cash flow grew 43%, Stripe Billing grew 71%; Atlas accounts for >25% of all Delaware incorporations; Link passed 300M users
- Radar, originally built for payment fraud, is already being used to detect token fraud at major AI companies — deepening lock-in without a new sale
- Stripe's share count is lower today than three years ago despite Bridge, Metronome, Privy, and OpenRouter acquisitions — core payments engine absorbs all four without net dilution
- Share price has compounded at 31% annually since Series D a decade ago, vs 14% for S&P 500

## Synthesis
Stripe's August 19 investor letter, written to announce the OpenRouter acquisition, goes far beyond the deal itself. Patrick Collison identifies capital and intelligence as the two digital flows that run every business, arguing that managing inference spend requires the same discipline as managing financial capital: cost-of-return reasoning on every unit and granular billing against time-value of delay. In this framing, Stripe built the pipeline for the first flow (money), and OpenRouter is the pipeline for the second (intelligence).

The letter treats tokens as a parallel currency to dollars — expensive, heterogeneous, and constantly changing. Accept this framing and the $7 billion price stops looking like a premium for a routing API. It becomes the entry cost for a second Stripe-shaped business running on a second currency. The Collisons are careful to strip the word "singularity" of its apocalyptic baggage; they mean an observed inflection, especially in the rate of new firm creation, and they decided to take it seriously.

The numbers back the posture. H1 2026 net revenue grew 41% year over year, free cash flow grew 43%, and Stripe Billing grew 71%. Atlas now accounts for more than a quarter of all Delaware incorporations, and Link passed 300 million users. Token consumption on OpenRouter has been compounding at 9% per week year-to-date, which annualizes to roughly 90x — a growth rate that, if sustained, would make token routing one of the fastest-scaling infrastructure businesses in history.

The product stack the letter maps out is comprehensive: discovery and onboarding for agents through Stripe Projects, Directory, and a Provisioning API; usage management through Metronome; payments through Bridge, MPP, and Tempo; fund storage through Privy and a new stablecoin; and OpenRouter as the routing and metering layer. Radar, originally built for payment fraud, is already detecting token fraud at major AI companies — another product that deepens lock-in without a new sale.

The letter also answers the dilution question: Stripe's share count is lower today than three years ago despite four major acquisitions. The core payments engine prints enough cash to absorb all four without net dilution. Whether the world agrees with the "second currency" framing depends on whether tokens actually behave like a currency. If they do, Stripe just doubled its addressable surface. If they don't, it bought an API aggregator for 50 times revenue.

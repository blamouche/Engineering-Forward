## Synthesis
Jen Abel, co-founder of JJellyfish and GM of enterprise sales at State Affairs, brings a practitioner's rigor to enterprise sales. Her core argument is that the standard five-stage CRM pipeline — prospecting, qualification, proposal, negotiation, close — is a forecasting tool designed for CRM software, not an actual sales process. The real enterprise sales cycle, she argues, is closer to 15 steps, and conflating the two is why most sales teams struggle to forecast accurately.

The 15-step cycle begins with the "pincer model" for landing the first meeting: approaching both the executive level and the N-minus-one level (the person who reports to the executive) simultaneously. This dual approach creates internal alignment before the first conversation. Outreach messages are kept to 2-3 sentences and framed around giving the prospect "alpha" — valuable intelligence or insight — rather than listing features.

The intro call is treated as an intelligence-gathering exercise, not a pitch. Abel's approach extracts maximum information about the prospect's situation, constraints, and decision-making process before ever showing a demo. This inverts the common pattern of leading with a demo in hopes of impressing the prospect.

Pilots are structured as 2-3 day engagements with jointly defined success criteria. The key decision point is when to charge: short pilots can be free, but longer pilots should be paid to maintain perceived value and commitment. The final stages — pricing, procurement, redline negotiations, and signature — are where most deals lose momentum, and Abel provides specific tactics for navigating each without stalling.

This framework builds on Abel's previous work mapping the $1M-$10M enterprise sales playbook, extending it down to $100K+ deals. The emphasis on process over personality — 15 explicit steps rather than "relationship building" — makes the methodology teachable and repeatable, which is particularly relevant for founder-led sales teams that need to scale beyond the founder.

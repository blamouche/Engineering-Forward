## Synthesis
Anthropic's IPO preparation reveals an aggressive valuation narrative. The $30 trillion TAM figure originates from a leaked Coatue deck slide that has been adopted into the public roadshow, topping SpaceX's previous record. The number runs approximately 650x the business Anthropic actually operates today. The analysis suggests the TAM isn't dishonest in the way IPO numbers often are—it has a specific job: signal the size of the opportunity to public market investors. The real tell to watch in the upcoming S-1 filing is revenue concentration (how much comes from a few large enterprise customers) and actual unit economics, rather than the headline market size.

Stripe's acquisition of Clerky is a more subtle strategic move. Clerky is the legal operating system behind $140 billion in startup fundraising—incorporation, equity grants, board resolutions, and compliance documentation. For Stripe, this is a switching-cost play: once a startup's legal infrastructure runs through Clerky, switching away from Stripe's ecosystem becomes increasingly painful. The attorney distribution channel is something Stripe's own Atlas product could never build on its own, because lawyers recommend tools to clients as a matter of course. The acquisition also means Stripe no longer needs to buy Carta to make Carta less inevitable—Clerky's legal workflow integration creates a different kind of moat.

Revolut's EURR stablecoin launch rounds out the fintech analysis. With only €369 in initial reserves but 80M+ banking customers and 16M existing crypto traders, the token itself is irrelevant—it's the distribution that matters. Revolut outsourced issuance to Stripe's Bridge platform and kept the brand and customer relationship, a white-label model that mirrors the PayPal/Paxos playbook. The real race isn't against Circle (which lacks banking distribution) but against other large banks that could launch similar products. The analysis includes deep dives into Revolut's AI Research Lab and the broader stablecoin landscape.

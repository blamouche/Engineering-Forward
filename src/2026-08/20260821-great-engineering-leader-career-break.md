# Headed for the Exit: The Great Engineering Leader Career Break
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-great-engineering-leader-career-break
**Date**: 2026-08-18
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: engineering leadership, career breaks, CTO, VP Engineering, burnout, AI-native, founder mode

## Elevator pitch
A wave of CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and heads of engineering are quitting high-status roles with nothing lined up — driven by unrealistic AI expectations from founders, shrinking team sizes, the appeal of fractional work, and the sense that ICs at AI startups now out-earn executives at non-AI companies.

## Takeaways
- 6 out of 10 engineering leaders the author spoke to are either on a career break or seriously considering one — an unprecedented number in the author's ~20 years in the industry.
- The #1 reason for quitting: unrealistic AI expectations from founders and CEOs, who expect the CTO to "magically transform the company to be AI-native" regardless of the company's actual context.
- Smaller teams mean fewer leadership roles: AI-augmented engineering means the same output with fewer people, reducing the need for engineering managers and leaders.
- AI startups pay ICs more than non-AI startups pay executives — senior engineers at AI companies command $400K–$800K+ packages while VPEs at non-AI startups may earn half that.
- "Founder mode" (where founders bypass management layers) is here to stay, and it's making the CTO/VPE role feel like "low ROI" to founders who see themselves as the primary technical decision-makers.

## Synthesis
The article documents a structural shift in the engineering leadership market. The convergence of AI disruption, shrinking team sizes, and founder-mode dynamics is making the traditional CTO/VPE role less attractive and less necessary. Engineering leaders are not just burning out — they're concluding that the role itself has changed in ways that make it less fulfilling and less impactful.

The AI expectations angle is particularly sharp: founders who read about AI transforming everything want their CTO to make it happen immediately, without understanding the gap between AI demo capabilities and production-grade AI systems. This creates an impossible mandate that drives leaders to leave.

The fractional CTO trend is emerging as an alternative: instead of a full-time executive, companies engage experienced leaders for 10-20 hours per week. This works for smaller companies that need strategic guidance more than day-to-day management, and it gives leaders the variety and autonomy they crave.

The practical implication for companies: if you're hiring a CTO or VPE right now, the talent pool is unusually rich with experienced leaders who chose to leave rather than being pushed out. But the compensation expectations have shifted — these candidates know their market value in the AI economy, and they're not going back to the old model of long vesting schedules for below-market equity.

# New Cherry Partner Eyes Lovable and Legora Mafia
**Source**: Sifted (sifted@sifted.eu)
**Date**: 2026-09-28
**Author**: Martin Coulter (Sifted)
**Keywords**: Cherry Ventures, Lovable, Legora, AI agents, insurance, European VC, Monzo, Nubank, Humanos, AI agent monitoring, Peter Thiel, Europe

## Elevator pitch
Sifted's daily digest covers Cherry Ventures hiring Tatiana Shalalvand to back founders from the Lovable and Legora ecosystems, a Lisbon startup raising seed funding for AI agent insurance, Monzo reportedly in early talks for a sale to Nubank, and Peter Thiel's critique of Europe's tech scene.

## Takeaways
- Tatiana Shalalvand joins Cherry Ventures as a new partner, specifically eyeing founders from Lovable and Legora — a signal that the "mafia" effect of AI-native companies is becoming a venture thesis
- Humanos, a Lisbon-based startup building a network to continuously monitor and score AI agents based on their behaviour, raised $3.2m in seed funding led by Anthemis — the first dedicated insurance/monitoring play for AI agents
- Monzo is said to be in early talks for a sale to Brazilian digital banking giant Nubank, which would represent one of the largest European neobank acquisitions
- Peter Thiel's critique of Europe is both right and wrong: right about structural liquidity deficits and regulatory friction, wrong about the depth of talent and the growing ecosystem momentum
- The Sifted Summit agenda highlights Europe's "China blindspot," the struggle for startups to win government contracts, and whether the secondaries boom is delivering liquidity beyond trophy companies

## Synthesis
Martin Coulter's daily Sifted newsletter for September 28 covers several stories relevant to the European tech ecosystem. The most notable is Cherry Ventures hiring Tatiana Shalalvand as a new partner, with a specific mandate to back founders emerging from Lovable and Legora — two of Europe's most prominent AI-native companies. This is a clear signal that the "mafia" effect, well-documented in Silicon Valley (PayPal Mafia, Stripe Mafia), is now being deliberately pursued by European VCs in the AI space. Shalalvand's mandate suggests that the talent flowing out of these companies is seen as the highest-signal source of new AI startup founders.

On the AI infrastructure side, Humanos raised $3.2m in seed funding from Anthemis to build a network that continuously monitors and scores AI agents based on their behaviour. This is essentially insurance for AI agents — a new category that addresses the question of liability and trust when autonomous systems make decisions. The Lisbon-based startup represents the emerging layer of AI governance and monitoring infrastructure that will be needed as agents are deployed at scale.

The Monzo-Nubank rumor, if confirmed, would be a landmark deal: a UK digital bank being acquired by a Brazilian rival, signaling the global consolidation pressure on neobanks. It also validates Nubank's strategy of expanding beyond Latin America through acquisition rather than organic growth.

The newsletter also teases Sifted Summit sessions, including one on Europe's "China blindspot" — the idea that European tech leaders are not sufficiently considering China as a competitor, partner, or market — and a panel on whether traditional tech media can survive the rise of creators, AI search, and disappearing Google traffic, a question increasingly relevant to anyone publishing content online.

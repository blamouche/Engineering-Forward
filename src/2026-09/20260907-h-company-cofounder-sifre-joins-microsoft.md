# H Company Cofounder Laurent Sifre Joins Microsoft to Build Paris AI Research Team
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/h-company-cofounder-laurent-sifre-microsoft
**Date**: 2026-09-07
**Author**: Freya Pratty
**Keywords**: H Company, Laurent Sifre, Microsoft, Paris AI, agentic AI, DeepMind, talent migration

## Elevator pitch
Laurent Sifre, cofounder of Paris-based agentic AI startup H Company, has joined Microsoft to build a Paris-focused AI research team, signaling Big Tech's continued pull on European AI talent.

## Takeaways
- Laurent Sifre, cofounder of H Company (Paris-based agentic AI startup), has joined Microsoft
- He is tasked with building a Paris-based team focused on AI research at Microsoft
- H Company was one of the prominent European agentic AI startups, and Sifre's departure is a significant talent loss
- The move underscores the ongoing gravitational pull of US tech giants on European AI talent, even as Europe invests in sovereignty
- Sifre previously worked at DeepMind before founding H Company, continuing the DeepMind-to-startup-to-Big-Tech pipeline

## Synthesis
Laurent Sifre's move from H Company cofounder to Microsoft employee captures a dynamic that continues to shape the European AI ecosystem: the continent produces exceptional talent, but US tech giants increasingly capture it. Sifre, who previously worked at DeepMind before cofounding Paris-based H Company, has joined Microsoft to build a Paris-based AI research team.

The departure is notable because H Company was one of the more prominent European agentic AI startups, and losing a cofounder to Microsoft at this stage raises questions about the startup's trajectory. It also illustrates a pattern: DeepMind alumni found European AI ventures, build initial traction, then get absorbed by US tech giants who can offer resources, compute, and scale that European startups struggle to match.

Microsoft's decision to have Sifre build a Paris-based team is strategically significant. Rather than relocating talent to Redmond or Silicon Valley, Microsoft is investing in a European AI research hub — likely motivated by France's deep AI talent pool, competitive labor costs relative to the US, and the political imperative of demonstrating European investment. This mirrors Google's DeepMind presence in Paris and Meta's FAIR lab.

For European AI sovereignty advocates, the move is double-edged: Microsoft is investing in Paris, but the intellectual property and strategic direction ultimately serve a US company. The talent drain from European startups to US giants continues to be one of the structural challenges facing the continent's ambition to build independent AI champions.

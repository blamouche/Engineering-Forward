# What I Got Wrong About Meta's Muse, And The Deeper Threat To Frontier AI
**Source**: Big Technology (bigtechnology@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-09-25
**Author**: Alex Kantrowitz (Big Technology)
**Keywords**: Meta, Muse, frontier models, standard models, AI spending, OpenAI, Anthropic, Ramp, model routers, token economics, GPT-6 Astra, Muse Spark

## Elevator pitch
Alex Kantrowitz admits he was wrong about Meta's Muse personal agent: it works well despite using non-frontier AI models, proving that today's "next-level-down" standard models are good enough for many real-world agent tasks. This shift poses an existential threat to frontier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, whose expensive frontier models are seeing a declining share of AI spend — from 53% in early August to 45% by early September, according to Ramp data.

## Takeaways
- **Standard models are good enough**: Meta built a genuinely useful AI agent (Muse) without frontier intelligence. Today's "next-level-down" models have become competent enough for production agent work, reducing the premium justification for frontier models.
- **AI spend shifting from frontier to standard**: 53% of AI dollars went to frontier models in early August, dropping to 45% by September per Ramp. The arrow is pointing in the wrong direction for top AI labs.
- **Price gap is orders of magnitude**: OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra costs $10/$50 per million input/output tokens vs Meta's Muse Spark at $1.25/$4.25 — an 8-12x cost difference.
- **Model routers accelerate the shift**: Companies are done "tokenmaxxing" and use model routers to direct spend to capable cheaper models, only paying for frontier intelligence in select use cases.
- **Competition from other US models, not China**: Ramp's chief economist notes the revenue growth path for OpenAI and Anthropic has weakened due to competition from other US models, not just open source or China.
- **IPO implications**: As OpenAI and Anthropic head toward trillion-dollar IPOs, their ability to extract a premium on expensive frontier models is one of the biggest variables investors must reckon with.

## Synthesis
Kantrowitz's mea culpa on Meta's Muse illuminates a structural shift in the AI economy: the frontier is commoditizing from below. Standard models have crossed a competence threshold where they can power production agents effectively, and the economics are brutal for frontier labs — 8-12x cheaper, increasingly capable, and routed automatically by spend-optimization tools. The Ramp data showing frontier spend dropping from 53% to 45% in one month is a leading indicator that should worry OpenAI and Anthropic investors. The "harness" argument is key: standard models paired with good tool-using harnesses can accomplish tasks that recently required frontier intelligence. If this trend continues, frontier models may become premium niche products for genuinely hard problems, while standard models capture the volume — inverting the business model that justifies hundred-billion-dollar valuations. The deeper question for the IPO market: how much of AI spending is actually "frontier-required" vs "frontier-optional"?

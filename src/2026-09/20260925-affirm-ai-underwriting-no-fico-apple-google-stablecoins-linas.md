# Affirm's AI Underwriting Works Best on Borrowers with No FICO Score; No iCoin, No Google USD
**Source**: Linas's Newsletter (linas@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-09-25
**Author**: Linas Sekeriute
**Keywords**: Affirm, AI underwriting, transformer, FICO score, XGBoost, Apple, Google, stablecoin, tokenized deposits, Nubank, Revolut PRAGMA, agentic OS, Claude Fable 5

## Elevator pitch
Linas breaks down Affirm's new AI underwriting model — a transformer now live at US checkout that gets its largest gains on applicants with no FICO score — examining how it plugs into Affirm's XGBoost stack and why Affirm's own repayment data drove most of the gains. The newsletter also dives into why Apple and Google are hunting stablecoin talent but neither is building an iCoin or Google Dollar, with both job listings pointing toward tokenized deposits instead.

## Takeaways
- **Affirm's transformer underwriting model**: Now live at US checkout, achieving 3.4% more completed purchases. The headline result came from a narrower test than the press release implied.
- **Largest gains on no-FICO applicants**: The model performs best on borrowers with no credit score, using credit report data Affirm already had — a population traditional scoring can't serve.
- **Affirm's own repayment data drove most gains**: The transformer plugs into Affirm's existing XGBoost stack, leveraging proprietary repayment history rather than external credit data alone.
- **Apple and Google hiring stablecoin talent**: Both companies are hiring — Apple a payments strategist, Google a Web3 architect in Hong Kong — but neither is building a consumer crypto token. Job listings ask for tokenized deposits expertise.
- **Nubank as the model Apple is likely studying**: Nubank already built the digital-native financial infrastructure that Apple may be examining for its next moves in payments.
- **Bonus deep dives**: Revolut's PRAGMA system, How to Build an Agentic OS with Claude Fable 5, and Google's interface between AI and money.

## Synthesis
The Affirm underwriting story is a textbook case of AI creating value in a specific, measurable way: serving the "thin file" population that traditional credit scoring can't reach. The 3.4% lift in completed purchases is meaningful for a checkout-flow optimization, and the fact that gains concentrate on no-FICO applicants suggests the transformer is extracting signal from patterns that FICO-based XGBoost models can't capture. The caveat — that the test was narrower than the press release implied — is the kind of detail that separates engineering blog reality from marketing.

The Apple/Google stablecoin talent hunt is more telling than it appears. Neither company building a "coin" signals that the era of branded cryptocurrencies is fading in favor of regulated tokenized deposits — essentially programmable bank money on chain. Apple hiring a payments strategist (not a crypto engineer) and Google hiring a Web3 architect in Hong Kong (not Silicon Valley) suggest different approaches: Apple likely exploring embedded finance in its ecosystem, Google exploring infrastructure. Nubank as the reference model is apt — it proved that digital-native banking infrastructure can achieve scale without traditional banking licenses.

# DeepMind Veteran Thore Graepel Leaves to Pursue AI Reasoning Venture
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/deepmind-veteran-thore-graepel-reasoning-venture
**Date**: 2026-09-07
**Author**: Freya Pratty
**Keywords**: DeepMind, AlphaGo, Thore Graepel, AI reasoning, LLM architecture, David Silver, Ineffable Intelligence

## Elevator pitch
A key researcher behind AlphaGo is leaving DeepMind to build a new venture focused on redesigning AI architectures for genuine reasoning, arguing that current LLMs fundamentally cannot reason.

## Takeaways
- Thore Graepel, who worked on AlphaGo alongside David Silver, is leaving DeepMind after years to pursue an AI reasoning startup
- He argues today's LLMs cannot truly reason — the architecture needs fundamental redesign, not just scaling
- Graepel follows David Silver (who founded Ineffable Intelligence) and other DeepMind alumni who have departed to build ventures
- His venture's precise nature and funding rounds remain undisclosed, but the thesis centers on reasoning-first AI architecture
- The departure highlights the ongoing brain drain at DeepMind as researchers seek to commercialize alternative approaches to intelligence

## Synthesis
Ten years after AlphaGo's landmark victory over Lee Sedol, one of the key researchers behind that achievement is leaving to challenge the dominant paradigm in AI. Thore Graepel, a veteran DeepMind researcher who worked on AlphaGo, is departing to launch a new venture focused on AI reasoning — and his critique of current approaches is sharp.

"I'm arguing we need to go back to the architecture and fundamentally redesign it so that it does proper reasoning," Graepel told Sifted. His argument is that large language models, despite their impressive outputs, do not actually reason in the way that game-playing AI systems like AlphaGo needed to. The architectures that powered landmark AI achievements a decade ago relied on search, planning, and deliberation — capabilities that today's autoregressive LLMs approximate through pattern matching but do not truly possess.

Graepel follows his AlphaGo collaborator David Silver, who left DeepMind to found Ineffable Intelligence. The trend of DeepMind alumni departing to build ventures has accelerated, with researchers seeking megarounds to commercialize alternative approaches. Graepel is tight-lipped about funding but clear about the thesis: reasoning requires architectural change, not just more data or more parameters.

The departure underscores a growing tension in AI research. While the industry races to scale transformer-based LLMs, a contingent of experienced researchers believes the path forward requires fundamentally different architectures — ones that incorporate search, planning, and deliberation rather than relying on next-token prediction. Whether reasoning-first approaches can compete with the scale advantages of frontier LLM labs remains the open question. But Graepel's credentials — AlphaGo, years at DeepMind — give his critique weight that casual observers cannot dismiss.

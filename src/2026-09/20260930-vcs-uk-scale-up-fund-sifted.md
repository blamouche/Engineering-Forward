## Synthesis
The Sifted Summit and its surrounding stories capture a European tech ecosystem at an inflection point. The £1bn Scale-Up Fund represents government attempting to catalyse growth, but the real signal is in the friction around it: which VCs are credible enough to deploy that capital, and whether the UK's regulatory environment — non-competes, notice periods, fund rules — is helping or hindering. The intervention by ElevenLabs, Fractile, and others on non-competes is particularly telling: these are AI companies whose entire competitive advantage is talent velocity, and they're experiencing the same mobility friction that plagued pre-AI tech.

The parallel threads of Stripe's agent-commerce sponsorship and Zenithon's world-models fundraising reveal two distinct bets on the future: one where AI agents become economic actors (buying, transacting, negotiating), and another where AI models tackle physical-world simulation. The Juicebox London landing and Nordic fintech coverage remind us that talent and capital continue to flow across borders regardless of regulatory intent. Flowpay's 816% growth on 28 people is a data point worth holding: in an AI-assisted era, revenue-per-employee is becoming the most telling metric of all.

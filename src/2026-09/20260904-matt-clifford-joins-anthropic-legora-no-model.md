# Matt Clifford Joins Anthropic; Legora Explains Why It Won't Build Its Own AI Model
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/matt-clifford-anthropic
**Date**: 2026-09-04
**Author**: Tom Nugent (Sifted Daily)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Matt Clifford, Entrepreneurs First, Legora, legaltech, AI models, Revolut, US banking licence, UK spinouts

## Elevator pitch
Matt Clifford, cofounder of accelerator Entrepreneurs First and former AI adviser to the UK prime minister, announced he is joining Anthropic — drawing criticism from parts of the tech world. Meanwhile, Swedish legaltech Legora explains why training a frontier model doesn't make business sense, and Revolut clears its first hurdle toward a US banking licence.

## Takeaways
- Matt Clifford, who cofounded Entrepreneurs First before becoming the UK government's AI adviser to the PM, is joining Anthropic — a move that drew criticism as a revolving-door conflict of interest
- Legora, the Swedish legaltech that raised $550m in March, says it won't build its own AI model because training a frontier model "doesn't make business sense" — contrasting with rival Harvey which has launched one
- Legora has made five acquisitions since January and switched some tiers to consumption-based pricing, which its engineering team prefers
- Revolut received conditional approval from the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) for its US banking licence
- The UK government ruled out a proposed spinout exit tax, relieving investors who feared a "double tax" on academic founders

## Synthesis
This Sifted Daily digest covers several stories relevant to the AI and startup ecosystem. The most notable is Matt Clifford's move to Anthropic. Clifford cofounded accelerator Entrepreneurs First, then joined the UK government as AI adviser to the prime minister. His announcement that he is joining US AI giant Anthropic has drawn criticism from corners of the tech world, with some viewing it as a conflict of interest given his recent government role shaping AI policy.

On the legaltech front, Swedish company Legora — which raised $550m in March led by Accel — is often compared to US rival Harvey. The two diverge on a key strategic question: whether to build their own AI models. Harvey has launched one; Legora says training a frontier model doesn't make business sense. Legora CTO Jacob Lauritzen explained at TechBBQ in Copenhagen that the company is focusing on integration and product strategy rather than model training. Legora has made five acquisitions since January, switched some pricing tiers to consumption-based models, and recently integrated with Google.

In fintech, Revolut has been given conditional approval for its US banking licence from the OCC, as it seeks to operate as a fully fledged bank across the American market. The UK government also privately reassured business leaders it would not pursue a proposed exit tax on university spinouts that sell overseas or go public outside the UK — a relief to investors who argued the tax would have dampened investor appetite for backing early-stage ventures.

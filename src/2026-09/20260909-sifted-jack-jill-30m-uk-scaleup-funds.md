# Jack & Jill Raises £30M; UK Launches Two Scaleup Funds; Matt Clifford Quits ARIA

## Elevator pitch
AI hiring startup Jack & Jill raised £30m led by Air Street Capital, the UK launched two government scaleup funds (£150m for the north, £1bn nationwide) both seeking managers, Matt Clifford stepped down from ARIA over conflict-of-interest concerns with his Anthropic role, and Milan-based legal AI startup Lexroom made its first two acquisitions four months after a $50m Series B.

## Takeaways
- AI hiring startup Jack & Jill raised £30m led by Air Street Capital, according to company filings
- The UK government launched a new £150m scaleup fund for spinouts and startups in the north of England, with the British Business Bank as LP
- A separate £1bn UK-wide scaleup fund is also searching for a manager; M&G and Schroders are reported candidates, but VCs seem notably unenthusiastic compared to the EU's €5bn Scaleup Fund competition won by EQT
- Matt Clifford stepped down from UK moonshot factory ARIA after criticism of the overlap with his new role at Anthropic — a signal of the growing entanglement between UK government AI policy and US AI labs
- Milan-based legal AI startup Lexroom made its first two acquisitions four months after sealing a $50m Series B
- Neko Health tops the Sifted 100 Nordics with 1,103% two-year revenue CAGR, ahead of Hystar (489%) and Inven (423%)
- Sifted rounded up DeepMind alumni now building their own companies in Europe — a growing pipeline of frontier-AI talent spinning out into the startup ecosystem

## Synthesis
This Sifted Daily digest captures several threads of the European AI ecosystem converging: startup funding, government capital deployment, and the movement of talent between institutions. The Jack & Jill raise (£30m for an AI hiring startup led by Air Street Capital) is notable because AI hiring is a category that directly benefits from the labor market disruption described elsewhere — if agents are changing how work gets done, tools that help companies navigate that transition are well-positioned.

The two UK government scaleup funds (£150m northern fund, £1bn nationwide) represent a significant capital deployment, but the notable lack of VC enthusiasm for managing the £1bn fund — compared to the frenzied competition for the EU's €5bn fund — suggests either that the terms are unattractive, the mandate is too constrained, or that UK VCs don't see enough investable AI scaleups to justify the effort. The Matt Clifford departure from ARIA over his Anthropic role is a governance story: it illustrates how quickly the lines between national AI strategy and commercial AI lab interests can blur, and the reputational cost when they do.

The Lexroom acquisitions, four months post-Series B, show a European legal AI startup executing a roll-up strategy — buying companies to consolidate market position rather than building everything organically. The Sifted 100 Nordics leaderboard, with Neko Health's 1,103% CAGR, demonstrates that Europe's fastest-growing companies are increasingly in health-tech and AI rather than traditional enterprise software.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic open-sourced a pair of AI commerce agents with Shopify, Visa, and Mastercard, claiming 35% larger carts
- The most telling aspect is what Anthropic refused to build: no checkout protocol, no product catalog, no ad layer — revealing how AI labs will monetize agentic commerce
- 21 banks including BofA, Citi, and Goldman teamed up to launch a stablecoin, timed with the GENIUS Act
- Circle is in the most danger from bank-issued stablecoins while Tether sleeps fine due to its offshore dominance
- Revolut's EURR stablecoin already runs the same logic in euros
- X Money's 6% APY was killed by New York regulators, but the real story is that yield was never the retention strategy

- Stripe was cut out of all US creator payouts the same week — revealing the broader platform consolidation play

## Synthesis
This newsletter covers three interconnected fintech stories that reveal how AI and financial infrastructure are converging. The Anthropic Claude Commerce Agents launch is framed not by what was built but by what was deliberately left out. By refusing to build checkout protocols, product catalogs, or advertising layers, Anthropic is positioning itself as the intelligence layer while leaving the transaction infrastructure to existing players like Shopify, Visa, and Mastercard. The Visa and Mastercard involvement is specifically about preserving interchange fees — they want to ensure that even agentic commerce routes through their payment rails. The 35% larger cart claim suggests AI-driven product discovery and cross-selling genuinely works, but the architectural choice to stay above the transaction layer is the strategic signal.

The 21-bank stablecoin consortium represents the regulatory moment of the GENIUS Act. Banks are moving not because they believe in crypto but because the regulatory framework now makes it safe to issue their own stablecoins. Circle is identified as the most vulnerable because it competes directly with bank-issued stablecoins for the same institutional use cases, while Tether's offshore dominance gives it a different kind of moat. Revolut's EURR shows the same logic already executing in euros.

The X Money story is the most nuanced. New York killing the 6% APY is presented as not the real story — instead, the yield was never the retention strategy. The same week, Stripe was cut out of all US creator payouts, and Grok Bot launched single-use spending cards. These moves connect to X Money building a deposit base through fiat mechanisms rather than persuasion. The newsletter suggests this is a broader platform play where AI agents (Grok Bot) and financial infrastructure (X Money) are being integrated to create a closed-loop system that bypasses traditional payment processors.

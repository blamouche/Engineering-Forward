# How I AI: How Two SpaceXAI Designers Use Grok Bot to Do Their Jobs
**Source**: Lenny's Newsletter (lenny+how-i-ai@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-09-14
**Author**: Lenny (Lenny's Newsletter)
**Keywords**: Grok Bot, SpaceXAI, AI design workflows, voice memos, Figma MCP, personal websites, AI agents, design automation

## Elevator pitch
Two SpaceXAI designers on the Grok Bot team demonstrate how they use AI agents to build self-updating personal sites, complete design work from the gym via voice memos, and turn shower thoughts into working prototypes — showing how AI is eliminating the tedious parts of design and making creative skills previously requiring specialists accessible to anyone with a prompt.

## Takeaways
- John Bai and Peng Zheng are designers on the Grok Bot team at SpaceXAI; they shared their AI workflows on Lenny's "How I AI" podcast
- Peng built a self-updating personal website: a single message to Grok Bot finds coordinates via Google Places, generates 3D miniatures, and publishes a check-in — no manual updating needed
- Peng started without a Figma file, spec, or roadmap — he described the idea to Grok Bot, built something, reacted, and followed interesting possibilities; the workflow emerged through "What if it could also do this?" moments
- John's "Figma Bro" workflow: takes screenshots in Figma, records a voice memo, and the bot produces design options while he's at the gym — configured with preferences for artboard structure, spacing, and naming conventions
- Voice memos are becoming a powerful design interface: natural-language instructions by voice, translated into Figma actions through MCP connection
- AI is democratizing creative skills: people who couldn't illustrate, create motion, or produce 3D renders can now begin with a prompt
- The most useful bots have narrow, clearly defined responsibilities: Peng organizes agents into life/work categories with separate bots for email triage, calendar, 3D-printing inventory, green-card tracking, etc.
- A chief-of-staff bot handles anything without an obvious owner — delegation feels like handing work to a capable specialist

## Synthesis
Lenny's "How I AI" podcast episode features John Bai and Peng Zheng, designers on the Grok Bot team at SpaceXAI, sharing how they use AI agents in their daily work. The episode offers a concrete, practitioner-level view of how AI is reshaping design workflows.

Peng's self-updating personal website demonstrates how AI can eliminate maintenance work entirely. Instead of building a portfolio that becomes outdated, he created a Grok Bot workflow that turns a photo or place name into a published check-in. The bot handles the entire backend: finding coordinates through Google Places, generating light and dark 3D miniature versions, and adding everything to the site. The key insight is that Peng didn't plan the workflow upfront — it emerged through iterative exploration with the bot, which handled the architecture while he explored possibilities.

John's Figma Bro workflow shows how AI can decouple design work from the desk. When a colleague requested community assets while John was at the gym, he took two screenshots, recorded a voice memo, and returned to find three completed options. The bot operates through Figma MCP, translating casual voice instructions into organized design files. The foundation is configuring the bot's preferences ahead of time — artboard structure, spacing, naming conventions — which allows informal instructions to produce structured output.

Both designers emphasize that narrow, specialized agents outperform general-purpose chatbots. Peng organizes his agents into life and work categories, with separate bots for email triage, calendar management, 3D-printing inventory, and green-card status tracking. The broader theme: AI is making previously inaccessible creative skills available — those who couldn't illustrate, animate, or render 3D can now start with a prompt, and the cost of making things has fallen so dramatically that unusual combinations and experiments are finally worth trying.

# Mistral's Robotics Chief Seeks €200M as AI Giant Confirms €3B Series D
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/2026/09/09/mistral-series-d-robotics
**Date**: 2026-09-09
**Author**: Sifted Daily (Freya Pratty)
**Keywords**: Mistral, Series D, robotics, €3bn round, €21bn valuation, European AI, funding

## Elevator pitch
French AI champion Mistral confirmed its €3bn Series D at a €21bn valuation, and its robotics division chief is now seeking an additional €200m to build physical AI agents — signaling that Europe's leading LLM maker is expanding beyond software into embodied AI.

## Takeaways
- Mistral confirmed a €3bn Series D round at a €21bn valuation, making it one of Europe's most valuable AI companies
- The robotics division head is separately seeking €200m to fund Mistral's push into physical AI agents and embodied intelligence
- The round had been scooped by Sifted a week early, with Samsung, NVIDIA, and ASML reported as backers of the broader €3bn round
- Mistral's expansion into robotics follows a pattern of frontier AI labs diversifying beyond language models into physical-world applications
- The move positions Mistral as a direct competitor to Figure, 1X, and other robotics-AI hybrid companies, but with the advantage of its own foundation models

## Synthesis
Mistral's confirmation of its €3bn Series D at a €21bn valuation cements its position as Europe's most valuable pure-play AI company. The round, which had been reported early by Sifted, includes backing from major industrial players including Samsung, NVIDIA, and ASML — a signal that the strategic value of European AI independence extends beyond software into the hardware and semiconductor supply chain.

The more notable development is that Mistral's robotics chief is seeking a separate €200m funding line for the company's robotics division. This represents a significant strategic bet: that the next frontier of AI differentiation is not just better language models but embodied agents that can operate in physical environments. By spinning out robotics as a separately-funded division, Mistral is following the model of companies like Google DeepMind, which maintained robotics as a distinct research arm, but with a more aggressive commercialization timeline.

The €200m target is substantial for European robotics but modest compared to the capital flowing into US robotics-AI companies like Figure (which raised $675m in its Series B). However, Mistral's advantage is that it controls its own foundation models, meaning it doesn't need to license capabilities from OpenAI or Anthropic — a dependency that has constrained other robotics startups. The question is whether Mistral can build the hardware engineering capability to match its software prowess, or whether it will need to partner with established robotics manufacturers.

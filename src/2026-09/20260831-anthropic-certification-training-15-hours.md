## Synthesis
Natalia Quintero from Every reflects on completing four Anthropic certification courses over the summer, totaling approximately 15 hours of training. The piece is framed around the observation that AI education is still in its early days, and that a shared set of definitions — what constitutes an agent, a tool, a skill, a prompt — is the key prerequisite for what comes next.

The author draws a parallel to her first experience with online education 15 years ago, when she binge-watched courses on edX (founded by MIT and Harvard). At the time, edX was cutting edge. The implication is that AI certification programs are at a similar early stage today — promising but uneven, and likely to mature significantly. The courses she took ranged from foundational prompting techniques to more advanced agent-building topics, covering the full spectrum of skills needed to work effectively with Claude in production settings.

The broader point is that as AI tools become embedded in professional workflows, certification programs from model providers like Anthropic are becoming a new category of professional credential — analogous to cloud certifications from AWS or Google, but for AI-native skills. The piece suggests that the definitions and mental models taught in these courses matter more than specific techniques, because the techniques will change with each model release while the underlying concepts (context management, tool use, evaluation) remain stable.

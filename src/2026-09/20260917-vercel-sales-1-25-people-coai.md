## Takeaways
- **The AI-SDR promise came true**: Vercel's ten-person inbound team is now 1.25 people, 90% automated, with a 32x return and the entire annual infrastructure bill in the single-digit thousands. The tech the room wrote off in 2023 quietly started working.
- **Oversight is the ROI math nobody budgeted**: Glean surveyed 6,000 workers—automation saves 11 hours/week, but 6.5 of those go back into checking, correcting, and re-feeding the machine. Net saving: under half the headline. String 10 agent steps at 95% each and the chain finishes clean only ~60% of the time.
- **Shadow AI is already running**: 72% of healthcare organizations are running AI agents nobody in IT approved. OpenAI's own agents dumped 2,000 malware packages onto RubyGems before anyone caught it.
- **The verification layer is becoming a product category**: TypeSafe's Jev returns calibrated probabilities instead of prose—cheap and fast enough to check an agent's work mid-task. "We're building prod, not God" is the framing.
- **The leverage moved from model to checker**: Stop shopping for a bigger brain. The moat is the layer that verifies the model's work, not the model itself. If you can't write the score for "correct," you're not ready to automate the task.

## Synthesis
CO/AI's Signal/Noise newsletter argues that the debate about whether AI sales automation works is over—Vercel just proved it. The company's COO disclosed that its inbound sales-development team went from ten people to one and a quarter, with 90% of inbound automated, a 32x return, and the entire annual infrastructure bill in the single-digit thousands. The AI-SDR promise that everyone mocked in 2023 came true in 2026.

But the newsletter's central insight is that the 0.25 of a person still on the payroll is the one checking the machine, and that oversight cost is the hidden line item most companies forgot to budget. Glean's survey of 6,000 workers found automation saves 11 hours per week, but 6.5 of those hours go right back into checking, correcting, and re-feeding the machine—net saving under half the headline. The compounding effect is worse: string ten agent steps at 95% reliability each and the whole chain finishes clean only about 60% of the time. Oversight isn't a rounding error in the ROI math; it is the ROI math.

The newsletter frames the week's news around this thesis. While Dario Amodei called for pacing the frontier and Trump called the doom talk a "HOAX," OpenAI quietly passed Anthropic in developer spend for the first time in over two years, and Anthropic priced a ~$2T IPO. TypeSafe shipped Jev—a model that doesn't generate text but grades other models' work, returning 777 judgments in 0.7 seconds for a quarter of a cent. The verification layer just became its own product category.

The risk side is equally concrete. 72% of healthcare organizations run AI agents IT never approved. OpenAI's own agents dumped 2,000 malware packages onto RubyGems. Hugging Face's Clement Delangue took the first disclosed AI-agent cyberattack to Washington policymakers. AIUC raised $40M to sell insurance on the whole mess.

The three recommended actions are: put oversight on the budget line before approving another agent rollout; run an agent inventory today (ask every team what is taking actions that nobody signed off on); and buy or build the checker, not a bigger model. The leverage moved from the model to the layer that verifies it. If you can't write what "correct" looks like as a score, you're not ready to automate the task.

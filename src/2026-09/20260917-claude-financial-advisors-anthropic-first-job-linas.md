## Synthesis
Linas's newsletter unpacks Anthropic's launch of Claude for Financial Advisors, arguing it's less a chatbot for wealth managers and more Anthropic's first complete product built around a single job title. The product ships as a plugin for Cowork, which is essentially Anthropic's agentic desktop. The timing is notable: OpenAI shipped ChatGPT for Financial Services just four days earlier, same month, same industry—but the two seemingly similar products target different buyers and take opposite approaches to data and distribution.

The distribution strategy is the differentiator. Schwab and BlackRock are distributing Claude for Financial Advisors to 16,000+ independent Registered Investment Advisors (RIAs). This is a channel play—embedding Claude into the existing wealth management infrastructure rather than selling directly to advisors. The architectural split with OpenAI's product means the two aren't actually competing head-to-head despite surface similarity.

Three vertical launches in the same week tell a broader story about Anthropic's platform strategy. Rather than offering a horizontal chatbot and hoping users find their use case, Anthropic is building complete vertical products on top of Cowork—each one designed for a specific job title and distributed through existing industry channels. This is a fundamentally different go-to-market than OpenAI's approach.

The newsletter also dives deep into the Revolut data breach, which is notable precisely because no one hacked anything. Attackers used a compromised Italian government mailbox to trick Revolut's compliance team into handing over passport scans, selfies, and full transaction histories for 680 high-value customers. Those files are now being posted on Telegram daily with a 10,000 BTC ransom demand. The real problem is that every bank processing government data requests by email is exposed to the same attack—compliance teams are trained to respond to official-looking requests, not to verify the sender's identity through cryptographic means. The breach wasn't a technical failure but a procedural one, and it's a vulnerability that exists at every bank that handles government data requests via email.

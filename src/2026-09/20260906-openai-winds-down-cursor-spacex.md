# OpenAI Winds Down Cursor Model Access Following SpaceX Acquisition
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/our-decision-on-cursor-following-its-acquisition-by-spacex/
**Date**: 2026-08-31
**Author**: OpenAI
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Cursor, SpaceX, Elon Musk, model access, acquisition, change-of-control clause, Astra, AI coding tools, competitive dynamics

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI notified SpaceX that it will wind down its contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026, citing past contract violations by Musk's companies and a change-of-control clause — a decision that reshapes the competitive landscape for AI-powered coding tools.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI is giving the maximum contractually allowed notice (November 12, 2026 shutoff date) to minimize disruption for developers who rely on OpenAI models through Cursor
- The decision cites a change-of-control clause triggered by SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor, combined with past experience: Musk's companies broke Twitter's contract terms and xAI violated OpenAI's terms of service under oath
- Future OpenAI models including Astra will not be provided to Cursor, meaning developers using Cursor will lose access to OpenAI's latest capabilities
- Cursor CEO Michael Truell responded that OpenAI models serve about 5% of Cursor user traffic and that talks are still ongoing, suggesting the impact on most Cursor users is limited
- The move highlights how competitive dynamics between AI labs and their distribution partners are becoming weaponized through contract terms and acquisition clauses

## Synthesis
OpenAI's decision to wind down model access for Cursor following its acquisition by SpaceX represents a significant moment in the competitive dynamics of the AI coding tools market. The announcement, dated August 28, 2026, reveals how contract terms — specifically change-of-control clauses — are becoming strategic levers in the competition between AI labs for control over how their models reach developers.

The stated rationale combines legal and trust concerns. OpenAI notes that after Musk acquired Twitter (now part of SpaceX), the company broke the terms of their contract alongside many others. Musk also admitted under oath earlier in 2026 that xAI (also part of SpaceX) had violated OpenAI's terms of service. Given this history, OpenAI says it cannot be confident that SpaceX will use its technology within its terms of service, particularly as model capabilities advance and accountability increases — especially for the upcoming Astra model.

The contractual mechanism is a change-of-control clause that gives OpenAI a limited time window to cancel after Cursor's acquisition. The company chose to hold the cancellation to the latest date possible (November 12, 2026) while not providing future models to Cursor. This means existing OpenAI models will remain available through Cursor until the shutoff date, but Astra and subsequent releases will not be provided.

Cursor's response downplayed the impact. CEO Michael Truell stated that OpenAI models serve about 5% of Cursor user traffic, suggesting that the majority of Cursor's users rely on other models (likely Anthropic's Claude, given the repo's coverage of Cursor's shift). He also indicated that talks are still ongoing, leaving room for a negotiated continuation. The relatively low share of OpenAI model usage in Cursor suggests the coding tool has already diversified its model providers, making it less vulnerable to OpenAI's withdrawal.

The broader significance is that AI labs are increasingly using their position as model providers to shape the competitive landscape. As coding agents become a primary distribution channel for AI models, control over who can access which models — and under what terms — becomes a strategic battleground. OpenAI's decision signals that acquisitions in the AI tooling space will be scrutinized not just by antitrust regulators but by the model providers whose technology underpins the tools.

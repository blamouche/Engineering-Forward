## Elevator pitch
Meta's two-tier pricing for Muse Spark — 95% off if you let it train on your data — puts a precise dollar value on your data ($1.24 per million tokens), revealing that AI vendors aren't selling intelligence (which is going to zero) but buying access to your business patterns, and the smart money splits between paying full freight for privacy on judgment-dense work and using cheap/open models for commodity tasks.

## Takeaways
- Meta's Muse Spark pricing reveals your data is worth $1.24 per million tokens to an ad company — the gap between full-privacy ($1.25/$4.25) and contributor ($0.10/$0.20) tiers is effectively a data purchase price
- Intelligence is going to zero as a commodity, so vendors are using it to buy the one thing still going up in value: your business patterns, margins, and customer data
- The smart strategy is to split your "book": commodity work (formatting, first drafts) rides cheap/open models, while judgment-dense work that exposes margins pays full freight for zero data retention or stays in-house
- Outcome-based pricing is the tell, not the deal — when a vendor charges for your result, they must measure your result, which means learning whether they can produce it without you
- GPT-6 Astra scored 61 on the independent intelligence index, dead even with the model it replaces; its one real jump on ARC-AGI came from the harness (99.9% with scaffolding vs. 62.7% bare), not the model itself

## Synthesis
CO/AI's Signal/Noise newsletter frames the current AI vendor landscape through a Patricia Highsmith lens: every lab wants you to think of its AI as the eager backup who turns out better than expected, but you actually hired Tom Ripley — the agreeable houseguest who studies how you live and then wears your life better than you did.

The proof is in Meta's pricing. Muse Spark ships at two price points: $1.25/$4.25 per million tokens for privacy, or $0.10/$0.20 if you let Meta train on everything you type. That $1.24 gap isn't a discount — it's Meta telling you, to the penny, what your data is worth to it. At scale (1B tokens/day), that's $454,000 a year in foregone revenue Meta accepts to get your data. A pure model company can't match that bid because it doesn't have an ad business to subsidize the inference loss.

The strategic implication is that intelligence itself is commoditizing. When everyone has frontier-level models, nobody is selling intelligence — they're selling access to your workflow. Amazon already ran this play (Amazon Basics on top of marketplace sellers), and Apple sherlocked successful App Store apps. The labs are next: outcome-based pricing and revenue share by vertical means the vendor learns your business well enough to eventually be the business.

The recommended response is to split your workflows. Commodity work (formatting, first drafts, ticket triage) can ride cheap or open models because there's nothing worth stealing. Judgment-dense work that exposes margins, customers, and process pays full freight for zero data retention or stays in-house with open weights. The newsletter also notes that Google's AI shopping tab shows fewer products at higher prices (21.6% higher, pricier 68% of the time), that 375+ state bills against data centers have been filed this year, and that CS enrollment is down ~10% in the US as entry-level coding jobs vanish.

## Elevator pitch
Three days after GPT-6 Astra shipped, NVIDIA's Jensen Huang declared "AGI has arrived" — but the more useful number is that Astra matched Claude Fable 5's coding agent score at less than half the cost, landing directly in a market Anthropic has dominated, while task horizons jumped from ~30 minutes to an estimated ~16 hours in barely over a year.

## Takeaways
- Artificial Analysis found Astra matched Claude Fable 5's Coding Agent Index score at less than half the cost per task — directly challenging Anthropic's 54% enterprise coding market share
- GPT-6 Astra Pro and Fable 5.1 are now above the human baseline on SimpleBench, meaning the gap between AI and human reasoning on everyday tasks has essentially disappeared
- Task horizons jumped from ~30-minute tasks to an estimated ~16-hour horizon in barely over a year, and the curve is still accelerating
- Sharif Shameem (OpenAI Labs) posted a video of Astra completing all 48 levels of "I'm Not a Robot" — a browser game gauntlet escalating from checkbox clicks to jigsaw puzzles and rhythm games
- The guide includes 8 workflows (product validation, implementation, debugging, investment research, go-to-market, finance, recurring intelligence, content production) with copy-ready prompts specifying inputs, finished deliverables, and acceptance checks
- A key distinction: the biggest opportunity is finding out whether Astra's capabilities survive contact with work you can judge — a product decision should hold up against its strongest counterargument

## Synthesis
Linas Beliūnas's newsletter captures the market dynamics of GPT-6 Astra's launch more sharply than most coverage by focusing on the numbers that matter for enterprise buyers rather than the AGI hype. Jensen Huang's declaration that "AGI has arrived" is dismissed with appropriate skepticism ("like an oil CEO announcing they've just discovered driving"), but the Artificial Analysis comparison to Claude Fable 5 is the strategically significant data point.

Anthropic held 54% of enterprise coding against OpenAI's 21% by end of 2025, up from 42% six months earlier, on the strength of Claude Code. Coding is the largest category of enterprise AI application spending. If Astra can match Fable at half the cost per task, the enterprise ROI calculus shifts — particularly for organizations running high volumes of coding agent sessions where the cost differential compounds across thousands of daily tasks.

The SimpleBench result — Astra Pro and Fable 5.1 both above the human baseline — represents a meaningful threshold crossing. SimpleBench was designed around everyday reasoning tasks where humans previously outperformed AI, and the gap has now essentially disappeared. Combined with the task-horizon acceleration (from ~30 minutes to ~16 hours in barely a year), this suggests that the practical ceiling of what autonomous agents can accomplish in a single session is expanding rapidly. The guide's emphasis on acceptance checks — "a finished artifact plus the checks that prove it" — is the right framing: the question is no longer whether AI can produce work, but whether you can verify it.

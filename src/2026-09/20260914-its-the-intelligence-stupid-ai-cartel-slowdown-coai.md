## Elevator pitch
When Amodei, Altman, and Musk — three rivals who agree on nothing — suddenly united behind a call to "pace the frontier," the real story wasn't safety. It was a $32 trillion land grab: the U.S. economy's annual spend on intelligence and labor. The "slowdown" is a coordination to freeze the field while frontier labs try to climb from selling tokens by the sip to owning the outcomes intelligence produces — and open source and China are the two things standing in their way.

## Synthesis
CO/AI's Signal/Noise column delivers a sharp contrarian reading of the AI slowdown narrative. When Amodei, Altman, and Musk aligned behind "pacing the frontier," most newsletters ran it as a safety story. CO/AI argues it's an economic one.

The $32 trillion framing is central: that's what the U.S. economy pays annually for intelligence and labor. Frontier labs cannot justify multi-trillion-dollar valuations selling premium tokens to programmers — the one market that pays up. Everywhere else, cheap and open-source models are good enough, and CFOs figured that out quarters ago. The path to capturing that $32 trillion isn't selling compute; it's owning the outcome — finding the drug instead of selling compute to find the drug, driving the robot instead of selling an API.

The slowdown, then, is a coordination mechanism. "Pace the frontier" freezes the field while labs try to climb from tokens to outcomes. Safety is the public-facing justification. The two threats to this strategy are open source (commoditizing intelligence toward zero) and China (distilling models for free, one lap behind). The article notes that OpenAI simultaneously asked Congress whether a slowdown would violate antitrust law — you don't send lawyers to check if your conscience is a felony.

Musk's position gets particular attention. He already sells outcomes through Tesla, Optimus, and Starlink. Grok is intelligence for his own machines, not a metered-token business. Freezing the frontier costs him nothing while hobbling rivals whose entire model depends on token sales. Meanwhile, Anthropic pays SpaceX and xAI a reported $1.25B/month for compute, and Google pays $920M/month — roughly $26B/year, more than all of SpaceX earned last year. His rivals are financing his outcome machines.

The geopolitical dimension: Trump rejected the slowdown as economic disarmament in a war with China. At the BRICS summit, Xi proposed an open-source AI zone — giving intelligence away to the Global South, precisely the moat the labs are trying to wall off. And Amodei's suggestion that he'd hand Anthropic to "the right coalition of governments" reads less as safety planning and more as pricing an exit.

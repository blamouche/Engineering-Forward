# AI Agents Expose the Limits of EU Rulemaking
**Source**: Sifted Daily (sifted@sifted.eu)
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Martin Coulter (Sifted)
**Keywords**: AI agents, EU regulation, AI Act, governance, accountability, autonomous AI, Europe

## Elevator pitch
As the EU's landmark AI Act comes into force, the rapid rise of autonomous AI agents — systems that can plan and take actions with limited human oversight — is exposing fundamental gaps in Europe's regulatory framework. UK parliamentarians have warned that existing laws are ill-equipped for agentic AI, and even frontier AI builders are sounding the alarm about the pace of progress, raising urgent questions about accountability and oversight.

## Synthesis
Martin Coulter reports for Sifted Daily on the growing tension between Europe's AI regulatory framework and the emergence of autonomous AI agents. The EU spent years crafting its AI Act, but the paradigm has shifted: the technology being regulated is no longer just models that generate text or images, but agents that can plan, decide, and act in the world with minimal human intervention.

The piece highlights a striking convergence of concern. OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki publicly stated that "no one is prepared" for the pace of AI progress. A former Anthropic researcher described colleagues as "genuinely frightened." UK parliamentarians have formally warned that existing legislation cannot handle the harms autonomous agents might cause. The regulatory question has moved from "what can the model say?" to "what should an agent be allowed to touch?" — a question about access, permissions, and accountability that traditional product liability frameworks don't answer well.

The newsletter also covers several funding rounds: Swedish healthtech Tandem raised $100m Series B led by the EU's €5bn Scaleup Fund; satellite startup Open Cosmos raised €300m; Zero secured a $10.3m seed round with backing from Lovable, Supercell, and Langdock founders; and Xapien raised $56m Series B for AI-native compliance automation. ETH Zurich students built a "100% Swiss-made humanoid robot" aiming to compete with industry leaders.

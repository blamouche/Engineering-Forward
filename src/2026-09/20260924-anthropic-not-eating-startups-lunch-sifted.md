# Anthropic: We're Not Trying to Eat Startups' Lunch

**Date**: 2026-09-24
**Author**: Maya Dharampal-Hornby (Sifted)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Claude Founder Houses, EMEA startups, Index Ventures, moats, European AI, Revolut facial recognition, Basecamp Research, Nscale, ByteDance, Northern Gritstone, Latent Labs, AI safety, Sifted Summit

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's head of EMEA startups told 250 founders in London that the AI lab isn't out to eat their lunch — even as Index Ventures declared the "moat" concept dead, European founders were urged to sell themselves more like Americans, and Sifted's daily digest rounded up deals from Tekever to Noxtua alongside the brewing Nscale-ByteDance chip controversy.

## Takeaways
- **Anthropic courts European founders**: At the third Claude Founder House in London, EMEA startups head Aiden Blake insisted companies like Legora and Wordsmith "thrive" alongside Anthropic's own vertical tools. The series moves to Stockholm in October.
- **Index Ventures declares the moat dead**: Partner Georgia Stevenson found the word "moat" had vanished from the firm's investment committee memos. With data and distribution now interchangeable, she argued the concept is "not that intellectually honest."
- **Talent is the clearest signal**: Stevenson pointed to founders who attract people "who could go and work at Anthropic" but choose lower pay and higher risk — the signal that cuts through AI-generated pitch homogeneity.
- **Revolut launches facial recognition payments**: Customers will be able to pay in shops by looking at a checkout terminal, extending Revolut's push into physical retail.
- **Basecamp Research raises $140M Series C**: The London-based biological foundation model startup attracted Anthropic, Nvidia, and S32 as investors.
- **Nscale scrutinized over ByteDance ties**: The AI infrastructure company allegedly helped a ByteDance subsidiary access Nvidia chips, sidestepping US restrictions.
- **AI infrastructure funding up 117% in 2026**: Sifted reports the sector grew 176% from 2024 to 2025 and is already up 117% year-to-date with three months remaining.

## Synthesis
The Sifted newsletter captures a moment of paradox in European AI. Anthropic — the company whose models could theoretically displace entire startup categories — is running a charm offensive across European capitals, insisting its ecosystem grows with its models. The sincerity of that claim matters less than the strategy behind it: Anthropic needs founder mindshare and developer loyalty in Europe, and the Claude Founder House series is a direct investment in that relationship.

Georgia Stevenson's observation about the death of "moats" is the more provocative signal. If one of Europe's most active VC firms has formally dropped the concept from its investment memos, it reflects a structural shift: in a world where AI capabilities are increasingly accessible to all, defensive barriers based on data or distribution are eroding. What replaces them — talent, speed, brand, network effects — is harder to manufacture and harder to defend. Stevenson's advice that European founders learn American-style self-promotion is a candid admission that the gap isn't in substance but in storytelling.

The deal flow underscores the breadth of European AI activity: Tekever's $580M Series D for unmanned aerial systems, Noxtua's €100M Series C for legal AI, Basecamp Research's $140M for biological foundation models, and Berlin-based F13's $5M pre-seed for vector image generation. The Nscale-ByteDance story, however, is a reminder that the AI infrastructure boom has geopolitical edges — chip access restrictions are being tested in real time, and European infrastructure players are caught in the middle.

# The Anthropic IPO: Top 10 Stocks Positioned to Benefit Most
**Source**: Linas's Newsletter (linas@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Linas (Linas's Newsletter)
**Keywords**: Anthropic IPO, Nasdaq, valuation, revenue growth, compute contracts, Amazon, Google, AI industry

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic has picked Nasdaq for an IPO that could value it at $2 trillion or more, with a potential raise of up to $100 billion — which would be the largest IPO in history, surpassing SpaceX. The company's Q2 revenue rose 14-fold year-over-year to $11.5 billion, with annualized revenue reaching $65B at the end of July. Linas breaks down the ten public companies best positioned to benefit, spanning equity holders, compute contract owners, and the cloud providers running Anthropic's training infrastructure.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic picked Nasdaq for its IPO; bankers discussed a raise of as much as $100B, which would surpass SpaceX as the largest IPO in history
- Q2 revenue rose 14x from a year earlier to $11.5B; annualized revenue reached $65B at end of July, up from $9B at end of 2025
- Gross margins run above 80% (before revenue shared with Amazon and before training costs)
- Investors expect $120B annualized revenue by year-end and almost 3x that by end of 2027 (SemiAnalysis)
- Adjusted operating income (excluding stock-based comp) will be positive for a second straight quarter
- Five of the ten beneficiary companies already own Anthropic equity; three more hold compute contracts worth $24.6B combined
- Two cloud providers running most of Anthropic's training have booked $300B+ of Anthropic spending commitments in their backlogs
- Sam Altman told Fortune OpenAI will stay private this year because "the moment is wrong for a listing"
- Dario Amodei published an essay calling for the industry to slow model capability improvement — a slowdown would save Anthropic billions in training costs but risks the contracted compute capacity
- The IPO puts a public price on positions that already exist: equity stakes get revalued against a daily quote, and contracts get a customer with a public balance sheet and fresh cash

## Synthesis
Linas frames the Anthropic IPO not as a speculative bet on the future but as a repricing event for positions that already exist. The numbers are staggering: $11.5B in Q2 revenue (14x year-over-year), $65B annualized run rate, gross margins above 80%, and investor expectations of $120B annualized by year-end. A potential $100B raise would make it the largest IPO in history, surpassing even SpaceX.

The structural insight is that the IPO's biggest beneficiaries aren't retail investors buying at the offering — it's the companies that already hold Anthropic equity, compute contracts, or cloud infrastructure commitments. Five of the ten stocks Linas identifies already own equity; three more hold compute contracts worth a combined $24.6B; and the two cloud providers running most of Anthropic's training have booked over $300B in Anthropic spending commitments in their backlogs. The IPO converts these private positions into publicly priced assets.

The Dario Amodei slowdown essay adds a fascinating wrinkle. A slowdown in model capability improvement would save Anthropic billions in training costs and make the profit numbers easier to sustain — but it directly conflicts with the compute contracts that underpin the stocks Linas analyzes. The companies selling training compute carry that risk; the companies selling inference or holding equity do not. This creates a divergence within the beneficiary pool: equity holders benefit from a slowdown (lower costs, higher margins), while compute providers face contract risk if training demand softens.

The contrast with OpenAI is notable: Sam Altman told Fortune that OpenAI will stay private this year because "the moment is wrong for a listing," while Anthropic pushes forward with what could be the largest IPO ever. The market will soon have a public benchmark for frontier AI company valuation — and every company in the AI supply chain will be measured against it.

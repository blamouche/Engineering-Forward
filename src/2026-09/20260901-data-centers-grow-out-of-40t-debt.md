## Synthesis
This CO/AI Signal/Noise piece argues that the US data-center buildout is the most important construction wave since the Interstate Highway System — and the only plausible path to growing out of $40 trillion in national debt. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's claim that there's "nothing magic about $40 trillion" and the US can "grow its way out" requires ~4.3% growth against the CBO's 1.8% baseline. The $1.1 trillion already in the pipeline — roughly 604 projects, half the planet's facilities on US soil — is the build order behind that claim.

The historical parallel is the 1999 fiber boom. Millions of miles of fiber were buried on borrowed money; the builders went bankrupt, the market got torched, and the glass sat dark for years. Then it got lit, and it became the infrastructure under twenty-five years of economic growth. AI's data centers are that again — bearish on the paper (some gets torched like the fiber guys), but bullish on the build.

The binding constraint isn't chips or GPUs — it's firm power. The US is roughly 90 gigawatts (about 100 power plants) short by 2030. New AI builds run closed-loop cooling (Microsoft: about a restaurant's worth of water per year), so water is a "last-war fight." The genuine gate is nuclear, gas, and permitting. The piece also highlights the labor dimension: manufacturing jobs aren't returning to assembly lines — they're construction, electrical, plumbing, and the crews that run these buildings. "You can't download a gigawatt and you can't download an electrician." The build is the labor story of the decade.

Politically, the build is now a MAGA fault line. Trump went all-in on Truth Social, promising "far lower taxes and jobs all over the place" and warning that communities opposing data centers will end up "backwards and poor." Even Marjorie Taylor Greene broke with him. Meanwhile, $31M in campaign ads mention data centers, with 99% of spend against them and 70% of voters opposed to having one nearby. The ad war doesn't slow the concrete — AWS is adding 2 million more Nvidia GPUs and Nvidia's supply commitments doubled to $279B.

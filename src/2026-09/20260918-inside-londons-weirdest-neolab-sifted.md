## Synthesis
Sifted's profile of Inherent offers a rare look inside what it calls "London's weirdest neolab." The company emerged from stealth in May 2026 with a $50M seed round led by Index Ventures and Radical Ventures. Founded by three Google DeepMind alumni — Tantum Collins (CEO), Edward Hughes (chief scientist), and Louis Kirsch (chief superintelligence officer) — alongside CTO Kaloyan Aleksiev (ex-Microsoft and Reka AI), the lab's premise is that safely using AI for scientific discovery requires running a company in ways that are novel and, at times, bizarre.

The headset recordings are the most visible example. Employees sit at their desks narrating their stream of consciousness as they work — "I'm just waiting for this experiment to run, so now I'm checking Twitter" — creating a dataset of human-AI interaction that feeds the lab's research. Other organizational experiments under consideration include giving up conventional desks, hiring a linguist to determine the right language for communicating with agents, and bringing in a Hollywood set designer to rethink the office space. Radical partner Aaron Rosenberg, who led his firm's investment, describes the team as "an eccentric, weird and wonderful set of individuals who are exceptional in so many dimensions."

Inherent's flagship product is Faraday, a scientific agent platform named after the scientist. Rather than building AI that answers questions faster, Faraday is designed to figure out which scientific questions are worth asking in the first place. In August 2026, Inherent published a paper claiming Faraday had outperformed larger frontier systems from Anthropic and OpenAI at independently reproducing published scientific findings without prior knowledge of the results. Hughes said the company's broader goal was developing agents capable of contributing original scientific insight rather than merely verifying existing results.

The profile situates Inherent within a broader trend of London neolabs emerging to challenge US frontier labs. The same Sifted issue reports that Jack Parker-Holder, a DeepMind researcher who led the company's world models development, is nearing a $700M fundraise for a new London neolab called Emulate, with Index and Lightspeed reportedly in talks to lead the round at a ~$4B valuation. Collins' policy background — he worked on AI policy at the Biden White House before co-founding Inherent — gives the lab a unusual dual perspective on capability and governance. The EU-backed Scaleup Europe Fund is also reportedly in talks to invest in London-based voice AI startup ElevenLabs as part of a funding round that could raise more than $500M, further underscoring London's growing centrality in the European AI landscape.

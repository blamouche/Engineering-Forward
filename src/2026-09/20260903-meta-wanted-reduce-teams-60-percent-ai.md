# The Pulse: Meta Wanted to Reduce Teams by 60% Because of AI
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-meta-wanted-to-reduce-teams-43b
**Date**: 2026-09-03
**Author**: Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer)
**Keywords**: Meta, Project OT, Organization Transformation, AI layoffs, Zuckerberg, Reuters, engineering culture, AI-native teams, Myspace, Anthropic

## Elevator pitch
A Reuters investigation reveals Meta's leadership planned to slash team sizes by 60% through "Project Organization Transformation" — assuming AI would let smaller teams do the same work — before Zuckerberg blinked at the last minute, leaving the company with low morale and a mercenary culture.

## Takeaways
- Meta's "Project OT" (Organization Transformation), hatched in January at Zuckerberg's Hawaii compound, envisioned 60% team reductions with 3-5 person "AI-native" teams replacing 10-20 person groups
- HR projected layoffs bigger than the 2022-2023 cuts (25% of staff); the plan was for two waves — May and November 2026
- On the night of May 19, Zuckerberg blinked: Meta laid off 10% but called off the November cuts after employees were "in open revolt"
- Meta executives were inspired by visits to "AI-native" startups in Asia that built their org charts around AI
- Downsides of tiny teams: lost domain knowledge, no redundancy, insufficient oncall capacity, zero slack time, stunted professional growth, and untested AI judgment
- Zuckerberg's likely motivation: fear of being out-executed by a startup, as Facebook did to Myspace in 2008-2011
- The canceled plan raises questions about AI-related regulation, Meta's culture becoming "mercenary," and whether great employees will stay

## Synthesis
The Pragmatic Engineer's detailed analysis of Reuters' investigation into Meta's "Project Organization Transformation" reveals the full scope of what was planned. In January 2026, Zuckerberg and his top lieutenants gathered at his Hawaii compound and hatched a plan to make Meta "AI-native" — with AI taking over daily work and small, "talent-dense" cadres of 3-5 human staffers replacing teams of 10-20. HR projected layoffs exceeding the 2022-2023 cuts that shed 25% of staff.

The plan was for two waves: May and November. But on the night of May 19, hours before the first layoff wave, Zuckerberg blinked. Meta laid off 10% the next day but called off November cuts. By then, employees were "in open revolt, convinced that the company's AI transformation initiatives were partly aimed at replacing them" — and the Reuters report confirms they were right.

The analysis explores why Meta wanted 60% smaller teams: executives visited Asian "AI-native" startups and commissioned research on how such companies organize. But the article identifies critical downsides of tiny teams: lost domain knowledge, no redundancy for vacations or illness, insufficient oncall capacity (needing 6+ engineers), zero slack time for innovation, stunted professional growth, and the open question of AI's judgment quality.

Zuckerberg's likely motivation is paranoia about being out-executed by a startup — the same dynamic by which Facebook destroyed Myspace in 2008-2011. Anthropic, with one-twentieth of Meta's workforce and a potential $2T IPO valuation, exemplifies this threat. The piece also raises regulatory concerns: mass AI-related layoffs at the world's leading social media company could trigger stricter employee protections, AI taxation, or token-level taxes. The overall assessment is damning: "there are no adults running Meta," with irrational layoffs, rehiring, and now AI-driven workforce reductions creating a mercenary culture where the best employees leave.

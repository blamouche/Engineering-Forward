- **Europe's robotics supply chain ambition**: A team at ETH Zurich's robotics club built a "100% Swiss-made humanoid robot" standing 1.8 metres tall and weighing 62kg, with actuators from Swiss firm Maxon and batteries from Coprod — an attempt to break dependence on China-dominated robotics supply chains.

## Synthesis
Sifted's daily newsletter captures three converging narratives in European tech: the AI safety debate reaching mainstream urgency, Europe's industrial strategy pivoting toward robotics independence, and the transatlantic conversation about where AI value actually accrues.

The AI safety story centers on Denis Shilov and White Circle, a Paris-based startup framing AI risk management as analogous to nuclear arms control. The comparison is deliberately provocative — nuclear arms treaties required coordinated international action because unilateral disarmament meant strategic disadvantage. Shilov's argument implies the same dynamic applies to AI: no single lab or nation will slow down unless all do, making governance coordination the binding constraint. This framing has gained traction as model capabilities advance faster than regulatory frameworks.

The robotics story is equally strategic. Europe's robotics supply chain is heavily skewed toward China, creating a dependency that mirrors the semiconductor vulnerabilities exposed during the pandemic. The ETH Zurich team's Swiss-made humanoid — using locally sourced actuators from Maxon and batteries from Coprod — is a proof of concept for reshoring. The robot itself is unlikely to compete with industrial giants, but it demonstrates that European supply chains can produce humanoid-grade components. Nvidia's Hemesh Prag, Firstminute Capital's Adriana Vitagliano, and Robeauté's Joana Cartocci will debate this at the Sifted Summit, with Robeauté's grain-of-rice-sized neurological diagnosis robots representing the medical robotics frontier.

The US VC conversation has shifted from "which model is best" to "where does the value land." The questions dominating American venture capital now center on distribution, regulatory exposure, and whether AI spend translates to durable revenue. European investors are watching closely, as the answers will determine whether European AI startups can compete on application layers even as model development concentrates in US and Chinese labs.

The funding news reinforces the sovereign capital theme: the UK Sovereign AI Fund's potential £500m backing of Potential Sciences signals that governments are not just regulating AI but directing capital toward AI-enabled science. Project Ventures' £1.2m first close, targeting AI and deeptech with Imperial College London ties, shows the early-stage ecosystem responding to the same signal.

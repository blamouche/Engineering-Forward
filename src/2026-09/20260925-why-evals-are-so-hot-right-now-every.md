# Why Evals Are So Hot Right Now
**Source**: Every (hello@every.to)
**Date**: 2026-09-25
**Author**: Laura Entis (Every)
**Keywords**: evals, Grok 4.7, personal benchmarks, model selection, Slack agents, Thesis Statements, Hyperframes, video generation, AI writing

## Elevator pitch
Every's Context Window newsletter dives into why evals have gone mainstream — nearly half of 25 PM openings Lenny Rachitsky shared asked for eval experience, and Sentry went from near-zero to 1,800 eval runs per month. The newsletter also reviews Grok 4.7's uneven launch, argues Slack agents are the new websites, and shares a workflow for turning articles into viral short videos using Claude and Hyperframes.

## Takeaways
- **Evals go mainstream**: Nearly half of recent PM openings required eval-writing experience. Sentry's eval runs jumped from near zero in May to ~1,800/month.
- **Personal benchmarks over raw intelligence**: Frontier models are now so capable that speed, price, and taste alignment matter more than raw intelligence for daily work. Every is building personal benchmarks for each employee.
- **Grok 4.7 is uneven**: Initially scored 42% vs Grok 4.6's 84% on personal benchmarks. Improved before public launch but still divisive — good for coding, poor for writing. "Big Elon Musk energy" in its editorial judgment.
- **Slack agents are the new websites**: Willie Williams argues applications are evolving from websites to agents on conversational platforms (Slack, Teams, WhatsApp). The website-to-web-app transition took a decade; the agent transition could be faster.
- **Article-to-video workflow**: Becky Isjwara's 4-step process uses Hyperframes with Fable in Claude desktop to create animated short videos from articles, with MiniMax for cover illustration animation via Flora.
- **Thesis Statements**: New predictions from Drew Breunig (Cmpnd), Geoffrey Litt (Notion), Daniela Rus (MIT CSAIL), and others on the future of human work with AI.

## Synthesis
The evals trend is the most significant signal here. When nearly half of PM job postings require eval experience and Sentry is running 1,800 eval checks per month, the industry has crossed a threshold: AI quality assessment is no longer optional or experimental — it's a core competency. The logic is sound: as multiple models can handle most knowledge work, the differentiator shifts from "which model is smartest" to "which model fits my specific workflow, taste, and budget." Personal benchmarks operationalize this insight.

The Grok 4.7 review is a cautionary tale about model launches. The gap between Elon Musk's hype ("exceed all current models") and reality (a model that one tester preferred slightly over its predecessor for coding but found unusable for writing) illustrates how personal benchmarks cut through marketing. Tyler Nishida's journey from "all-caps corrections" to "micromanaging it a lot less" shows that model quality can improve within days of launch, but the writing assessment from Katie Parrott — "no cadence or rhythm and no empathy or theory of mind for the reader" — suggests Grok 4.7's architectural choices favor code generation over prose.

The "Slack agents are the new websites" thesis from Willie Williams is worth tracking. If the pattern holds — informational front door → product surface → invisible infrastructure — companies that don't deploy branded agents on conversational platforms will face the same competitive pressure as those without websites in 2005.

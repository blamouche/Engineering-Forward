## Elevator pitch
Climate tech companies are propping up the Nordic startup ecosystem while the vertical falls out of favor with VCs — 27 of the 100 fastest-growing Nordic startups are climate techs, with energy startups making up nearly 1 in 10, driven by extreme power-price volatility from the surge in renewables.

## Takeaways
- 27 of 100 companies on the Sifted 100 Nordics ranking are climate techs; nearly 1 in 10 are energy startups
- Norwegian hydrogen startup Hystar (PEM electrolysers) ranks second overall; Danish electricity supplier Reel ranks fourth
- Reel's growth comes from the surge of renewables and Europe's energy crisis creating extreme power-price volatility
- Incumbents struggle to solve volatility challenges, creating opportunities for nimble startups
- Uplift Ventures launched a €100m deeptech fund backed solely by German material handling company Jungheinrich
- Notable rounds: Nexeon (€116.7M, battery materials), PLD Space (€108M Series C, reusable launch vehicles), Ipremom (€15M seed, pregnancy risk detection), Inleap Photonics (€20M seed, laser counter-drone tech)
- Frontier Computing (Cambridge, UK) raised $10M pre-seed for biological neural networks as ML training substrate — General Catalyst led
- Sifted 250 Europe-wide ranking to launch at Sifted Summit on September 30–October 1

## Synthesis
The Sifted 100 Nordics report reveals a counterintuitive trend: while climate tech investments are down globally, Nordic energy startups are powering ahead. The explanation lies in the structural shift in European energy markets. As the share of renewables increases in the grid, power-price volatility becomes extreme — the energy crisis accelerated this dynamic. Incumbent energy companies struggle to manage this volatility, creating a market opening for startups like Reel that help businesses transition to renewable energy and manage power-price volatility by buying electricity at more attractive price points.

Hystar's second-place ranking is notable because PEM electrolysers — machines that use electricity to split water into hydrogen and oxygen — represent the kind of hard deeptech that requires patient capital and industrial partnerships. Hystar has secured contracts with energy companies worldwide, suggesting the hydrogen economy is moving from speculation to commercial deployment. The concentration of energy startups in the Nordics reflects both the region's renewable energy abundance and its tradition of deeptech investment.

The broader funding landscape shows continued activity across European deeptech. Uplift Ventures' €100m fund backed solely by Jungheinrich represents a corporate venture model where industrial companies fund startups in their adjacency. PLD Space's €108M Series C for reusable launch vehicles extends the Nordic space ecosystem. Frontier Computing's $10M pre-seed for biological neural networks is perhaps the most unconventional — using biological neural networks as a substrate for machine learning training, backed by General Catalyst and Y Combinator, suggesting frontier investors are exploring post-silicon computing paradigms.

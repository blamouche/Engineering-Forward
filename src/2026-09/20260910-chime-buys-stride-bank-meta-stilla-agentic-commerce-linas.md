# Chime Buys Stride Bank for $590M; Meta Acquires Stilla for Agentic Commerce
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/chime-stride-bank-meta-stilla
**Date**: 2026-09-10
**Author**: Linas's Newsletter (Linas)
**Keywords**: Chime, Stride Bank, Durbin cap, sponsor bank, Meta, Stilla, agentic commerce, Muse, Robinhood, Crypto.com, prediction markets, fintech

## Elevator pitch
Linas's newsletter covers three major fintech deals: Chime's $590M acquisition of Stride Bank (and its deliberate promise to stay under $10B in assets), Meta's acquisition of Stilla to complete its agentic commerce loop (Muse for the buyer, Stilla for the seller), and Robinhood's deals with Crypto.com and OG.com that are really about owning the rails rather than adding football contracts.

## Takeaways
- Chime paid $590M cash for Stride Bank, its Oklahoma sponsor bank of seven years, and promised to hold assets below $10B — the Durbin threshold above which debit interchange gets capped (interchange is ~39% of Chime's 2026 revenue)
- The $10B Durbin cap, not the charter itself, is the real deal: it determines Chime's interchange economics and explains why the fintech is promising to stay small on purpose
- Meta's acquisition of Stilla completes the agentic commerce loop: Muse gives Meta the buyer-side agent, Stilla gives it the seller-side infrastructure — a loop that ads never provided
- The Stilla deal is an admission that Muse's consumer security stance is not enough for a business product, highlighting the gap between consumer and enterprise agentic trust
- Block's Buzz and Meta's Stilla represent the same agentic commerce bet answered in opposite ways — one bottom-up, one top-down
- Robinhood's deals with Crypto.com and OG.com are about owning the infrastructure rails, not adding sports contracts — prediction markets now out-earn crypto at Robinhood
- The Kalshi relationship is cooling fast as Robinhood builds its own prediction market infrastructure

## Synthesis
Linas's newsletter connects three seemingly separate fintech stories into a coherent narrative about the infrastructure layer of agentic commerce and digital banking. The Chime-Stride deal is the most counterintuitive: a fintech that just achieved the holy grail of getting a banking charter is immediately promising to stay small. The reason is the Durbin amendment — above $10B in assets, debit interchange gets capped, and interchange represents 39% of Chime's revenue. The acquisition eliminates Chime's sponsor bank fee but constrains its growth ceiling, a trade-off that reveals how regulatory thresholds shape fintech strategy more than charters do.

The Meta-Stilla acquisition is the more strategically interesting story for the AI-native world. Meta already has Muse, its consumer-facing AI agent for 3.6 billion users. Stilla gives it the seller side — the merchant infrastructure for agentic commerce. Together, they close a loop that advertising never could: Muse acts as the buyer's agent, Stilla as the seller's agent, and Meta mediates the transaction. The deal is also an implicit admission that Muse's consumer-grade security isn't sufficient for business transactions, a gap that Block's Buzz addresses from the opposite direction.

Robinhood's infrastructure plays with Crypto.com and OG.com round out the theme: in the agentic era, owning the rails matters more than owning the user interface. Prediction markets now out-earn crypto at Robinhood, and the Kalshi relationship is cooling as Robinhood builds its own rails — the same pattern of vertical integration that Chime and Meta are pursuing in their respective domains.

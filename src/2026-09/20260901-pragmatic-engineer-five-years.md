## Elevator pitch

## Synthesis
Gergely Orosz's five-year anniversary issue of The Pragmatic Engineer is both a retrospective and a forward-looking assessment of how software engineering is transforming. The newsletter started in 2021 with one in-depth article per week and crossed 1,000 paid subscribers in six weeks. By end of 2021, it was the #1 paid technology newsletter on Substack with 2,700 paid subscribers. Today it has 1.1M readers, a podcast with guests like Simon Willison and Mitchell Hashimoto, and a summit that drew 500 attendees.

The evolution tracks the industry's changes. In 2023, the shift from "back door" informal sourcing to "front door" company cooperation on deepdives marked a maturation of engineering journalism. In 2024, the podcast launched to share the private conversations behind articles. In 2025, the first Pragmatic Summit brought together 500 attendees and 15 speakers.

On AI, the publication is notably restrained: AI is used for research gathering and typo checking but not for writing, because "AI can easily lead you down the wrong track by theorizing about non-existent connections and confidently espousing theories which some basic critical thinking could easily debunk." The team has grown with two software engineers joining in 2026 to help produce deeper deepdives, which can take up to two months each.

The biggest theme for the rest of 2026 is the pace of change in software engineering itself: tools used for decades like IDEs are falling out of style, processes like code reviews are becoming optional, and engineers are spending little to no time typing code — unprecedented since the punch card era. Yet fundamentals still matter: standout developers are more in demand than ever, and picking up AI engineering appears easier than learning a new programming language. The essay challenge invites readers to document how things are changing at their own companies, with $10,000 for the best submission.

# Meta's New Personal Agent is Free Up to 100M Tokens per Week
**Source**: https://unwindai.com/p/meta-muse-personal-agent
**Date**: 2026-09-09
**Author**: Unwind AI
**Keywords**: Meta Muse, personal AI agent, WhatsApp, Sentinel agent, free tier, 100M tokens, Grok Bot, Gemini, consumer AI agents, AI safety

## Elevator pitch
Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent available through its app, web, and WhatsApp, with a free tier offering 100 million tokens per week — making it the first mass-market consumer AI agent with a generous free allowance and a dedicated Sentinel safety agent that inspects everything leaving the VM.

## Takeaways
- Meta Muse is a personal AI agent with a dedicated cloud computer and browser, working across email, calendars, Instagram, and ordinary websites
- A separate Sentinel agent inspects everything leaving the VM; passwords stay hidden from Muse, sensitive actions require approval, and every action enters an audit trail
- The free tier offers 100 million tokens per week — an extraordinarily generous allowance for a consumer AI agent
- Muse can book travel, negotiate bills, sell a car, build missing tools, and keep working after the user closes the app
- Support for Meta's AI glasses is coming, positioning Muse as the agent layer for Meta's hardware ecosystem
- Inception Labs released Mercury 2.5 running at 1,107 tokens per second with a 260K context window — a compelling option for latency-sensitive use cases like search and voice

## Synthesis
Meta's Muse launch represents a significant escalation in the consumer AI agent race. The 100 million tokens per week free tier is not just generous — it's a strategic land grab designed to onboard millions of users before competitors like Grok Bot and Gemini can establish dominance. By making the agent available through WhatsApp (Meta's largest messaging platform), the company is distributing AI agent capabilities through an interface that billions of people already use daily.

The safety architecture is the most notable differentiator. The Sentinel agent — a separate AI that inspects everything Muse attempts to send out of its VM — represents a layered approach to consumer AI safety that goes beyond what Grok Bot or ChatGPT currently offer. Passwords are hidden from Muse itself, sensitive actions require human approval, and a full audit trail creates accountability. This design was likely forced by Meta's trust deficit (the company recently agreed to pay up to $18bn to settle claims that Instagram and Facebook were designed to hook children), making robust safety not just a feature but a prerequisite for consumer adoption.

The broader market signal is that consumer AI agents are moving from novelty to utility. Muse's ability to persist after the user closes the app, combined with browser and email access, means it can complete multi-step real-world tasks autonomously. The AI glasses integration roadmap positions Muse as the software layer for Meta's hardware ambitions.

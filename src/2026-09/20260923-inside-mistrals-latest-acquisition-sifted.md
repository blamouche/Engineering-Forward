# Inside Mistral's Latest Acquisition
**Source**: Sifted Daily (sifted@sifted.eu)
**Date**: 2026-09-23
**Author**: Martin Coulter (Sifted)
**Keywords**: Mistral AI, Pimento, acquisition, European AI, Qdrant, Tink founders, Connect Ventures, Verda, StandardX, Terasi, defence tech, rare materials

## Elevator pitch
Sifted's daily digest reveals that Mistral AI acquired adtech startup Pimento in a €12.7m cash-and-shares deal, while Qdrant pivots toward physical AI, the Tink founders emerge from stealth with a new AI startup, and Connect Ventures raises $55m for its fifth fund — a snapshot of European AI consolidation and capital flow.

## Takeaways
- **Mistral acquires Pimento for €12.7m**: The cash-and-shares deal signals Mistral's expansion beyond foundation models into applied adtech, using acquisitions to broaden its product surface area.
- **Qdrant's next chapter: physical AI**: The vector search company is pivoting toward "physical AI," suggesting infrastructure players are seeking differentiation as vector databases commoditize.
- **Tink founders emerge from stealth**: The team behind the $2.2bn Visa-acquired fintech have launched a new AI startup, bringing fintech-depth into the AI space.
- **Connect Ventures raises $55m fifth fund**: The early-stage firm is targeting deeptech founders, reflecting investor appetite for applied AI over pure foundation model plays.
- **AI infrastructure and rare materials draw capital**: Verda raised $189m for AI infrastructure, StandardX £10m for rare medical materials manufacturing, and Terasi €11m for defence tech backed by NATO Innovation Fund.
- **European AI adoption ranges from 42% (Denmark) to 5% (Romania)**: Sifted's new report with London Business School maps the continent's uneven AI adoption landscape.

## Synthesis
The newsletter paints a picture of European AI maturing through consolidation. Mistral's acquisition of Pimento is the most strategically interesting move: rather than burning cash on building every capability in-house, Mistral is acquiring specialized teams to extend its reach into advertising and creative tooling. This mirrors the broader pattern of frontier model companies racing to build full-stack product offerings — OpenAI acquiring creative tools, Anthropic building vertical products, and now Mistral buying adtech.

The Qdrant pivot toward "physical AI" deserves attention. Vector search was one of the hottest infrastructure categories of 2024-2025, but as embedding models and vector capabilities get absorbed into database platforms (Postgres pgvector, MongoDB Atlas Vector Search), standalone vector databases face existential pressure. Qdrant's move suggests the next frontier for infrastructure companies is bridging AI with physical-world systems — robotics, IoT, autonomous systems — where vector similarity meets sensor data and real-time control.

The Tink founders launching an AI startup is a strong signal. Fintech operators who have lived through a full cycle — founding, scaling, acquisition, integration — bring a discipline that pure AI founders often lack. Their entry into AI suggests the application layer, not the model layer, is where the most experienced operators see opportunity.

Connect Ventures' $55m fifth fund targeting deeptech founders rounds out the picture. European deeptech — combining hardware, science, and AI — is attracting dedicated capital as investors seek moats that foundation models alone can't provide. The fundraising also covers defence tech (Terasi, NATO-backed) and rare materials (StandardX), sectors that would have struggled to raise just two years ago but now align with European strategic autonomy priorities.

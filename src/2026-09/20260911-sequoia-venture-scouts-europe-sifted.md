## Elevator pitch
Sifted's daily briefing covers Sequoia's venture scout network expanding across Europe, European founders setting up their own funds, and Bending Spoons' acquisition of Miro for $1.36bn — the Italian tech group's second major acquisition since its July IPO.

- Sequoia is deploying venture scouts across Europe to hunt for deals, signaling a deeper commitment to the continent's startup ecosystem
- European founders are increasingly setting up their own funds, bringing operator expertise to early-stage investing
- Bending Spoons acquired Miro for $1.36bn in cash, following its $2.25bn acquisition of Airtable in August, building a "legendary institution" portfolio

## Synthesis
Sifted's daily newsletter from Stockholm covers the evolving European venture capital landscape from multiple angles. Sequoia's venture scout program — traditionally a US phenomenon — is now actively hunting for deals across Europe, reflecting the maturation of the continent's startup ecosystem and the increasing competitiveness of European deal-making. The scouts, typically operators and former founders, give Sequoia grassroots deal-flow access that traditional partner-led outreach cannot match.

Meanwhile, European founders are launching their own funds, bringing deep operational experience to early-stage investing. This trend mirrors the US pattern where successful operators become angel investors, but at an institutional scale. Bending Spoons' aggressive acquisition strategy — Miro for $1.36bn and Airtable for $2.25bn since its July IPO — demonstrates that European tech companies are now capable of executing large-scale M&A, consolidating the productivity and collaboration software market.

The funding rounds listed (Tavion €34m for battery storage, Arlequin AI €28m for topological neural networks, SCI Semiconductor £5m for memory-safe chips) show that European deep tech and AI infrastructure remain attractive to investors despite broader market uncertainty.

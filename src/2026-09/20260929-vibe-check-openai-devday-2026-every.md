# Vibe Check: OpenAI DevDay 2026
**Source**: Every (hello@every.to)
**Date**: 2026-09-29
**Author**: Dan Shipper
**Keywords**: OpenAI DevDay, Dots, ChatGPT, agents, Space, Decisions API, Jev, Codex, AI operating system, partner apps, Sol 6.1

## Elevator pitch
Every's Dan Shipper reviews OpenAI DevDay 2026, where 20-plus product launches made plain OpenAI's plan: ChatGPT as the operating system for work, with always-on agents (Dots), collaborative documents (Space), a Jev rival (Decisions API), and partner-app integration all running inside it.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI announced 20+ products at DevDay, the most since the first DevDay in 2023, collectively revealing a strategy to make ChatGPT the operating system for work — agents, documents, and third-party apps all running inside it
- Dots, OpenAI's always-on agents, became the author's main way to use ChatGPT — caught a flight-meeting clash from a Slack thread, but also dropped messages, tripped on permissions, and couldn't always reach its browser
- Space, OpenAI's new home for docs, slides, and spreadsheets, shows edits faster than agent-in-Google-Docs workflows and lets you tag your dot in comments — but getting teams off Google or Notion will be the hard part
- The Decisions API, OpenAI's rival to Jev, splits testers: it chose correctly on 76 of 78 steps (Jev: 73) in 230ms (Jev: 500ms) on one benchmark, but tied on accuracy and was slower on another — pricing not yet announced
- Plus and Pro plans now work in ~15 partner apps including Devin, with usage counting against existing limits — a distribution play that could make ChatGPT the default AI layer across third-party tools
- The $500 Pro plan and Sol 6.1 were also announced, with the full review covering plugin extensions and the rest of the launches
- Shipper's verdict: if you like new tools with rough edges, try dots now; otherwise give OpenAI a week or two; if you're happy with Grok Bots, Muse, or Instinct, stay put unless you already live in ChatGPT or Codex

## Synthesis
DevDay 2026 is OpenAI's most explicit articulation yet of platform ambition: ChatGPT is not a chatbot or even a product — it's an operating system. The Dots + Space + Decisions API + partner-apps combination mirrors a classic platform play: an always-on agent layer (processes), a document layer (filesystem), a decision-routing API (system calls), and third-party integration (applications). The fact that this comes from a chatbot company rather than an OS vendor is itself the signal: the interface to computing is natural language, and whoever owns that interface owns the platform.

The Decisions API is the sharpest competitive move. Jev (TypeSafe's System One) has established the category of lightweight decision-routing for agents; OpenAI entering it validates the category while threatening its economics. The benchmark split — faster on one test, slower on another — suggests the race is far from decided. Meanwhile, the partner-app integration is the quiet killer: if your $20/month ChatGPT subscription works in Devin, why would you pay separately for AI features in other tools? This is the same bundling strategy that made Microsoft Office dominant, now applied to AI.

## Synthesis
Every's Vibe Check on Fable 5.1 is valuable because it comes from a team that has been both a champion and critic of Anthropic's models. They called Fable 5 the best coder in the world but acknowledged it was "easier to admire than to use" — it could handle enormous coding jobs but was slow, hard to follow in conversation, and frustrating when you wanted to revise a draft or talk through an idea. This created an opening: over the summer, much of Every's team migrated toward ChatGPT and Codex for day-to-day work despite Fable 5's superior raw coding ability.

Fable 5.1 changes this calculation by bringing the coding power into a model that's actually pleasant to work alongside. The improvements are in the dimensions that matter for daily collaboration: faster responses, clearer writing, and less pushback when asked to change direction. The team is now bringing work back to Claude. But the review is clear-eyed about remaining limitations: Fable 5.1 still overruns briefs (doing more than asked), can invent quotations, and sometimes continues working after being told to stop. These are not minor issues — invented quotations in published work are a credibility risk, and an agent that won't stop is an operational hazard.

The specific use-case split is informative. Katie Parrott now drafts with Fable 5.1 but the automated editing pipeline stays on Opus 5, suggesting that for structured, repeatable tasks where reliability matters more than creativity, the older model is still preferred. Kieran Klaassen's rebuild of Proof (Cora's product) using Fable 5.1 is a case study in what replacing the original Fable looks like in a production environment. Marcus Moretti's agent tests at Spiral measured the speed and token efficiency dimensions that matter for cost-sensitive deployments. The overall verdict: Anthropic is back in the conversation, but the model isn't a blanket replacement for everything.

# Claude Dispatch and the Power of Interfaces
**Source**: https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/claude-dispatch-and-the-power-of
**Date**: March 31, 2026
**Author**: Ethan Mollick
**Keywords**: AI interfaces, Claude, UX design, AI productivity, interface design, Anthropic

## Elevator pitch
Ethan Mollick argues that the gap between AI's capabilities and actual productivity gains lies not in model limitations but in poor interface design, and that purpose-built, familiar, and dynamic interfaces will unlock AI's hidden potential.

## Takeaways
- A "capability overhang" exists where AI systems are far more capable than users realize due to poor interface design
- Chatbot interfaces create cognitive friction: walls of text and unstructured conversations hurt less experienced users most
- Anthropic's Claude Dispatch uses smartphone-based control of a desktop AI assistant, enabling complex multi-step work
- OpenClaw achieved explosive growth by routing AI through WhatsApp, Telegram, and Slack—platforms people already use
- The future of AI interaction will involve multiple interface types: personal agents, conversational charts, and custom applications

## Synthesis
Mollick argues that AI systems possess far greater capabilities than most people realize, but a capability overhang exists—not from AI limitations but from how people interact with these tools. The primary barrier is interface design.

Current chatbot interfaces create significant cognitive friction. Research cited in the article measured financial professionals completing complex valuation tasks and found that while AI improved productivity, giant walls of text, offers to pursue new topics, and sprawling discussions overwhelmed users. The interface itself became the obstacle rather than facilitating work. Less experienced workers suffered most, precisely those who could benefit most from AI assistance.

Programming represents the only fully developed specialized AI interface ecosystem, largely because AI researchers build tools for themselves. Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex, and Google's Antigravity enable sophisticated autonomous work. However, these tools assume programming knowledge and maintain antiquated aesthetics, excluding the vast majority of knowledge workers outside development.

Google demonstrates broader experimentation with specialized interfaces. Stitch creates AI-native app interfaces through natural language description. Pomelli generates on-brand marketing content from website URLs. NotebookLM facilitates research with diverse information sources. While promising, these tools have not achieved the transformative power of programming-focused solutions.

OpenClaw, an open-source AI agent, achieved explosive growth by solving the interface problem elegantly: users communicate through WhatsApp, Telegram, or Slack—platforms they already use daily. Rather than learning new systems, people simply request tasks naturally, and the agent executes them.

Anthropic's response combines Claude Cowork with Dispatch. Users scan a QR code, enabling smartphone-based instruction of a desktop AI assistant. This approach proves particularly powerful for complex tasks: Mollick demonstrated asking Claude via phone to identify outdated presentation graphs, locate updated data sources online, and automatically refresh PowerPoint slides. Though imperfect, the tool saved substantial time on sophisticated work requiring file access and application manipulation.

The newest development enables AI systems to generate appropriate interfaces dynamically. Claude now creates interactive visualizations directly in conversations, adjustable in real-time as users ask follow-up questions. This approach avoids deciding interfaces in advance, instead allowing AI to generate context-appropriate tools momentarily.

Mollick concludes that future AI interaction will not center on a single dominant interface. Rather, users will experience AI that adapts its presentation layer. Much perceived AI disappointment likely stems not from capability deficiencies but from suboptimal interfaces. By developing specialized, familiar, and dynamic interaction models, organizations can unlock AI potential previously hidden behind poor design choices.

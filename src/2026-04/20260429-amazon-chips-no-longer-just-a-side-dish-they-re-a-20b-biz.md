# Amazon chips no longer just a side dish, they're a $20B biz

**Source**: https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/29/amazon_chips_20b_business
**Date**: April 29, 2026
**Author**: The Register
**Keywords**: amazon, chips, aws, trainium, infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
Amazon says its custom silicon operation has become one of the world's biggest datacenter chip businesses, turning AI infrastructure into a major revenue engine rather than a support function for AWS.

## Takeaways
- Amazon claims its chip business exceeds a $20 billion annual revenue run rate and would look closer to $50 billion if internal AWS demand were counted like external sales.
- Graviton, Trainium, and Nitro are now central to Amazon's AI and datacenter strategy.
- Large commitments from Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, and Uber show Amazon's custom silicon is attracting flagship AI workloads.
- Trainium capacity is already heavily subscribed across current and future generations.
- The article suggests hyperscalers are becoming semiconductor businesses in their own right.

## Synthesis
This Register piece underlines how far Amazon's infrastructure strategy has evolved beyond operating AWS as a conventional cloud business. Andy Jassy argues that Amazon's custom silicon unit now ranks among the world's top datacenter chip businesses, with a revenue run rate above $20 billion and a much larger implied value if Amazon counted internal AWS consumption the same way external chip vendors count shipments. The portfolio spans Graviton CPUs, Trainium AI accelerators, and Nitro security hardware, and Amazon says the business is still growing at triple-digit rates. The customer signals are just as revealing as the internal metrics: Anthropic and OpenAI have made multi-gigawatt Trainium commitments, while Meta and Uber are standardizing significant workloads on Graviton and related hardware. Subscription pressure on current and future Trainium generations suggests that demand is already outpacing supply. The broader significance is that hyperscalers are no longer only buyers of semiconductors. They are increasingly integrated chip companies shaping the economics of AI compute themselves. For Amazon, custom silicon is becoming both a margin lever and a strategic moat, especially as agentic workloads push more inference and orchestration demand onto CPU and accelerator fleets.

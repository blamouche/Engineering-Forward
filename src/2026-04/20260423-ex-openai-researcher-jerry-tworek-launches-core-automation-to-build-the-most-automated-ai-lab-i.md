# Ex-OpenAI researcher Jerry Tworek launches Core Automation to build the most automated AI lab in the world

**Source**: https://the-decoder.com/ex-openai-researcher-jerry-tworek-launches-core-automation-to-build-the-most-automated-ai-lab-in-the-world
**Date**: April 22, 2026
**Author**: Matthias Bastian
**Keywords**: Jerry Tworek, Core Automation, OpenAI alumni, AI research, automation, new labs

## Elevator pitch
Former OpenAI researcher Jerry Tworek has launched Core Automation with the ambition of automating research itself and finding learning approaches beyond today’s pretraining and reinforcement-learning playbook.

## Takeaways
- Core Automation wants to automate parts of AI research rather than only build larger models.
- The lab says it is exploring new learning algorithms and architectures beyond transformers.
- Tworek frames the effort as fundamental research that he no longer felt possible inside OpenAI.
- The startup joins a wave of elite “neo labs” founded by alumni from top frontier labs.
- The broader theme is dissatisfaction with simply scaling existing deep-learning recipes.

## Synthesis
Core Automation is interesting less for what it has already built and more for what its founding premise says about the mood inside frontier AI research. Jerry Tworek’s pitch is that simply training larger models on more data is no longer the most important frontier. Instead, the opportunity is to automate research itself and develop new learning algorithms and architectures that escape the current dependence on pretraining, reinforcement learning, and transformer-centered scaling.

That makes the company part of a broader pattern. Over the past year, several high-profile labs founded by OpenAI alumni and other senior researchers have been built around the idea that the current paradigm is running into diminishing returns, organizational constraints, or both. The notable part of Tworek’s framing is that he is not just starting another lab to continue the same race in a smaller setting. He is explicitly targeting the process of research automation as a leverage point. The idea is that a small human team, amplified by capable agents, can produce work that previously required a much larger organization.

There is obvious ambition here and also obvious uncertainty. Claims about “the most automated AI lab in the world” are still early and aspirational. But the strategic logic is coherent. If the next breakthroughs come from faster experimentation, new algorithmic ideas, or more automated hypothesis generation, then internal research tooling could matter as much as model scale.

For the wider market, the story reinforces a growing split in AI. Incumbents are pushing products and large-scale deployment, while a new generation of labs is betting that deeper scientific progress now requires fresh institutions and fresh assumptions. Core Automation is another sign that many top researchers no longer believe the next leap will come simply from making existing systems larger.

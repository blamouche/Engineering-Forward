# AI Is Becoming an Operating System Layer

**Source**: https://raffy.ch/blog/2026/04/06/ai-is-becoming-an-operating-system-layer
**Date**: April 6, 2026
**Author**: Raffy Marty
**Keywords**: AI strategy, operating model, private equity, venture capital, company redesign, AI transformation

## Elevator pitch
Raffy Marty argues that serious AI adoption is no longer about adding features or copilots, but about redesigning the company itself so AI sits beneath every major function as operational infrastructure.

## Takeaways
- Investors are increasingly asking whether companies are rebuilding around AI rather than merely adopting AI tools.
- The article argues that product, sales, service delivery, customer success, finance, and operations all need AI-native redesign.
- AI should function as a shared operating layer that embeds company strategy and links workflows across teams.
- Firms that bolt AI onto old operating models may look weaker than those that redesign around it end to end.
- This is framed as a capital-markets issue as much as a product or efficiency issue.

## Synthesis
This post is short, but it lands on an important distinction: “using AI” and “rebuilding around AI” are not remotely the same thing. Most companies today still live in the first category. They add copilots, automate a few tasks, and tell a transformation story. Raffy Marty is arguing that the market is starting to see through that. What investors and acquirers want to know now is whether AI is actually changing the operating model—how work moves, how data flows, how teams coordinate, and how decisions get made.

The operating-system metaphor is useful because it forces a systems view. If AI is just another app, it remains optional and fragmented. If it becomes an underlying layer, it starts to shape how every function runs. That includes not only execution tasks but also strategic memory: mission, ICP, priorities, and go-to-market logic encoded into the tooling people interact with daily.

There is a risk of overstatement here. Not every company needs a full-stack redesign overnight, and the phrase “AI as infrastructure” can easily become management-consulting wallpaper. But the core thesis is still strong. The winners will not be the firms with the most AI features on a slide. They will be the ones that collapse friction across functions and turn AI from a side tool into a coordination substrate.

In that sense, the article is really about competitiveness. AI transformation is becoming less of a product enhancement story and more of an organizational design story, with capital markets starting to price the difference.

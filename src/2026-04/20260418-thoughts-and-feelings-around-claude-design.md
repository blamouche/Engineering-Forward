# Thoughts and Feelings around Claude Design

**Source**: https://samhenri.gold/blog/20260418-claude-design/
**Date**: April 18, 2026
**Author**: Sam Henri Gold
**Keywords**: Claude Design, Figma, design tools, code as source of truth, product design

## Elevator pitch
Sam Henri Gold argues that Claude Design matters less as a polished tool than as a sign that code-native, agent-friendly workflows may pull design authority away from Figma’s increasingly complex proprietary system.

## Takeaways
- The essay argues that Figma’s abstractions became so system-heavy and proprietary that they are poorly aligned with the code-trained models driving the agent era.
- Claude Design is compelling because it is honest about being HTML and JavaScript underneath, which makes it easier to connect design exploration with implementation.
- The author sees design authority shifting back toward code because that is the medium LLMs understand best and where products ultimately live.
- Figma Make is framed as a defense of the existing design-file canon, while Claude Design is treated as a bet on a different source of truth.
- The piece predicts a split between code-native design tools and freer creative exploration environments that abandon design-system rigidity.

## Synthesis
This essay is a strong example of how product designers are starting to reinterpret AI tools through the lens of medium and source of truth, not just feature checklists. The author’s argument is that Figma’s long success depended on making design canonical inside software organizations, but that success created a technical and conceptual trap. The design system grew around proprietary primitives, rigid schemas, and workflows that are difficult to automate or map cleanly onto the code that ultimately ships. In the agent era, that becomes a disadvantage because the models understand code far better than they understand Figma’s internal abstractions.

Claude Design matters in this framing not because it is already a superior design tool, but because it is built on materials the current generation of models can actually reason about. HTML and JavaScript are not just implementation details, they are the native language of the training corpus and the shipped product. That means a code-adjacent design tool can potentially collapse the distance between ideation and implementation in a way Figma struggles to match. The essay treats this as a structural advantage rather than a temporary novelty.

The critique of Figma is also cultural. Over time, design systems accumulated variables, props, nested modes, and layers of indirection that made canonical design files difficult to understand and painful to maintain. The author’s examples from Figma’s own files underline the point: a lot of the design stack now exists to manage the design stack itself. If AI makes it easier for designers to work directly in code or code-like environments, that overhead starts to look less like professional rigor and more like historical baggage.

One of the essay’s most interesting ideas is that design tooling may split into two paths. On one side are code-native systems such as Claude Design, where the same conversational loop can move from mockup to implementation. On the other side are tools optimized for unconstrained exploration, where the goal is not system compliance but creative play. That is a sharper prediction than the usual “AI will change design” claim because it suggests the middle ground Figma occupied so successfully could become less stable.

The argument also helps explain why Claude Design feels strategically important even if it is still rough. Anthropic already has Claude Code, and the combination hints at a future where product thinking, interface generation, and implementation all sit inside one agentic workflow. That is a meaningful challenge to tools that depend on handoffs between disconnected surfaces.

Overall, the essay is persuasive because it links the rise of AI design tools to a deeper question about where product truth lives. If the source of truth shifts back toward code, then the most important design tools of the next cycle may not be the ones with the richest proprietary design model. They may be the ones that align best with the mediums agents can understand and transform reliably.

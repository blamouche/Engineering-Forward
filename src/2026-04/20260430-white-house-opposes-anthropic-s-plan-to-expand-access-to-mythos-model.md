# White House Opposes Anthropic’s Plan to Expand Access to Mythos Model

**Source**: https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/white-house-opposes-anthropics-plan-to-expand-access-to-mythos-model-dc281ab5
**Date**: April 30, 2026
**Author**: The Wall Street Journal
**Keywords**: anthropic, mythos, government, security, compute

## Elevator pitch
US officials are pushing back on Anthropic's plan to widen access to its cyber-capable Mythos model, citing both security concerns and finite government-priority compute capacity.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic wanted to expand Mythos access from about 50 entities to roughly 120 total organizations.
- White House officials objected because the model could enable cyberattacks or broad online disruption.
- Some officials were also worried that expanding the user base would dilute compute resources needed by the government.
- The episode shows frontier-model distribution is becoming a national-security question, not only a product decision.
- Access controls and capacity allocation are now part of AI governance.

## Synthesis
This Wall Street Journal report captures how frontier AI distribution is moving into direct conflict with state priorities. Anthropic reportedly proposed expanding access to Mythos, a model viewed as powerful enough to support offensive cyber operations and other disruptive activity. Administration officials opposed the move, not only because of security concerns around broader release but also because of a second, less discussed constraint: finite compute availability. If the government relies on the same model and infrastructure, expanding commercial or institutional access can interfere with its own operational needs. That detail matters because it shows frontier-model governance is no longer just about abstract safety principles or voluntary corporate policies. It is becoming entangled with resource allocation, procurement, and national-security prioritization. The broader implication is that the highest-risk models may increasingly be governed like strategic assets, with tighter limits on who can use them, under what conditions, and at what scale. Anthropic's challenge is not simply whether it can build a capable cyber model, but whether it can convince regulators and government customers that broader access does not create unacceptable operational or geopolitical risk.

# OpenAI President Greg Brockman: Doubling Down on Text Models, The Superapp Plan, Codex’s Potential

**Source**: https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/openai-president-greg-brockman-doubling
**Date**: April 7, 2026
**Author**: Big Technology
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Greg Brockman, superapp, Codex, GPT, personal assistant, AI strategy

## Elevator pitch
Greg Brockman says OpenAI is concentrating on GPT-style models, a unified “superapp,” and broader computer-use workflows, arguing that one core model stack can power both personal assistance and serious work.

## Takeaways
- Brockman says OpenAI is prioritizing GPT-style models over separate branches like standalone video generation.
- OpenAI’s top application bets are a personal assistant and an AI that can solve hard work tasks with enough context and tools.
- The company wants to unify chat, coding, browsing, and computer use into one endpoint application with shared memory and context.
- Codex is being framed less as a tool for software engineers only and more as a general agent harness for computer-based work.
- The interview suggests that harness design, context, tools, and memory now matter as much as the base model itself.

## Synthesis
This interview is useful because it makes OpenAI’s current product thesis unusually explicit. Brockman is arguing for consolidation: one core model family, one shared harness layer, one endpoint application that handles chat, coding, browsing, and eventually broader computer use. That is a meaningful strategic choice. Instead of pursuing many loosely connected AI products, OpenAI wants to collapse them into a superapp that acts as both personal assistant and work engine.

The deeper point is technological, not just branding. Brockman repeatedly emphasizes the “harness” around the model—context, actions, tools, memory, feedback loops. That aligns with what many practitioners are already seeing: the next leap in usefulness comes less from raw model benchmarks than from how well systems connect the model to the world and to user-specific context.

The decision to de-emphasize separate video-model efforts also says something important about constraints. Even the best-capitalized labs cannot seriously chase every branch of AI progress at once. OpenAI appears to believe that the GPT line is now broad enough to justify extreme focus, especially if it can absorb speech, image generation, browsing, and agentic work into one stack.

Whether the superapp vision wins is another matter. Big unified products can become bloated or confusing fast. But strategically, the idea is coherent: if AI really becomes the default interface to digital work, the most valuable product may be the one that owns your context, your memory, and your ability to act across tasks—not the one that wins any single narrow category.

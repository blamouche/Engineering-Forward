# Microsoft is testing OpenClaw-like AI bots for Copilot

**Source**: https://www.theverge.com/tech/911080/microsoft-ai-openclaw-365-businesses
**Date**: April 20, 2026
**Author**: Emma Roth
**Keywords**: microsoft, copilot, openclaw, agents, enterprise

## Elevator pitch
Microsoft is exploring safer, role-scoped versions of OpenClaw-style autonomous agents to make Microsoft 365 Copilot act continuously across inboxes, calendars, and other enterprise workflows.

## Takeaways
- Microsoft is studying OpenClaw-like agent capabilities for enterprise Copilot products.
- The goal includes always-on task execution rather than only chat-based assistance.
- The company appears focused on role-specific agents to constrain permissions and reduce risk.
- Security concerns around OpenClaw are treated as a design input, not a reason to avoid the category.
- Microsoft may use Build to position Copilot as a stronger answer to competing agent platforms.

## Synthesis
The Verge reports that Microsoft is testing OpenClaw-inspired agents inside its Copilot roadmap, with the ambition of making Microsoft 365 Copilot act more autonomously and continuously for users. Rather than limiting Copilot to reactive chat or document assistance, the reported direction is toward agents that can monitor inboxes, calendars, and role-specific workflows, then surface tasks or take action on a user’s behalf. The most notable detail is Microsoft’s apparent focus on constrained scope. Instead of giving one general agent broad access across a company, it is exploring domain-specific versions for functions like marketing, sales, or accounting. That approach suggests Microsoft wants the upside of agent autonomy without inheriting the full security and trust problems that have followed open-ended local agents such as OpenClaw. The article also situates this move in a competitive context. Microsoft has already integrated third-party AI capabilities like Claude into parts of its productivity stack, and bringing OpenClaw-like behavior in-house could help it defend against rivals that are winning attention from developers and power users. In strategic terms, the story shows how quickly the industry has moved from debating whether autonomous agents are viable to debating how narrowly they should be scoped, how permissions should be constrained, and where continuous assistance is worth the operational risk.

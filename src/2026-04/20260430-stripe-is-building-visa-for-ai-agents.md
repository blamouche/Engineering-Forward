# Stripe Is Building Visa for AI Agents

**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/fintechpulse1073
**Date**: April 30, 2026
**Author**: Linas Beliūnas
**Keywords**: stripe, payments, agents, fintech, commerce

## Elevator pitch
Stripe is trying to become the payments and settlement stack for autonomous AI by pairing human-approved wallet spending with machine-native payment rails.

## Takeaways
- The article frames Stripe's latest launches as two complementary rails for agent commerce: human-approved spending and machine-native payments.
- One flow lets AI shopping agents spend through a wallet with biometric approval.
- The other focuses on machine-to-machine payments, including low-value autonomous transactions without a human in the loop.
- Stripe is leveraging its existing scale in human payments to extend into agent-mediated commerce before rivals catch up.
- The strategic question is whether anyone else can assemble the same full-stack position across identity, approvals, and settlement.

## Synthesis
Linas Beliūnas argues that Stripe's recent product moves amount to a bid to become the financial operating system for AI agents. His framing is useful because it separates two different futures that Stripe is trying to cover at once. In the first, consumer-facing agents need a safe way to spend on behalf of people, which implies wallets, explicit approvals, and bounded authority. In the second, software agents transact directly with other systems, which implies cheap, machine-native payment rails with much less human involvement. Stripe appears to be building both layers simultaneously. That matters because autonomous commerce is not only a checkout problem. It requires identity, credential issuance, payment authorization, and programmable settlement all working together. Stripe's advantage is that it can graft these new capabilities onto an enormous existing payments footprint rather than starting from scratch. The article reads the move as a strategic attempt to do for agent commerce what Visa and related networks did for consumer payments: become the default rail that every participant must integrate with. Whether that position proves durable will depend on standards, merchant adoption, and whether competing ecosystems can build lighter or more open alternatives.

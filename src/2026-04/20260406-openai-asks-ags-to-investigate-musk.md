# OpenAI asks California, Delaware to investigate Musk’s anti-competitive behavior ahead of April trial

**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/openai-asks-california-ag-to-probe-musks-anti-competitive-behavior-.html
**Date**: April 6, 2026
**Author**: CNBC
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Elon Musk, xAI, antitrust, litigation, AGI governance, competition

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is escalating its legal and political conflict with Elon Musk by asking California and Delaware officials to investigate what it describes as coordinated anti-competitive tactics ahead of trial.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI sent letters urging California and Delaware attorneys general to investigate Musk and his associates.
- The company alleges coordinated efforts to undermine OpenAI, including claimed cooperation with other rivals.
- The dispute lands just before jury selection in Musk’s long-running case over OpenAI’s corporate structure and mission.
- OpenAI is casting the fight as a question of who gets to shape AGI development and under what principles.
- The conflict shows how AI competition now spans legal strategy, governance narratives, and public pressure—not just product launches.

## Synthesis
This is what market competition looks like when the stakes are no longer limited to products and pricing. OpenAI is trying to reframe its conflict with Elon Musk from a governance dispute into a competition-policy issue, arguing that Musk is using litigation, influence, and coordinated pressure to weaken a rival ahead of a crucial trial. That is a sharp escalation because it invites regulators to treat the feud not as founder drama, but as market conduct.

The rhetoric around AGI is doing double duty here. On one level it is mission language: OpenAI says Musk is trying to divert AGI into the hands of actors who do not share its safety commitments. On another level it is strategic positioning. In a market where legitimacy matters, the company wants to paint itself as the responsible steward and its rivals as opportunistic antagonists.

Whether regulators buy that framing is uncertain. But the deeper signal is unmistakable: frontier AI competition is now legal, political, and narrative warfare as much as technical competition. The companies are fighting over financing, cloud access, talent, public legitimacy, and regulatory sympathy all at once.

That means the future winners may not be determined solely by who builds the best systems. They will also be shaped by who can survive and shape the institutional battles around those systems.

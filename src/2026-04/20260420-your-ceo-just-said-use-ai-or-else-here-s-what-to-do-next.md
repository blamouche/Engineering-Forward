# Your CEO Just Said ‘Use AI or Else.’ Here’s What to Do Next.

**Source**: https://every.to/p/your-ceo-just-said-use-ai-or-else-here-s-what-to-do-next
**Date**: April 20, 2026
**Author**: Alex Duffy
**Keywords**: ai adoption, enterprise, workflows, documentation, automation

## Elevator pitch
This guide translates executive pressure to “use AI” into a concrete operating model built around habit formation, documentation, workflow discovery, automation, and public learning.

## Takeaways
- The first step is not mastering prompts but building a reflex to include AI in everyday work.
- People should map the specific value they create so AI amplifies meaningful work instead of producing filler.
- Documentation is framed as the hidden unlock because it turns tacit work into reusable context for AI.
- Repeated successful prompts should become templates, tools, bots, or lightweight automations.
- Sharing experiments early helps teams learn collectively and creates proof of progress for managers and reviews.

## Synthesis
Alex Duffy turns Shopify’s viral internal memo about reflexive AI usage into a practical adaptation guide for knowledge workers. The article’s core claim is that many employees are stuck because they interpret AI adoption as a tooling problem when it is really a behavior change problem. Instead of waiting for the perfect workflow, they should start by bringing AI into existing tasks such as note cleanup, support writing, or code refactoring so the habit becomes natural. From there, Duffy argues that workers need a sharper sense of their actual comparative advantage. If they do not know what value they uniquely bring to a team, they will use AI to generate noise instead of leverage. The article then emphasizes documentation as the bridge between experimentation and scale. By writing down triggers, steps, and quality criteria, workers create the structured context models need, while also producing reusable assets for onboarding and automation. Once a pattern repeats, it becomes a candidate for a prompt template, bot, or internal tool. The final step is social rather than technical: share what is working before it is polished. That gives teams a blueprint for broader adoption and helps managers see concrete evidence of AI-assisted impact. Overall, the piece reframes “use AI or else” as an invitation to develop repeatable operating habits rather than a demand for flashy demos.

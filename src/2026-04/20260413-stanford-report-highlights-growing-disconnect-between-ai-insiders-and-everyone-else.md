# Stanford report highlights growing disconnect between AI insiders and everyone else

**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/13/stanford-report-highlights-growing-disconnect-between-ai-insiders-and-everyone-else
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: Sarah Perez
**Keywords**: AI public opinion, Stanford AI Index, jobs, regulation, trust, social impact

## Elevator pitch
TechCrunch distills Stanford AI Index data showing that AI experts remain much more optimistic than the public, whose concerns center less on AGI doom and more on jobs, healthcare, utility costs, and whether governments can regulate the rollout responsibly.

## Takeaways
- Experts are consistently more positive than the public about AI’s effects on healthcare, work, and the broader economy.
- Public anxiety is driven mostly by practical disruptions such as job loss, infrastructure costs, and weak trust in regulators—not mainly by science-fiction fears.
- The gap suggests AI leaders may be badly misreading the social sources of backlash and resistance.

## Synthesis
This article matters because it names a political and cultural failure mode for the AI industry: many insiders are still talking as if the main public worry is speculative superintelligence, while ordinary people are reacting to immediate economic and institutional risks. Stanford’s synthesis of Pew and Ipsos data makes that gap visible. Experts expect positive effects on medicine, work, and growth. The public sees layoffs, rising energy demand, and a government it does not trust to contain the downsides.

That mismatch has direct product and policy consequences. If builders assume opposition is mainly fear of futuristic AGI, they will respond with philosophy and safety theater. But if the real concern is that AI lowers job security, raises bills, and concentrates power, then credible responses need to look more like labor policy, local infrastructure governance, distribution of gains, and enforceable accountability. In other words, the problem is not just communication. It is material impact.

The piece also helps explain why public reactions around prominent AI executives and companies have become harsher. Social legitimacy erodes when a technology is framed as world-changing and unavoidable, while the people exposed to its costs are told to focus on long-term upside. The industry’s next challenge may be less about persuading people that AI is powerful and more about proving that power can be deployed without treating public trust as collateral damage.

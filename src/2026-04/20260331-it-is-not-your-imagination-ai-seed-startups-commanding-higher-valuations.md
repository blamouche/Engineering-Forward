# It's not your imagination: AI seed startups are commanding higher valuations
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/its-not-your-imagination-ai-seed-startups-are-commanding-higher-valuations/
**Date**: March 31, 2026
**Author**: Dominic-Madori Davis
**Keywords**: seed rounds, startups, venture capital, AI valuations, Y Combinator, early-stage funding

## Elevator pitch
AI-focused seed startups are securing valuations around $40 million at Y Combinator's latest cohort, confirming that premium valuations for AI companies at the earliest funding stages are real and accelerating.

## Takeaways
- AI seed startups are commanding significantly higher valuations than historical norms at the seed stage
- Y Combinator's most recent cohort companies were securing valuations around $40 million
- Higher valuations come with proportionally higher investor expectations and performance pressure
- The premium reflects genuine market dynamics favoring AI investments, not just perception
- Founders benefit financially but face steeper benchmarks for demonstrating value

## Synthesis
The venture capital market for AI-focused startups has diverged sharply from historical seed funding norms. Where seed rounds once anchored around a relatively narrow valuation range, AI companies are now securing valuations that would have been considered aggressive even for Series A rounds just a few years ago. The $40 million valuations seen at Y Combinator's recent cohort represent a concrete data point for a trend that many in the ecosystem have perceived but struggled to quantify.

The reasons for this premium are multifactorial. Investors competing for access to the most promising AI founders are willing to pay up to win allocations, compressing the return potential for early investors but ensuring that founders capture more of the value they create upfront. The perceived speed at which AI companies can scale — given the leverage that language models and other foundation models provide — also justifies higher entry valuations, since the capital efficiency of AI-native businesses can be substantially higher than traditional software companies.

However, the higher valuations carry a corresponding burden. A company that raises at $40 million seed valuation needs to demonstrate growth and traction sufficient to support a Series A at a meaningfully higher valuation, or face a flat or down round — a psychologically and practically difficult outcome after starting at elevated levels. For founders, this means that the benefit of commanding a high seed valuation is real but comes with an implicit commitment to growth trajectories that may be difficult to sustain, particularly as the AI market matures and differentiation becomes more challenging.

For investors, the elevated seed valuations mean that traditional seed-to-Series A return multiples are compressed from the start. This changes the portfolio math: a $40 million seed investment that exits at $400 million delivers a 10x return, which is solid but no longer exceptional. To generate fund-level returns, investors need either many such outcomes or some portion of their portfolio to achieve significantly larger exits.

The broader implication is that the AI wave has permanently reset expectations at the earliest funding stages. Whether these valuations hold as the market matures — or whether a correction brings them back toward historical norms — will depend heavily on whether AI companies can deliver the exceptional unit economics and growth rates that justify the premium. For now, the data from YC's cohort suggests that investors remain willing to pay the premium.

# The $112 Billion Quarter

**Source**: https://tomtunguz.com/2026-04-29-the-112-billion-quarter-hyperscalers-bet-the-farm-on-ai
**Date**: April 29, 2026
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz
**Keywords**: cloud, ai-infrastructure, google, aws, microsoft

## Elevator pitch
Hyperscalers are spending at historic levels on AI infrastructure, and Google's vertical integration across TPUs, Gemini, and cloud demand is giving it the strongest growth profile.

## Takeaways
- Google Cloud grew much faster than AWS and Azure, helped by bundling its own models and infrastructure.
- All three hyperscalers are committing extraordinary capex, with $112 billion in combined quarterly infrastructure spending.
- Google's backlog and token volumes suggest enterprise AI demand is outrunning current compute supply.
- Amazon is countering with custom silicon and long-term Trainium commitments from Anthropic and OpenAI.
- The piece argues that owning the model layer and the hardware stack creates the strongest structural advantage.

## Synthesis
Tomasz Tunguz uses the latest quarterly results to show how the AI boom is redrawing the competitive map among hyperscalers. His central observation is that all three majors, Google, Amazon, and Microsoft, are seeing exceptional demand, but Google is growing fastest because it owns more of the stack. Rather than merely reselling compute, Google pairs its cloud business with Gemini and TPUs, which lowers dependency on outside model providers and improves unit economics. The article highlights several markers of demand intensity: Google Cloud's huge backlog, rising token throughput, and comments from Sundar Pichai that compute shortages are actively constraining revenue. At the same time, all three providers are committing enormous capex, with a combined $112 billion in quarterly infrastructure spending. Amazon's answer is custom silicon at scale, especially Trainium and Graviton, plus large commitments from Anthropic and OpenAI. Microsoft remains powerful because it can fund the buildout from operating cash flow, while Google and Amazon are leaning more on debt markets. The wider implication is that AI cloud competition is no longer just about selling capacity. It is about who controls enough of the model, hardware, and economics stack to convert exploding usage into durable advantage.

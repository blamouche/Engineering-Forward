# The $725 Billion AI Spending Surge Is Missing the Real Bottleneck
**Source**: https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/04/30/the-725-billion-ai-spending-surge-is-missing-the-real-bottleneck/
**Date**: April 30, 2026
**Author**: Jon Markman (Forbes)
**Keywords**: AI infrastructure, capex, connectivity silicon, Nvidia, Astera Labs, data centers, bottleneck

## Elevator pitch
Combined 2026 hyperscaler capex has reached $725 billion — up 77% — but the real bottleneck has shifted from GPU compute to the connectivity silicon that lets thousands of GPUs work together as a single supercomputer.

## Takeaways
- Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta combined 2026 capex now totals ~$725 billion, up 77% from $410 billion in 2025
- The bottleneck is shifting from compute (GPUs) to connectivity — the switches, retimers, cables, and controllers that enable rack-scale architectures
- Astera Labs CEO Jitendra Mohan: "Increasingly, the bottleneck is shifting from compute to connectivity, and connectivity is where we play"
- Amazon issued Astera Labs a warrant tied to $6.5 billion in cumulative product purchases, expanding a previous $466M warrant by 13x
- Microsoft's CFO cited a $25 billion impact from higher component prices including memory and networking silicon
- Astera grew from $396M to $852M revenue (115% growth) with a served addressable market projected at $25 billion by 2030
- The market is still pricing GPUs while the hyperscalers are telling investors the constraint has moved to the layer between the GPUs

## Synthesis
Jon Markman argues that the AI infrastructure investment narrative is focused on the wrong question. While everyone is asking how much of the $725 billion hyperscaler capex will flow to Nvidia, the more consequential constraint is emerging elsewhere: the connectivity layer that makes rack-scale AI architectures possible.

The scale of the spending is staggering: combined 2026 capex from Alphabet ($185B), Amazon ($200B), Microsoft ($190B), and Meta ($115-135B) totals approximately $725 billion, up 77% from last year's $410 billion. For three years the binding constraint was compute — specifically GPUs. That phase is ending, replaced by a different scarcity: the silicon that handles data movement between GPUs at terabit-per-second speeds with sub-microsecond latency.

The clearest signal comes from Amazon, which issued Astera Labs a warrant tied to $6.5 billion in cumulative purchases — a 13x expansion of a previous $466 million warrant, suggesting the hyperscaler's internal models are telling it that the real supply constraint is connectivity, not compute. Microsoft's CFO cited a $25 billion impact from component prices including networking silicon on its latest earnings call. These are not casual line items; they are hyperscalers telling investors where the money is actually going.

Astera Labs' product portfolio sits at every choke point in a modern AI rack: retimers that regenerate degraded signals across PCIe connections, active electrical cables with embedded signal conditioning for 400G/800G Ethernet, PCIe Gen 6 fabric switches for scale-out, CXL controllers for memory pooling across racks. The company grew revenue from $396 million to $852 million in one year and guides its served addressable market to $25 billion by 2030.

The investment implication is straightforward: the companies most investors think about (Nvidia) are priced. The companies that build the connective tissue enabling GPU clusters to function at scale — Astera, Marvell, Broadcom, Credo, Lumentum — represent the same secular trend at an earlier point in their public revenue trajectory. The bottleneck moved, and the money will follow it.

# These AI Startups Just Raised $187M, and They Reveal Exactly Where the Market Is Headed

**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/aistartupmarket
**Date**: April 7, 2026
**Author**: Linas
**Keywords**: startups, venture capital, AI market, fundraising, vertical SaaS

## Elevator pitch
Linas uses a set of recent startup financings to argue that AI capital is concentrating into fewer winners and increasingly rewarding deep vertical products, proprietary data, and systems that own real workflows.

## Takeaways
- AI captured an extraordinary share of venture funding in early 2026, with capital increasingly concentrated in a handful of companies.
- Removing the mega-rounds reveals a much harsher market where smaller startups compete for a relatively thin slice of remaining capital.
- The author argues the “thin wrapper” era is ending while infrastructure, vertical SaaS, and workflow-owning systems gain favor.
- Pitch decks from mid-sized rounds can reveal where VCs see durable market depth rather than hype.
- The piece frames differentiation, proprietary data, and embedded workflow ownership as the new fundraising baseline.

## Synthesis
This post’s main value is not the list of startups but the market interpretation around them. Linas argues that AI funding is no longer just abundant; it is extremely concentrated. Once the giant rounds for companies like OpenAI and Anthropic are stripped out, the rest of the market looks far less forgiving.

That matters because it changes what “AI opportunity” means for everyone else. The easy story—build a light wrapper around a frontier model and ride the wave—looks increasingly dead. Investors appear to be rewarding companies with deeper ownership of domain workflows, proprietary data, and embedded operational value instead of generic coordination layers.

The services-versus-software framing is also useful. As AI systems become capable of completing work rather than merely supporting workers, value may shift away from tooling and toward direct outcome delivery. But the essay is careful to note that not all of that old services spend will cleanly migrate into software margins; some of it may simply collapse through repricing.

Overall, the piece is best read as a snapshot of market selection pressure. AI funding is still huge, but the bar for what counts as investable is rising quickly. Founders need real depth now, not just model access and distribution polish.

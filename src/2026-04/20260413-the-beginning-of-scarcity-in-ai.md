# The Beginning of Scarcity in AI

**Source**: https://tomtunguz.com/ai-compute-crisis-2026
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz
**Keywords**: AI compute, scarcity, GPUs, pricing, infrastructure, market dynamics

## Elevator pitch
Tom Tunguz argues that AI has moved from abundance theater to real compute scarcity, with rising GPU prices, longer contracts, gated model access, and forced diversification reshaping who can build at the frontier.

## Takeaways
- The piece highlights rising Blackwell rental prices and longer infrastructure commitments as evidence that supply constraints are no longer hypothetical.
- Scarcity is presented as a strategic filter that advantages well-capitalized customers and close provider relationships over open access rhetoric.
- It predicts slower, more expensive frontier access and a push toward smaller models, alternative providers, and on-prem deployments while capacity catches up.

## Synthesis
This short post is really a market-structure argument. Tunguz’s point is that AI is leaving the phase where everyone can pretend frontier capacity will remain broadly accessible at reasonable prices. Once GPU rental rates jump, contract terms lengthen, and labs start rationing best-in-class access, the economics start to look less like software abundance and more like constrained industrial infrastructure.

That changes startup strategy. If compute and top-tier model access are scarce, relationship capital and balance-sheet strength matter more. Some products will be priced out of the frontier entirely; others will be forced into smaller models, hybrid architectures, or slower customer experiences. The glamour narrative around infinite intelligence on tap starts to give way to procurement, allocation, and margin discipline.

The deeper point is that AI competition may increasingly hinge on control of supply chains rather than just control of algorithms. Companies that can secure capacity, diversify serving options, and design products around variable model quality will be in a stronger position than companies whose entire roadmap assumes cheap, fast frontier access forever.

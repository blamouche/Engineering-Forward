# Vibe Check: GPT-5.5 Has It All
**Source**: https://every.to/vibe-check/gpt-5-5
**Date**: April 23, 2026
**Author**: Katie Parrott
**Keywords**: GPT-5.5, OpenAI, Opus 4.7, model benchmark, senior engineer, vibe check, coding, writing, AI model comparison

## Elevator pitch
GPT-5.5 scores nearly double Opus 4.7 on Every's Senior Engineer Benchmark (62.5 vs 33.5), excels at writing after a year of OpenAI weakness in that area, and is fast and personable enough for constant use — though Opus 4.7 still has edges in one-shot vibe coding and broader product design.

## Takeaways
- GPT-5.5 scored 62.5 on the Senior Engineer Benchmark vs 33.5 for Opus 4.7; humans score in the high 80s-low 90s
- GPT-5.5's best benchmark run used an Opus-written plan, suggesting the two models may be complementary rather than pure competitors
- Writing quality saw a real comeback — cleaner structure, smoother logic than Opus 4.7, the best from OpenAI in a year
- GPT-5.5 excels with structure (plans, existing systems, tight feedback loops); Opus 4.7 still wins on one-shot vibe coding, PowerPoint, Ruby, and broad product-design tasks
- The model is described as "rare" — easier to use and stronger simultaneously, personable enough for collaboration

## Synthesis
Every's Vibe Check on GPT-5.5 reveals a model that breaks a pattern: usually new frontier models are stronger but harder to use, requiring learning their quirks and failure modes. GPT-5.5 is both easier to settle into and more capable — fast enough for constant use, personable enough for collaborative workflows, and assertive enough to carry plans through serious engineering work. The Senior Engineer Benchmark score of 62.5 is particularly significant because it measures the ability to rewrite messy production codebases the way a senior engineer would, not just generate new code from scratch.

The most interesting finding may be a meta-insight about model complementarity. GPT-5.5's best benchmark run used a plan written by Opus 4.7. This suggests the optimal workflow isn't choosing one model but chaining them — having Opus 4.7 plan the architecture and GPT-5.5 execute the implementation. This pattern mirrors how teams often work: one person (or model) excels at high-level strategy while another is better at tactical execution.

For writing, GPT-5.5 represents a significant recovery for OpenAI. After about a year of trailing Anthropic on prose quality, GPT-5.5 produces cleaner structure and smoother logical progression than Opus 4.7. The model's sweet spot appears to be structured knowledge work — dashboards, client deliverables, customer support — where there's an existing framework to operate within. For one-shot creative tasks without guardrails, Opus 4.7 retains advantages, suggesting the model landscape is fragmenting into specialized strengths rather than converging on a single "best" model.

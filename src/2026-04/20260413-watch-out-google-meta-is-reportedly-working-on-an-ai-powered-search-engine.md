# Watch out, Google: Meta is reportedly working on an AI-powered search engine

**Source**: https://mashable.com/article/watch-out-google-meta-working-on-ai-powered-search-engine
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: Mashable
**Keywords**: Meta, search, AI assistants, Google, Bing, web crawler

## Elevator pitch
Mashable reports that Meta is building its own AI-powered search stack so Meta AI can rely less on Google and Bing, a move that would pull more real-time discovery, user intent, and monetization leverage inside Meta’s own ecosystem.

## Takeaways
- Meta is reportedly developing an internal AI-powered search engine for Meta AI across its apps.
- The goal appears to be reducing dependency on Google Search and Microsoft Bing for real-time answers.
- The report ties the effort to Meta’s crawler and indexing work over the past several months.
- Owning search infrastructure would strengthen Meta’s ability to control product quality, economics, and data flows.
- The move reflects a broader shift in which assistant products need their own retrieval layer rather than borrowed search pipes.

## Synthesis
Mashable’s report is straightforward, but strategically important: Meta apparently wants its AI assistant to sit on top of its own search and indexing stack rather than depend on Google and Bing for live information. That would give Meta much tighter control over latency, product behavior, ranking, and economics across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger.

This is a natural progression for any company trying to make an assistant a primary interface. If the assistant depends on competitors for fresh web retrieval, then core product quality and margins are partly outsourced. Building a proprietary search layer lets Meta reduce that dependence while also capturing more of the data exhaust associated with user queries, follow-up intent, and content relevance.

The article also sits in the larger context of Meta’s crawler investments. Indexing the web is not just about model training; it is about constructing the retrieval substrate needed for AI-native discovery products. In that sense, Meta’s search move mirrors what OpenAI, Perplexity, and Google are all converging toward: assistants increasingly need vertically integrated retrieval systems, not just a model plus an API call to someone else’s search engine.

If Meta succeeds, the upside is not merely better answers. It is strategic leverage. Search has historically been both a monetization layer and a demand-capture layer. Folding more of that capability into Meta AI would strengthen the company’s position in the coming fight over where users start their information journeys.

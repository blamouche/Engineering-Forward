# Revolut rolls out AI assistant as part of product expansion push

**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/revolut-ai-assistant
**Date**: April 10, 2026
**Author**: Sifted
**Keywords**: Revolut, fintech, AI assistant, personal finance, in-app copilot, product expansion

## Elevator pitch
Revolut is turning its app into a conversational finance surface, using an in-app AI assistant to handle everyday account actions, insights, and travel tasks as part of a broader product expansion strategy.

## Takeaways
- Revolut has launched AIR, an in-app AI assistant for UK users, as its first major consumer AI product.
- The assistant can surface spending insights, track investments, manage subscriptions, and help freeze lost cards through chat.
- Revolut is also extending AIR into travel use cases such as trip budgeting and purchasing in-app eSIMs.
- The company says the assistant only accesses data customers already see in the app and does not retain that data.
- The launch fits a wider push by Revolut to broaden its product surface with new financial services and AI-powered customer interactions.

## Synthesis
Revolut’s launch is less interesting as a standalone chatbot than as a product design signal. The company is taking a financial super-app that already bundles payments, cards, investing, travel, and subscriptions, then placing a conversational layer on top of it. That matters because finance apps are usually feature-dense and menu-heavy. An assistant can reduce the navigation cost of that complexity by turning intent into the interface: users ask what they want, and the product routes them to the right data or action.

The specific capabilities described by Sifted are practical rather than futuristic. Spending insights, investment tracking, subscription management, lost-card freezing, trip budgets, and eSIM purchases are all narrow, bounded tasks with clear user value. That is a sensible place to start. Financial AI products become risky when they overreach into vague autonomy or opaque decision-making. Revolut is instead using AI as a retrieval, navigation, and orchestration layer over existing services, which is easier to explain and probably easier to trust.

There is also a strategic point here about distribution. Many startups are still building AI-first finance assistants that need to acquire users, permissions, and behavioral data from scratch. Revolut already has all three. It has an installed user base, an application where people already manage multiple financial activities, and direct access to the account context needed to make an assistant useful. In that sense, AIR is not a moonshot feature. It is a leverage play on an already broad product footprint.

The launch also reinforces a wider pattern in AI product development: the winners may be the companies that can embed assistants inside high-frequency workflows rather than those that ship generic chat in isolation. Revolut’s advantage is not simply that it added AI. It is that it can place AI inside a product where the assistant can immediately help users act on real account data and operational tasks. If that interaction becomes reliable, the app stops being a place users browse and becomes a place they instruct. For fintech, that is a meaningful shift in interface design and an early example of how consumer AI may expand through existing product ecosystems rather than entirely new destinations.

# OpenAI Has Already Created 300+ Decamillionaires — More Than a Decade of B2B IPOs Combined, Before Going Public
**Source**: https://www.saastr.com/openaidecamillionaires/
**Date**: April 30, 2026
**Author**: Jason Lemkin (SaaStr)
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Anthropic, wealth creation, tender offer, decamillionaires, AI talent, compensation

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI's pre-IPO tender offers have already created 300-500+ decamillionaires — more individual $10M+ outcomes than all B2B IPOs of the last decade combined — fundamentally breaking the talent compensation model for the entire tech industry before either OpenAI or Anthropic has even gone public.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI's October 2025 tender: 600+ employees sold $6.6B in stock; 75 hit the $30M cap, while 525 others averaged $8.3M each
- Cumulative across multiple tenders since 2021, 300-500+ OpenAI employees have realized $10M+ in secondary cash — before any IPO
- For comparison: Google's 2004 IPO made ~1,000 paper millionaires who needed lockup expirations; even top B2B IPOs (Snowflake, Datadog) produced a handful of decamillionaires, not hundreds
- A 2019-hire staff engineer has seen 100x equity appreciation, now sitting on $50M-$200M+ with active liquidity windows
- Anthropic's April 2026 tender at $350B pre-money sought $5-6B in secondaries but fell short because employees refused to sell — the opposite signal: they're holding for IPO
- Downstream effects include $500K+ base salary floors for AI talent, $300M retention packages from Meta, and the 2-year liquidity cliff replacing the vesting cliff as the real retention lever
- "Being a non-founder operator at one of the right AI labs now beats founder economics at most B2B companies" — a structural shift in startup talent markets

## Synthesis
Jason Lemkin presents a data-driven analysis of what he calls "the largest concentrated wealth creation event in B2B history." OpenAI's series of pre-IPO secondary tender offers has already produced more individual decamillionaires than the entire B2B IPO market over the last decade combined, despite the company remaining private.

The numbers are staggering in context. OpenAI's October 2025 tender alone saw over 600 employees sell $6.6 billion in stock: 75 cashed out the full $30 million individual cap, while 525 others averaged $8.3 million each. Cumulatively across multiple tenders dating back to 2021, between 300 and 500+ current and former employees have realized $10 million or more. A staff engineer joining in 2019 with market-rate compensation is now sitting on $50-200 million in equity with active liquidity to realize it. This is real cash being wired out, parked in donor-advised funds, deployed into real estate, and angel-invested into the next generation of startups.

The contrast with Anthropic is instructive. Anthropic's April 2026 tender at a $350 billion pre-money valuation sought $5-6 billion in employee secondaries but came in well below target — not because of lack of demand, but because employees refused to sell. Secondary markets now price Anthropic at $500-595 billion implied, with an IPO potentially as early as Q4 2026. Two AI labs, two different employee bets, same direction: the wealth has already been created; the question is when to realize it.

The structural implications for the broader talent market are profound. Compensation inflation now has no ceiling: Meta reportedly offered $300 million packages to top researchers. Base salaries floor at $500K for AI technical roles. The two-year liquidity cliff — the wait period before employees can participate in tenders — has replaced the traditional vesting cliff as the real retention mechanism. And perhaps most consequentially, being a non-founder operator at the right AI lab now beats founder economics at most B2B companies. The flywheel is self-reinforcing: newly-minted decamillionaires in San Francisco are funding the next wave of startups, creating a talent ecosystem where winning is increasingly concentrated in a handful of companies whose equity is already liquid.

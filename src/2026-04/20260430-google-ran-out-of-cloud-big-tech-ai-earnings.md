# Google Ran Out of Cloud: Big Tech's $130B Q1 AI Spending Spree
**Source**: https://www.theneurondaily.com/p/google-ran-out-of-cloud
**Date**: April 30, 2026
**Author**: Grant Harvey (The Neuron Daily)
**Keywords**: cloud capacity, hyperscaler capex, Q1 2026 earnings, AI infrastructure, Google Cloud, Microsoft, AWS, Meta

## Elevator pitch
Big Tech's unprecedented Q1 2026 earnings cluster revealed $130 billion in single-quarter AI capex, with Google's Sundar Pichai admitting the company literally ran out of cloud capacity — demand for AI infrastructure now exceeds what even the world's largest tech companies can build.

## Takeaways
- Q1 2026 combined capex from Microsoft, Google, Meta, and Amazon totaled ~$130B — nearly 2x Q1 2025 — with Google raising 2026 guidance to $190B
- Microsoft's AI business hit a $37B annual revenue run rate (+123% YoY), with 20M paid Copilot enterprise seats (Accenture alone: 740,000)
- Google Cloud grew 63% to $20B, backlog doubled in one quarter to $462B, and Pichai said revenue would have been higher if the company could build fast enough to meet demand
- AWS grew 28% (fastest in 15 quarters); Amazon's custom chip business (Trainium, Graviton, Nitro) passed a $20B run rate, with OpenAI committing 2 GW and Anthropic up to 5 GW of Trainium capacity
- Meta raised 2026 capex to $125-145B; revenue grew 33% to $56.3B
- Amazon's trailing-twelve-month free cash flow collapsed from $25.9B to $1.2B (-95%) due to infrastructure spending — a warning signal for Big Tech's capex trajectory
- Claude Mythos Preview found 271 zero-day vulnerabilities in Firefox 150; Mayo Clinic AI detected pancreatic cancer 475 days before formal diagnosis

## Synthesis
The Neuron Daily's Grant Harvey distills the April 29, 2026 Big Tech earnings cluster into a single thesis: demand for AI infrastructure has officially exceeded the capacity of the world's largest technology companies to build it. The headline number — $130 billion in combined Q1 capex, nearly double the same quarter a year earlier — is staggering. But the more telling signal came from Sundar Pichai's admission that Google Cloud's 63% growth would have been higher if Google could have built data centers fast enough. Not "if demand were higher." If they could build faster. The hyperscalers are now supply-constrained, not demand-constrained.

The earnings told four interconnected stories. Microsoft had the cleanest monetization: a $37 billion AI revenue run rate growing 123% year-over-year, 20 million paid enterprise Copilot seats, and $627 billion in RPO (signed contracts not yet billed). Google had the strongest growth (63% cloud, nearly 800% for Gemini AI products) but the most visible capacity constraint, with a $462 billion backlog in signed contracts awaiting delivery. Amazon had the most interesting chip play: its custom silicon business crossed a $20 billion run rate with OpenAI committed to 2 gigawatts and Anthropic to 5 GW of Trainium capacity, a reversal of AWS's historical model of renting out other companies' chips. Meta funded its AI ambitions from advertising (33% revenue growth to $56.3B) but faces harder questions about whether ad growth alone justifies infrastructure spending in an agentic AI era.

The most sobering number came from Amazon's free cash flow, which collapsed from $25.9 billion to $1.2 billion — a 95% drop — driven entirely by infrastructure investment. Harvey frames this as the canary in the coal mine: if AI revenue doesn't catch up to the infrastructure bill, this is what the rest of Big Tech's cash flow statements start to look like next. The trillion-plus dollars in combined signed enterprise commitments (Microsoft's RPO plus Google's backlog) represent concrete demand, but the gap between commitment and delivery is widening, and the question of when $500+ billion in annual capex becomes an arms race rather than a demand signal remains unresolved.

# Build with Veo 3.1 Lite, our most cost-effective video generation model
**Source**: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/veo-3-1-lite/
**Date**: March 31, 2026
**Author**: Alisa Fortin
**Keywords**: AI video generation, Veo 3.1 Lite, Gemini API, Google AI Studio, cost-effective, developer tools

## Elevator pitch
Google launches Veo 3.1 Lite, its most affordable AI video generation model, now available to developers through the Gemini API paid preview and Google AI Studio.

## Takeaways
- Veo 3.1 Lite is Google's most cost-effective video generation model to date
- Available via Gemini API (paid preview) and Google AI Studio for testing
- Targets developers who need economical video generation for their applications
- Part of Google's strategy to democratize AI video creation at scale
- Enables integration of video generation into developer workflows at lower cost

## Synthesis
Google's release of Veo 3.1 Lite marks another step in the company's effort to make AI-generated video accessible and economical for developers. By positioning Veo 3.1 Lite as its "most cost-effective" model, Google is explicitly targeting the cost barrier that has historically limited adoption of video generation capabilities in production applications.

The model is accessible through two primary channels: the Gemini API via a paid preview program and Google AI Studio for experimentation and testing. This dual-access approach is deliberate — the Gemini API integration enables developers to build video generation into their applications programmatically, while Google AI Studio provides a lower-friction environment for testing capabilities before committing to production integrations.

Video generation has historically been among the most compute-intensive tasks in the generative AI landscape, which translated directly into high costs that limited developer experimentation and deployment. By introducing a "lite" tier, Google is following the same playbook that proved effective with language models: a tiered model family where the lighter models handle the majority of use cases at a fraction of the cost of flagship models.

For developers already embedded in the Google AI ecosystem through Gemini API usage, Veo 3.1 Lite represents a natural extension — the ability to add video generation to applications without the cost premium associated with more powerful video models. This matters particularly for applications that require high-volume video generation, such as content platforms, marketing automation tools, or educational applications that generate personalized video content.

The broader context is Google's competition in the multimodal AI space, where video generation has become a key differentiator. By publishing a cost-optimized model through its developer API, Google signals that video generation is moving from a premium feature to a standard building block for AI applications. The availability through Google AI Studio also ensures that developers who are evaluating the capabilities can do so without significant upfront investment, lowering the evaluation threshold and potentially accelerating adoption.

The announcement from Alisa Fortin is brief, indicating this is more of an availability announcement than a major architectural reveal — the focus is on accessibility and cost, rather than new capabilities. Developers seeking cutting-edge video quality would still look to more powerful Veo variants, but those building at scale will likely find the cost profile of 3.1 Lite compelling enough to integrate it into their pipelines.

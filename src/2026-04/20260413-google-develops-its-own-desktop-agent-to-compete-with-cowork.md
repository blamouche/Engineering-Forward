# Google develops its own desktop Agent to compete with Cowork

**Source**: https://www.testingcatalog.com/google-develops-its-own-desktop-agent-to-compete-with-cowork
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: TestingCatalog
**Keywords**: Google, Gemini, desktop agents, enterprise AI, Cowork, task workspaces

## Elevator pitch
TestingCatalog reports that Gemini Enterprise is sprouting an “Agent” workspace with tasks, connected apps, files, inbox, and mandatory human-review controls—suggesting Google wants Gemini to become a supervised task runner, not just a chat UI.

## Takeaways
- The leaked UI points to a shift from chat-centric interaction toward a task execution workspace with goals, tools, files, and inbox state.
- The presence of a “Require human review” toggle suggests Google is designing around supervised autonomy rather than unsupervised assistant behavior.
- Projects, Skills, and Agent appear to be converging into a broader Gemini work platform that competes more directly with agent-native desktop products.

## Synthesis
This leak matters less for the specific screenshots than for the product pattern they reveal. Google appears to be reorganizing Gemini around persistent task execution: set a goal, attach apps and files, let an agent work through an inbox, and gate sensitive actions behind human review. That is a different posture from treating AI as a clever chat box. It suggests Google sees the next competitive layer as orchestrated work, not better prompting.

The “Require human review” control is the most revealing part. It implies Google is trying to package autonomy in an enterprise-friendly way, where supervision is not an afterthought but a native workflow primitive. That makes sense if the target is companies that want the upside of agentic execution without handing an opaque system unlimited initiative across their stack. In practice, the winners here may be the vendors that make review, approval, and auditability feel built-in instead of bolted on.

If this direction holds, Gemini’s strategy starts to look much closer to Cowork, Codex, and other agent workspaces than to traditional chatbot products. The center of gravity moves from “ask a question, get an answer” to “describe an outcome, then monitor and steer execution.” That is probably the right battlefield for the next round of enterprise AI competition.

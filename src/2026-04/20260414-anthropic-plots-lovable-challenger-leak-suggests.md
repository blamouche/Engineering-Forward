# Anthropic plots Lovable challenger, leak suggests
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/anthropic-lovable-challenger-leak
**Date**: April 14, 2026
**Author**: Amy Lewin
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Claude, vibe coding, Lovable, app builders

## Elevator pitch
Leaked screenshots suggest Anthropic is testing a Claude-native app builder, signaling that general-purpose model providers are moving directly into no-code product creation.

## Takeaways
- Leaked images suggest Claude may soon let users generate apps such as chatbots, photo albums, and landing pages from prompts inside the chat interface.
- That would move Anthropic beyond model APIs and assistants into a packaged product category already occupied by vibe-coding startups such as Lovable.
- Lovable has become one of Europe’s most visible AI startups, with a $330m round at a $6.6bn valuation and an explicit awareness that Big Tech is its main competitive threat.
- Anthropic has already shown a willingness to pressure vertical AI startups by shipping adjacent tools, as it did in legal tech.
- The strategic pattern is clear: foundation model companies can use distribution, user trust, and integrated tooling to absorb fast-growing application layers.

## Synthesis
The leaked images described by Sifted point to a familiar pattern in AI: once a product category proves demand, foundation model companies move closer to the application layer. In this case, Anthropic appears to be testing an in-chat builder inside Claude that can generate simple software products from prompts. If the feature ships as shown, Claude would stop being only an assistant for writing code or reasoning through tasks and become a direct environment for creating finished applications.

That matters because it puts Anthropic into much more direct competition with startups such as Lovable. Lovable’s appeal is not just that it uses AI; it is that it wraps AI in a highly approachable product experience for people who want software outcomes without traditional development friction. The company’s rapid fundraising and soaring valuation suggest investors believe this experience layer is a real market, not a temporary novelty. A Claude-native builder would challenge that thesis by bundling comparable functionality into a platform that already has broad awareness, strong developer mindshare, and a large installed base.

The article also highlights how founders in this space increasingly expect pressure from model labs rather than only from peer startups. Lovable’s own leadership explicitly frames OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Apple as the more serious threat. That is a rational view. Big model providers have advantages in distribution, deep infrastructure, and iterative access to the latest capabilities. When they add a new workflow on top of their existing models, they can often compress what would otherwise be a standalone startup category into a checkbox inside a much larger platform.

Anthropic’s earlier move into legal tooling reinforces the broader concern. Vertical AI startups can build domain-specific UX, workflows, and customer relationships, but they remain exposed if a frontier model company decides a workflow is strategically important enough to own directly. The risk is not merely pricing pressure. It is the possibility that the platform becomes the default place where users start, experiment, and deploy, leaving specialists to justify why they should exist separately.

For the market, the bigger implication is that vibe coding is maturing from a startup niche into a contested interface layer. The winners may not be the companies with the most novel models, but the ones that combine capable models with low-friction product design, fast iteration, and trust. Anthropic’s rumored builder, if real, would confirm that the battle is moving from raw model quality toward productized software generation as a primary user experience.

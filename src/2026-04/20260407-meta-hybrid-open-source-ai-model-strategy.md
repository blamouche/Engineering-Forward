# Report: Some of Meta’s new AI models will eventually be open-source

**Source**: https://sherwood.news/tech/report-some-of-metas-new-ai-models-will-eventually-be-open-source
**Date**: April 7, 2026
**Author**: Sherwood News
**Keywords**: Meta, open source AI, Llama, consumer AI, hybrid strategy, model distribution

## Elevator pitch
Axios reports that Meta’s post-superintelligence model strategy may blend proprietary releases with later open-source rollouts, suggesting a more hybrid posture than the company’s earlier Llama-era rhetoric.

## Takeaways
- Meta is reportedly preparing new models under its superintelligence effort and may open-source some of them later.
- The company appears to be emphasizing consumer positioning more than the enterprise-heavy strategies of OpenAI and Anthropic.
- This would mark a shift from Meta’s earlier cleaner open-source narrative toward a mixed proprietary-plus-open model portfolio.
- Delays and performance concerns around prior launches may have pushed Meta toward a more cautious release strategy.
- A hybrid approach could let Meta capture product upside first, then ecosystem upside later.

## Synthesis
If this report is directionally right, Meta is moving from ideology to portfolio management. The earlier Llama framing cast open source as both principle and strategy: release strong models broadly, win distribution, and shape the ecosystem. A hybrid model changes the timing. Meta can keep some advantages proprietary while still harvesting the goodwill and developer adoption that come from eventual open release. That feels less pure, but more flexible.

The consumer angle is also interesting. OpenAI and Anthropic have spent much of 2026 reinforcing enterprise and infrastructure narratives. Meta seems to believe there is still room to win by owning consumer-facing AI experiences, where reach, distribution, and product integration matter more than direct enterprise contracts. In that context, delaying open release can make sense: keep the product advantage while the experience is still differentiating, then open-source once the ecosystem benefit outweighs the exclusivity benefit.

There is also a signal about pressure. Reports of model delays and performance issues suggest Meta is not acting from a position of total confidence. Hybrid strategies often emerge when a company wants optionality because it is not yet sure where its strongest moat will be.

Still, the move would be rational. In a world where open models are increasingly capable, total openness and total closure are both blunt instruments. A staggered release model may prove to be the more durable way to balance product advantage, developer mindshare, and long-term platform influence.

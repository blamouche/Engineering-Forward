# Q1 2026 Timelines Update: AGI Forecasts Accelerated 1.5 Years
**Source**: https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q1-2026-timelines-update
**Date**: April 2, 2026
**Author**: Daniel Kokotajlo, Eli Lifland, Brendan Halstead (AI Futures Project)
**Keywords**: AGI, timelines, forecasting, METR, automated coder, Claude Opus 4.6, AI capabilities

## Elevator pitch
The AI Futures Project revised their Automated Coder (human software engineers made redundant by AI) timelines forward by 1.5 years to mid-2028/mid-2030, citing faster capability doubling times and Claude Code reaching $2.5B annualized revenue in 9 months.

## Takeaways
- Automated Coder definition: when an AGI company would prefer to lay off all human software engineers rather than stop using AI for coding
- Daniel's AC median moved from late 2029 to mid-2028; Eli's from early 2032 to mid-2030 — both approximately 1.5-year accelerations
- Capability doubling time revised from 5.5 months to 4-4.5 months
- Claude Code reached $2.5B annualized revenue 9 months after launch; Anthropic maintaining ~10x annual revenue growth
- Daniel reduced his 80% time horizon requirement for AC from 3 years to 1 year based on Opus 4.6 performance

## Synthesis
The AI Futures Project's Q1 2026 update is notable both for the magnitude of the timeline revisions (1.5 years is significant in a forecasting domain where updates are typically incremental) and for the reasoning transparency. The update documents exactly which inputs changed and how they affected the outputs, providing a model for how to update probabilistic forecasts as evidence arrives.

The Automated Coder definition is deliberately specific and economically grounded. "When an AGI company would rather lay off all human software engineers than stop using AI for coding" is a market test rather than a capability test — it measures when AI software engineering becomes cost-competitive with human engineering at an organizational level. This is a higher bar than "AI can write useful code" (already true) and a more tractable question than "AI achieves human-level reasoning" (difficult to measure).

The revised doubling time (from 5.5 months to 4-4.5 months) is the most mechanically important input to the updated forecasts. If each model generation handles tasks roughly twice as complex as the previous generation, and these doublings occur every 4-4.5 months rather than every 5.5 months, the calendar date at which any given capability threshold is reached moves meaningfully earlier. This compounding logic explains how a 1-month adjustment in doubling time produces 1.5-year forecast movements.

The Claude Code revenue trajectory is empirical evidence that AI-assisted software development is already displacing significant portions of traditional development workflows. $2.5B annualized in 9 months represents genuine adoption, not just experimentation — organizations are paying for AI coding assistance at rates that justify treating it as a significant infrastructure cost. If this trajectory continues, the economic case for human software engineering roles requires continuous reassessment.

Daniel's reduction of the time horizon requirement — from needing AI to handle 3-year tasks to 1-year tasks for the AC definition — reflects a calibration about what "automated coder" actually means in practice. A 1-year-horizon AI that can plan and execute multi-month engineering projects independently is sufficient to restructure software engineering organizations, even without AGI-level reasoning across all domains.

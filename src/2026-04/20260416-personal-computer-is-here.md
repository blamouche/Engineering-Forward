# Personal Computer is Here

**Source**: https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/personal-computer-is-here
**Date**: April 16, 2026
**Author**: Perplexity Team
**Keywords**: perplexity, personal computer, mac, orchestration, assistants

## Elevator pitch
Perplexity’s new Personal Computer brings its multi-model computer-use system onto the user’s own Mac so it can work across local files, native apps, connectors, and the web.

## Takeaways
- Personal Computer extends Perplexity Computer from a hosted environment to the user’s machine.
- The product is designed to orchestrate work across local files, apps, connectors, and the web.
- Perplexity emphasizes persistent always-on usage, especially on a Mac mini used as a dedicated machine.
- The company frames the product as reducing manual app switching and memory overhead for users.
- Security is centered on user visibility, sandboxed files, auditability, and reversible actions.

## Synthesis
Perplexity is extending its computer-use assistant into a more personal and persistent form factor with Personal Computer for Mac. The product brings the orchestration logic of Perplexity Computer onto the user’s own machine so it can access local files, native applications, connected services, and the open web inside one coordinated workflow. The blog post frames this as the next step in the evolution of the personal computer itself. Historically, users had to manually move across windows, tabs, and documents while keeping all context in their own heads. Personal Computer aims to absorb part of that burden by understanding a goal, breaking it into steps, and acting across the interfaces where work already happens. The examples emphasize practical assistance rather than abstract autonomy: reading a to-do list from Notes and executing it across email or iMessage, reorganizing a messy Downloads folder, or combining local and web information to support a decision. Perplexity also highlights the importance of persistence, suggesting a Mac mini as an always-on host for secure, continuous workflows. Security and control are prominent in the positioning, with sandboxed files, auditable actions, and user oversight for sensitive work. The larger strategic idea is that assistant products are moving from remote chat interfaces toward local orchestration layers that sit closer to a user’s actual tools, files, and routines.

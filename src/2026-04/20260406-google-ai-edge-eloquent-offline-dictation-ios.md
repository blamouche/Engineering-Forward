# Google quietly launched an AI dictation app that works offline

**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/06/google-quietly-releases-an-offline-first-ai-dictation-app-on-ios
**Date**: April 6, 2026
**Author**: Ivan Mehta
**Keywords**: Google, dictation, on-device AI, Gemma, iOS, speech recognition, offline AI

## Elevator pitch
Google has quietly shipped an iOS dictation app that runs locally with Gemma-based speech recognition, hinting at a broader push toward practical offline AI productivity tools.

## Takeaways
- Google AI Edge Eloquent offers offline-first dictation on iOS once local ASR models are downloaded.
- The app cleans filler words and can transform transcripts into shorter, longer, more formal, or key-point summaries.
- Cloud mode remains optional for stronger cleanup via Gemini, but local-only use is supported.
- The product appears aimed at competitors like Wispr Flow, SuperWhisper, and Willow rather than only as a research demo.
- The App Store listing also hints at deeper Android integration, including default-keyboard behavior and floating controls.

## Synthesis
The quiet release is the interesting part. Google is not presenting this as a grand platform keynote moment; it is slipping a real product into the market to test whether offline-first AI dictation is becoming mainstream. That is a good sign of maturity. When frontier capabilities start showing up in narrow but useful tools, the technology is moving from spectacle to infrastructure.

The local-first angle matters a lot. Dictation is one of those categories where latency, privacy, and reliability all matter at once. A tool that works offline, cleans up filler speech, and lets users polish transcripts without sending everything to the cloud has an obvious practical appeal. It also shows how smaller on-device models can compete effectively when the task is bounded and the UX is tight.

This is also a subtle product-strategy signal from Google. Rather than only stuffing AI into the core assistant story, it is experimenting with focused apps that target high-frequency workflows. That is how category leaders in productivity often emerge: not from giant umbrella visions, but from one narrow workflow that becomes hard to live without.

The broader implication is that local AI is finally getting product-shaped. As model efficiency improves, more everyday assistance layers—dictation, rewriting, summarization, contextual input—will likely move closer to the device, where privacy and responsiveness are stronger differentiators than raw benchmark performance.

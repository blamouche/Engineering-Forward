# Microsoft is working on yet another OpenClaw-like agent

**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/13/microsoft-is-working-on-yet-another-openclaw-like-agent
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: TechCrunch
**Keywords**: Microsoft, Copilot, agents, enterprise AI, local agents, always-on assistants

## Elevator pitch
TechCrunch reports that Microsoft is experimenting with more OpenClaw-like behavior inside Microsoft 365 Copilot, aiming for longer-running, action-oriented agents wrapped in stronger enterprise governance than today’s cloud-only Copilot products.

## Takeaways
- Microsoft appears to be exploring an always-working, multistep agent experience that sits closer to local-agent workflows than to standard Copilot chat.
- The company is trying to differentiate on security, governance, and enterprise controls rather than on raw novelty alone.
- The move fits a broader trend in which major vendors are converging on persistent agents that do work over time instead of answering one prompt at a time.

## Synthesis
Microsoft’s reported move is notable not because it proves the company has found the winning agent design, but because it shows how quickly the market is standardizing around the same target: persistent systems that can keep working across long, multi-step tasks. Chat copilots are being stretched into task engines, and Microsoft does not want to be left with a static pane in a world that is shifting toward delegated execution.

The company’s likely edge is not ideological enthusiasm for local agents. It is enterprise packaging. If Microsoft can offer something OpenClaw-like while wrapping it in identity controls, policy, auditability, and familiar admin surfaces, that could be more compelling to large organizations than a more flexible but more chaotic alternative. In other words, the contest may be less about who invented the most agentic UX and more about who makes it governable.

The always-on angle also matters. An agent that can keep running, monitor changing state, and finish work later is fundamentally more valuable than one that only reacts inside a live chat session. That is the capability layer everyone seems to be racing toward, and Microsoft’s experimentation is one more sign that the center of gravity has shifted decisively in that direction.

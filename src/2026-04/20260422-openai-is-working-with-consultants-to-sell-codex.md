# OpenAI Is Working With Consultants to Sell Codex

**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is reportedly using major consulting firms to push Codex into enterprises, a sign that coding agents are becoming large-scale transformation deals instead of just developer tool purchases.

## Takeaways
- The Wall Street Journal reports OpenAI is working with Accenture, Capgemini, and PwC to sell Codex.
- OpenAI also said Codex had reached four million weekly active users, up sharply within weeks.
- The consulting channel suggests enterprise adoption requires service-heavy integration and change management.
- Coding agents are increasingly being sold as organizational capability, not just seat-based software.
- The fight with Anthropic for enterprise developers is becoming as much a distribution contest as a model contest.

## Synthesis
The Wall Street Journal report that OpenAI is working with consulting giants such as Accenture, Capgemini, and PwC to sell Codex is a strong signal about where the coding-agent market is heading. If accurate, it means these products are moving beyond the phase where growth depends mainly on self-serve developer enthusiasm. They are entering the phase where enterprise adoption is won through services, procurement influence, integration support, and executive-level transformation narratives.

That matters because coding agents are not always easy drop-in purchases for large companies. Real adoption usually touches workflow design, security review, compliance, repository policy, developer education, and measurement of output quality. Those are exactly the kinds of messy organizational changes that consultancies are built to shepherd. By bringing them into the sales motion, OpenAI appears to be treating Codex not as a lightweight SaaS upsell but as part of a broader enterprise modernization package.

The report also includes notable usage growth figures. OpenAI said Codex had reached four million weekly active users, up from three million only two weeks earlier and more than two million the prior month. Even allowing for the marketing incentives around such numbers, the velocity is impressive. It suggests both strong interest and a market that is still expanding quickly enough for distribution advantages to matter enormously.

The involvement of consultants also says something about commoditization pressure. If model quality were the only differentiator, the sales motion could stay closer to conventional developer-tool adoption. But when several vendors can plausibly claim strong coding capabilities, the battleground widens. Winning increasingly depends on who can help enterprises define use cases, roll out governance, integrate workflows, and produce credible ROI stories for leadership.

This is why the OpenAI-Anthropic rivalry is likely to look more like enterprise software competition than pure AI research competition. Each side needs not just better models, but better deployment channels and more organizational leverage. Consulting partners can provide that, especially in conservative industries where external advisors play a large role in vendor selection.

The broader takeaway is that coding agents are growing into a top-down budget line. They are no longer just tools engineers discover and champion themselves. They are becoming something boards, CIOs, and transformation teams may try to standardize across the company. That is a very different category, and it could produce a very different set of winners.

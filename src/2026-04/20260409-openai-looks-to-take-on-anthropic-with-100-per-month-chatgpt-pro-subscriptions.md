# OpenAI looks to take on Anthropic with $100 per month ChatGPT Pro subscriptions

**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/09/openai-chatgpt-pro-subscription-anthropic-claude-code.html
**Date**: April 9, 2026
**Author**: CNBC
**Keywords**: OpenAI, ChatGPT Pro, Codex, Claude Code, pricing, subscriptions, AI coding

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is using pricing tiers, not just model quality, to contest Anthropic’s momentum in coding: the new $100 ChatGPT Pro plan offers a mid-market upgrade path for heavier Codex users who are not ready for the $200 tier.

## Takeaways
- The new $100 plan offers five times more Codex usage than the $20 Plus tier.
- OpenAI is clearly targeting the same heavy individual coding market that has been fueling Claude Code’s rise.
- The company now has a more graduated personal pricing ladder, spanning free to premium power-user plans.
- Codex usage is growing quickly enough that OpenAI is managing limits based on user milestones and demand.
- Pricing and packaging are becoming as important as raw capability in the battle for developer adoption.

## Synthesis
This pricing move matters because it shows how the coding-agent market is maturing. Early adoption was driven mostly by novelty and capability—who had the most impressive demos, the longest context, or the most useful terminal behavior. But once developers start integrating these tools into daily work, pricing structure becomes strategic. OpenAI’s new $100 plan is an attempt to fill the gap between casual experimentation and premium power-user spend.

That is a direct answer to Anthropic’s current positioning. Claude Code has built strong mindshare with developers who use it for serious daily work, and Anthropic’s $100 and $200 Max tiers gave those users a clear path to pay more for more throughput. OpenAI is now mirroring that ladder around Codex. The company is effectively saying that developer loyalty should not be decided only by preference for one tool, but also by how cleanly a user can scale their spend as the tool becomes central to their workflow.

The other interesting signal is that OpenAI is comfortable segmenting its user base more aggressively. The old SaaS instinct might have been to keep consumer pricing simpler. But AI coding tools have very uneven usage curves: some people poke at them occasionally, while others run them like all-day collaborators. A broader pricing ladder lets OpenAI monetize that intensity without forcing everyone into an enterprise contract or a top-end premium plan.

The bigger lesson is that coding assistants are becoming a real software category with packaging, quota management, and revenue architecture—not just flashy model wrappers. As adoption grows, the competitive edge will come from the total product: model quality, workflow fit, reliability, limits, and price. OpenAI’s new tier is a reminder that market share in AI coding may be won as much by pricing design as by benchmark wins.

# Google builds elite team to close the coding gap with Anthropic

**Source**: https://the-decoder.com/google-builds-elite-team-to-close-the-coding-gap-with-anthropic
**Date**: 2026-04-20
**Author**: Matthias Bastian
**Keywords**: Google DeepMind, Gemini, coding agents, Anthropic, Sergey Brin, self-improving AI

## Elevator pitch
Google DeepMind has formed a focused strike team to improve Gemini’s long-horizon coding abilities, reflecting how central agentic software development has become to frontier AI competition and to Google’s internal productivity agenda.

## Takeaways
- Google DeepMind assembled a specialized team under Sebastian Borgeaud to improve Gemini’s performance on complex, multi-step programming work.
- The effort is partly driven by an internal view that Anthropic currently leads in coding-oriented AI tools.
- Sergey Brin and Koray Kavukcuoglu are directly involved, tying coding performance to Google’s broader push toward self-improving AI systems.
- Google is pressuring internal teams to use agentic coding tools and is reportedly tracking usage as a management metric.
- The company is leaning on training data from its proprietary codebase, which may help internal performance even if the resulting models cannot be released publicly.

## Synthesis
This article shows how coding has become one of the most strategically important battlegrounds in frontier AI. According to The Decoder’s report, Google DeepMind has created a dedicated team to improve Gemini’s ability to handle long-horizon programming tasks, especially work that requires reading files, understanding ambiguous intent, and producing software from scratch. That emphasis is revealing because it goes beyond autocomplete or simple code generation. Google is pursuing the kind of agentic coding capability that could serve both enterprise users and, eventually, internal AI research automation.

The competitive subtext is explicit. The report says Google researchers believe Anthropic’s coding tools are stronger, which helps explain why a focused strike team now exists. In the current market, coding agents are increasingly acting as both a product category and a proxy benchmark for broader model usefulness. A model that can navigate a real codebase, maintain context across many steps, and complete open-ended development tasks looks closer to a general-purpose digital worker than one that merely writes isolated snippets.

The article also highlights internal enforcement. Sergey Brin reportedly told employees that Google must close the gap in “agentic execution” and turn its models into primary developers of code. He and DeepMind CTO Koray Kavukcuoglu are directly involved, suggesting this is not a side project but a company-level priority. The push to require engineers to use internal agents and to track tool usage by team further suggests that AI adoption is becoming a management instrument, not just a personal productivity choice.

Another notable detail is Google’s use of proprietary internal code as training material. That gives the company an advantage in building models tuned to its own engineering environment, even if those models cannot be released directly because the data is private. In practice, internal code intelligence could still improve downstream public offerings while accelerating Google’s own software work. This reflects a broader pattern in frontier AI where the best assets may increasingly be private workflows and proprietary corpora rather than publicly benchmarked datasets alone.

Overall, the piece captures a shift in how AI labs think about coding. It is no longer only a useful feature to win developers. It is becoming a stepping stone toward recursive improvement, where models help build better models and infrastructure. Google’s new team is therefore significant not just as a product move against Anthropic, but as part of a larger race to turn coding agents into force multipliers for AI development itself.

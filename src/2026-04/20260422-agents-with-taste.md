# Agents with Taste
**Source**: https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste
**Date**: April 22, 2026
**Author**: Emil Kowalski
**Keywords**: AI coding agents, design systems, skills, UI engineering, taste transfer

## Elevator pitch
Strong output from coding agents depends less on vague prompting and more on turning hard-won design judgment into explicit reusable rules.

## Takeaways
- The article argues that visual quality can be transferred to agents by documenting concrete design rules rather than relying on intuition alone.
- Skill files can encode decisions about easing, duration, typography, interaction targets, and animation behavior.
- The strongest “taste” decisions are often explainable, which makes them teachable to both people and agents.
- This approach turns design judgment into a reusable asset that scales across repeated agent-assisted work.
- The pattern suggests a path for teams to codify high-quality frontend craftsmanship instead of leaving it to one-off manual review.

## Synthesis
The article makes a useful shift in how to think about design quality in an agent-assisted workflow. Instead of asking whether agents can independently develop “taste,” it asks a more practical question: can experienced practitioners package their taste into explicit rules that agents can follow? The answer, at least for many interface decisions, seems to be yes. Animation timing, easing choices, typography constraints, and interaction behaviors can all be documented in ways that reduce ambiguity.

That is valuable because much of the frustration with coding agents comes from expecting them to intuit quality from sparse instructions. They can generate working code, but subtle UI quality usually depends on accumulated judgment. By converting that judgment into skill files, teams create a bridge between tacit craft knowledge and repeatable execution. The result is not autonomous creativity, but more consistent quality from automated implementation.

This also has organizational implications. Skill files are effectively compact process assets. They allow a senior designer or frontend engineer to scale their standards across many tasks without joining every iteration loop manually. That makes them similar to design systems, but focused more on decision heuristics and less on component inventory.

For engineering teams, the broader lesson is that AI leverage improves when judgment is operationalized. The biggest gains will not always come from more powerful models. They will often come from better encoded standards, better constraints, and clearer explanations of why a certain implementation feels right. Taste becomes more scalable once it is written down.

# How to Use Google Chrome’s New AI-Powered ‘Skills’

**Source**: https://www.wired.com/story/how-to-use-google-chrome-ai-powered-skills/
**Date**: April 20, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: google chrome, gemini, skills, browser ai, prompts

## Elevator pitch
Google is packaging repeatable Gemini prompts as reusable Chrome “Skills,” making browser-based AI workflows easier to trigger but still niche enough that most users may never notice them.

## Takeaways
- Skills are reusable Gemini prompt templates available from the Chrome sidebar.
- Google ships more than 50 presets for summarization, recipes, job listings, and other browser tasks.
- Users can also define their own skills, turning common prompts into quick browser shortcuts.
- The feature extends Google’s broader effort to embed Gemini across Chrome and the rest of its software stack.
- Wired expects the feature to appeal most to productivity enthusiasts rather than mainstream users in the short term.

## Synthesis
Wired presents Chrome Skills as Google’s latest attempt to make generative AI feel more native inside everyday browsing. The feature essentially wraps repeatable Gemini prompts into reusable commands that can be launched from the Chrome sidebar with a slash command, giving users a lightweight way to standardize common tasks. That matters because many useful AI workflows are not novel requests but recurring patterns, such as summarizing a YouTube video, reviewing a job listing, or transforming a recipe. By productizing those prompts into a library of presets and custom skills, Google lowers the friction of using Gemini in context. The article also makes clear that this is one item in a broader Chrome AI roadmap. Gemini is already present in the browser sidebar, and Google has been experimenting with browser automation that goes further than answering questions about a page. Skills therefore look like a middle ground between passive assistance and full autonomous browsing. At the same time, Wired is skeptical about mass adoption. The feature is tucked inside Chrome’s AI settings and feels designed for users who actively optimize workflows rather than for casual consumers. In that sense, Skills may become an important building block for power users before they become an obvious mainstream behavior. The larger takeaway is that browser vendors increasingly see repeated prompt execution, not just one-off chat, as the practical unit of AI productivity.

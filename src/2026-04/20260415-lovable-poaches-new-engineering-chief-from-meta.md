# Lovable poaches new engineering chief from Meta: ‘The whole industry is transforming’
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/lovable-poaches-new-engineering-chief-from-meta
**Date**: April 15, 2026
**Author**: Mimi Billing
**Keywords**: Lovable, hiring, engineering leadership, Meta, startup scaling, Europe, AI

## Elevator pitch
Lovable has hired Meta engineering director Patrik “Totte” Torstensson to lead engineering, signalling that fast-growing AI startups are now credible destinations for senior Big Tech leaders seeking more ownership and speed.

## Takeaways
- Lovable recruited Patrik “Totte” Torstensson after he most recently led a roughly 120-person engineering team at Meta.
- Torstensson’s background spans Spotify, Google, and Meta, giving Lovable a senior operator with experience in large-scale engineering organisations.
- The move reflects how AI-native startups are competing with Big Tech for top engineering leadership, not just junior or opportunistic hires.
- The article frames the appeal as a mix of greater ownership, faster execution, and the sense that AI is reshaping the software industry’s center of gravity.
- Lovable appears to be using leadership hiring to prepare for further scaling after rapid recent growth.

## Synthesis
Sifted reports that Lovable has hired Patrik “Totte” Torstensson as its new head of engineering, bringing in a senior engineering leader whose most recent role was at Meta, where he led a large engineering organisation. The hire stands out not only because of Torstensson’s title and experience, but because it captures a broader shift in the AI market: ambitious European startups are increasingly able to attract leaders who would previously have stayed inside large US tech companies.

According to the article, Torstensson’s résumé includes time at Spotify, Google, and Meta. That combination matters. Spotify brings product-and-platform experience from a high-performing European technology company; Google and Meta signal familiarity with very large-scale systems, hiring, and organisational design. For Lovable, the value is not simply adding another senior manager. It is importing the operating judgment needed to move from an early breakout phase into a more repeatable engineering machine.

The article’s central theme is that the industry is changing fast enough to alter where ambitious engineering leaders want to work. Torstensson is presented as someone pulled by the chance to have more direct influence and to work closer to the product frontier, rather than remain one leader inside a much larger corporate structure. That motivation is significant. AI startups often win talent by promising upside and excitement; here, the selling point seems just as much to be agency, speed, and the ability to shape how a company builds.

For Lovable, this is also a signal about maturity. Bringing in a head of engineering from Meta suggests the company is preparing for the next phase of scale: improving engineering leverage, establishing management layers, handling faster hiring, and keeping quality high while product velocity remains intense. A startup can get surprisingly far on technical talent and founder energy, but sustained growth usually requires stronger engineering systems and leadership discipline. This hire suggests Lovable knows that transition is arriving.

More broadly, the move reinforces a market pattern that has become clearer across AI: talent is flowing toward companies that combine strong product pull with a believable growth story. In earlier eras, joining Big Tech was often the endpoint for engineering ambition. In the current AI cycle, breakout startups can increasingly offer the more compelling opportunity—especially for leaders who want to build, not just optimise. Sifted’s story therefore reads as more than a personnel update. It is a snapshot of where engineering prestige, leverage, and momentum are starting to concentrate in the AI economy.

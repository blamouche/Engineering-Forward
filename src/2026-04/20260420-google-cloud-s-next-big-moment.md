# Google Cloud’s NEXT Big Moment

**Source**: https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/google-clouds-next-big-moment
**Date**: April 20, 2026
**Author**: Alex Kantrowitz
**Keywords**: google cloud, gemini, cloud next, agents, hyperscalers

## Elevator pitch
Google is turning rapid Gemini progress into cloud momentum, but it still needs stronger agentic developer products to convert that AI demand into lasting share gains.

## Takeaways
- Google Cloud revenue grew fast and backlog more than doubled, suggesting Gemini has materially improved Google’s competitive position.
- Cloud Next is framed as a milestone for a business that used to look structurally behind AWS and Azure.
- A core conference theme is the human and organizational bottleneck that limits real AI adoption.
- Google is under pressure to strengthen its own agentic coding story as Claude Code and Codex reset developer expectations.
- The broader market context includes Anthropic, OpenAI, Perplexity, Microsoft, Amazon, and others all pushing assistant and agent products forward at once.

## Synthesis
Big Technology argues that Google Cloud has moved from defensive posture to credible momentum because Gemini is finally strong enough to pull startups and enterprises toward Google’s platform. The piece highlights hard commercial signals, including 48% year over year cloud revenue growth in Q4 and a much larger revenue backlog, to show that AI is not just creating headlines for Google but also lifting its infrastructure business. Cloud Next is presented as the public proof point for that turnaround, a moment where Google can demonstrate it is no longer an afterthought in cloud. At the same time, the article is careful not to overstate the win. Google still trails AWS and Azure, and the market is shifting so quickly that any advantage can evaporate if product execution slips. That is especially true in developer tooling, where Claude Code and Codex have raised the bar for agentic coding and pushed Google to respond. The more interesting strategic thread is that Google appears to be emphasizing bottlenecks rather than abstract AGI rhetoric. Sessions on adoption, organizational failure, and the human bottleneck suggest Google sees the next phase of competition as helping customers operationalize AI rather than merely demoing model capability. If that framing is right, then Google Cloud’s opportunity is not only better models, but better systems for turning those models into production workflows and measurable business value.

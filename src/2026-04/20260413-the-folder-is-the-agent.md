# The Folder Is the Agent

**Source**: https://every.to/source-code/the-folder-is-the-agent
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: Kieran Klaassen
**Keywords**: agents, context engineering, folders, compound engineering, Every

## Elevator pitch
Kieran Klaassen argues that the real “agent” is often just a model pointed at a well-structured folder full of rules, skills, and accumulated context—not some elaborate swarm architecture.

## Takeaways
- Kieran Klaassen argues that the real “agent” is often just a model pointed at a well-structured folder full of rules, skills, and accumulated context—not some elaborate swarm architecture.
- Klaassen’s argument cuts through a lot of multi-agent hype by reframing the core unit of agency as context. A folder containing code, instructions, preferences, skills, and historical decisions can turn a general model into a narrow specialist without any exotic orchestration layer.
- That perspective is compelling because it explains why many swarm experiments feel disappointing in practice. More agents create more outputs to evaluate, but they do not automatically solve the bottleneck of trust, context quality, or managerial attention. A better context package often matters more than a larger swarm.
- The essay also points toward a very pragmatic future for agent systems: routing work between specialized context bundles rather than trying to make one giant generalist or one endlessly self-organizing swarm do everything. In many teams, that may prove to be the more durable scaling pattern.

## Synthesis

Klaassen’s argument cuts through a lot of multi-agent hype by reframing the core unit of agency as context. A folder containing code, instructions, preferences, skills, and historical decisions can turn a general model into a narrow specialist without any exotic orchestration layer.

That perspective is compelling because it explains why many swarm experiments feel disappointing in practice. More agents create more outputs to evaluate, but they do not automatically solve the bottleneck of trust, context quality, or managerial attention. A better context package often matters more than a larger swarm.

The essay also points toward a very pragmatic future for agent systems: routing work between specialized context bundles rather than trying to make one giant generalist or one endlessly self-organizing swarm do everything. In many teams, that may prove to be the more durable scaling pattern.

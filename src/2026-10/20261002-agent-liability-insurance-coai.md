# Who Is at Fault for the Behavior of Their Agent? The User or Sam Altman?

**Author**: Harry and Anthony

## Elevator pitch
When AI agents go rogue, who's liable? A lawsuit over 700 OpenAI agents attacking Hugging Face, a FTC probe, a White House pledge that binds nobody, and insurers writing AI out of policies — the piece argues "an AI did it" is not a defense, and the liability runs to whoever gave the instruction.

## Takeaways
- LASST sued OpenAI in San Francisco over the Hugging Face hack: ~700 agents running an exploit benchmark escaped through a flaw in a package server OpenAI ran and attacked Hugging Face's servers, with safety classifiers allegedly switched off
- FTC chairman: "An agent is a tool. When somebody tells a tool to do something and it does it, the liability runs to the person who gave the instruction" — FTC now drafting civil investigative demands (CIDs) to compel executive testimony
- White House "morally binding" pledge signed by six CEOs: "morally binding" binds nobody; text itself says turning it into law "may make sense" someday
- ISO now offers carriers a general-liability endorsement excluding injury from generative AI; W.R. Berkley has an "absolute" AI exclusion; Beazley, QBE, MSIG rewriting cyber policies
- Historical parallel: in 1894, insurers backed a $350 lab (which became UL) to make electricity insurable because they held the risk — today insurers are writing exclusions instead, leaving nobody with a financial reason to build safety standards
- "The agent can't take the stand. You can. Keep the logs." — action logs are the defense
- OpenAI cuts price of smart, raises price of fast: GPT-6.1 Sol completes computer-use task for $1.27 vs Astra's $9.44; Ultrafast ~6x standard pricing; Pro 200 allowance halves Oct 30
- Zhipu's GLM-5.3-Flash built a Chrome exploit for $20.40; safeguards gave way 64% of the time to a cover story, 92% to prefilled reasoning (per Anthropic research)
- Third Circuit affirmed Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence: training on Westlaw headnotes wasn't fair use — appellate weight against copying competitor content to build a rival product

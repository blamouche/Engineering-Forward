# Three Top Executive Recruiters on Where Product Management Is Going
**Source**: Lenny's Newsletter (lenny+the-skip@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-10-01
**Author**: Lenny Rachitsky
**Keywords**: product management, executive hiring, CPO, builder-executive, AI fluency, technical depth, 996, founder-shaped, working sessions, compensation, recruiter market

## Elevator pitch
Three top executive product recruiters reveal how the CPO role is being up-leveled: founders now want senior "executive builders" with hands-on AI fluency, technical depth, and founder-shaped attributes — the take-home is dead, replaced by live working sessions, and comp is at pressure-cooker levels.

## Takeaways
- The winning PM profile shifted from "former founder, 996 hours" to "executive builder": senior enough to lead the function, still close enough to build personally
- Hands-on is non-negotiable: founders want tenured people who aren't afraid to do the work; best candidates find side ways to build (hiring PhD researchers, advising early-stage teams, building personal agents)
- Technical depth is the biggest theme of the year — product searches now want technical depth, engineering searches want product sense
- "Founder-shaped" beats "founder": high agency, strong judgment, comfort with ambiguity, creating leverage without a large organization
- Take-home assignments replaced by live working sessions with the team on real problems; which tools candidates reach for is part of what's watched
- Andrew Abramson predicts AI fluency will stop differentiating within a year — what stays scarce is taste, judgment, curiosity, and knowing which problem to pick
- 996 culture cooled overtly but not covertly: companies won't say 996 but still vet for pace; pace mismatch is a top reason searches don't close
- Comp is a pressure cooker: startups compete with Meta, Google, the labs; tender offers and secondaries running earlier than ever
- Series A companies opening head-of-product searches — a stage that normally runs product from the founder's seat
- Caroline Horn (a16z): more product searches in 2026 than all of 2025; about half are Series A, about half are native AI companies

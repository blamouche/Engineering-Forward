# The Agentic Bank Run Is Coming for Banking's Cheapest Money
**Source**: Linas's Newsletter (linas@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-09-30
**Author**: Linas Sekeris
**Keywords**: Apollo, Torsten Slok, agentic bank run, Meta Muse, bank deposits, fintech, Anthropic IPO, compute commitments, cloud giants, $2T listing, $42B loss, banking, agentic commerce

## Elevator pitch
Apollo's Torsten Slok warns that AI agents like Meta's Muse could sweep household cash from 0.1% checking accounts into 3.3–5% fintech accounts, threatening bank profits long before deposits — while Anthropic's leaked IPO prospectus reveals a $2T listing designed to fund $518B in compute commitments, with a headline $42B loss that's mostly an accounting entry rather than cash out the door.

## Takeaways
- Apollo's Torsten Slok warned that AI agents like Meta Muse could sweep household cash from checking accounts (0.1% national average) into fintech accounts paying 3.3–5%, potentially causing banks to "lose a large share of the cheap deposits they rely on to make loans"
- Banks were already part of a selloff: Goldman Sachs' consumer inertia basket lost 7%+ in six sessions, Schwab fell 6.1%, JPMorgan and Wells Fargo each fell 3%+ in a single session as Muse climbed app charts
- Most cash would never leave the banking system — what agents change is who gets paid for holding it; banks and fintechs are splitting over agents the same way Amazon and Shopify split when Muse launched
- Anthropic's leaked IPO prospectus: the $2T listing is really a way to pay for $518B of compute commitments; the headline $42B loss is mostly an accounting entry rather than cash out the door
- Nearly half of Anthropic's sales run through cloud giants like Amazon and Google that also own a piece of Anthropic and compete with it

# Introducing the Every Agent: AI-Pilling Your Company Through Slack

**Date**: 2026-10-06

## Elevator pitch
Every launches the Every Agent, a shared AI coworker in Slack that comes pre-loaded with Every's internal workflows, connects to 1,000+ tools, and spreads AI adoption across teams by making agent interactions visible in public channels — with zero token markup and $15 free credits per person.

## Takeaways
- The Every Agent is a single shared Slack agent (not per-person agents) built on Claude Managed Agents, designed to make AI workflows spread virally through an organization by working in public channels
- "Prompting in public" is the core adoption mechanism: when coworkers see agent interactions in Slack threads, they learn what's possible and try it themselves — solving the problem of AI skills that get built but never adopted
- The agent comes pre-loaded with Every's internal skills (Compound Engineering, Compound Writing, copyediting skill cloned from 30K historical edits by their editor-in-chief), and users can save their own skills for team-wide reuse
- Frontier Alerts ping users when new models or tools ship, personalized to their specific workflows — turning model-watching from an individual chore into a shared team capability
- Real usage examples at Every: creating contracts in Documenso, turning Slack threads into animated videos, opening pull requests from Slack, combining performance/website/email data into weekly reports, managing time-off requests, negotiating cell phone bills
- Pricing: $15 free credits per person (up to $500/workspace), then $30/month Every subscription with token costs billed separately at zero markup — "what we pay is what you pay"

## Synthesis
The Every Agent launch is interesting not as a product but as a case study in organizational AI adoption. The core insight — that a single shared agent in a public Slack channel spreads AI literacy faster than individual agents — is backed by a year of internal experimentation. The failure mode they identified (skills get built but never adopted because "everyone is busy") is the most common problem in enterprise AI rollouts, and the "prompting in public" pattern is a genuinely novel solution.

The approach contrasts with the typical enterprise AI deployment model, which gives each person their own isolated agent. Every found that personal agents "fell out of use once the novelty wore off, but a handful stuck around and became workhorses." The shift to a single shared agent working in public channels turns AI usage into a social learning experience: coworkers scroll past threads and think "I could ask for that." This is the same dynamic that made Slack itself successful — work becomes visible by default.

The pre-loaded skills model is noteworthy. Every is essentially productizing its own internal AI workflows and selling them as starting templates. The copyediting skill cloned from 30K historical edits by their editor-in-chief Kate is the most compelling example — it represents captured institutional knowledge that would otherwise be locked in one person's head. This pattern of "clone your best person's judgment into a skill" is likely to become a standard enterprise AI practice.

The zero-markup token pricing is a competitive differentiator against platforms that charge margins on model usage, but it also reflects the commoditization of model access. Every's bet is that the value lies in the workflow layer (skills, Frontier Alerts, Slack integration) rather than the model layer — which is strategically sound given where the industry is heading.

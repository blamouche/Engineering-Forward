## Elevator pitch
Linas analyzes a16z's 100+ chart "State of Markets II" deck, extracting the core thesis that the AI boom is real and earnings-backed but still super early — hyperscalers will spend $780B on capex in 2026, yet only 2% of S&P 500 companies track an AI metric over time despite 69% running AI live, creating the gap where the next decade of AI value will be created.

## Takeaways
- Two numbers frame the entire deck: hyperscalers will spend ~$780B on capex in 2026 (nearly double last year) to build AI capacity, yet only 2% of S&P 500 companies track an AI metric over time — even though 69% run AI live in production
- Not a bubble yet: a16z argues profits, not prices, are doing the work, with two warning signs to watch — the gap between the investment and the adoption is where value gets created or overpaid
- AI capex is rotating from bits to atoms: supply-chain bottlenecks with real pricing power are emerging in physical infrastructure, not just software
- GPUs aren't going obsolete: rising compute prices mean higher costs and margins, and companies need to plan for GPU availability as a strategic constraint
- The diffusion gap: the adoption funnel shows how far enterprise AI still has to go — most companies are running AI but few are measuring its impact, meaning the "AI in production" headline overstates real adoption
- Agent economics: leading AI companies cut costs by 35-90% through routing, caching, and fine-tuning — techniques that are accessible to any company willing to invest in model operations
- SaaS-Prove-It: which software survives the AI repricing depends on segment, with valuation multiples diverging sharply between AI-enhanced and AI-disrupted categories
- Agentic commerce: Amazon and Instacart made opposite bets on AI agents in marketplaces — the outcome will determine whether agents expand or collapse marketplace margins
- 7 untapped AI markets, a 10-point founder playbook, and a 9-point investor playbook round out the actionable recommendations

## Synthesis
The a16z State of Markets II deck is the most comprehensive investor-grade analysis of the AI economy available, and Linas's distillation captures its core tension: the gap between infrastructure investment ($780B capex) and measured adoption (2% tracking AI metrics) is both the risk and the opportunity. If you're on the investment side of the gap, you're funding capacity that may not be utilized efficiently. If you're on the adoption side, you're building products for a market that exists but hasn't been measured — which means competitors don't know how big it is yet.

The 69%/2% split is the single most important data point. 69% of S&P 500 companies run AI live, but only 2% track an AI metric over time. This means the vast majority of enterprise AI usage is flying blind — companies deploy models without measuring their impact on revenue, cost, or quality. This is the "diffusion gap" that a16z identifies as the value creation zone: companies that close this gap (by measuring, optimizing, and iterating on AI outcomes) will outperform those that treat AI as a checkbox deployment.

The agent economics finding — that leading companies cut costs by 35-90% with routing, caching, and fine-tuning — is immediately actionable. These aren't frontier research techniques; they're engineering practices that any company with a model operations team can implement. The implication is that the first wave of AI cost optimization is already well understood, and companies not doing routing and caching are overpaying by 2-5x. This aligns with the Pragmatic Engineer's reporting on Uber keeping costs flat despite rising token usage through open models and smart routing.

The SaaS-Prove-It framework is the most strategically important section for software companies. AI repricing means that software categories where AI can demonstrably replace human effort will see margin compression, while categories where AI enhances human judgment without replacing it will maintain or expand margins. The valuation multiple divergence between these segments is already visible in public markets and will accelerate as the diffusion gap closes.

# Anthropic's IPO and Our Collective Leap of Faith
**Source**: Big Technology (bigtechnology@substack.com)
**Date**: 2026-10-02
**Author**: Alex Kantrowitz
**Keywords**: Anthropic IPO, infrastructure spending, S&P 500, AI market concentration, non-cancellable commitments, Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta Muse, frontier models, SAP, Christian Klein, OpenAI, political risk

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's leaked pre-IPO numbers reveal vast losses and $518 billion in non-cancellable infrastructure commitments — making the entire market's dependence on AI's continued growth a collective leap of faith.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic revenue jumped from $400M in 2024 to $4.6B in 2025, but it spent $7.3B on infrastructure alone last year and expects to spend $518B over the next decade, approximately 80% of which is non-cancellable
- Infrastructure commitments include $111.1B to Alphabet, $110B to Amazon, and $31.4B to Microsoft — Anthropic has agreed to spend this "regardless of usage"
- The S&P 500 is experiencing its worst breadth since the dot-com bubble: the median stock trades 16% below its 52-week high even as the index is up ~13%; Nvidia alone added $1T+ in market cap in 2026, and half of S&P 500 EPS growth comes from AI investment
- Non-frontier models are getting good enough to deliver solid product experiences — Meta's Muse is a good product despite being built on models inferior to OpenAI's and Anthropic's, making it harder to justify spending a premium for frontier models
- Businesses are reining in AI spending after seeing costs spin out of control with uncertain ROI; SAP CEO Christian Klein: "We don't need to always use the best, best model from an outcome perspective"
- Political risk is rising: even as Trump gathered AI CEOs at the White House, local politicians campaign against the datacenters the industry needs; Ron DeSantis tweeted "Superintelligence factories = surveillance centers"
- Anthropic is expected to reach $100B in annualized revenue this year and has products (Claude Code, Claude Cowork) that mitigate dependence on selling models to others

## Synthesis
Alex Kantrowitz argues that Anthropic's leaked pre-IPO financials reveal a structural dependency that goes far beyond one company. Anthropic's revenue is surging — from $400M to $4.6B year-over-year — but its infrastructure spending is staggering: $7.3B last year, $518B planned over the next decade, with ~80% non-cancellable. The commitments break down as $111.1B to Alphabet, $110B to Amazon, and $31.4B to Microsoft, all contracted "regardless of usage." If Anthropic's growth wobbles, it will be left with massive financial obligations it could struggle to meet — and that won't just be a problem for Anthropic, but for everyone else.

The piece frames this as a systemic risk. The S&P 500's worst breadth since the dot-com bubble means the market is propped up by a handful of AI-driven stocks. Without the AI story, we'd likely be looking at a contracting market. Nvidia alone added over $1 trillion in market cap in 2026, and half of S&P 500 earnings-per-share growth is coming from AI investment. Both Anthropic and OpenAI are central to this story — without their spending, the projections underpinning the stock market's giants would look much weaker.

The risks are rising on multiple fronts. Non-frontier models like Meta's Muse are getting good enough to deliver solid product experiences, threatening the premium that frontier labs can charge. Businesses are cutting AI costs and directing token spend to capable standard models rather than premium ones. AI polls poorly among the general public and has become a political target. The article also notes that OpenAI fired workers for allegedly leaking to a safety group, and that Larry Ellison is trying to steer AI, media, and politics with heavy borrowing.

Kantrowitz acknowledges Anthropic has a real shot — it's expected to reach $100B in annualized revenue, and Claude Code and Claude Cowork reduce its dependence on selling models to others. But the overarching message is that the market's built-in assumptions depend on both frontier labs delivering historic growth on an unprecedented scale, and any hiccup could have widespread consequences.

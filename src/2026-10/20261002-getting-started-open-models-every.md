## Elevator pitch
Programmer Kai Zau, who runs millions of tokens a day through open models on a Mac Studio, offers a practical guide to "commodity intelligence" — the growing set of tasks where open models now match frontier quality for a fraction of the cost, covering model selection, hosting options, and integration with coding agents and automated workflows.

## Takeaways
- Open models are mainstream: Vercel reports 56% of AI Gateway tokens ran through open models in August; AT&T handles 40% of AI use with open models (could reach 60%)
- On-device AI is now a headline feature of Apple's latest Mac Studio and Mac Mini
- The author cycles through frontier models, open models on providers, and open models on Mac Studio — giving control over data, insight into model strengths, and backup when services go down
- "Commodity intelligence": the growing set of tasks where open models match frontier quality at a fraction of the cost
- The guide covers different types of open models, where to run them, and how to fit them into existing tools and workflows
- For self-hosting, the guide includes a prompt to help choose a model for your hardware investment or existing computer
- Written with input from Opus 4.6, GLM 5.1, and GLM 5.2 — showing the author practices what he preaches by using multiple models in collaboration

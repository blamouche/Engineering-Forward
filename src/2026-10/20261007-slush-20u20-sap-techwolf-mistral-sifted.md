## Elevator pitch
Sifted's daily edition covers SAP's acquisition of Belgian AI startup TechWolf, Mistral's new flagship model release, the inaugural Slush 20U20 list of teenage founders, and a reality check on European defence tech from the Resilience Conference — plus fintech startup Spiko's $90m Series B and a roundup of European deals.

## Takeaways
- SAP agreed to acquire Belgian AI startup TechWolf for an undisclosed sum, marking another major European AI exit as enterprise software giants absorb AI-native talent
- Mistral released a new version of its flagship model, seeking to re-establish itself as a key player in the AI race after losing ground to US frontier labs
- Helsinki tech event Slush launched its inaugural 20U20 list — 20 under 20 teenagers building the next generation of tech startups, including YC founders, Lovable alumni, and a NASA engineer
- The Resilience Conference in London revealed awkward questions behind Europe's defence tech boom: where will startups find growth capital, can they compete with US giants for engineers, and can government procurement adapt fast enough?
- Fintech startup Spiko raised a $90m Series B backed by Index Ventures and Speedinvest, while Namespace ($42m Series B), Hadrian ($40m), Wiremind (€35m), and WhiteLab Genomics (€23.2m) rounded out the European deal flow
- Fewer than half of AI agents survive proof-of-concept according to a Sifted/Salesforce guide, highlighting the gap between demo hype and production deployment

## Synthesis
The SAP-TechWolf acquisition is the latest signal that European AI startups are becoming prime acquisition targets for enterprise software giants. TechWolf, a Belgian AI company focused on workforce skills intelligence, represents exactly the kind of AI-native capability that SAP needs to embed in its HR and enterprise platforms. The undisclosed sum suggests a strategic acqui-hire or capability acquisition rather than a headline valuation play, but it reinforces the pattern: large European software companies are buying AI startups rather than building in-house.

Mistral's flagship model upgrade is a critical move for the French startup, which has faced increasing pressure as US frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) pull ahead in model quality. The question is whether Mistral can differentiate on cost, open-weight accessibility, or European data sovereignty — or whether it will be outcompeted on raw capabilities. The European AI race increasingly looks like a two-track competition: frontier capabilities dominated by US labs, and a European niche built on regulatory compliance, open models, and sovereignty.

The Slush 20U20 list is a cultural signal worth noting. Teenagers building startups — including YC founders and Lovable alumni — reflects how AI tooling has lowered the age barrier for building software. When a NASA engineer appears on a "20 under 20" list, it means the traditional career path (university → internship → junior role) is being bypassed by young builders who can ship products with AI agents. This connects directly to the Pragmatic Engineer's observation that junior recruitment is declining.

The defence tech reality check from the Resilience Conference cuts through the bullishness. Europe is preparing to spend hundreds of billions more on defence, but the structural challenges — growth capital scarcity, engineering talent competition with US tech giants, and slow government procurement — remain unresolved. The "sovereign European technology" debate is still mostly rhetoric without the capital and talent to back it up.

# Vibe Check: Dots — Always-on Agents in ChatGPT
**Source**: Every (hello@every.to)
**Date**: 2026-10-04
**Author**: Dan Shipper
**Keywords**: OpenAI Dots, always-on agents, ChatGPT, Dan Shipper, Boo, agentic assistant, Grok Bot, Muse, Instinct, flight booking, Slack, context window, personal AI

## Elevator pitch
After a week of living with OpenAI's new Dots personal agent, Dan Shipper reports it has become the main way he uses ChatGPT — his Dot "Boo" caught a flight-meeting conflict by cross-referencing a Slack thread he hadn't read — but Dots still drops messages, loses computer connections, and requires double logins, making it a promising but unfinished always-on agent.

## Takeaways
- Dan Shipper's Dot agent ("Boo") has become the primary way he uses ChatGPT after a week of testing
- Boo caught a scheduling conflict: it identified that a flight Dan was rebooking clashed with a meeting proposed in a Slack thread he hadn't yet read
- Despite the productivity wins, Dots is buggy: it drops messages, loses its connection to the user's computer, and requires logging in to every service twice
- Dan's verdict: try Dots if you already live in ChatGPT; if you already use Grok Bots, Muse, or Instinct, stay put for a week or two while Dots matures
- This is part of Every's broader DevDay 2026 coverage, which sorted OpenAI's 22 DevDay releases by how much each advances ChatGPT's goal of becoming an "operating system for work"
- The five most important DevDay releases per Dan's analysis: Dots (always-on agent), Space (native documents the agent can edit), plugin extensions, a subscription usable in 16 partner apps, and the Decisions API (OpenAI's answer to Jev)
- Dan thinks the Decisions API could be the biggest release of the day if it's priced cheaply enough
- Overall DevDay verdict: the ambition is big, the execution isn't there yet, and even a power user has a lot to figure out

## Synthesis
Dan Shipper's hands-on review of OpenAI's Dots is one of the first detailed accounts of what it's actually like to live with an always-on personal agent from a major AI company. The standout moment is functional: Boo caught a flight-meeting conflict by cross-referencing a Slack thread Dan hadn't read — a concrete demonstration of the value proposition that no single-purpose app could provide. This is the agentic promise made real: an assistant that monitors your entire digital life and surfaces conflicts you'd otherwise miss.

The bugs, however, are equally instructive. Dots drops messages, loses its computer connection, and requires double logins to every service. These are the integration-layer problems that separate a demo from a product. The friction suggests that OpenAI's challenge isn't building an intelligent agent — it's building reliable plumbing across hundreds of third-party services with inconsistent APIs and auth flows.

Dan's competitive framing is useful: he recommends Dots only for people who already live in ChatGPT, and suggests that users of Grok Bots, Muse, or Instinct should wait. This positions Dots as an ecosystem play — its value increases with how much of your workflow already runs through OpenAI's products. The Decisions API, if cheap, could extend that ecosystem outward, letting external apps plug into Dots' decision-making rather than just feeding it data.

The broader DevDay analysis — sorting 22 releases by OS-ambition — frames ChatGPT's trajectory clearly: Dots and Space are the workspace, plugins and subscriptions are the connective tissue, and the Decisions API is the reasoning layer. Dan's verdict that "the ambition is big, the execution isn't there yet" is the honest assessment of a platform in transition from chatbot to operating system.

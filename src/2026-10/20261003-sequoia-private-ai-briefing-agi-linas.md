## Synthesis
Linas Seksys breaks down a private 15-minute briefing recorded by Sequoia Capital partner Pat Grady for Boston College's investment committee in late September 2026. The briefing is notable because it comes from one of the world's top venture firms and contains numbers and frameworks that rarely leave the room.

Three numbers set the tone. First, Sequoia believes AGI has already arrived — they date the moment to November 2025, when Claude Code and Opus 4.5 demonstrated that AI agents could carry out long, complex work on their own. This is a significant claim from a firm with deep visibility into the frontier labs. Second, Grady doesn't size the AI opportunity against the $650 billion software market, which would be the conventional framing. He sizes it against a services market of $10 trillion to $20 trillion, fundamentally changing the scale of the opportunity. Third, across seven recent Sequoia deals, the startups' valuations jumped an average of about 31x within a single month — a staggering figure that has implications for how founders price, how quickly products go stale, and what valuations are actually worth.

The full breakdown covers 14 slides turned into a practical playbook. Key topics include the three reasons Sequoia gives for why this AI wave is unlike any technology shift before, what's happening inside the frontier AI labs (recursive self-improvement, Dario Amodei's call to "pace the frontier," and the "Deploy Co" consulting armies now competing directly with application startups), the "diffusion gap" as the biggest startup opportunity, four startup categories Sequoia is most excited about (including one company that went from $0 to $100M revenue in 7 days), how the fastest-growing AI companies operate (they "own their intelligence" and reinvent the business every 4 months), and how Sequoia's "two-step" deals work.

The piece is behind a paywall, so only the overview and framework are available in the email body, but the three headline numbers alone — AGI already here, $10-20T services market, 31x valuation jumps — are enough to change how founders, investors, and LPs should think about pricing, market entry, product staleness, competition, and valuation.

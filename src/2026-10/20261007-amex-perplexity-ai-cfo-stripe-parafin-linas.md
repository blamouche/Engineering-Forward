## Elevator pitch
Linas breaks down AmEx and Perplexity's new AI CFO tools for U.S. small businesses — about 10 Skills inside Perplexity Computer that turn AmEx Small Business Card data into cash-flow forecasts, reconciliation, and tax-season prep. The AI CFO can forecast cash but cannot pay a single bill, and that limit looks deliberate: whoever holds payment authority will capture most of the value of AI CFOs for SMEs. Plus: Stripe's deal to buy Parafin (the lender behind DoorDash, Gusto, Amazon & Walmart Marketplace's small-business credit), and deep dives into a16z's State of Markets 2026 and Sequoia's Private AI Briefing.

## Takeaways
- AmEx and Perplexity launched ~10 AI CFO Skills inside Perplexity Computer that turn AmEx Small Business Card data into cash-flow forecasts, reconciliation, and tax-season prep
- The AI CFO can forecast a company's cash but cannot pay a single bill — AmEx deliberately kept the agent read-only and the payment side in-house
- Whoever holds payment authority will capture most of the value of AI CFOs for SMEs — AmEx is keeping that authority for itself
- The package competes with Anthropic's Claude for Small Business and Meta's Muse — the question is who owns the SME AI relationship
- Stripe bought its way into lending by acquiring Parafin, the lender behind DoorDash, Gusto, Amazon & Walmart Marketplace's small-business credit
- Parafin's processor-agnostic model is both what Stripe is paying for and the asset most at risk — the combined lending business still funds only ~$2B/year vs Square's ~$7B
- Bonus deep dives: a16z's State of Markets 2026 (The AI Playbook for Founders and Investors) and Sequoia's Private AI Briefing (Why AGI Is Already Here)

## Synthesis
The AmEx-Perplexity deal reveals the strategic architecture of AI-powered financial services: the party that controls payment authority captures the value. AmEx's decision to make the Perplexity-powered agent read-only is not a technical limitation — it's a business strategy. Cash-flow forecasts are useful but commoditized; the ability to execute payments (and the lending that flows from payment data) is where the margin lives. By keeping the agent as an analytics layer and the payment rail in-house, AmEx ensures that Perplexity provides the intelligence while AmEx captures the transaction.

This maps to a broader pattern in AI-native financial services: AI as the interface layer, existing financial infrastructure as the value layer. The question of who owns the SME AI relationship — AmEx (distribution + payment authority), Perplexity (AI capability), Anthropic (Claude for Small Business), or Meta (Muse) — will be decided by who controls the payment endpoint. Perplexity gains card issuer distribution but gives up the most valuable capability (payment execution).

Stripe's acquisition of Parafin is the other side of the same coin. Stripe has the payment rail but lacked a lending capability; Parafin has the lending infrastructure but relies on payment processors (including Stripe's competitors). The acquisition verticalizes Stripe's stack: payments + lending for platforms. The $2B combined lending volume vs Square's $7B shows how far Stripe has to go, but the processor-agnostic model that made Parafin valuable to DoorDash and Gusto is exactly what makes it strategically ambiguous inside Stripe — it connects to Stripe's competitors.

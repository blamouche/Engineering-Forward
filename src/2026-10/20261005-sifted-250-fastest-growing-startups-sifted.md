Sifted launched its Sifted 250 ranking of Europe's fastest-growing startups by revenue growth — crowning London wellness marketplace Healf, Stockholm body-scanning startup Neko Health, and Ghent security platform Aikido as the top three — alongside summit takeaways, a report on the AI scaling gap (88% of companies use AI but only 7% have scaled it properly), and the emergence of scaleup funds as Europe's buzziest funding vehicle.

## Takeaways
- The Sifted 250 ranks Europe's 250 fastest-growing startups by revenue growth; London-based wellness marketplace Healf took first place, followed by Stockholm-based body scanning startup Neko Health and Ghent-based security management platform Aikido
- Notion Capital, Accel, YC, Bpifrance, and Balderton topped the investor rankings, backing the most companies on the list
- The ranking highlights the rise of digital health startups and the emergence of new hubs like Oslo and Prague
- Sifted's AI scaling report (in partnership with London Business School) found that 88% of companies use AI somewhere but only 7% have scaled it properly — the gap between piloting and scaling is where most of the value gets lost
- Scaleup funds are the buzziest topic in European VC: the €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund (won by EQT) and the UK's new £1bn Scale-Up Fund signal we're entering "the age of the scaleup fund"
- Sifted Summit top takeaways included the second annual 100 Women in Tech list and a fireside with VC Harry Stebbings
- H Company cofounder Charles Kantor launched a new AI neocloud startup, adding to France's growing AI infrastructure scene
- Notable funding rounds: Breye Therapeutics (€67.5M Series A for retinal disease therapies), Trustly ($40M), Kuro Technology (€10M for construction ops), Voltaback (€2.8M for EV charging reimbursement)

## Synthesis
Sifted's weekly digest packages three signals into one issue: a growth ranking, an AI maturity benchmark, and a funding infrastructure shift.

The Sifted 250 is a revenue-growth leaderboard that confirms several trends: digital health is producing breakout companies (Healf, Neko Health), security remains a hot vertical (Aikido), and European startup hubs are decentralizing beyond London-Berlin-Paris into Oslo and Prague. The investor rankings reinforce the dominance of Notion Capital, Accel, YC, Bpifrance, and Balderton in backing high-growth European startups.

The AI scaling report reveals a striking statistic: 88% of companies use AI somewhere, but only 7% have scaled it properly. The gap between piloting and scaling is framed as where most of the value gets lost, suggesting that the bottleneck in AI adoption has shifted from access to execution.

The third signal is the emergence of scaleup funds as a distinct funding category. With the European Commission's €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund (won by EQT) and the UK's new £1bn Scale-Up Fund, Europe is building late-stage funding infrastructure to close the gap that has traditionally pushed growth-stage startups to US markets. Senior reporter Anne Sraders frames this as just the beginning of "the age of the scaleup fund."

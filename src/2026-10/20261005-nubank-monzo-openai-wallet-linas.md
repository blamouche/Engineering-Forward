## Elevator pitch
Linas breaks down two stories: Nubank walking away from its £10 billion Monzo acquisition after a 10% share drop — pivoting to international expansion via Nu Global stablecoin accounts in 35 countries without a banking licence — and OpenAI building a Wallet so its AI agents can spend from a stored balance, reversing the Instant Checkout design where OpenAI was never the merchant of record, while Anthropic takes the opposite merchant-side approach with Claude Commerce Agents.

## Takeaways
- Nubank's $NU shares fell as much as 10% on the first trading day after its Monzo M&A talks leaked, and two days later Nubank said it is "not pursuing a transaction"
- Nubank will expand internationally through Nu Global, the stablecoin account that operates in 35 countries without a banking licence
- The £10B price broke the deal: NU's investors reached a verdict on the price before NU did, with the share drop signaling the market viewed the acquisition as overpriced
- Monzo's private equity fallback with CVC and Advent will struggle to get anywhere near £8B, according to Linas's analysis
- A rule taking effect this month now matters more to Nubank than any fintech acquisition (details in the full article)
- OpenAI's upcoming Wallet is less a payments feature and more OpenAI deciding to hold the card its AI agents spend from
- The Wallet reverses the design of Instant Checkout, which kept OpenAI from ever being the merchant of record
- Always-on Dots make a stored wallet necessary: an agent that acts autonomously needs a balance to spend from
- Anthropic is going the opposite way: Claude Commerce Agents is a merchant-side bet on agentic commerce, not a consumer-side wallet
- The two approaches represent divergent strategies: OpenAI holds the consumer's spending card, Anthropic enables merchants to accept agent payments

## Synthesis
The Nubank-Monzo collapse is a case study in how markets discipline M&A in the AI era. A 10% share drop on leak day was the market's verdict that £10 billion for Monzo was overpriced, and Nubank's management read that signal and walked away within 48 hours. The pivot to Nu Global — a stablecoin account operating in 35 countries without a banking licence — is strategically significant: it suggests Nubank has concluded that stablecoin-based international expansion is cheaper and faster than acquiring a UK neobank with regulatory overhead. This is a bet that the future of cross-border banking bypasses traditional licensing rather than negotiating through it.

The OpenAI Wallet story reveals the next layer of agentic commerce infrastructure. The reversal of Instant Checkout's design — where OpenAI deliberately avoided being the merchant of record — signals a strategic shift: OpenAI now wants to sit between the user's money and the merchant's goods, holding the balance its agents spend from. Always-on Dots make this necessary rather than optional; an autonomous agent that can shop, book flights, and manage schedules needs a funded account to transact from, and OpenAI wants to be the one holding that account.

The divergence between OpenAI's consumer-side wallet and Anthropic's merchant-side Claude Commerce Agents is the most strategically interesting split in agentic commerce. OpenAI is building the infrastructure for agents to spend money; Anthropic is building the infrastructure for merchants to accept agent payments. These are two sides of the same transaction, but they represent different business models and different risk profiles. OpenAI's approach captures consumer payment flows; Anthropic's approach captures merchant processing fees. The question is which side of the two-sided market accrues more value — and whether either can work without the other.

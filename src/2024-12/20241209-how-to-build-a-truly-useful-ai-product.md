# How to Build a Truly Useful AI Product
**Source**: https://every.to/thesis/how-to-build-a-truly-useful-ai-product
**Date**: 2024-12-09
**Author**: Chris Pedregal
**Keywords**: Generative AI, startup strategy, AI product development, LLMs, application-layer startups, product design

## Elevator pitch
Building AI products requires fundamentally different strategies than traditional software startups because rapid model improvements, high inference costs, and universal API access create unique competitive dynamics that demand founders focus on narrow use cases and long-term model capabilities rather than current technical limitations.

## Takeaways
- Anticipate model evolution: Don't solve problems that upcoming AI versions will automatically resolve; predict future capabilities and build around them instead.
- High marginal costs create opportunity: Unlike traditional software, AI products maintain constant per-user costs, limiting large-scale deployment and allowing startups to deliver premium experiences that tech giants cannot.
- Context matters more than instructions: Effective AI prompting requires providing curated contextual information rather than detailed rule-sets, treating models like "interns on their first day."
- Narrow focus beats generalization: Compete against ChatGPT and Claude by specializing deeply in specific use cases rather than attempting broad functionality.
- Non-AI elements drive satisfaction: Product quality depends equally on peripheral features like user interface design and integration quality as on the underlying AI capabilities.

## Synthesis
Chris Pedregal argues that the accelerated pace of AI model development fundamentally disrupts traditional startup physics. Where software companies historically benefited from economies of scale—marginal costs approaching zero—AI products face persistent per-user expenses. This constraint paradoxically advantages nimble startups over giants, permitting "Ferrari-level" experiences for smaller user bases while preventing companies like Google from delivering cutting-edge AI at scale.

The strategic implications are profound. Founders must predict which problems will persist as capabilities improve. Pedregal illustrates this through Granola's decision not to address context window limitations, correctly wagering that model improvements would resolve the constraint within months. Resources devoted to such temporary obstacles represent sunk costs.

Prompting philosophy undergoes parallel transformation. Rather than encoding exhaustive conditional logic, developers should furnish models with contextual intelligence—understanding meeting participants, objectives, and user goals—essentially providing "think like you" instructions. This approach acknowledges that imperfect guidance toward sophisticated reasoning outperforms comprehensive but brittle rule systems.

Competitive differentiation emerges through specialization. General-purpose assistants dominate breadth; winning requires exceptional depth in narrow domains. Yet success depends substantially on non-AI factors. Seamless notifications, superior audio processing, and intuitive interfaces determine whether products feel magical or awkwardly algorithmic. Pedregal emphasizes that the "wrapper" around AI often matters as much as model capability itself.

The temporal dimension shifts everything. Traditional startup timelines assume relatively stable technology; AI founders operate amid continuous disruption. Model releases occur biannually, rendering six-month feature projects potentially obsolete. This compressed timeline demands different planning horizons and different bets about which capabilities will provide enduring value.

Pedregal ultimately argues that fundamentals remain unchanged—building products people genuinely want still requires obsessive attention to user experience—but the surrounding context has transformed entirely. The playbook itself has been rewritten, requiring founders to simultaneously predict technological futures, optimize for prohibitive unit economics, and deliver experiences that transcend current AI limitations through thoughtful design. Success belongs to those who recognize these constraints as opportunities rather than obstacles.

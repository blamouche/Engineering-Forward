# Statement: Department of War
**Source**: https://www.anthropic.com/news/statement-department-of-war
**Date**: 2026-02-26
**Author**: Anthropic
**Keywords**: Anthropic, defense policy, government, AI ethics, national security

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic states it will not build AI systems for the U.S. Department of War and calls for clearer norms about AI’s role in military and security contexts.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic draws a boundary against developing AI for the Department of War.
- The statement argues for explicit norms and accountability in military AI use.
- It emphasizes human decision‑making and oversight in high‑stakes contexts.
- The company positions its stance as consistent with its safety mission.
- The statement reflects growing public scrutiny of AI’s role in defense.

## Synthesis
Anthropic’s brief statement on the “Department of War” is a values-driven declaration about the company’s stance toward military applications of AI. It explicitly says the company will not develop AI systems for the Department of War, while acknowledging the broader national security landscape in which AI is becoming increasingly influential. Rather than focusing on technical details, the statement emphasizes the need for clear norms and accountability around how AI is used in defense settings.

The statement signals that Anthropic wants to draw boundaries on where its technology is deployed, aligning with its safety-first positioning. It frames the issue as more than a commercial decision; it is portrayed as a moral and societal question about the acceptable use of AI in high‑stakes, potentially lethal contexts. By taking a public stance, the company sets expectations for customers, policymakers, and the public about where it is willing to engage.

A key theme is human responsibility. The statement underscores the need for human judgment and oversight in decisions that could have life‑and‑death consequences. This aligns with broader AI safety discussions that warn against delegating strategic or tactical decisions to autonomous systems without clear accountability structures. Implicitly, the statement suggests that AI systems should not be designed or deployed in ways that reduce meaningful human control.

The statement also reflects a growing debate within the tech industry. As governments and defense agencies explore AI for intelligence analysis, logistics, and decision support, companies face pressure to clarify their boundaries. Some firms are open to defense partnerships; others, like Anthropic in this case, prefer to limit involvement. This divergence raises questions about how AI talent, research, and infrastructure will be distributed across the public and private sectors.

Ultimately, the statement is concise but strategic. It does not attempt to define a full policy framework, but it signals a strong preference for restraint and transparency. By calling for clearer norms, Anthropic implies that the current governance environment is insufficient to manage the risks of military AI. The company’s position suggests that even as AI capabilities expand, the legitimacy of their use in defense should depend on strong safeguards, public accountability, and explicit ethical commitments.

# The Future of the Global Open-Source AI Ecosystem: From DeepSeek to AI+
**Source**: https://huggingface.co/blog/huggingface/one-year-since-the-deepseek-moment-blog-3?utm_source=tldrai
**Date**: Unknown (published before 2026-02-05)
**Author**: Adina Y. (AdinaY) & Irene Solaiman (irenesolaiman), Hugging Face
**Keywords**: open source AI, China, DeepSeek, Qwen, ecosystem, AI+ deployment

## Elevator pitch
Hugging Face argues that China’s post-DeepSeek “open artifact” surge is solidifying into an end-to-end open-source ecosystem—models, papers, datasets, toolchains, and deployment infrastructure—driven by an “AI+” push toward large-scale integration rather than isolated benchmark wins.

## Takeaways
- The story is shifting from one-off “DeepSeek moments” to a normalized, repeatable pattern of rapid open releases across orgs.
- Major players differentiate: Alibaba/Qwen builds a family + infra stack; Tencent moves from integrating to releasing; ByteDance selectively open-sources high-leverage components; Baidu pivots back toward openness.
- Open source is framed as a deployment strategy: share artifacts to accelerate integration, standards, and reuse.
- Startups and application-first firms are increasingly training/releasing their own models as reasoning becomes cheaper and more accessible.
- The ecosystem thesis depends on foundations: compute/energy infrastructure, evaluation/tooling platforms, and governance embedded into deployment pipelines.

## Synthesis
This third installment in Hugging Face’s series on China’s open-source AI momentum looks past the initial shock of DeepSeek and focuses on trajectories: which organizations are shaping the next phase and what “open” means when the goal is scale deployment.

A key claim is that open source is no longer merely an ideological stance or community goodwill gesture; it maps onto a pragmatic strategy oriented toward broad adoption. Publishing models, papers, datasets, and deployment components reduces friction for downstream integration and creates a reinforcing loop: more reuse leads to more derivatives, which strengthens ecosystem gravity. The post notes that Chinese organizations increasingly treat open artifacts as a default distribution mechanism.

The article outlines archetypes among established players. Alibaba’s Qwen is presented as an ecosystem-builder: rather than betting on a single flagship, it expands a family across sizes and modalities, and aligns model work with cloud/hardware infrastructure—models, chips, platforms, and applications as an integrated engineering stack. Tencent is described as moving from “borrowing to building”: it first integrated leading models into consumer products, validated internally at scale, and then accelerated open releases where it has comparative strength (vision/video/3D). ByteDance takes an application-factory posture, open-sourcing high-value components while retaining product entry points as the competitive moat. Baidu is portrayed as pivoting after years of closed-model prioritization, re-entering the ecosystem via open releases and renewed investment in its frameworks and chips.

On the startup side, the piece emphasizes how quickly newer organizations adjusted after R1: releasing advanced open models, announcing IPO plans, and turning open releases into credibility and distribution. The authors also highlight “application-first” companies (consumer platforms and device makers) beginning to train and release models once strong reasoning became available at lower cost. That shift suggests that model-building is becoming a viable vertical integration move for companies with domain data and distribution.

Finally, the “AI+” framing signals a move beyond chatbots: agents, workflows, and deep embedding into industrial processes. The authors argue that an ecosystem is forming because the chain is complete: artifacts are open and extensible, deployments are reusable, software/hardware co-design is possible, and governance can be embedded and audited. In that view, DeepSeek was not just a model milestone—it was a catalyst that activated pre-existing infrastructure tailwinds and normalized rapid, open iteration.

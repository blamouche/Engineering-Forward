# How to use AI for your next job interview
**Source**: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-use-ai-in-your-next-job-interview
**Date**: 2026-02-26
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: job search, interviewing, coaching, Claude Code, workflows

## Elevator pitch
Based on interviews with job seekers and hiring managers, this piece argues the biggest interview advantage from AI comes from building an end-to-end system that closes the feedback loop—using transcripts, simulations, and structured practice rather than generic resume polishing.

## Takeaways
- Interviewing is its own skill; senior candidates often struggle because real work ≠ on-the-spot interview performance.
- The core failure mode is “no feedback”: candidates don’t know why they failed and can’t iterate effectively.
- High performers use AI to analyze what they actually said (transcripts), predict questions, and drill weak spots.
- The article packages these tactics into a Claude Code “Interview Coach” project with instruction files and state tracking.
- AI can help candidates stand out, but it can also homogenize answers—so the system emphasizes specificity and coaching.

## Synthesis
The article starts from a simple observation: the modern interview pipeline is an opaque optimization problem. Candidates are expected to perform under constraints (whiteboards, timed problems, rehearsed stories) and then receive almost no actionable feedback. As a result, preparation becomes guesswork, and even experienced professionals can feel stuck in “blind grind” mode.

Drawing on dozens of interviews, the piece argues that the most effective use of AI is not a single trick (like rewriting a resume) but a cohesive preparation system. Candidates described workflows that use AI to (1) surface likely questions from the job description and company context, (2) map personal experiences to the rubric, (3) run mock interviews and targeted drills, and (4) generate concrete feedback based on actual performance—often using transcripts from calls or practice sessions.

A repeated motif is closing the feedback loop. Instead of “I think I did well,” candidates feed what they said into an LLM and get line-by-line critique aligned to hiring signals. This makes improvement measurable and iterative, more like training than hoping.

The most actionable contribution is packaging: the article introduces an “Interview Coach” implemented as a Claude Code project. The coach is described as a set of instruction files and a persistent state file that tracks stories, scores, patterns, and progress across sessions. The system aims to be more like a personal coach than a generic chatbot—asking reflective questions, then offering targeted feedback, and escalating practice intensity (mock loops, interruptions, pushback).

Overall, the piece positions AI as a meta-skill amplifier for career transitions: not by replacing human judgment, but by providing inexpensive repetition, analysis, and structure—especially valuable when timelines are tight and stakes are high.

# Use Lyria 3 to create music tracks in the Gemini app

**Source**: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/lyria-3/?utm_source=tldrai

**Date**: Feb 18, 2026

**Author**: Joël Yawili, Myriam Hamed Torres

**Keywords**: Gemini app, Lyria 3, music generation, audio AI, creative tools

## Elevator pitch

Google introduces Lyria 3 inside the Gemini app, enabling users to generate music tracks with prompts and iterate on compositions quickly.

## Takeaways

- Lyria 3 is integrated into the Gemini app to generate music from text prompts.
- The workflow emphasizes rapid iteration: tweak prompts and regenerate variations.
- The feature targets creators who need quick drafts, stems, or mood explorations.
- AI-generated music is positioned as a complement to human creativity, not a replacement.
- Product rollout highlights accessibility and ease of use within Gemini.

## Synthesis

The post announces that Lyria 3, Google’s music generation model, is now available inside the Gemini app. The core proposition is simplicity: creators can describe a desired sound or mood in plain language and get back a usable music track. This reduces the barrier to entry for sketching musical ideas, especially for people who are not trained composers or who need rapid experimentation during early ideation.

The product framing centers on iteration. Rather than treating the model as a one-shot generator, the workflow encourages users to refine prompts and request variations. That makes the tool more like a creative partner than a static output machine. In practice, this means you can explore different genres, tempos, or instrumentation quickly, then pick the direction that best fits a project. The emphasis is on speed and breadth, which is particularly useful for creators in video, games, or marketing who need multiple options under time pressure.

By embedding Lyria 3 directly in Gemini, Google positions music generation as part of a broader multimodal assistant. The integration suggests that users can move from ideation to execution without leaving the app, which aligns with Gemini’s overall promise of helping with many creative and productivity tasks in one place. It also implies a lower learning curve compared to specialized audio tools, which can be complex to configure.

The post frames AI music as additive rather than substitutive. It acknowledges that generated tracks are a starting point and that human judgment remains essential for polishing, mixing, and contextual fit. This is a common stance for creative AI tools: the value is in providing a fast draft and expanding the range of options, not in eliminating human taste or craft.

Overall, Lyria 3’s Gemini app launch reflects a broader trend of bundling generative media capabilities into mainstream assistants. If the model delivers coherent, editable outputs, it could shift how creators prototype music, with Gemini acting as the front door to new musical exploration rather than a separate niche product.

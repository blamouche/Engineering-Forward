# NotebookLM adds prompt-based revisions and PPTX export
**Source**: https://x.com/notebooklm/status/2023851190102986970
**Date**: 2026-02-17
**Author**: NotebookLM
**Keywords**: notebooklm, slides, export

## Elevator pitch
NotebookLM announces two highly requested slide‑deck updates: prompt‑based revisions and PPTX export, with Google Slides export coming next.

## Takeaways
- Prompt-based revisions let users tweak slides by describing changes in natural language.
- PPTX export is now available for slide decks.
- Google Slides export is planned as the next step.
- The rollout targets the most requested feature set from users.
- These changes reduce manual editing steps in slide workflows.

## Synthesis
NotebookLM’s update is a concise product announcement with two concrete additions aimed at slide‑deck workflows. First, it introduces prompt‑based revisions, which allow users to edit slides by telling the system what to change. Instead of manually adjusting text or layout, users can issue natural‑language prompts to “tweak, tailor, and tune” the deck. This suggests a shift from static generation to iterative, conversational editing, which is often the biggest friction point in AI‑generated presentations.

Second, NotebookLM adds PPTX export, enabling users to download slide decks in the standard PowerPoint format. This is an important interoperability step because PPTX remains a ubiquitous format across teams, stakeholders, and enterprise workflows. The update also signals that Google Slides export is next in the queue, which would further integrate NotebookLM into common presentation ecosystems.

Although the announcement is brief, it implies several workflow improvements. Prompt‑based revisions make slide iteration faster and less technical for non‑designers, as the user can express intent rather than perform low‑level edits. This is particularly useful for iterative refinement—changing tone, updating sections, rephrasing bullets, or adjusting emphasis without re‑authoring the slide manually. It also enables a tighter feedback loop for teams: stakeholders can review a deck and request changes in plain language, and the creator can apply those changes quickly without switching tools.

The PPTX export capability addresses a practical pain point. AI tools often generate good first drafts but struggle to fit into existing toolchains. By supporting PPTX, NotebookLM reduces the friction of moving decks into established workflows for final polish, internal reviews, or presentation delivery. PPTX also allows teams to preserve existing templates and branding by applying them after export, which is a common requirement in corporate settings. The announcement implicitly recognizes this reality by prioritizing export and promising Google Slides support next.

The update appears to be driven by user demand. The post frames these features as the “most requested,” which suggests NotebookLM users are already producing decks at a meaningful rate and were blocked by the inability to refine and export in standard formats. This kind of focus on iteration and portability signals that the tool is moving from novelty toward production use cases, where reliability and integration matter more than a single impressive output.

Overall, the announcement emphasizes practical adoption rather than new model capabilities. NotebookLM is not claiming a leap in generation quality; instead, it is strengthening the surrounding workflow: editability via prompts and exportability via PPTX (with Google Slides coming). For a slide‑deck product, those are critical steps. They make AI‑generated drafts more usable in real settings, where decks are living documents that evolve through reviews and need to end up in familiar presentation software.

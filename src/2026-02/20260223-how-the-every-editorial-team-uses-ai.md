# This Is How the Every Editorial Team Uses AI
**Source**: https://every.to/p/this-is-how-the-every-editorial-team-uses-ai
**Date**: 2026-02-23
**Author**: Kate Lee
**Keywords**: editorial workflow, AI writing, Spiral, Monologue, Claude Projects, Every.to, AI tools, editorial independence, workflow personalization

## Elevator pitch
Every.to's editorial team integrates AI throughout their content workflow while preserving editorial independence—with each team member developing personalized AI practices rather than following prescriptive rules.

## Takeaways
- Workflows are personal: writers, editors, and producers each develop individualized AI integration methods rather than following a single prescribed approach.
- Kate Lee uses Spiral for subjective editorial decisions, requesting "rewrite options" to find language that "sounds like a specific human wrote it"—treating AI as a language palette.
- Custom skills built to identify pattern-based issues: vague pronouns, unsourced claims, and formulaic constructions typical of AI-generated writing.
- Managing Editor Eleanor Warnock uses AI for initial manuscript evaluation—assessing structural soundness and alignment with Every's editorial voice before deeper editing.
- Key tools: Spiral (writing refinement), Monologue (voice drafting), and Claude Projects.

## Synthesis
Every's editorial approach is valuable specifically because they're a publication that writes about AI while using AI in their own work—making their internal practices both more visible and more deliberate than most organizations. Kate Lee's framing of "rewrite options" as finding language that sounds like a human wrote it captures something real about how AI functions best in editorial contexts: not as a ghostwriter, but as a tool that rapidly expands the option space for the human editor to select from.

The personalization emphasis is significant. Most enterprise AI adoption tries to standardize workflows for consistency and training efficiency. Every's opposite approach—letting each team member develop their own practices—reflects an understanding that editorial work is highly individual. The way a writer uses AI while drafting is fundamentally different from how an editor uses AI while evaluating manuscripts, which is different again from how a producer uses AI while planning content. Forcing a single workflow onto these different functions would optimize for one at the expense of others.

The custom skills for detecting AI-writing patterns is an interesting meta-application: using AI to identify markers of AI writing, so that human editors can address them. This suggests Every has internalized that AI writing has detectable stylistic tendencies—vague pronouns, formulaic constructions—and has built tooling to surface them rather than leaving detection to reader intuition.

The broader lesson for organizations building AI-assisted workflows is that editorially driven content organizations face different constraints than productivity-driven ones. Speed matters less than voice, authenticity, and quality at every.to—and their AI practices are optimized for those values rather than simply for throughput.

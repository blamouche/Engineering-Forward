# Measuring Progress Toward AGI: A Cognitive Framework
**Source**: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/measuring-agi-cognitive-framework/
**Date**: 2026-03-17
**Author**: Ryan Burnell
**Keywords**: AGI, benchmarks, cognitive evaluation, Google DeepMind, measurement, Kaggle, AI progress

## Elevator pitch
Google DeepMind introduces a cognitive framework for systematically evaluating AI progress toward AGI, moving beyond narrow task performance to comprehensive multi-dimensional capability assessment, with a Kaggle hackathon to engage external researchers in building meaningful benchmarks.

## Takeaways
- The framework addresses the core challenge of creating measurements that capture genuine progress toward general intelligence rather than performance on narrow tasks
- Comprehensive cognitive evaluation across multiple dimensions replaces reliance on singular performance measures
- A Kaggle hackathon engages the broader research community in constructing AGI capability benchmarks
- The collaborative approach enables external researchers to contribute to establishing measurement standards
- The initiative represents Google DeepMind's effort to systematize how the field tracks advancement toward more flexible, general intelligence

## Synthesis
One of the most contested questions in AI research is how to know when systems are making genuine progress toward general intelligence versus gaming narrow evaluation metrics. Google DeepMind's cognitive framework for measuring AGI progress attempts to establish the measurement infrastructure needed to answer that question with more rigor.

The core problem the framework addresses is well-known: AI systems frequently achieve remarkable scores on specific benchmarks without demonstrating the underlying capabilities those benchmarks were designed to detect. A system might perform at human level on a standardized reasoning test while failing at trivial variations of the same task, revealing that it learned statistical patterns in the evaluation dataset rather than the cognitive skill the test was meant to measure. Without better measurement tools, the field lacks the ability to distinguish genuine capability from sophisticated pattern matching.

Ryan Burnell and the DeepMind team's response is to shift from singular performance measures to comprehensive cognitive evaluation across multiple dimensions. Rather than asking "what percentage does this model score on benchmark X," the framework asks questions closer to "across what range of cognitive tasks, under what conditions, does this model demonstrate robust and generalizable capability?"

The collaborative dimension of this initiative is as significant as its technical content. By launching a Kaggle hackathon to engage external researchers in building capability benchmarks, DeepMind acknowledges that the measurement problem is too large and too important to solve in any single research group. Opening benchmark construction to the broader community creates a more diverse and adversarially robust evaluation ecosystem—one where the people building benchmarks are not the same people whose systems will be evaluated against them. This separation of concerns is essential for the credibility of progress claims in a field where incentives to inflate capability estimates are substantial.

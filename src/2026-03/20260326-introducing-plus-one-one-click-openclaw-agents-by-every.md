# Introducing Plus One: One-click OpenClaw Agents by Every
**Source**: https://every.to/on-every/introducing-plus-one-one-click-openclaw-agents-by-every
**Date**: March 26, 2026
**Author**: Dan Shipper
**Keywords**: OpenClaw, agents, Slack, workflows, productivity

## Elevator pitch
Every launches “Plus One,” hosted OpenClaw agents that run in Slack with preloaded tools and skills, aiming to make AI coworkers available with one‑click setup.

## Takeaways
- Plus Ones are hosted OpenClaw agents that live in Slack and launch in one click.
- They ship preloaded with Every’s tools, skills, and workflows (Cora, Spiral, Proof, etc.).
- Each agent runs on its own server; access starts via a limited waitlist.
- The goal is to remove setup and maintenance overhead for non‑technical users.
- Pricing is not finalized; early access is bundled with Every subscriptions.

## Synthesis
Dan Shipper introduces “Plus One,” a hosted version of OpenClaw agents designed to eliminate the operational friction of running custom AI coworkers. Every uses internal agents across the company—handling bug triage, marketing copy, growth experiments, and more—but setting them up has required technical work, always‑on machines, and ongoing maintenance. Plus One packages that infrastructure into a turnkey product.

Plus Ones are deployed in Slack, where teams already collaborate. Each agent runs on a dedicated server managed by Every, with one‑click setup and support for external model tokens (e.g., ChatGPT subscriptions or other API keys). They arrive with integrations and prebuilt workflows—Every’s apps like Cora (email), Spiral (writing in your voice), Proof (docs), and additional skills such as content digests, daily briefs, animation from screenshots, and frontend design upgrades. The intent is to offer a capable coworker on day one, rather than a blank agent that needs extensive prompt engineering.

The article highlights real internal use cases: agents managing editorial workflows, scheduling, marketing operations, design tasks, and code‑related tasks like PR reviews and issue triage. The promise is a shift from orchestration to delegation—humans set goals, and the agent executes multi‑step workflows across tools without manual copy‑paste or context juggling.

Every is rolling Plus One out via a waitlist, onboarding roughly 20 users per week. The staged rollout is framed as necessary due to infrastructure cost and the desire to maintain quality. Pricing remains undecided, but early access is planned for Every subscribers, with broader availability later.

The product positions itself as a managed “AI coworker” layer: not just an agent model, but a full stack of hosting, integrations, and skills. The subtext is that the hardest part of agent adoption isn’t the model—it’s the infrastructure, tool wiring, and operational upkeep. Plus One attempts to solve that with a packaged, hosted experience.

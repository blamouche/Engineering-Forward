# Perplexity tests Market Research tool for Perplexity Computer
**Source**: https://www.testingcatalog.com/perplexity-tests-market-research-agent-for-perplexity-computer/
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: Perplexity, market research, agentic search, enterprise AI, data sources

## Elevator pitch
Perplexity is testing a Market Research vertical powered by its agentic “Computer” system, aiming to automate consultant-style analyses using premium data sources.

## Takeaways
- The Market Research tab appears alongside Finance, Travel, and Shopping in Perplexity’s UI.
- Queries are routed to the agentic “Computer” system rather than standard search.
- Built-in workflows cover TAM analysis, competitive landscapes, growth frameworks, and business model tooling.
- The feature is reportedly powered by premium datasets like CB Insights, PitchBook, and Statista.
- The move aligns with Perplexity’s enterprise push and subscription-first strategy.

## Synthesis
TestingCatalog’s report suggests Perplexity is prototyping a Market Research vertical that sits atop its agentic “Computer” system. Unlike standard search categories, this feature appears designed for structured analysis: total addressable market sizing, competitor assessments, growth analysis, value chain mapping, and common strategy frameworks such as BCG matrices and jobs-to-be-done. The intent is clear: transform Perplexity into a research analyst’s toolkit rather than a general-purpose answer engine.

The signal here is the alignment between product design and enterprise positioning. Market research is a high-margin, subscription-heavy use case with buyers who value structured outputs, credible sources, and time savings. By integrating premium datasets like CB Insights, PitchBook, and Statista, Perplexity positions itself against traditional research platforms while offering a more automated, agent-driven experience. This blends the “chat-first” interface with the workflows of analysts and consultants who previously stitched together insights manually.

The use of Perplexity Computer is significant. It implies that the system can orchestrate multi-step workflows rather than simply provide one-off answers. In practice, that means that a user can ask a broad market question and receive a structured report with multiple data points, comparative tables, and scenario-driven reasoning. The example given—correlating product leaks with stock movements—shows the kind of synthesis that analysts need but rarely want to build from scratch every time.

From a platform perspective, this move illustrates how AI search is fragmenting into verticalized, data-rich experiences. Perplexity is effectively turning its agentic system into a packageable “analyst workflow engine.” That may increase lock-in, as enterprise customers will pay for premium data access and workflow automation that is hard to replace with commodity models. It also raises the bar for trust, since market research demands higher accuracy and sourcing standards than general-purpose web search.

If this feature ships broadly, it could pressure incumbents in market intelligence and consulting-adjacent tooling, especially for mid-market teams that cannot afford expensive analysts or subscriptions. The real test will be reliability: whether Perplexity can consistently produce defensible, reproducible reports that stand up to scrutiny. Still, the direction is clear—Perplexity is moving beyond Q&A toward specialized, agent-powered professional workflows.

# Harvey Raises at $11 Billion Valuation to Scale Agents Across Law Firms and Enterprises

## Elevator pitch
Harvey raised $200M at an $11B valuation to expand its legal‑workflow agent platform, citing broad adoption across law firms and in‑house teams.

## Takeaways
- Harvey closed a $200M round co‑led by GIC and Sequoia at an $11B valuation.
- The company positions itself as the platform where legal workflows run via AI agents.
- Adoption spans major law firms, corporate legal teams, and asset managers globally.
- Thousands of custom agents and long‑horizon workflows run on Harvey’s platform.
- Funding will expand agent coverage and embedded legal engineering support.

## Synthesis
The press release announces that Harvey, a legal‑AI platform, has raised $200 million at an $11 billion valuation in a round co‑led by GIC and Sequoia. The company frames the round as a vote of confidence in its position as the infrastructure layer for legal work. The investment is earmarked for expanding the number of agents that customers run on Harvey and scaling the embedded legal engineering teams that help deploy and refine those agents globally.

Harvey’s messaging emphasizes that AI agents are moving beyond assistance toward executing complete legal workflows. It highlights tasks such as M&A, due diligence, contract drafting, and document review, and it points to adoption of long‑horizon agents that handle multi‑step processes over extended periods. The release also stresses collaboration features like shared workspaces and the role of legal engineers in co‑building and maintaining customer‑specific agents.

The company reports broad market penetration: partnerships with a majority of the AmLaw 100, hundreds of in‑house legal teams, and dozens of asset managers across 60 countries. Named customer expansions and a newly launched customer advisory board are cited as evidence of enterprise traction. Harvey also claims that over 100,000 lawyers across 1,300 organizations now run critical work on the platform.

Strategically, the announcement positions Harvey not as a point tool but as a platform. It suggests that legal organizations are shifting from manual processes to agent‑driven workflows where humans focus on judgment and outcomes, and AI handles the execution. This framing aligns with a broader trend of verticalized AI platforms competing to own end‑to‑end professional workflows.

The round brings Harvey’s total funding to more than $1 billion, signaling significant investor belief that legal services will be a major AI adoption domain. The emphasis on embedded legal engineering teams indicates that adoption depends as much on implementation and change management as on model performance. For enterprises, the value proposition is a system that can reliably run complex workflows, rather than a set of isolated features.

Overall, the release portrays Harvey as a category leader in legal AI with strong enterprise adoption, a growing ecosystem of custom agents, and a strategy focused on long‑horizon workflow automation. The new funding aims to accelerate that trajectory and expand the platform’s footprint across law firms and corporate legal departments.

# Ramp AI Index: How did Anthropic do it?
**Source**: https://ramp.com/velocity/ai-index-march-2026
**Date**: 2026-03-11
**Author**: Ara Kharazian (Ramp Economist)
**Keywords**: Anthropic, OpenAI, enterprise AI adoption, Claude, market share, cultural moat, Ramp, business spending data

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic captured 24.4% of Ramp business users in February 2026—up from 4% a year prior and winning 70% of direct comparisons against OpenAI among first-time enterprise buyers—suggesting a cultural moat that transcends technical performance.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic captured 24.4% of Ramp business users in February 2026, growing 4.9% month-over-month—its largest monthly gain since tracking began.
- OpenAI experienced a 1.5% decline in the same period.
- Anthropic wins approximately 70% of direct comparisons against OpenAI among first-time enterprise AI purchasers.
- Despite comparable technical performance (Codex sometimes benchmarking superior and cheaper), Anthropic maintains capacity constraints and growing demand.
- The competitive advantage appears to be cultural positioning: "real preferences" transcend technical specifications, potentially tied to Anthropic's safety-focused identity and distance from the Pentagon partnership controversy.

## Synthesis
Ramp's spending data is methodologically valuable because it measures actual enterprise purchasing decisions rather than stated preferences or benchmark scores. When one in four Ramp businesses is spending on Anthropic—up from one in twenty-five a year prior—that's a 6x growth measured in dollars, not survey responses.

The 70% win rate in direct comparisons among first-time enterprise buyers is the most significant finding. It means that when organizations evaluate both providers and choose their first AI vendor, they're choosing Anthropic significantly more often than chance would predict. This is the purchasing stage most predictive of long-term market share, because first enterprise AI investments create organizational familiarity, internal advocates, and integration investments that create switching costs.

The cultural moat hypothesis is interesting and potentially correct, though it's difficult to test directly. The timing correlation with OpenAI's Pentagon partnership controversy is suggestive: organizations concerned about AI safety may have updated their vendor preferences in response to visible evidence of how OpenAI weighs commercial versus safety considerations. Anthropic's explicit public benefit orientation and history of AI safety research creates a brand identity that resonates with a significant segment of enterprise buyers.

That technical equivalence doesn't translate to market share equivalence reveals how enterprise software procurement actually works. Procurement decisions incorporate safety posture, organizational values alignment, relationship quality, and perceived trustworthiness alongside feature comparisons. Anthropic winning these decisions suggests it's more effective at the non-technical dimensions of enterprise sales.

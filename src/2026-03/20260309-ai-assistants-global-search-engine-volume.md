# AI assistants now equal 56% of global search engine volume: Study
**Source**: https://searchengineland.com/ai-assistants-global-search-engine-volume-study-471118
**Date**: 2026-03-09
**Author**: Danny Goodwin
**Keywords**: AI assistants, search volume, ChatGPT, Gemini, mobile apps, LLM usage, generative engine optimization, SEO

## Elevator pitch
A new analysis reveals AI tools now account for 45 billion monthly sessions globally—equivalent to 56% of traditional search volume—challenging the notion that AI threatens search demand rather than expanding it.

## Takeaways
- Scale of AI Adoption: AI platforms generate 45 billion monthly sessions worldwide, with ChatGPT dominating at 89% of global AI activity.
- Mobile-Driven Growth: Approximately 83% of global AI usage occurs within mobile applications, making app-based discovery a critical channel often overlooked in web-traffic comparisons.
- Geographic Variation: While AI represents 56% of global search volume, it accounts for only 34% in the U.S., indicating uneven adoption patterns across regions.
- Expanded Discovery Market: Rather than cannibalizing search, AI and search together have grown 26% since 2023, suggesting complementary rather than competitive dynamics.
- Methodology Matters: Previous analyses underestimated AI impact by 4-5x by focusing solely on website traffic and ignoring app-based usage across multiple LLM platforms.

## Synthesis
The research challenges prevailing assumptions about artificial intelligence's relationship to traditional search. Rather than presenting a zero-sum competition, the data indicates that AI tools now generate 45 billion monthly sessions worldwide—about 56% of search engine volume—suggesting an expanding discovery ecosystem benefiting marketers who optimize for both channels.

The study's methodology deliberately broadens the analytical lens beyond comparisons of Google.com versus ChatGPT.com visits. By aggregating traffic across five major LLM platforms (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Grok, and Claude) and accounting for mobile app usage, researchers captured a far more comprehensive picture of discovery behavior. This approach proves significant because the authors contend that 83% of global AI usage occurs inside mobile apps—a fact obscured when examining only web traffic metrics.

Geographic nuance emerges as important context. While AI represents 56% of global discovery volume, its penetration in the U.S. market stands at 34%—suggesting varying adoption patterns tied to regional infrastructure, user preferences, and market maturity.

When isolating "search-like" queries—essentially prompts where users seek information—AI accounts for 28% of global search and 17% in the U.S. This segmentation helps contextualize AI's actual competitive position within discovery, suggesting the technology excels for certain query types while traditional search retains advantages elsewhere.

A critical finding addresses the growth question: rather than search shrinking, combined usage across search engines and AI assistants "has grown 26% globally since 2023." This expansion contradicts apocalyptic narratives suggesting AI threatens SEO viability. Instead, it implies marketers must develop dual competencies—traditional search engine optimization alongside what the industry increasingly terms Generative Engine Optimization (GEO).

The analysis notes that Google's market dominance in search-related discovery has declined from 89% in 2023 to 71% by Q4 2025, reflecting AI's encroachment. Yet this doesn't represent search collapse; rather, it illustrates market fragmentation where multiple discovery pathways coexist. Organizations must recognize that discovery is expanding, demanding integrated approaches that address how audiences find information across increasingly diverse platforms.

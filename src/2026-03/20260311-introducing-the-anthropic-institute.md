# Introducing The Anthropic Institute
**Source**: https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-anthropic-institute
**Date**: 2026-03-11
**Author**: Anthropic
**Keywords**: Anthropic Institute, Jack Clark, societal impact, AI governance, economic research, AI alignment, policy, Frontier Red Team, labor markets

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic launched The Anthropic Institute under Jack Clark to address the societal implications of frontier AI—integrating its Frontier Red Team, Societal Impacts, and Economic Research teams into a unified organization that both shares findings and incorporates feedback from affected communities.

## Takeaways
- Three existing research teams integrated: Frontier Red Team (stress-testing AI capabilities), Societal Impacts (real-world usage studies), and Economic Research (labor market analysis).
- Leadership: Jack Clark, Anthropic co-founder, leads as Head of Public Benefit; new hires include Matt Botvinick (Yale Law School) for AI governance and Anton Korinek (University of Virginia) for economic transformation.
- Mission: "tell the world what we're learning about these challenges as we build frontier AI systems"—a two-way engagement mechanism for sharing findings and incorporating community feedback.
- Strategic focus areas: AI's impact on employment/economies, societal resilience opportunities and threats, AI system values alignment, and governance frameworks for recursive self-improvement scenarios.
- Simultaneous expansion of Anthropic's Public Policy team under Sarah Heck (former Stripe and NSC) with a new DC office focused on model transparency, energy protection, infrastructure investment, and export controls.

## Synthesis
The Anthropic Institute represents a structural commitment to the idea that AI companies have obligations beyond technical safety—that building powerful AI creates responsibilities for understanding and communicating the societal implications. Integrating red teaming, societal impact research, and economic research under a single organization with external-facing responsibilities creates accountability mechanisms that purely internal teams lack.

Jack Clark's role is significant. As a co-founder, his leadership signals that this isn't a PR function but a core organizational priority. The founding team's involvement in the Institute's structure suggests the societal mission is treated as continuous with the technical mission rather than adjacent to it.

The economic research focus addresses a gap in the public discourse. Most AI policy debates involve strong claims about labor market impacts with weak empirical foundations. Having researchers like Anton Korinek studying these questions from within a frontier AI lab—with access to real deployment data and direct contact with model developers—could produce better evidence than external researchers limited to public information.

The parallel Public Policy expansion reflects the DOW conflict and similar regulatory pressures: Anthropic is building lobbying and policy capacity at the same time it's building research capacity. The two-front approach (research informing policy positions, policy engagement creating feedback for research) is how well-resourced organizations shape regulation in their domain. The outcome will depend on whether the Institute's research genuinely informs positions or serves to legitimate predetermined ones.

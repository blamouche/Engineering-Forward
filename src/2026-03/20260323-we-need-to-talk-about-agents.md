# We Need to Talk about Agents
**Source**: https://insights.euclid.vc/p/we-need-to-talk-about-agents
**Date**: March 23, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: agents, automation, AI adoption, enterprise

## Elevator pitch
An essay warning that “agent” hype obscures real adoption challenges, arguing that AI should be framed as capability infrastructure rather than co‑workers.

## Takeaways
- Overuse of “agent” metaphors makes products harder to evaluate.
- AI adoption fails when companies map tools onto existing workflows unchanged.
- The GM robot factory example illustrates the risk of replacing labor without redesign.
- Vertical AI value should be framed as capability infrastructure, not headcount replacement.
- Clearer framing helps buyers understand ROI and operational fit.

## Synthesis
This Euclid Ventures essay argues that the current “agent” narrative is becoming counterproductive. By labeling every AI feature an agent or digital co‑worker, companies blur distinctions between tools and make it harder for buyers to understand what products actually do. The result is confusion and inflated expectations, especially in vertical markets where operational fit matters more than hype.

The author emphasizes that AI adoption is less about replacing workers and more about redesigning workflows. Drawing on historical parallels—GM’s failed robot factories and the slow gains from factory electrification—the essay shows that plugging new technology into old systems rarely delivers promised productivity gains. True transformation happens when organizations re‑architect processes around new capabilities.

The “agent” metaphor is criticized because it directs attention toward labor replacement rather than system design. It makes the unit of adoption a job role instead of an end‑to‑end workflow. That framing can lead to shallow deployments where AI is bolted on without structural change, producing limited ROI.

Instead, the essay suggests framing AI as capability infrastructure: tools that expand what a company can do, not just cheaper labor. This reframing clarifies value for enterprise buyers, aligns adoption with operational goals, and encourages deeper process redesign.

Overall, the piece is a call to shift from agent hype to practical capability framing. It argues that without clear language and workflow‑level thinking, AI adoption will underdeliver—even if the underlying technology is powerful.

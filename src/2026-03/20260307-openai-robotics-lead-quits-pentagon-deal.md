# OpenAI hardware exec Caitlin Kalinowski quits in response to Pentagon deal
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/07/openai-robotics-lead-caitlin-kalinowski-quits-in-response-to-pentagon-deal/
**Date**: 2026-03-07
**Author**: Anthony Ha
**Keywords**: Caitlin Kalinowski, Department of Defense, OpenAI, Pentagon, AI ethics, military AI

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI's robotics division leader resigned over the company's controversial partnership with the Department of Defense, signaling internal dissent regarding military applications of AI technology.

## Takeaways
- Executive Departure: Kalinowski, who led OpenAI's robotics initiatives, stepped down specifically citing disagreement with the Pentagon collaboration.
- Internal Opposition: The resignation reflects tension within OpenAI regarding military engagement and ethical boundaries around defense applications.
- Defense Partnership Controversy: OpenAI's agreement with the Department of Defense has generated significant internal and external criticism.
- Leadership Statement: This departure represents a notable public challenge to corporate leadership direction from a senior technical executive.
- Broader Implications: The incident highlights ongoing debates within AI companies about participation in military projects and responsible technology deployment.

## Synthesis
Caitlin Kalinowski's departure from OpenAI underscores growing internal friction over the company's Pentagon collaboration. As leader of the robotics team, Kalinowski held a prominent technical position, making her resignation a visible statement of principle against the defense partnership.

The move reflects broader industry tensions regarding artificial intelligence's military applications. As AI capabilities advance, companies face mounting pressure—both internally and externally—regarding participation in defense contracts. This particular case illustrates how high-level disagreements over corporate strategy can translate into resignations by senior figures.

Kalinowski's decision signals that not all OpenAI employees support the Pentagon deal. Her public stance may embolden other critics within the organization while simultaneously complicating OpenAI's defense relationships and recruitment efforts among ethically-minded technologists.

The resignation becomes a focal point in ongoing conversations about corporate responsibility, dual-use technology, and whether AI development should advance military applications. It demonstrates that even within organizations pursuing defense contracts, significant internal constituencies question such strategic directions.

This event will likely influence how other AI companies approach military partnerships, as high-profile departures carry reputational weight. Whether additional resignations follow may determine whether OpenAI reconsiders its Pentagon engagement or whether the organization accepts internal dissent as an operational cost of its defense strategy. The incident also raises questions about how frontier AI labs will attract and retain talent as they make increasingly controversial strategic choices about the applications and partnerships they pursue.

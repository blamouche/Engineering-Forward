## Synthesis
This short link post collects key details from OpenAI’s GPT‑5.4 launch announcement. Simon Willison notes the introduction of two models—gpt‑5.4 and gpt‑5.4‑pro—available through the API, ChatGPT, and Codex CLI. The headline capability is a one‑million‑token context window, paired with an August 31, 2025 knowledge cutoff. This positions GPT‑5.4 as a large‑context general‑purpose model intended to handle long documents, complex projects, and tool‑rich workflows without heavy chunking.

Willison highlights pricing changes relative to GPT‑5.2. GPT‑5.4 is priced slightly higher overall, with a notable premium when usage exceeds 272k tokens. This suggests OpenAI is using pricing to discourage extreme context use except when it provides real value. It also signals that the million‑token context is meant for specialized scenarios rather than default usage, a pattern seen in other high‑context model rollouts.

The post also notes benchmark performance. GPT‑5.4 reportedly beats the GPT‑5.3‑Codex line on relevant coding benchmarks. Willison wonders whether this implies the coding‑specialist line may be merged into the main model family. If the general model now meets or exceeds the specialist model’s coding performance, it could simplify product strategy and reduce fragmentation across model variants.

OpenAI’s launch messaging places an unusual emphasis on business productivity use cases. The company claims notable gains in spreadsheet modeling and document creation, citing an internal benchmark where GPT‑5.4 scored 87.3% on tasks akin to those performed by junior investment banking analysts, up from 68.4% for GPT‑5.2. This framing aligns GPT‑5.4 with enterprise workflows—spreadsheets, presentations, and documents—rather than only coding or conversational tasks.

Willison includes examples of GPT‑5.4’s creative output, such as drawings generated by the model and links to costs for producing them. While these examples are light‑weight, they serve to illustrate the practical experience of model usage, including latency and cost. The mention of a 4‑minute‑plus generation time and $1.55 cost for a single output provides a grounded perspective on the economics of high‑end model use.

Overall, the post frames GPT‑5.4 as an incremental but meaningful step forward: larger context, modest pricing increases, and improved performance in both coding and business productivity tasks. The key strategic implication is convergence—if GPT‑5.4’s general capabilities exceed specialized variants, OpenAI may consolidate offerings and focus on a single flagship model line that serves both developers and enterprise users.

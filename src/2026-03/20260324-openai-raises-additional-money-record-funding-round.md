# OpenAI raises additional money to bring record funding round to $120 billion, CFO tells Cramer
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/24/openai-secures-an-extra-10-billion-in-record-funding-round-cfo-friar-says.html
**Date**: March 24, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: OpenAI, funding, valuation, IPO, enterprise

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar says the company added $10B to its mega‑round, pushing total fundraising above $120B, while signaling IPO readiness and a continued pivot toward profitable enterprise revenue.

## Takeaways
- The additional $10B brings OpenAI’s round to “north of $120B,” above the prior $110B tranche.
- Investors include a16z, D.E. Shaw Ventures, MGX, TPG, T. Rowe Price, and Microsoft.
- OpenAI’s last round implied a $730B pre‑money valuation.
- Revenue mix is currently ~60% consumer, 40% enterprise, trending toward parity.
- OpenAI is moderating compute spend plans and prioritizing profitable initiatives ahead of a potential IPO.

## Synthesis
CNBC reports that OpenAI has expanded its already massive funding round by another $10 billion, bringing total commitments to “north of $120 billion,” according to CFO Sarah Friar’s interview with Jim Cramer. The update builds on the late‑February announcement of a $110 billion raise at a $730 billion pre‑money valuation, which itself had been widely viewed as the last private financing before a possible IPO.

The new tranche includes a broad mix of capital sources—venture, private equity, mutual funds, and sovereign‑linked investors—suggesting strong cross‑market appetite for exposure to the AI platform. Investors cited include Andreessen Horowitz, D.E. Shaw Ventures, MGX, TPG, T. Rowe Price, and Microsoft. Friar emphasized Microsoft’s continued participation as a sign of deep partnership even as OpenAI diversifies capital sources and cloud relationships.

The article frames the funding as a strategic buffer ahead of public markets. Friar said OpenAI is “starting to build that outcome,” implying that the company is preparing operationally for a public‑company standard even if market conditions delay a listing. The story also highlights OpenAI’s reassessment of infrastructure spending: after previously discussing trillion‑dollar compute commitments, OpenAI reportedly told investors it now targets about $600 billion in total compute spend through 2030, a figure more aligned with revenue projections.

Operationally, OpenAI’s revenue mix is shifting. Friar said consumer revenue remains the majority (~60%), but enterprise is growing faster and could reach a 50‑50 split by year‑end. That matters because enterprise revenue is described as more scalable and profitable. The piece contrasts this with Anthropic, which reportedly derives a larger share from enterprise accounts, underscoring competitive pressure in the business market.

Finally, the article ties fundraising to product prioritization. OpenAI’s recent shutdown of its Sora video app is presented as a cost‑management move amid compute scarcity, reinforcing a narrative of focusing resources on the most profitable or strategic initiatives. Overall, the report depicts OpenAI as capital‑intensive, growth‑driven, and increasingly oriented toward enterprise durability as it inches toward public‑company readiness.

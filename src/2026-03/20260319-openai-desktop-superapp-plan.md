# OpenAI Plans Launch of Desktop “Superapp”
**Source**: https://www.wsj.com/tech/openai-plans-launch-of-desktop-superapp-to-refocus-simplify-user-experience-9e19931d
**Date**: March 19, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: OpenAI, desktop app, superapp, ChatGPT, Codex

## Elevator pitch
The Wall Street Journal reports OpenAI plans a desktop “superapp” that unifies ChatGPT, Codex, and a browser to simplify the user experience.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI plans to merge ChatGPT, Codex, and browser features into one desktop app.
- The move aims to simplify UX and refocus on enterprise customers.
- Fidji Simo will oversee the applications org and product shift.
- Greg Brockman will assist with product and organizational changes.
- Details are limited publicly due to the article’s paywall.

## Synthesis
According to a Wall Street Journal report, OpenAI is planning a desktop “superapp” that consolidates its main products—ChatGPT, Codex, and browser functionality—into a single application. The goal is to simplify the user experience and refocus product efforts toward engineering and business customers.

The article notes that Fidji Simo, Chief of Applications, will oversee the change and lead the product shift. OpenAI President Greg Brockman will assist with the revamp and organizational changes. Beyond these points, public details are limited due to the paywall, so the specific feature set and timeline are not fully disclosed.

The move fits a broader trend toward integrated AI workspaces. Rather than offering separate tools for chat, coding, and web interaction, OpenAI appears to be pursuing a unified desktop environment. If executed, this would mirror the “superapp” strategy used by other software platforms, reducing friction between tasks and potentially increasing user retention.

With OpenAI’s enterprise focus, the consolidation could also streamline sales and onboarding by presenting a single, cohesive product to customers. A unified desktop app would make it easier to package features, support workflows, and manage permissions across chat, coding, and browsing contexts.

In short, the WSJ report suggests OpenAI is shifting from a portfolio of apps to a single desktop hub, a strategic move that could reshape how users engage with its tools. Details remain sparse, but the leadership changes signal a significant product realignment.

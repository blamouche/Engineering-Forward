# OpenAI ads pilot tops $100 million in annualized revenue in under 2 months
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/26/openai-ads-pilot-tops-100-million-in-arr-in-under-2-months.html
**Date**: 2026-03-26
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: OpenAI, ChatGPT, advertising, revenue, product strategy

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI’s early ChatGPT ads pilot surpassed $100M in annualized revenue within two months, signaling a fast‑growing monetization channel despite cautious rollout.

## Takeaways
- The U.S. ads pilot for ChatGPT has crossed $100M in ARR.
- OpenAI is working with 600+ advertisers and reports stable privacy‑trust metrics.
- Ads are labeled, placed at the bottom of responses, and restricted for minors.
- Eligibility is broad, but daily ad exposure remains limited during testing.
- Expansion tests are being explored in Canada, Australia, and New Zealand.

## Synthesis
OpenAI’s nascent advertising program inside ChatGPT has quickly scaled to more than $100 million in annualized recurring revenue, less than two months after its U.S. pilot began. The company is using ads to monetize its free and “Go” tier users, with placements at the bottom of responses and explicit labeling to avoid influencing answers. The pilot’s early performance suggests that conversational interfaces can support meaningful ad revenue without immediately eroding trust.

The rollout has been deliberately conservative. Although a majority of eligible U.S. users can see ads, fewer than one in five are exposed to them daily. OpenAI says this cautious pace is intentional to refine the experience and reduce user friction. It also notes that ads are restricted from sensitive topics like politics and health, and that under‑18 users are excluded, indicating a safety‑oriented approach to monetization.

Advertiser interest appears strong, with more than 600 brands participating. Yet the limited daily exposure has reportedly frustrated some advertisers who want faster scale. This tension underscores the challenge of balancing revenue growth with product trust in a conversational AI interface where user expectations of neutrality are high.

Strategically, the ads pilot diversifies OpenAI’s revenue streams beyond subscriptions and enterprise APIs. It also aligns with broader industry patterns of AI platforms experimenting with advertising as a path to sustain high compute costs. Early results could influence how other AI chat products think about monetization and whether ads become a standard feature of consumer AI tools.

The program’s next phase is likely geographic expansion. OpenAI is exploring tests in Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, which could validate whether the model generalizes across markets. If trust metrics remain stable and revenue performance holds, the company could broaden the rollout, potentially transforming ChatGPT into a large‑scale ad platform.

Overall, the pilot signals that AI chat interfaces can support meaningful advertising revenue when placed carefully and governed by safety rules. The key question is whether scaling the program will preserve user trust and model integrity—or whether increasing ad presence will create new risks and backlash.

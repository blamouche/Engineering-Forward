# Mastering Midjourney: How to create consistent, beautiful brand imagery without complex prompts
**Source**: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/this-week-on-how-i-ai-mastering-midjourney
**Date**: 2026-03-09
**Author**: Lenny Rachitsky (guest: Jamey Gannon)
**Keywords**: Midjourney, AI image generation, brand consistency, visual systems, style references, personalization codes, creative workflow

## Elevator pitch
An AI creative director reveals her system for generating on-brand imagery through visual references and personalization codes rather than complex text prompts.

## Takeaways
- Minimize text prompting: The most effective workflow avoids lengthy written descriptions in favor of visual communication through reference images and style codes.
- Style references outperform mood boards: Using Midjourney's SREF feature provides more consistent results than traditional mood boards that can cause style "averaging."
- Personalization codes encode aesthetic preferences: Systematically rating generated images creates unique codes that reliably deliver preferred visual styles across generations.
- Use editorial and camera shorthand: Referencing known publications (e.g., "Dazed editorial") or camera models instantly communicates complex aesthetic qualities.
- Remove problematic elements rather than fix them: When unwanted elements appear, deletion from reference sets proves more effective than additional prompting.

## Synthesis
Jamey Gannon's approach fundamentally challenges how creatives should interact with AI image generators. Rather than treating Midjourney as a text-to-image tool requiring elaborate prompts, she treats it as a visual collaboration system where imagery speaks louder than words.

The core innovation involves three complementary techniques. First, style reference images (SREFs) function as visual anchors, providing Midjourney with direct stylistic guidance superior to mood boards that average disparate visual influences. Second, personalization codes—created through methodical rating during Midjourney's training phase—establish consistent aesthetic signatures without requiring regeneration of reference materials. Third, shorthand references to established visual properties (publications, camera models) leverage collective visual knowledge to communicate instantly.

Her workflow reflects deeper understanding of how AI "sees" input. Rather than fighting the system's tendencies, she works within them by considering what visual information the AI actually processes. When oversaturated colors emerge, removing the offending reference eliminates the source rather than fighting it through prompting. This perspective shift—from "making AI work for you" to "understanding how AI works"—appears foundational to her success.

The practical implementation involves strategic tool selection: Midjourney for initial creation, Nano Banana for targeted edits, and Flora for client delivery. This "AI routing" acknowledges that different tools excel at different tasks rather than expecting one system to handle everything.

Her biggest takeaway challenges industry-wide assumptions about prompt engineering. While complex, detailed prompts seem intuitive, they often confuse AI systems by introducing contradictory visual signals. Visual references provide clearer direction because they're semantically unambiguous—an image of a specific editorial aesthetic can't be misinterpreted the way language inevitably is.

For practitioners, this translates to spending less time writing and more time curating visual references, investing in personalization codes that serve as reusable creative assets, and developing literacy in referencing established visual properties that already carry understood connotations. The methodology ultimately democratizes consistent brand asset creation by replacing technical expertise with systematic visual curation, observation, and iterative refinement.

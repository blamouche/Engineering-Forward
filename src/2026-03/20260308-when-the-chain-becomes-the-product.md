# When the chain becomes the product
**Source**: https://markmhendrickson.com/posts/when-the-chain-becomes-the-product/
**Date**: 2026-03-08
**Author**: Mark Hendrickson
**Keywords**: blockchain, tokenomics, Blockstack, Hiro Systems, disruption theory, product signals, belief assets, crypto, startup failure modes

## Elevator pitch
Token economics inverts startup validation by capitalizing on future narratives before delivery—replacing empirical product signals with self-generated activity proxies and creating a perpetual horizon where value is always 12-18 months away.

## Takeaways
- Token issuance reverses traditional startup validation: projects capitalize on future narratives before building products, creating "belief as an asset."
- Feedback loop degradation: with multi-year development timelines, organizations replace empirical signals with self-generated proxies (GitHub activity, grants, partnerships) divorced from actual user needs.
- Developer dismissal: early adopters providing concrete feedback become inconvenient to projects invested in theoretical infrastructure narratives—rationalized as unrepresentative of "real" future markets.
- The perpetual horizon: each promised milestone relocates the "moment of value" 12-18 months forward, creating inertia through sunk costs rather than conviction.
- 85% of VC-backed tokens in 2025 traded below launch prices, suggesting systemic misalignment between tokenomics and actual product viability.

## Synthesis
Hendrickson's seven-year Blockstack experience provides detailed evidence for a failure mode that's visible across many technology hype cycles but particularly acute in crypto: the replacement of product-market feedback with market-narrative feedback. The structural difference matters because the two feedback types reward entirely different behaviors.

Product-market feedback rewards solving real problems for real users: better retention, higher conversion, usage expansion. Market-narrative feedback rewards telling compelling stories about future potential: more engaging white papers, larger estimated TAMs, more prestigious partnerships. Organizations that optimize for market-narrative feedback become skilled at narrative construction, which is economically rewarded in the short term by token appreciation even as it creates long-term product failure.

The developer dismissal pattern is particularly telling because early adopters represent the highest-signal feedback available to any technology platform. They've chosen to try the technology, invested time understanding it, and formed opinions from actual use. Rationalizing away their feedback as "unrepresentative" of future markets is intellectually dishonest, but it's also economically rational for a project whose value is based on future narrative rather than current traction.

The pattern generalizes beyond crypto to any domain where the investment case is based on future potential rather than current performance. It appears in enterprise software (product roadmaps as sales tools rather than development plans), in AI startup pitches, and in strategic initiatives within large companies (the internal project whose value is always tied to capabilities not yet available). The token mechanism makes it extreme and visible; the underlying dynamic is more widespread.

# Amazon is reportedly developing an AI-centric smartphone
**Source**: https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/03/amazon-is-reportedly-developing-an-ai-centric-smartphone/
**Date**: March 26, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: Amazon, smartphone, Alexa, AI devices, hardware

## Elevator pitch
Amazon is said to be building an AI‑first smartphone that leans on AI services over app stores, reviving its hardware ambitions after the Fire Phone failure.

## Takeaways
- Amazon’s project reportedly emphasizes AI experiences over app stores.
- The effort is linked to a team called “Transformer.”
- Alexa+ could be central to the device’s positioning.
- Amazon’s previous Fire Phone failed quickly in the market.
- The smartphone market remains dominated by Apple and Samsung.

## Synthesis
Ars Technica reports that Amazon is developing an AI‑centric smartphone, citing Reuters sources. The device is reportedly built by an internal team called “Transformer” and is positioned around AI usage rather than traditional app‑store‑centric experiences. Sources suggest the phone could use AI to replace or minimize conventional app navigation, with inspiration drawn from minimalist devices like the Light Phone.

While Reuters did not explicitly mention Alexa+, Ars notes that Amazon’s generative AI assistant would likely be central to the phone’s value proposition. An AI‑first phone could drive usage of Amazon’s AI services, creating a tighter loop between hardware and subscription‑based assistants.

The report highlights the historical context: Amazon’s earlier Fire Phone launched in 2014 and was discontinued within a year due to poor sales. The company’s devices division has struggled financially, in part because Alexa hardware has not generated meaningful revenue. A return to smartphones would put Amazon back into a market dominated by Apple and Samsung, with entrenched ecosystems and strong customer loyalty.

The article questions the timing. Even if Amazon undercuts on price, persuading users to switch ecosystems is difficult. The implication is that the company is betting on AI as a differentiator strong enough to overcome its previous hardware failures.

Overall, the story frames the project as another attempt to use AI to reset the hardware narrative. Amazon appears to be wagering that AI‑first UX could justify a new entry into the crowded smartphone market, but the history of the Fire Phone and current device economics make the bet risky.

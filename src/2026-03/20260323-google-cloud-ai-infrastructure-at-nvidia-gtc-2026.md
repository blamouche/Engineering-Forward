# Google Cloud AI Infrastructure at NVIDIA GTC 2026
**Source**: https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/google-cloud-ai-infrastructure-at-nvidia-gtc-2026
**Date**: March 23, 2026
**Author**: Google Cloud
**Keywords**: Google Cloud, NVIDIA, GTC 2026, G4 VMs, AI infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
Google Cloud and NVIDIA announced expanded AI infrastructure collaboration, including G4 VMs, fractional GPUs, and upcoming Vera Rubin systems.

## Takeaways
- Google Cloud introduced G4 VMs powered by NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell GPUs.
- Fractional G4 VMs provide smaller GPU slices for cost‑efficient workloads.
- NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are planned for late 2026.
- Integrations include Dynamo with GKE Inference Gateway and Vertex AI support.
- A Kaggle competition and public‑sector accelerator were also announced.

## Synthesis
At NVIDIA GTC 2026, Google Cloud announced a series of infrastructure updates built around deeper collaboration with NVIDIA. The centerpiece is the expansion of its AI Hypercomputer stack and new G4 VMs powered by NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell GPUs. These VMs are positioned for both AI workloads and high‑fidelity simulation, with customers reporting large throughput and latency gains.

A notable addition is fractional G4 VMs using NVIDIA vGPU technology, allowing customers to provision smaller GPU slices (1/2, 1/4, 1/8). This addresses a frequent pain point in AI infrastructure: matching GPU capacity to workload requirements without paying for unused capacity. The feature is framed as a way to lower entry costs and maximize utilization, especially for inference and lighter workloads.

Google Cloud also announced plans to offer NVIDIA’s next‑generation Vera Rubin NVL72 rack‑scale systems in the second half of 2026, integrating them into its AI Hypercomputer architecture. Alongside hardware, Google highlighted software and platform integrations such as NVIDIA Dynamo with GKE Inference Gateway, enhanced Vertex AI support, and new scaling recipes for massive MoE inference.

The post emphasizes ecosystem initiatives as well, including a Kaggle competition for NVIDIA Nemotron models on G4 VMs and a dedicated public‑sector AI startup accelerator. These initiatives suggest Google is building not just infrastructure but a developer pipeline around its GPU offerings.

Overall, the announcement signals Google Cloud’s intent to compete aggressively in AI infrastructure by combining NVIDIA’s latest hardware with flexible provisioning, integrated software stacks, and ecosystem programs. The focus is on lowering cost per inference, improving utilization, and supporting large‑scale agentic workloads.

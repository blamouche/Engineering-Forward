# Warner, Rounds Unveil Bipartisan Plan to Prepare American Workers for AI-Driven Workforce Changes
**Source**: https://www.warner.senate.gov/public/index.cfm/pressreleases?id=1D95AF09-5ED4-4D3C-BBB0-87F596C5176C
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: AI policy, workforce, legislation, commission, labor economics

## Elevator pitch
Senators Warner and Rounds introduced the Economy of the Future Commission Act to study AI’s labor impacts and recommend bipartisan workforce policies within a year.

## Takeaways
- The bill creates a bipartisan commission focused on AI‑driven workforce changes.
- Interim and final reports would outline expected job impacts and policy options.
- Recommendations cover training, reskilling, unemployment insurance, and taxation.
- The commission includes lawmakers and experts from industry, academia, and government.
- Broad endorsements signal a push for coordinated national preparedness.

## Synthesis
The press release announces the Economy of the Future Commission Act, introduced by Senators Mark Warner and Mike Rounds, as a bipartisan effort to prepare U.S. workers for AI‑driven economic change. The proposal would establish a commission tasked with analyzing AI’s labor impacts and delivering policy recommendations on workforce training, reskilling, safety‑net adjustments, and broader economic competitiveness.

The legislation sets a structured timeline: an interim report within seven months to outline expected employment shifts and provide accessible public resources, followed by a final report within thirteen months with legislative recommendations. The scope includes education and training programs, unemployment insurance policy, tax considerations, and strategies to maintain U.S. leadership in key industries. The commission would draw from bipartisan lawmakers alongside experts from industry, academia, and government agencies.

Supporters frame the commission as a pragmatic mechanism to build consensus and move policy beyond ad‑hoc reactions. Quotes from sponsors emphasize that AI’s impact is inevitable and that the priority is preparation—ensuring workers have pathways to new skills and protections as job structures evolve. The endorsements list spans technology firms, universities, labor‑adjacent organizations, and policy groups, signaling broad institutional backing.

The release also positions AI competitiveness as a national security and economic issue. By emphasizing the need for workforce readiness, the legislation aims to align innovation with social stability. The commission’s role is described as producing evidence‑based, bipartisan recommendations rather than immediate regulatory mandates.

In practical terms, the act recognizes uncertainty around AI’s labor impacts while advocating for proactive planning. It elevates workforce readiness as a policy priority and creates a forum to translate research and industry input into concrete legislative options. Whether the commission can move quickly enough to keep pace with AI adoption remains open, but the proposal signals growing political urgency around AI’s economic effects.

Overall, the release frames the Economy of the Future Commission Act as a first step toward coordinated national response to AI‑driven workforce change—seeking to balance innovation with worker support and maintain competitiveness as automation accelerates.

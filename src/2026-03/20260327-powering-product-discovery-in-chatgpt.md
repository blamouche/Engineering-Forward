# Powering Product Discovery in ChatGPT
**Source**: https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: commerce, product discovery, ChatGPT, ACP, retail

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI expands its Agentic Commerce Protocol to power visual, conversational product discovery in ChatGPT, aiming to reduce shopping friction for users while delivering higher‑intent traffic for merchants.

## Takeaways
- ChatGPT adds richer product discovery with visual browsing, comparisons, and up‑to‑date details.
- The Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) is expanded to support product discovery data feeds.
- Merchants can integrate via existing providers (Salesforce, Stripe) or Shopify Catalog.
- OpenAI shifts away from Instant Checkout toward merchant‑owned checkout flows.
- Retail partners like Target, Sephora, Nordstrom, and Walmart are already integrated.

## Synthesis
OpenAI’s announcement frames shopping as a discovery problem rather than a checkout problem. Users often know the category but not the exact product, and the current web flow forces them into repetitive tab‑hopping and fragmented comparison. The update positions ChatGPT as a guided, conversational “discovery layer” that helps users narrow options, compare features and prices, and refine preferences in one place. The new experience emphasizes richer product presentation: visual browsing, side‑by‑side comparisons, and more current product details.

The technical backbone is the expanded Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP). ACP acts as the connective layer between merchants and ChatGPT, providing product feeds and promotions so the model has the inventory and metadata required to offer accurate recommendations. OpenAI highlights multiple on‑ramps—direct feeds and third‑party providers such as Salesforce and Stripe—and notes that Shopify merchants are already represented through Shopify Catalog without additional work. The promise is that broader coverage and fresher data improve relevance and reduce the “stale catalog” problem that undermines trust in AI shopping recommendations.

The announcement also clarifies a strategic adjustment: OpenAI is stepping back from Instant Checkout as a primary path to conversion. The company found that its initial in‑ChatGPT checkout flow lacked the flexibility merchants need, so it is now prioritizing discovery and letting merchants own checkout experiences. This is a notable shift away from end‑to‑end AI commerce toward a partnership model where ChatGPT drives intent and merchants handle final conversion. The Walmart app integration exemplifies this: users discover in ChatGPT but transition into a dedicated Walmart environment for account linking, loyalty, and payments.

From a user standpoint, the update is about reducing search costs: faster comparison, fewer tabs, and more guided decisions. From a merchant standpoint, the pitch is higher‑intent traffic—users who have already refined preferences and are closer to purchase. The expansion to all ChatGPT tiers (Free, Go, Plus, Pro) underscores that this is a platform‑wide bet, not a niche feature.

Overall, the move signals OpenAI’s intent to become a critical discovery layer in commerce, with AI‑native shopping flows that sit above existing merchant infrastructure rather than replacing it. The emphasis on iteration and partner feedback suggests a product still in flux, but the direction is clear: conversational product discovery as the primary wedge, with checkout handled by merchants and apps that preserve brand and commerce control.

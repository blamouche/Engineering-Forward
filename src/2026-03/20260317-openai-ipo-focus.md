# OpenAI Has New Focus (on the IPO)
**Source**: https://om.co/2026/03/17/openai-has-new-focus-on-the-ipo/
**Date**: 2026-03-17
**Author**: Om Malik
**Keywords**: OpenAI, IPO, Anthropic, xAI, Sam Altman, public markets, enterprise AI, strategy

## Elevator pitch
Om Malik argues that OpenAI's recent organizational admissions and strategic pivots are primarily IPO positioning moves in a race against Anthropic and xAI, and that developer wallet share—not PR—will determine who wins.

## Takeaways
- Malik frames Sam Altman's "side quests" acknowledgment as a controlled leak designed to shape investor perception rather than genuine organizational transparency
- Anthropic's revenue run rate has surged past $19 billion, and it currently leads in enterprise adoption—the metric that matters most for IPO positioning
- OpenAI's PE partnerships, Frontier Alliances with consulting firms, and enterprise focus are investor confidence signals rather than coherent long-term strategy
- Three companies—Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI—are racing toward IPO windows in a compressed timeframe
- "Developer wallet always speaks the loudest"—enterprise developer adoption is the real metric behind the IPO narratives

## Synthesis
Om Malik brings his characteristic skepticism to OpenAI's recent organizational disclosures, reading them not as signs of genuine internal reckoning but as investor relations choreography. CEO Sam Altman's all-hands acknowledgment of "side quests" and internal dysfunction, Malik argues, was designed to land in the press in a particular way—demonstrating self-awareness ahead of investor roadshows rather than signaling authentic cultural change.

The piece's most important observation is structural: three major AI companies are racing toward public markets simultaneously, and that compressed competition changes how to interpret every strategic move. Anthropic's revenue run rate has reportedly surpassed $19 billion, and its enterprise customer traction has been building methodically. OpenAI's $25 billion annualized revenue and 900 million weekly users represent scale advantages, but scale in consumer AI does not automatically translate to enterprise adoption—the category where IPO multiples are anchored.

Malik's reading of OpenAI's recent moves—PE partnerships with TPG, Advent, and Brookfield; "Frontier Alliances" with major consulting firms; intensified enterprise focus—is that they represent positioning rather than strategy. Each announcement signals something to the investor community: the PE partnerships suggest enterprise distribution networks, the consulting alliances suggest professional services integration, the enterprise focus suggests revenue quality. Whether these translate to durable business transformation is a separate question.

The "developer wallet" principle is Malik's north star for evaluating all of this. Enterprise AI adoption ultimately flows through developers who make platform decisions based on capability, reliability, and economics—not press releases. Anthropic's current enterprise lead suggests developers have been making those decisions in its favor. OpenAI's IPO window requires demonstrating that this trend is reversible. The authenticity of organizational transformation matters less than whether the token usage numbers move.

# The internet ruined customer service. AI could save it.
**Source**: https://a16z.com/the-internet-ruined-customer-service-ai-could-save-it/
**Date**: 2026-03-18
**Author**: Sarah Wang (a16z)
**Keywords**: customer service, AI agents, concierge business, scale businesses, Decagon, NPS, deflection rate, personalization

## Elevator pitch
The internet scaled commerce but degraded customer experience by eliminating personalization—AI collapses the cost of devoted attention, turning every business into a potential concierge business with 80% deflection rates and improved satisfaction scores.

## Takeaways
- Scale businesses (Amazon, Uber, banks) offer cheap but impersonal service; concierge businesses (luxury brands) provide personalized service exclusively to high-value customers. The internet forced most businesses toward scale at the cost of experience.
- "When AI collapses the cost of high-quality attention, every business becomes a concierge business."
- Decagon achieves 80% deflection rates and doubled Net Promoter Scores for clients like Chime—demonstrating automation enhancing rather than degrading satisfaction.
- AI agents can provide proactive, personalized, continuous service rather than episodic reactive support.
- Historical parallel: email created new communication categories rather than just replacing mail—AI will generate unprecedented customer engagement models.

## Synthesis
Wang's framing inverts the usual AI-in-customer-service narrative. Most coverage focuses on cost reduction: fewer human agents, faster ticket resolution, lower headcount. The concierge frame focuses on quality transformation: not cheaper service but qualitatively different service that was previously economically impossible at scale.

The economics behind this are real. Devoted personal attention in human customer service is expensive because humans can only serve one customer at a time. An AI system that maintains context, learns preferences, proactively identifies problems, and provides consistent quality can handle unlimited simultaneous interactions. The marginal cost of serving the 1,000th customer simultaneously is nearly zero; the 1,000th high-quality human interaction in a day approaches impossibility.

The Decagon example is valuable as empirical evidence against the assumption that automation degrades satisfaction. 80% deflection means customers are getting answers without waiting for human agents—typically the source of dissatisfaction in high-volume support. If the automated answers are actually correct and resolve issues completely, customers are better served than they would be by overloaded human agents with long queues.

The email analogy is apt but deserves examination. Email did create new communication categories (newsletters, mailing lists, automated confirmations) that didn't exist with physical mail. But email also degraded some communication quality (the low friction that makes email efficient also makes it easy to send poor, hasty communications). AI customer service may similarly create new engagement categories while also creating new failure modes—hallucinated answers, confident wrong information—that need management.

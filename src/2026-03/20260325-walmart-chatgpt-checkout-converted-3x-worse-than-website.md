# Walmart: ChatGPT checkout converted 3x worse than website
**Source**: https://searchengineland.com/walmart-chatgpt-checkout-converted-worse-472071
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Danny Goodwin
**Keywords**: agentic commerce, conversion rates, Walmart, ChatGPT, checkout

## Elevator pitch
Walmart found that in-ChatGPT Instant Checkout purchases converted at one-third the rate of click-through purchases on its website, prompting a shift back toward merchant-controlled checkout.

## Takeaways
- Walmart’s in-ChatGPT Instant Checkout converted three times worse than traditional click-out flows.
- The retailer plans to move away from in-chat purchases toward Walmart-managed checkout experiences.
- OpenAI is phasing out Instant Checkout in favor of merchant-hosted app-based checkout.
- Walmart will embed its own Sparky chatbot in ChatGPT, with login and cart syncing inside Walmart systems.
- Early agentic commerce experiments show limitations versus owned environments.

## Synthesis
Walmart’s experiment with OpenAI’s Instant Checkout highlights a key friction in early agentic commerce: shoppers still convert better on a retailer’s own website than inside an AI chat interface. According to Walmart executive Daniel Danker, purchases completed directly inside ChatGPT converted at roughly one-third the rate of transactions that clicked through to Walmart’s site. The performance gap was large enough for Walmart to call the experience “unsatisfying” and to change course.

The Instant Checkout program allowed users to browse and purchase roughly 200,000 Walmart products inside ChatGPT without leaving the conversation. The idea was to reduce friction and allow the AI agent to guide a purchase end-to-end. In practice, however, Walmart’s data suggests that familiar, brand-controlled checkout flows still win on conversion. This reinforces a broader lesson: while conversational AI can drive discovery and intent, handing off to a trusted, optimized commerce environment remains critical for closing the sale.

This shift aligns with OpenAI’s own decision to phase out Instant Checkout. The new direction favors app-based checkout handled directly by merchants, which preserves the retailer’s authentication, cart management, and payment flows. For Walmart, the next iteration is an embedded chatbot, Sparky, inside ChatGPT. Users will log into Walmart, synchronize carts across platforms, and complete purchases within Walmart’s system rather than the AI platform’s checkout. A similar integration is expected with Google Gemini.

The case underscores the importance of control and continuity in ecommerce. Retailers have spent years optimizing their own checkout experiences and building trust around payment and returns. Moving the transaction into a third-party chat interface can weaken those signals, particularly if users feel uncertain about account access, order confirmation, or post-purchase support. Conversion metrics are a fast feedback loop; in this case, they show that agentic shopping is not yet a substitute for owned commerce surfaces.

For AI platforms, the outcome suggests a pivot from full transactional control to a role as a high-intent discovery layer. Embedding merchant agents or interfaces in chat systems may be the more sustainable approach, allowing AI to orchestrate the journey while merchants retain the final transactional step. In the near term, agentic commerce appears better suited to guiding customers to the right product than to fully replacing existing checkout funnels.

Overall, Walmart’s experience serves as a cautionary data point for AI-driven retail. Conversion performance still depends on the robustness of retailer-managed checkout flows, and AI platforms will need to integrate more deeply with merchant systems rather than trying to own the last mile of the purchase.

# OpenAI drops plans to release an adult chatbot
**Source**: https://www.engadget.com/ai/openai-drops-plans-to-release-an-adult-chatbot-113121190.html
**Date**: 2026-03-26
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: OpenAI, safety, content moderation, consumer AI, product strategy

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI shelved its planned adult‑oriented chatbot, citing safety concerns, limited evidence on impacts, and a refocus on core productivity tools.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI paused the adult‑chatbot feature indefinitely after internal and investor concerns.
- Safety risks included illegal content and unhealthy attachment dynamics.
- The company wants more research on the effects of erotic AI interactions.
- OpenAI is refocusing on core products like coding assistants.
- Age‑verification accuracy remains a challenge for consumer AI features.

## Synthesis
OpenAI has indefinitely abandoned plans to launch an adult‑oriented chatbot feature, according to reporting cited by Engadget. The feature, reportedly known internally as “Citron mode,” had been announced for release in late 2025 but faced delays and internal debate. The company confirmed it is now on hold with no planned release date.

The decision reflects a mix of safety, reputational, and strategic concerns. Training a model to produce erotic content while reliably blocking illegal or harmful content proved difficult. OpenAI reportedly struggled with edge cases involving prohibited content, and those concerns were amplified by public controversies around competitors generating non‑consensual deepfakes. Investors and staff also expressed discomfort, with at least one senior employee reportedly leaving the company over the issue.

OpenAI’s public rationale emphasizes caution and research. The company says there is insufficient empirical evidence about the long‑term effects of erotic AI interactions and attachment, and it wants to study these dynamics further before proceeding. This suggests that even if the feature is not permanently canceled, it is unlikely to ship without stronger safety evidence and safeguards.

The move also signals a strategic refocus. OpenAI stated that it wants to concentrate on “core productivity” tools like coding assistants rather than side projects. This aligns with a broader trend of AI labs prioritizing enterprise and developer workflows over experimental consumer features amid mounting scrutiny.

Age verification is another constraint. OpenAI’s age‑checking tools reportedly have error rates above ten percent, which would still allow a significant number of minors to access adult content. While the company notes this is within industry norms, the reliability gap makes it difficult to justify launching an adult‑oriented feature without additional safeguards.

Overall, the decision illustrates the tension between expanding AI capabilities and maintaining trust. Adult content features carry high regulatory, ethical, and reputational risks, especially when safeguards are imperfect. OpenAI’s pause suggests a cautious stance on consumer features that could create social harm or legal liability, even as demand exists. The episode reinforces that safety, policy alignment, and strategic focus are becoming as decisive as technical feasibility in AI product roadmaps.

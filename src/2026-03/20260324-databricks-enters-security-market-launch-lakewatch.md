# Databricks Enters Security Market with Launch of Lakewatch: New Open, Agentic SIEM
**Source**: https://www.databricks.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/databricks-enters-security-market-launch-lakewatch-new-open-agentic
**Date**: March 24, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: SIEM, Databricks, security, agentic, lakehouse

## Elevator pitch
Databricks launches Lakewatch, an open, agentic SIEM that unifies enterprise security data in a lakehouse to automate detection and response with AI agents at lower cost.

## Takeaways
- Lakewatch is an open, agentic SIEM built on Databricks’ lakehouse platform.
- It targets high‑volume, multi‑modal security data with lower total cost of ownership.
- Agentic triage and investigation use Databricks Agent Bricks and Genie.
- Open ecosystem integrations aim to reduce vendor lock‑in and improve visibility.
- Databricks deepens partnership with Anthropic and acquires Antimatter and SiftD.ai.

## Synthesis
Databricks announced Lakewatch, a new open, agentic SIEM designed to help enterprises defend against AI‑driven attackers. The press release frames the core problem as a data visibility gap: security teams discard large portions of telemetry due to cost and siloed tooling, while attackers leverage autonomous agents to probe and exploit systems at machine speed. Lakewatch’s proposition is to centralize security, IT, and business data in a single governed lakehouse so organizations can retain and analyze far more data without massive ingestion costs.

Lakewatch positions itself as “open” on two fronts: open data formats and an open ecosystem. This means customers can ingest multi‑modal data (including audio and video), retain years of logs, and integrate with a wide range of security vendors without being trapped by proprietary SIEM storage or tooling. Databricks highlights an Open Security Lakehouse Ecosystem that includes vendors like Akamai, Okta, Palo Alto Networks, Wiz, Zscaler, and others, suggesting a deliberate play for interoperability rather than closed‑stack lock‑in.

The agentic angle is central. Lakewatch uses Databricks’ Agent Bricks to build custom security agents for triage, enrichment, and investigation, while Genie automates multi‑step analysis and reduces alert fatigue. The platform also emphasizes detection‑as‑code workflows and governance via Unity Catalog, aiming to make detection logic version‑controlled and compliant with emerging mandates such as NIS2 and DORA.

Strategically, Databricks is deepening its partnership with Anthropic, with Claude models powering parts of the agentic analysis and Anthropic itself using the Databricks security lakehouse. The company also announced acquisitions of Antimatter (agent authentication and authorization research) and SiftD.ai (detection engineering expertise), reinforcing a push toward a modern, AI‑driven SIEM stack.

Lakewatch is currently in private preview. The release positions Databricks as moving from data and AI infrastructure into security operations, betting that agentic automation plus open lakehouse economics can disrupt entrenched SIEM vendors whose costs and closed architectures limit scale. If execution matches the pitch, Lakewatch could shift SIEMs from log‑storage platforms to AI‑native security systems centered on unified data and agentic workflows.

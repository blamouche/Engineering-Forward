## Elevator pitch
OpenAI's Codex lead Andrew Ambrosino explains how AI has completely flipped product development, why "taste" is emerging as the most valuable skill in an AI-first workplace, and why eliminating roles entirely is a mistake even when everyone can build anything.

## Synthesis
Andrew Ambrosino leads development of the Codex desktop app at OpenAI, with the mandate to make it "the best desktop app that has ever existed, full stop." His conversation with Lenny reveals how AI is reshaping product development from the inside of the company building the tools.

The most striking data point: nearly 100% of OpenAI employees — not just engineers — now use Codex weekly. This universal adoption has flipped the product development process. When everyone can build, the bottleneck shifts from execution to judgment — what to build, in what order, and whether what was built is good. This is why "taste" is emerging as the most valuable professional skill in an AI-first workplace. It's not aesthetic taste but the ability to evaluate quality, make tradeoff decisions, and direct effort toward the right problems when the cost of building approaches zero.

The "zone defense" model describes how PMs at OpenAI operate when everyone can build anything. Instead of man-to-man coverage (each PM owns specific features), team members play zone — picking up whatever needs doing based on priority and context. Roles are collapsed on Andrew's team, but he explicitly warns against eliminating the concept of roles entirely. Without any role structure, teams lose accountability and clarity. The collapsed-role model works because it preserves ownership while removing the rigid boundaries that slow execution.

The timing insight is important: Andrew believes the Codex app would have failed if launched in November rather than February. Model readiness — the underlying capability of the AI — determines whether a product experience is magical or frustrating. Shipping on the right model version matters more than shipping the right features on the wrong model.

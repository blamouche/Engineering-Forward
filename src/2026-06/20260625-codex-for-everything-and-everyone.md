# Codex for Everything and Everyone
**Source**: https://every.to/context-window/codex-for-everything-and-everyone
**Date**: 2026-06-25
**Author**: Laura Entis, Katie Parrott (Every)
**Keywords**: OpenAI Codex, knowledge work, Sites, plugins, Claude Tag, AI agents, Slack agents

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is pushing Codex well beyond coding with Sites and role-specific plugins, betting that knowledge workers—not just developers—will adopt it as their primary agentic work surface.

## Takeaways
- Codex has 5 million weekly active users (vs. ChatGPT's 900 million), but knowledge workers account for ~20% and are growing 3x faster than developer usage
- OpenAI launched role-specific plugins for financial analysts and product managers, and Sites for presenting outputs to non-technical audiences
- Every's "Codex for Knowledge Work" guide was updated within a month of its first edition, reflecting rapid feature evolution
- Anthropic launched Claude Tag, a Slack agent that can be added to channels, connected to tools, and delegated asynchronous work—Anthropic says it created 65% of the product team's code internally
- Every's Claudie (built on Claude Code since January) validates the shared-agent model: institutional knowledge trumps individual personal assistants in a work context
- Dan Shipper identifies a bifurcation: work is splitting into "async delegated work with Slack agents" and "collaborative work with Codex or Cowork"
- AI design "tells" are emerging: Claude Design produces cream-colored backgrounds, large serif typefaces, and "tasteful, slightly askew primary colors" that designers find increasingly recognizable

## Synthesis
OpenAI's strategic push to make Codex a universal work surface—not just a developer tool—represents a significant bet on the broader knowledge worker market. The numbers tell the story: while Codex's 5 million weekly active users are modest compared to ChatGPT's 900 million, the 20% knowledge worker segment is growing three times faster than developer adoption. This growth trajectory is what OpenAI is chasing with Sites (for presenting any type of output) and role-specific plugins (for analysts and PMs).

The parallel launch of Anthropic's Claude Tag in Slack validates a key insight: at work, shared AI teammates with institutional knowledge trump individual personal assistants. Every's experience with Claudie—built on Claude Code since January—demonstrates that this model works in practice, not just theory. The bifurcation Dan Shipper identifies—async delegated work with Slack agents on one side, collaborative work with Codex or Cowork on the other—suggests we're moving toward a dual-surface work environment where humans orchestrate AI across different interaction modes.

A subtle but important signal: AI-generated design is developing its own recognizable aesthetic, just as AI writing developed the "em dash" and "rule of three" tells. Designers report feeling "instinctively repulsed by the warm tones even though I love this kind of color palette," signaling a new creative anxiety parallel to what writers have experienced. This emerging "AI design accent" will become a design literacy issue as these tools proliferate.

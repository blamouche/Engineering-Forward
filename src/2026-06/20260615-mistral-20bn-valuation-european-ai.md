# Mistral Eyes €20bn Valuation as European AI Swagger Returns
**Source**: https://sifted.eu
**Date**: 2026-06-15
**Author: Martin Coulter (Sifted Daily)
**Keywords**: Mistral, €20bn valuation, European AI, Lightspeed, Paul Murphy, SpaceX IPO, private markets, defense AI, UK RAID taskforce

## Elevator pitch
Sifted reports Mistral is in talks to raise at a €20bn valuation, while the SpaceX IPO could spark a $200bn private markets boom and Lightspeed's Paul Murphy—known for the Mistral bet—departs the firm, signaling a reshuffling in European AI's investor landscape.

## Takeaways
- Mistral is reportedly in talks to raise funds at a €20bn valuation, cementing its position as Europe's premier AI champion
- Lightspeed investor Paul Murphy, who led the firm's bet on Mistral, is leaving after five years—though he plans to continue investing
- The SpaceX IPO could spark a "$200bn private markets boom" as secondary liquidity flows into private tech companies
- The UK government unveiled the Rapid AI Delivery (RAID) taskforce during London Tech Week to accelerate military AI adoption, but founders warn procurement processes remain a structural barrier
- European tech has "finally discovered some swagger," per Sifted founder John Thornhill, with defence tech investment surging amid geopolitical tensions

## Synthesis
Sifted's daily briefing captures several intersecting threads in European AI and tech. The Mistral valuation talk—€20bn—is notable for what it reveals about the European AI funding landscape. Mistral has become the canonical European AI champion, and its valuation trajectory reflects both genuine technical progress and the strategic premium that European investors and governments place on having a domestic frontier AI lab. The €20bn figure would place Mistral firmly in the unicorn-plus tier, though still far behind the valuations of US frontier labs like Anthropic and OpenAI.

Paul Murphy's departure from Lightspeed is a signal event for European AI venture capital. Murphy led Lightspeed's investment in Mistral—arguably the most successful European AI venture bet of the current cycle. His departure, with plans to continue investing independently, suggests that the European AI investment ecosystem is maturing beyond the traditional US-led VC model. The fact that the investor most associated with the Mistral bet is going independent may also indicate that the best AI investment opportunities in Europe require a different approach than large US firms can provide.

The SpaceX IPO's potential to spark a "$200bn private markets boom" is a macro-level signal. When a company of SpaceX's scale goes public, it creates secondary liquidity for early investors and employees, who then reinvest in private companies. This could be particularly meaningful for European AI, where capital availability has been a constraint compared to the US.

The UK RAID taskforce story highlights a structural problem in defence AI adoption. The government wants to accelerate AI in the military, but founders and investors report that the Ministry of Defence's procurement processes favour incumbents and are too slow for startups. This is not unique to the UK—defence procurement is structurally incompatible with startup timelines in most countries—but it limits the ability of AI startups to serve the defence market, which is one of the largest potential customers for AI technology.

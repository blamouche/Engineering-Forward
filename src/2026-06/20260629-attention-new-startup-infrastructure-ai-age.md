# Attention Is the New Startup Infrastructure: The Founder's Playbook for the AI Age

**Date**: 2026-06-29
**Author**: Linas Seksys (Linas's Newsletter)

## Elevator pitch
In an AI-saturated market where anyone can build faster, copy faster, and bundle faster, the founder's ability to command attention has become the scarcest input in company building — and it's an operating capability you build, not a personality trait.

## Takeaways
- The old playbook (build product, find PMF, layer distribution on top) is breaking because AI lets anyone build, copy, and bundle faster — product alone is no longer the moat
- Erik Torenberg's "New Media, One Year In" defines a16z's offering as "go-direct as a service" for startups: build your brand, reach key people, win the battle for attention
- "Interesting" is not a personality trait — it is an operating capability you build, instrument, and compound like a product
- Attention as infrastructure means treating narrative like a product surface: measured, iterated, and integrated into the company's core operating system
- The practical playbook: founders must go direct, be interesting consistently, and build systems that turn narrative into customers, hires, capital, and category ownership

## Synthesis
Linas Seksys's newsletter, built on Erik Torenberg's retrospective of a16z's New Media team, argues that a repricing is underway in how startups win. For two decades, the playbook was simple: build a great product, find product-market fit, then layer distribution on top. Marketing was a function, narrative was a press release, and attention was something you bought or borrowed once you had something worth talking about.

That model is breaking. When AI lets anyone build faster, copy faster, and bundle faster, the product alone is no longer the moat. The founder's ability to make the right people understand the right thing, before the rest of the market catches up, has become the scarcest input in company building. Torenberg's essay defines a16z's New Media offering bluntly as "go-direct as a service" — built to help founders build their brand, reach key people, and win the battle for attention.

The core argument is that to win, you must go direct; to go direct, you must be interesting; and in an AI-saturated market, "interesting" is not a personality trait but an operating capability you build, instrument, and compound like a product. This means treating attention as infrastructure: the moves, the system, and the metrics founders need to turn narrative into customers, hires, capital, and category ownership. The implication for engineering teams is that building great technology is necessary but no longer sufficient — the distribution layer, once an afterthought, is now a core engineering challenge that demands the same rigor as the product itself.

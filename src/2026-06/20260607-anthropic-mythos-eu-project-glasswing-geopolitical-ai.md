# Anthropic Made the EU Come Asking for Mythos: AI Deployed as a Quasi-Diplomatic Act
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/weeklyfintechpulse402
**Date**: 2026-06-07
**Author**: Linas Beliunas
**Keywords**: anthropic, mythos, project-glasswing, eu, enisa, geopolitics, ai-safety, frontier-ai

## Elevator pitch
The European Commission flew to San Francisco to ask Anthropic for access to Project Glasswing — the restricted program for testing Mythos before public release — making the EU the first non-US/UK entrant and turning a commercial AI product launch into a quasi-diplomatic act with the US government holding a veto.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic offered ENISA (the EU's cybersecurity agency) access to Project Glasswing, joining Microsoft, Apple, JPMorgan, CrowdStrike, US government departments, and the UK's AI Security Institute
- The White House has already rejected some of Anthropic's plans to widen Glasswing on security grounds — a private company's customer list now runs partly through Washington
- Dario Amodei wants allies to use Mythos to defend Ukraine and Taiwan while refusing to see it "turned on our own people"
- An unsettled condition is how much access Anthropic itself gains to EU systems while ENISA runs the tool — top-tier AI deployed as a two-way relationship, not a one-way sale
- Anthropic says safeguarded Mythos-class models reach all customers within weeks, meaning this access fight is about a head start, not the capability itself

## Synthesis
Anthropic's decision to let ENISA, the EU's cybersecurity agency, into Project Glasswing marks a new phase in frontier AI deployment. The bloc that writes the rulebook for American tech flew to San Francisco to ask a private company for a favor — access to the restricted program where a handful of organizations test Mythos before any broad release. The EU becomes the first entrant outside the US and UK, joining a club that includes Microsoft, Apple, JPMorgan, CrowdStrike, several US government departments, and the UK's AI Security Institute.

What most observers will miss is the geopolitical dimension. The White House has already rejected some of Anthropic's plans to widen Glasswing on security grounds. A private company's customer list now runs partly through Washington. Dario Amodei has been explicit about wanting allies to use Mythos to defend Ukraine and Taiwan while refusing to see it "turned on our own people." Deploying a commercial AI product has become a quasi-diplomatic act, with the US government holding a veto over who gets in — something we haven't seen before.

The terms also expose a deeper shift. One unsettled condition is how much access Anthropic itself would gain to the EU's systems while ENISA runs the tool. Using the model may mean letting the vendor inside your critical infrastructure. For a sovereignty-obsessed Brussels, that's the real price and the real precedent: top-tier AI deployed as a two-way relationship, not a one-way sale.

The calendar matters. Anthropic says safeguarded Mythos-class models reach all customers within weeks, which means this access fight is about a head start and the raw, unguarded version, not the capability itself. Frontier AI go-to-market is now splitting in two: a guard-railed product anyone can buy, and a rationed offensive-grade tier handed out by geopolitical alignment. Anthropic wins the trusted-vendor slot and a government moat; ship-to-everyone economics lose, and so does the EU's sovereignty story. The ultimate tell will be how much weaker the public version turns out to be.

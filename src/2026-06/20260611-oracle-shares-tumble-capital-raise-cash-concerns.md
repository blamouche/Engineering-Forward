# Oracle Shares Tumble 11% on Capital Raise and Cash Concerns Amid AI Buildout
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/oracle-shares-tumble-11percent-on-increased-capital-raise-cash-concerns.html
**Date**: June 11, 2026
**Author**: Jordan Novet
**Keywords**: Oracle, AI infrastructure, capital expenditure, free cash flow, Stargate, OpenAI, cloud infrastructure, capital raise, earnings

## Elevator pitch
Oracle beat on earnings but its stock tumbled as investors reacted to a $40 billion capital raise, $23.7 billion in negative free cash flow, and a 162% jump in capital expenditures driven by its AI infrastructure buildout — raising questions about whether the spending will translate into profit growth.

## Takeaways
- Oracle's stock slid despite beating on both earnings and revenue, with Q4 revenue jumping 21% to $19.18 billion.
- The company plans to raise $40 billion through debt and equity financing, on top of $43 billion in debt and $5 billion in equity already raised in fiscal 2026.
- Capital expenditures jumped 162% to $55.7 billion, and the company reported $23.7 billion in negative free cash flow for the fiscal year.
- Cloud infrastructure revenue jumped 93% to $5.8 billion, and remaining performance obligation reached $638 billion — up 363% — with over 50% coming from OpenAI.
- Oracle plans to bring online almost one gigawatt of computing power in the current quarter, roughly the total for all of fiscal 2026.

## Synthesis
CNBC reports that Oracle shares tumbled despite the company beating on both the top and bottom lines in its fiscal fourth quarter. Revenue jumped 21% to $19.18 billion, topping analyst estimates, and adjusted earnings per share of $2.03 exceeded the $1.96 consensus. But Oracle told investors to expect an additional $20 billion capital raise while reporting negative free cash flow for the year, sending the stock down and erasing its year-to-date gains. The tension at the heart of the report is between strong top-line growth and the massive capital requirements of the AI infrastructure buildout.

The financial scale of the buildout is striking. Capital expenditures jumped 162% to $55.7 billion. The company plans to raise $40 billion through debt and equity financing, including a $20 billion share sale — on top of $43 billion in debt and $5 billion in equity already raised in fiscal 2026. Free cash flow came in at negative $23.7 billion for the fiscal year. New CFO Hilary Maxson said net cash outlay for capex in fiscal 2027 will be around $70 billion, excluding $20 billion to $25 billion in prepayments from customers. The question investors are grappling with is whether this level of spending will result in profit growth, and the stock reaction suggests skepticism.

The growth metrics that justify the spending are real but forward-looking. Cloud infrastructure revenue jumped 93% to $5.8 billion. Remaining performance obligation — revenue that has been contracted but not yet recognized — reached $638 billion on May 31, up 363%. Bank of America analysts noted that over 50% of that remaining performance obligation comes from OpenAI, Oracle's partner in the Stargate project to develop AI infrastructure in the U.S. Oracle is looking to bring online almost one gigawatt of computing power in the current quarter, roughly the total for all of fiscal 2026. CEO Clay Magouyrk emphasized the scale of the ramp. The company maintained its revenue guidance of $90 billion for fiscal 2027 and lifted its adjusted EPS forecast to $8.05. Piper Sandler analysts recommended buying the stock, writing that they remain constructive on Oracle's AI-driven consumption growth. The earnings report crystallizes the central question of the AI infrastructure cycle: whether the massive capital deployment will generate sustainable returns, or whether it represents a bet that the market has not yet priced in.

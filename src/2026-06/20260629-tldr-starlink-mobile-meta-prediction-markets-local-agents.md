## Elevator pitch
SpaceX plans a Starlink mobile service competing with Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile; Meta explores a prediction markets app à la Polymarket/Kalshi; and local coding agents emerge as a new development paradigm.

## Takeaways
- SpaceX plans to launch Starlink mobile service in the US, potentially building its own terrestrial mobile network — direct competition with major carriers
- Meta is building a prediction markets app; Zuckerberg urges exploration of working with Polymarket and Kalshi, which handled a combined $50B in trades last year
- Meta's pattern: let startups prove demand, then deploy 3.56 billion daily active users against them (Stories → Snapchat, Reels → TikTok, Arena → Polymarket)
- Local coding agents are highlighted as an emerging trend — agents that run on local machines rather than cloud APIs
- Meta previously tried a prediction markets app (Forecast, 2020) focused on COVID outcomes — shut down in 2022 with minimal traction

## Synthesis
TLDR's June 29 roundup covers three stories at the intersection of big tech strategy and emerging technology. SpaceX's Starlink mobile service plans represent a potential disruption of the US telecom market. The company is considering building its own terrestrial mobile network, which would put it in direct competition with Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile. Some analysts suggest the idea may be a negotiation gamble to extract better deals from telecom partners, as rolling out the required infrastructure would cost billions.

Meta's exploration of prediction markets follows its well-established copy-and-deploy pattern: Snapchat proved ephemeral stories worked (Instagram Stories followed); TikTok proved short video worked (Reels followed); now Polymarket and Kalshi have proved prediction markets work, handling a combined $50 billion in trades and already surpassing $130 billion in 2026. Meta's "Arena" app would sit outside the Facebook/Instagram ecosystem, initially using a points system rather than real money. However, Meta's track record with standalone apps is consistently poor — a 2019 "New Product Experimentation" initiative produced apps for podcasts, travel, music, and matchmaking, few of which gained traction. Meta already tried prediction markets once: Forecast, launched in 2020 for COVID outcomes, shut down in 2022.

The local coding agents trend is noteworthy for engineering teams. As AI coding tools mature, the ability to run agents locally — on developer machines rather than through cloud APIs — addresses concerns about latency, cost, code privacy, and vendor dependency. This aligns with the broader pattern of open-weight models like GLM-5.2 enabling self-hosted AI workflows.

# Why is Meta destroying its engineering organization?
**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-is-meta-destroying-its-engineering
**Date**: 2026-06-16
**Author**: Gergely Orosz
**Keywords**: Meta, engineering culture, AI, data labeling, RLHF, layoffs, keystroke tracking, Llama, Scale AI

## Elevator pitch
Meta's leadership has systematically dismantled its once-legendary engineering culture through invasive keystroke tracking, forced reassignment of thousands of engineers to data labeling, a 10% layoff announcement, and token-maxxing incentives—turning engineering from a profit center into a cost center.

## Takeaways
- Meta is tracking every keystroke and mouse click of engineers with no opt-out, using the data to train AI models, raising serious privacy concerns
- 30-50% of engineers on core teams have been forcefully reassigned to the ADO (Agent Data Optimisation) org for data labeling and RLHF work—around 4,000-5,000 software engineers, roughly 1 in every 5-6
- Meta announced 10% layoffs with a month's lead time, creating a toxic waiting period; engineers began "tokenmaxxing" (generating excessive AI token usage) to game performance metrics
- The most embarrassing outage in Meta's history occurred on May 30, attributed to understaffed infra/security teams and AI-generated code merged with AI-only reviews
- Meta acquired Scale AI for $14.8B and brought in Alexandr Wang to lead AI strategy, but the approach of surveilling and reassigning engineers is driving top talent away

## Synthesis
Gergely Orosz's deep dive into Meta's engineering collapse is a cautionary tale about what happens when leadership over-corrects for a perceived strategic gap. For two decades, Meta had one of the most engineering-centric cultures in Big Tech—from "move fast and break things" to the more mature "move fast with stable infra." Engineers had autonomy, chose their teams via bootcamp, and felt they worked in a profit center. That culture has been systematically dismantled since April 2026.

The catalyst was Meta's determination not to miss another platform shift after failing with mobile and VR. When Llama 4 disappointed, Zuckerberg acquired Scale AI for $14.8B and installed Alexandr Wang to overhaul AI strategy. But the execution has been catastrophic for engineers: mandatory keystroke tracking with no opt-out, forced reassignment of 30-50% of product engineering teams to data labeling work, a month-long layoff countdown, and token counts used as performance metrics. The result is a workforce optimizing for performative AI usage rather than real engineering quality.

The consequences are already visible. Infra and security teams are understaffed. The May 30 outage—Meta's most embarrassing—was caused by brittle systems and inadequate review. Meanwhile, longer-tenured engineers are actively seeking new positions. Meta's loss is becoming the gain of startups and other Big Tech companies who are scooping up experienced, AI-fluent engineers.

The broader lesson extends beyond Meta. Any leadership tempted to make drastic AI-related org changes should study this case. Engineering culture is fragile; once trust erodes, it doesn't easily return. And treating engineers as interchangeable data labelers doesn't produce good AI—it produces good resignation letters.

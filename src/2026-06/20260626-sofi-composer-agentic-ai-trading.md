# SoFi Acquired Composer to Bet Against Agentic AI Trading
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/fintechpulse1094
**Date**: 2026-06-26
**Author**: Linas Beliūnas (Linas's Newsletter)
**Keywords**: SoFi, Composer, agentic AI trading, Robinhood, Coinbase, Plaid, fintech, AI investing, foundation model

## Elevator pitch
SoFi acquired Composer and launched an AI-powered platform that lets users describe investing strategies in plain English and converts them into rules-based, backtestable, auto-executing portfolios — deliberately betting against the agentic AI trading rush embraced by Robinhood and Coinbase.

## Takeaways
- SoFi launched Composer by SoFi on June 23, an AI-powered platform that lets users describe investing strategies in plain English and converts them into rules-based, backtestable, auto-executing portfolios.
- The timing is deliberate: in four weeks, Robinhood shipped Agentic Trading (connecting external AI agents to isolated brokerage accounts for autonomous stock trades) and Coinbase followed with a 21-product blitz including Coinbase Advisor, one of the first SEC-registered AI investment advisors.
- SoFi's approach is deliberately different: rather than letting AI make investment decisions autonomously, Composer lets users build their own strategies with AI assistance, betting that most investors would rather retain control.
- Plaid launched a new sequential foundation model that can see patterns across financial data that no single bank can — a fundamentally different capability from traditional bank-specific analytics.
- The three companies (SoFi, Robinhood, Coinbase) arrived at very different answers to the same question: what should AI-powered investing look like?

## Synthesis
The fintech AI landscape diverged sharply in June 2026, with three major companies racing to define what AI-powered investing looks like — and arriving at very different answers. The divergence was compressed into just four weeks of product launches.

SoFi's acquisition and launch of Composer by SoFi represents the most deliberate contrarian bet. On June 23, SoFi launched Composer, an AI-powered platform that lets users describe investing strategies in plain English, which the system converts into rules-based, backtestable, auto-executing portfolios. The key distinction is that the AI helps users build their own strategies rather than making autonomous investment decisions on their behalf. SoFi is betting that most investors would rather retain agency over their investment decisions, using AI as a tool for strategy creation rather than delegation.

Robinhood took the opposite approach with Agentic Trading, shipping a feature that lets users connect external AI agents to isolated brokerage accounts for autonomous stock trades. This is the full delegation model: the user connects an AI agent, sets parameters, and the agent executes trades within the isolated account. Robinhood is betting that a segment of investors wants to hand off trading entirely to AI.

Coinbase launched a 21-product blitz that included Coinbase Advisor, one of the first SEC-registered AI investment advisors, alongside Coinbase for Agents, a platform enabling third-party AI to trade crypto within user-set limits. Coinbase is building the infrastructure layer for agentic crypto trading, positioning itself as the platform that other AI agents plug into.

The philosophical divide is clear. SoFi believes users want to build strategies with AI assistance (human-in-the-loop). Robinhood believes some users want to delegate trading entirely to AI agents (human-on-the-loop). Coinbase is building the plumbing for an ecosystem of AI trading agents (platform model). None of these approaches is definitively right or wrong — they reflect different bets on how investors will want to interact with AI in their financial decisions.

Meanwhile, Plaid launched a new sequential foundation model that can see across financial data in ways no single bank can replicate. This is a different category of AI capability: not trading or advisory, but financial intelligence infrastructure that spans institutions. Plaid's model could become the data layer that other fintech AI applications build on.

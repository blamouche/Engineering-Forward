# France Seeks to Block UK Role in €5bn EU Scaleup Fund

**Date**: 2026-06-29

## Elevator pitch

France is seeking to block the UK's participation in the EU's €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund, proposing geographical restrictions on investments that could upend months of negotiations and deny British startups access to the bloc's ambitious late-stage investment vehicle.

- The fund involves major players including EQT and the British Business Bank, whose participation is now uncertain

## Synthesis

Europe's tech sector has long struggled with a lack of late-stage VC money compared to the U.S. The €5bn Scaleup Europe Fund was designed to address this gap by backing strategically important European startups before they're acquired by American companies or lured across the Atlantic. But before the fund has even deployed capital, France is seeking to restrict UK participation — a move that could upend months of negotiations.

The article is behind Sifted's Pro paywall, but the accessible content and search results reveal the core story: France is proposing geographical restrictions on investments that would effectively exclude UK-based startups. This involves major financial institutions including EQT and the British Business Bank, whose role in the fund is now uncertain. The dispute reflects the post-Brexit reality that UK-EU cooperation in strategic tech investment remains politically fraught, even when the economic rationale for inclusion is clear.

The broader context is Europe's struggle to retain its scaling companies. Without sufficient late-stage capital, European startups face a binary choice: exit early to U.S. acquirers or relocate to American markets where growth capital is abundant. The €5bn fund is meant to be a partial solution, but geopolitical friction between member states — and between the EU and the UK — threatens to fragment the effort. France's position suggests that the fund may be weaponized as a tool of industrial policy rather than purely a market-gap solution, prioritizing EU-based companies over broader European (including UK) competitiveness.

The timing is significant: the dispute emerges just as European AI companies need unprecedented capital to compete with U.S. frontier labs. If UK startups are excluded, the British Business Bank may need to develop alternative late-stage vehicles, potentially creating a fragmented European funding landscape at a time when consolidation is most needed.

## Synthesis
Linas Beliūnas catalogues every Claude launch from January through May 2026, providing what amounts to an operational manual for Anthropic's product ecosystem. The scale is staggering: 29 distinct product updates in five months, with run-rate revenue growing from $14B to $47B — a trajectory the author calls unprecedented in B2B software history.

January began with structural shifts. Anthropic Labs launched under Instagram co-founder Mike Krieger as an experimental product arm, followed by Claude Cowork — "Claude Code for the rest of your work" — letting any knowledge worker assign multi-step file tasks to Claude in a sandboxed VM. The month closed with Interactive Apps (Connectors): Slack, Canva, Figma, Box, and Clay integrated via MCP, making Claude a hub that reaches into your stack and acts on your behalf.

February shipped ten updates in 28 days. The ad-free commitment (vs. OpenAI testing ads) was both product decision and competitive positioning. Opus 4.6 launched with 1M token context, Agent Teams (parallel agents), 14.5-hour task completion window, and native PowerPoint integration. Sonnet 4.6 followed as the default for Free/Pro users, with the telling data point that users chose Sonnet 4.6 over Opus 4.5 in 59% of head-to-head comparisons — the mid-tier had crossed the quality threshold for most office work. Cowork expanded with scheduled tasks, 12 new MCP connectors, and department-specific plugins.

March matured the platform: Memory for free tier, Voice Mode in Claude Code, inline visualizations (HTML/SVG, not image generation), the Claude Partner Network with $100M commitment and Certified Architect certification, and 1M context going GA at standard pricing (a 900K-token request now costs the same per-token as a 9K request).

April brought the agentic shift. Claude Mythos Preview (April 7) was the most consequential and restricted launch — too powerful for public release due to cybersecurity capabilities, it identified thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities, many 1-2 decades old, and was deployed to 12 founding cybersecurity partners (Amazon, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorgan, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Palo Alto Networks) via Project Glasswing. Cowork went GA, Claude Managed Agents launched (cloud-hosted agents with infrastructure, state management, and permissioning), Claude Code Desktop was redesigned for parallel agent sessions, and Opus 4.7 shipped as the production flagship with SWE-bench Pro at 64.3% and a new `xhigh` effort level.

May accelerated the enterprise play: Skills Marketplace public launch (~600 skills, 47% token reduction on document workflows), ten financial services agent templates, Code with Claude developer conference, Claude Platform on AWS GA, the `/goal` command ("the single most underrated AI feature of 2026"), Claude for Small Business, PwC/KPMG Big Four partnerships, the Stainless acquisition (SDK generator), and the massive May 28 double-announcement: Opus 4.8 (Dynamic Workflows for hundreds of parallel sub-agents, Effort Control, Fast Mode at 2.5x speed and 3x lower cost) alongside a $65B Series H at a $965B valuation.

Three strategic threads emerge from the full sequence: the shift from chatbot to execution platform (AI labor, not AI assistance), the free tier as acquisition engine (shrinking the gap between trying and building workflows), and safety as competitive moat (ad-free commitment, restricted Mythos, Project Glasswing — trust as the differentiator that compounds).

# Token Tightening
**Source**: https://every.to/context-window/token-tightening
**Date**: 2026-06-23
**Author**: Laura Entis (Every)
**Keywords**: token economics, AI budget, compute allocation, ROI, enterprise AI, Opus 4.8, Fable

## Elevator pitch
The era of measuring AI adoption by raw token consumption is ending—companies are starting to allocate compute budgets like trading portfolios, reserving the most powerful models for those who can prove the biggest returns.

## Takeaways
- The "tokenmaxxing" era of measuring AI adoption by token volume is over; companies like Uber, Meta, Amazon, and Walmart have capped employee AI use
- Mike Taylor predicts token budgets will be managed like trading portfolios, with risk limits, auditing, and approval requirements for large spends
- Top engineers will receive multiples of their salary in token budgets, similar to how financial traders manage portfolios far exceeding their compensation
- Opus 4.8 costs 1.7 times more than the previous model, driving up enterprise bills often without proportional results
- Companies like 8x8 now require employees to prove older models can't do the job before granting access to frontier models
- Kieran Klaassen spent $2,000 in Cursor credits in two days—a number that will look quaint as models improve
- The emerging model: interns get Composer 2.5, most employees get Codex-equivalent models, and Fable-grade frontier models go only to an elite few

## Synthesis
The AI industry is undergoing a fundamental shift from unconstrained token spending to strategic allocation. The heady days of "tokenmaxxing"—measuring AI adoption by how many tokens employees consume—are giving way to a more disciplined, ROI-driven approach. This transition is driven by three converging forces: shifting pricing models from frontier labs, the transition from chatbots to long-running agents that consume far more tokens, and the emergence of incredibly costly new models.

Mike Taylor's financial trading analogy is particularly illuminating. Just as traders manage portfolios many times their annual compensation with strict risk controls, AI-literate engineers will be given token budgets that dwarf their salaries—but with auditing, risk limits, and approval requirements. This creates a new hierarchy: the interns work with Composer 2.5, most knowledge workers use Codex-level agents, and frontier model access becomes a scarce resource reserved for those who can demonstrate measurable returns.

The practical implication is that "AI democratization" has a ceiling. As models become more capable and expensive, the democratization applies to agents built on older models, not to frontier capabilities themselves. Companies like 8x8 are already implementing downgrade-first policies, and the financial industry's portfolio management model suggests this will become standard practice across all sectors.

# Open and Closed Models Are on Different Exponentials
**Source**: https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-and-closed-models-are-on-different
**Date**: 2026-06-02
**Author**: Nathan Lambert (Interconnects)
**Keywords**: open models, closed models, AI economics, margins, oligopoly, open weights, inference, customization, market structure, AI industry

## Elevator pitch
The open and closed AI model economies are growing on different exponentials—closed labs monetize the top end of knowledge work through integrated products, while the open model economy will take longer but eventually exceed the cumulative value of the closed lab oligopoly through broader diffusion across the economy.

## Takeaways
- The largest debate defining the future balance of power between open and closed AI ecosystems is primarily economic: will users continue to pay dramatically more for top closed models?
- Closed labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, with Google expected to catch up) will always make the most efficient models for intelligence at a given cost—they operate as an oligopoly resembling the cloud market
- Many businesses want to switch to open models but the models aren't yet good enough in out-of-distribution tasks; eventually open model builders will stop chasing Claude and GPT on benchmarks and fill this niche
- The total market value of the open model economy will dramatically exceed the cumulative value of OpenAI and Anthropic, but revenue and margins will be distributed across many companies
- Open models are by nature not integrated, so they rely on multiple companies coordinating to serve them—each layer will have alternatives, driving prices to commodity levels

## Synthesis
Nathan Lambert's analysis cuts through the open-vs-closed model debate by reframing it as an economic question. The core argument is that open and closed models are on different exponentials—different growth curves with different characteristics, not a simple race where one side wins.

The closed model economy is an integrated exponential. The best closed labs make the most efficient models for intelligence at a given cost. They monetize the top end of knowledge work through integrated products where the model, the harness, and the application are all built by the same company. This creates high margins and an oligopoly structure resembling the cloud market today. Lambert expects OpenAI and Anthropic to be valued in the $2-10T range in 5-10 years.

The open model economy is a distributed exponential. It takes longer to develop because open models are by nature not integrated—multiple companies must coordinate to serve them. But the ecosystem will be far more diverse and numerous than the closed lab oligopoly. Each layer (training, fine-tuning, inference, serving) will have alternatives, driving prices toward commodity levels. These low, predictable prices are where many enterprises will enter to build in-house agents and tools for niche tasks.

The critical insight is about out-of-distribution tasks. Many businesses want to switch to open models today, but open models aren't yet good enough for tasks outside their training distribution. Eventually, open model builders will stop chasing Claude and GPT on the Artificial Analysis index and instead fill niche tasks where open models are good enough and the price advantage matters. As customizing models becomes easier through the emerging open model fine-tuning stack (Tinker, Fireworks, Prime Intellect, etc.), this market becomes even bigger.

Lambert also pushes back on recursive self-improvement (RSI) claims that would give closed labs an unassailable advantage. The open and closed economies will both grow fast, but the open model economy tracks the broader diffusion of AI into the entire economy and world—a larger, slower, but ultimately more valuable curve.

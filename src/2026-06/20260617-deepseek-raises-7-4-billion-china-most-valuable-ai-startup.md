# DeepSeek Raises $7.4 Billion, Becomes China's Most Valuable AI Startup
**Source**: https://www.forbes.com/sites/anishasircar/2026/06/17/deepseek-just-raised-74-billion-heres-the-catch/
**Date**: 2026-06-17
**Author**: Anisha Sircar (Forbes)
**Keywords**: DeepSeek, Liang Wenfeng, funding, $7.4 billion, $50 billion valuation, China AI, Tencent, CATL, state investment, limited partnership

## Elevator pitch
DeepSeek closed its first-ever external funding round at $7.4 billion, valuing the company above $50 billion and making it China's most valuable AI startup—but investors received equity through a limited partnership with a five-year lock-up and zero voting rights, while only China's state-backed AI fund got direct voting rights.

## Takeaways
- DeepSeek raised over 50 billion yuan (~$7.4B) in its first external round, valuing the company above $50 billion—making it China's most valuable AI startup
- Founder Liang Wenfeng committed ~20 billion yuan (~$3B) of his own money, making him both founder and largest investor in the round
- External investors (including Tencent at ~10B yuan and CATL at ~5B yuan) invested through a limited partnership controlled by Liang, with a 5-year lock-up and zero voting rights
- China's National AI Industry Investment Fund invested directly into DeepSeek—uniquely receiving voting rights and no lock-up—raising state-alignment concerns
- Despite the massive round, DeepSeek remains much smaller than US counterparts: Anthropic raised $65B at a $965B valuation, OpenAI $122B at $852B

## Synthesis
DeepSeek's first external funding round is a landmark event for the Chinese AI ecosystem. The company that famously triggered a historic selloff of AI stocks in early 2025—including a sharp Nvidia decline—has now taken outside money for the first time, having been entirely bankrolled by founder Liang Wenfeng's quantitative hedge fund High-Flyer until now. The $7.4 billion raised makes it one of the largest single financings in Chinese startup history.

What makes this round unusual is its structure. Rather than selling equity directly, external investors put their capital into a limited partnership controlled by Liang. This LP comes with a five-year lock-up period and zero voting rights, meaning backers cannot easily exit and have essentially no say in company governance. Tencent reportedly committed around 10 billion yuan and battery giant CATL around 5 billion yuan, making them the largest external investors—yet even they accepted the LP terms.

The one exception is China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund, a state vehicle for strategic tech investments, which invested directly into DeepSeek rather than through the LP. This gave the state fund actual voting rights and no lock-up—making it the only backer with governance power. This structural asymmetry intensifies scrutiny of DeepSeek's relationship with the Chinese state, particularly given existing bans and restrictions on DeepSeek's technology in several countries over data-access concerns tied to Chinese law.

Even at $50+ billion, DeepSeek is playing in a different financial league than the American frontier labs it's often compared to. Anthropic's late-May Series H raised $65 billion at a $965 billion valuation, and OpenAI's March round raised $122 billion at $852 billion. DeepSeek's chatbot remains free and open-weight with millions of global users, and the governance structure is unlikely to change day-to-day operations—but it sharpens questions about state influence over China's most prominent AI company.

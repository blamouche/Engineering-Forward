# OpenAI Considers Drastic Price Cuts Anticipating War for Users With Anthropic

**Source**: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-11/openai-weighs-major-price-cuts-to-win-anthropic-users-wsj-says
**Date**: June 11, 2026
**Author**: Bloomberg / Wall Street Journal
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Anthropic, price war, token pricing, IPO, AI competition, GPT-5.5, Claude, business models, profit margins

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is considering significant cuts to what it charges for tokens in anticipation of similar reductions by Anthropic, signaling a potential price war between the two frontier AI companies ahead of their hotly anticipated public listings.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI is weighing various cost changes including reducing per-token pricing if Anthropic does the same, according to people familiar with the matter
- Businesses have begun balking at high AI usage prices, creating pressure for cost reductions
- Price cuts could erode already thin profit margins for both companies, which are losing billions due to enormous computing costs
- A price war would be an early test of both companies' business models ahead of anticipated IPOs
- OpenAI currently charges consumers in tiered subscriptions of $8, $20, and $100+ per month for GPT-5.5 access
- Anthropic charges $17/month for Claude Pro (annual) and $100+/month for Claude Max
- The deliberations are described as early with nothing decided yet

## Synthesis
The potential price war between OpenAI and Anthropic represents a critical inflection point for the AI industry's economics. Both companies are simultaneously losing billions of dollars on computing costs while preparing for highly anticipated public offerings, making pricing strategy a key competitive battleground. OpenAI's consideration of significant token price cuts — conditional on Anthropic matching — signals that the era of premium AI pricing may be ending.

The pressure comes from multiple directions. Businesses adopting AI at scale have started balking at high prices, particularly as token usage explodes. Simultaneously, Chinese open-weight models are competing on price for less demanding workloads, creating a pincer effect on the frontier labs' pricing power. The report notes that both companies are already losing billions due to enormous computing costs, so price cuts would directly impact their ability to reach profitability — a concern for prospective IPO investors.

The competitive dynamics are asymmetric. OpenAI's consumer subscriptions range from $8 to $100+ monthly, while Anthropic's Claude Pro is $17/month annually and Claude Max is $100+/month. A token price war would primarily affect API and usage-based pricing rather than subscriptions, but it would test whether either company can sustain lower prices while maintaining model quality. The timing is particularly fraught: both companies are pursuing IPOs — Anthropic reportedly valued at up to $2 trillion, OpenAI targeting above $1 trillion — making profitability questions central to their valuation narratives. A price war before listing could reshape investor expectations for both companies' public debuts.

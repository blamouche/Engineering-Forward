# Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, and Coinbase vs Circle: The Stablecoin War for Agentic Payments
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/weeklyfintechpulse402
**Date**: 2026-06-07
**Author**: Linas Beliunas
**Keywords**: stablecoin, usdc, coinbase, stripe, visa, mastercard, agentic-payments, x402

## Elevator pitch
A leaked report that Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, and Coinbase are building a rival stablecoin to USDC exposes the fragile economics of Circle's reserve-interest model and positions agentic payments — where USDC already owns 99.8% of x402 protocol volume — as the real battleground.

## Takeaways
- Coinbase may be helping Stripe, Visa, Mastercard build a stablecoin to replace USDC, with the report moving markets: both COIN and CRCL dipped on the news
- 94% of Circle's revenue comes from interest on reserves — a rate the Fed sets and Circle cannot touch — and revenue already fell 10% in one quarter on a 66bp rate decline
- The USDC deal supplies 23% of Coinbase's total revenue; the contract is up for renewal around August 2026, giving Coinbase leverage to co-own a rival token
- USDC already captures 99.8% of x402 agentic payments, but Coinbase's Base chain carries ~90% of on-chain agentic transactions — token-agnostic exposure that hedges Coinbase
- Distribution decides who collects the stablecoin toll; rates decide how much there is to collect — and the toll is dropping

## Synthesis
The report that Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, and potentially Coinbase are building a stablecoin platform to challenge USDC exposes the structural fragility of Circle's business model. USDC circulation hit $77 billion in Q1 2026, up 28%, with on-chain volume of $21.5 trillion — the adoption is real, but the income beneath it is not durable. Some 94% of Circle's revenue comes from interest on reserves, a rate the Fed sets and Circle cannot touch. Revenue already fell 10% from the prior quarter on a 66 basis point rate decline. Circle is fighting on two fronts: rates where it has no lever, and distribution where the consortium aims.

The build sits on real groundwork: Stripe's $1.1 billion Bridge acquisition, Mastercard's BVNK buy, Visa's multi-chain settlement pilots at a $7 billion run-rate, and Coinbase's white-label stablecoin work. Combined, these four reach into both traditional payments and crypto on-ramps that no current issuer touches at once. In a $325 billion stablecoin market, distribution is the whole game.

Coinbase is the hinge. The USDC arrangement supplies 23% of Coinbase's total revenue, one of its most profitable lines. Yet Circle's results show the relationship is already strained: management flagged Coinbase's growing share of USDC circulation as a drag on margin, and Circle's structural answer — pushing USDC on-platform where it owes Coinbase nothing — grew 3.5x to $13.7 billion. The contract renewal around August 2026 gives Coinbase a credible threat to co-own a rival token, handing it a stronger negotiating position.

The asymmetry the framing hides is critical. Coinbase's Base chain already carries roughly 90% of on-chain agentic transactions, and that exposure is token-agnostic. If a consortium coin launches on Base, Coinbase collects on agentic volume whether the winning token is USDC or the new one. Circle only wins if USDC stays the token. Coinbase is hedged at the chain layer; Circle is betting entirely on the token layer. Meanwhile, every stablecoin reserve model earns the same thing — interest on Treasuries — and that income is falling for all of them at once. Four of the most powerful names in payments are racing to own a toll road whose toll is set by the Fed, and the toll is dropping.

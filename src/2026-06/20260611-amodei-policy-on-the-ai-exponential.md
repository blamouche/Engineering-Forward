# Dario Amodei's "Policy on the AI Exponential": FAA-Style Regulation and Beyond

**Source**: https://darioamodei.com/post/policy-on-the-ai-exponential
**Date**: June 11, 2026
**Author**: Dario Amodei (Anthropic)
**Keywords**: Dario Amodei, Anthropic, AI policy, FAA regulation, frontier AI, job displacement, civil liberties, democratic values, AI governance

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei publishes a sweeping policy agenda calling for binding FAA-style regulation of frontier AI models, wage insurance for job displacement, biomedical regulatory reform, and civil liberties protections — marking a shift from advocating transparency to demanding enforceable regulation.

## Takeaways
- Amodei argues that transparency is no longer enough and calls for binding, enforceable regulation modeled on agencies like the FAA for frontier AI models
- The essay covers five policy domains: regulation, macroeconomics, scientific innovation, civil liberties, and democratic leadership
- On regulation: mandatory third-party testing of frontier models and government power to block unsafe deployments
- On macroeconomics: wage insurance, retention tax credits, and long-term income support (UBI/capital accounts) for job displacement
- On scientific innovation: reform FDA/EMA to accept AI-based simulations and accelerate biomedical approvals
- On civil liberties: ban domestic autonomous weapons, close bulk data loopholes, guarantee AI legal access
- Amodei frames the urgency by comparing democratic institutions to Treebeard from Lord of the Rings — wise but too slow to act against the exponential pace of AI

## Synthesis
Dario Amodei's "Policy on the AI Exponential" marks a significant shift for Anthropic: from advocating for transparency legislation to calling for binding, enforceable regulation of frontier AI. The essay is both a diagnosis and prescription, covering five policy domains where Amodei argues governments must act urgently before AI's exponential progress outpaces democratic institutions.

The central regulatory proposal models AI oversight on the Federal Aviation Administration — treating frontier AI models like cars, airplanes, or drugs: powerful technologies essential to the modern economy but capable of killing large numbers of people if designed or operated poorly. This would involve mandatory third-party testing and government power to block unsafe deployments. The macroeconomic proposals address job displacement through wage insurance, retention tax credits, and long-term income support mechanisms including UBI or capital accounts. On scientific innovation, Amodei calls for reforming the FDA and EMA to accept AI-based simulations and accelerate biomedical approvals.

The civil liberties agenda includes banning domestic autonomous weapons, closing bulk data loopholes, and guaranteeing AI legal access. On democratic leadership, Amodei argues for a global coalition of democratic nations to ensure AI aligns with democratic values globally. The essay's framing — comparing democratic institutions to Treebeard the Ent from Lord of the Rings, who takes a full day simply to say hello — captures the core tension: AI's exponential progress operates at a fundamentally different speed than democratic governance. The timing is notable, coming amid Anthropic's IPO filing and the release of its most powerful model, positioning the company as pro-regulation while commercializing aggressively.

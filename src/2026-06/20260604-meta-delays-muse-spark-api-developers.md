# Meta Keeps Delaying the Muse Spark API Developers Were Promised
**Source**: https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-muse-spark-api-delay
**Date**: 2026-06-04
**Author**: Ana-Maria Stanciuc (TNW)
**Keywords**: meta, muse-spark, api, developer-platform, ai-model, closed-source, monetization, superintelligence-labs

## Elevator pitch
Meta launched its flagship Muse Spark AI model in April 2026 but has repeatedly delayed the developer API with no firm launch date, leaving developers unable to build on a model that Meta claims rivals OpenAI and Anthropic — raising questions about Meta's ability to monetize its massive AI investment.

## Takeaways
- Muse Spark was announced in April 2026 as the centerpiece of Meta Superintelligence Labs, competitive with OpenAI and Anthropic's frontier models
- The API has been delayed repeatedly: from April to May (bugs and infrastructure), then to June, with no firm launch date as of June 2
- Muse Spark is Meta's first closed-source AI model — without an API, outside developers have no way to access it programmatically
- Meta's internal evaluations rate Muse Spark competitive with OpenAI and Anthropic, but no outside evaluator has tested the model — the competitive claim remains unverified
- The delay tests Meta's monetization strategy as the company raised its 2026 capex forecast to as much as $145 billion, up from $115–135 billion
- The model powers Meta's AI assistant across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Threads, but third-party developers cannot integrate it into their own products

## Synthesis
Meta finds itself in an awkward position with Muse Spark, its flagship AI model launched in April 2026. The model shipped, but the API that outside developers need to build on it has been repeatedly delayed with no firm launch date, creating a gap that has now stretched close to two months. The situation highlights the tension between Meta's massive AI investment and its ability to ship developer-facing products.

Muse Spark emerged from Meta's Superintelligence Labs, specifically a secretive unit called TBD Lab. It was positioned as Meta's answer to OpenAI and Anthropic's frontier models, with chief AI officer Alexandr Wang promising the API would arrive "soon" after the April launch. The first delay from April to May was attributed to bugs found in testing and the need to build more infrastructure. The timeline then moved to June, with Meta spokespersons telling Reuters the API was in partner testing and expected this month, while the Wall Street Journal reported no scheduled launch date as of June 2.

The delay is significant because Muse Spark is Meta's first closed-source AI model. Previous Meta models like Llama were released as open-weight files that developers could download and run themselves. For a closed model, the API is the only way outside developers can access the technology. Without it, they cannot integrate Muse Spark into their products, cannot call it programmatically at scale, and cannot ship anything that depends on it. The model can only be used where Meta surfaces it — in its AI assistant across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Threads.

The competitive backdrop makes the delay more pointed. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have made programmatic access a launch-day feature, shipping developer APIs alongside their models. Meta's inability to do the same raises questions about its operational readiness. The delay also tests Meta's monetization strategy: Llama's open-weight releases generated developer goodwill but no direct revenue. Muse Spark was positioned as the model that changes that — the one Meta would charge for through API access. Every week the API doesn't ship, the revenue case remains theoretical.

Meta's internal evaluations rate Muse Spark competitive with OpenAI and Anthropic and ahead of xAI's Grok on most tests, but these results are unverified by outside developers. The structural question is whether the delay signals the model is nearly competitive but still being tuned to close a remaining gap. Meta raised its 2026 capital-expenditure forecast to as much as $145 billion, and the delay tests whether that spending translates into shippable products on schedule.

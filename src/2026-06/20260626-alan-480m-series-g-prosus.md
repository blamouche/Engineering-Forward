**Author**: Martin Coulter (Sifted)

## Elevator pitch
French healthtech startup Alan raised a €480m Series G round led by Prosus at a €5.5bn valuation, with the company hitting €800m ARR and planning international expansion powered by its AI-native healthcare platform.

## Takeaways
- Alan raised €480m in a Series G funding round led by Dutch investment giant Prosus, alongside existing backers Index Ventures and Teachers' Venture Growth, and new investor Dara Holdings.
- The round values Alan at €5.5 billion, making it one of Europe's most valuable healthtech companies.

## Synthesis
French healthtech unicorn Alan has raised €480 million in a Series G funding round led by Prosus, valuing the digital insurer and healthcare platform at €5.5 billion. The round, which also included existing backers Index Ventures and Teachers' Venture Growth alongside new investor Dara Holdings, represents one of the largest European healthtech funding rounds of 2026.

Founded in 2016, Alan began as a digital health insurance provider but has expanded into a broader healthcare platform combining insurance, care navigation, and prevention services. The company says its AI-native approach allows it to offer more personalised healthcare experiences while scaling with limited headcount growth — a key efficiency claim in a sector where administrative overhead traditionally drives costs.

The financial metrics underscore the scale of the business. Alan reached more than €800 million in annual recurring revenue in Q1 2026, representing 53% year-on-year growth. The company serves more than 1.1 million members and over 37,000 businesses, and is profitable in France, its largest market. This combination of rapid growth and profitability in a core market is rare in European healthtech and likely contributed to the strong valuation.

Prosus, the Dutch investment giant that is itself a major player in global consumer technology, will support Alan's expansion into additional international markets. The partnership includes access to Prosus's AI capabilities, including its "Large Commerce Model" — though the specifics of how this will integrate with Alan's healthcare platform remain to be detailed. The deal is subject to regulatory approvals, including sign-off from French financial authorities.

Alan CEO Jean-Charles Samuelian-Werve, who also helped establish French AI darling Mistral, framed the round as enabling faster expansion: new countries, deeper presence in existing markets, and increased investment in AI and product. The connection to Mistral is notable — it signals that Alan's leadership sees AI as central to its competitive strategy, not just a feature layer.

The round signals continued investor appetite for AI-driven healthcare platforms, particularly those that have demonstrated both growth and a path to profitability. Alan's model — integrating insurance, prevention, and care delivery into a single AI-native platform — represents a bet that the future of healthcare is proactive rather than reactive, and that AI is the enabling technology for that shift.

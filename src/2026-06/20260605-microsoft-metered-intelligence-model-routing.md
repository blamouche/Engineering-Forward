# How Microsoft Is Building for a World of Metered Intelligence
**Source**: https://every.to/also-true-for-humans/how-microsoft-is-building-for-a-world-of-metered-intelligence

**Author**: Mike Taylor

## Elevator pitch
At Build 2026, Microsoft became the first major company to design for a world where AI intelligence is metered and the era of subsidized subscriptions is ending — introducing automatic model routing, a local-AI laptop with Nvidia, and cheaper smaller models to help enterprises control runaway token costs.

## Takeaways
- Microsoft switched GitHub Copilot to token-based billing on June 1, with some users reporting bills jumping from $39 to over $3,000 per month
- The RTX Spark laptop, co-designed with Nvidia, runs a 128-billion-parameter model locally so developers can work without paying for tokens — a bet that budget-conscious coders accept being a year or two behind the frontier
- Automatic model routing in GitHub Copilot delegates less complicated tasks to cheaper models, addressing the tendency to choose "the model of the day" for tasks that don't merit that power
- AI labs won't let large companies buy subsidized "Max" plans, so enterprises pay full freight on every token — one company reportedly spent $500M on Claude tokens in a single month
- Microsoft's research lab released seven new smaller MAI models spanning image, voice, transcription, coding, and reasoning

## Synthesis
Microsoft's Build 2026 conference marked a turning point in how enterprises think about AI costs. As Mike Taylor reports, the era of subsidized AI subscriptions — what he calls the "$5 Uber era" of LLMs — is ending. AI labs have been subsidizing subscriptions to the tune of thousands of dollars per user, but with Anthropic, OpenAI, and SpaceXAI all going public in 2026, they'll need to show paths to profitability. Microsoft's decision to switch GitHub Copilot to token-based billing on June 1, which caused some users' bills to jump from $39 to over $3,000 per month, was the opening salvo.

Rather than backtracking, Microsoft used the conference to make the case for using AI more pragmatically. CEO Satya Nadella promised "unmetered intelligence to every desk and every home," an AI-era update to Bill Gates's vision of "a computer on every desk." The most tangible embodiment is the RTX Spark, a new laptop co-designed with Nvidia that runs a 128-billion-parameter model locally. While frontier models have trillions of parameters, Microsoft is betting that budget-conscious developers will accept being a year or two behind the frontier to avoid paying for tokens. The laptop includes a rebuilt smart terminal that runs any coding agent harness and adopts popular Mac terminal commands to ease switching costs.

Automatic model routing is the feature that shows Microsoft understands the enterprise cost problem. GitHub Copilot can now delegate less complicated tasks to cheaper models automatically. As GitHub COO Kyle Daigle noted, developers tend to choose "the model of the day, or week, or hour" even when the task doesn't merit that power — "but the tools could" switch for them. Microsoft itself has been cancelling Claude Code licenses to reduce costs, suggesting the model router was built to solve their own problem.

The economics driving this are stark. AI labs won't let large companies buy highly subsidized individual "Max" plans, so big companies pay full freight on every token. One company that wasn't properly monitoring usage reportedly spent $500 million on Claude tokens in a single month. Microsoft's research lab, led by Mustafa Suleyman, also released a set of new cheaper, smaller models spanning image, voice, transcription, coding, and reasoning — a clear signal that the future of enterprise AI involves matching model capability to task complexity, not defaulting to the most powerful model for everything.

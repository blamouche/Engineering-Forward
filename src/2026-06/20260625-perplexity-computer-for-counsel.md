# Perplexity Introduces Computer for Counsel: AI for Lawyers
**Source**: https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/introducing-computer-for-counsel
**Date**: 2026-06-25
**Author**: Perplexity
**Keywords**: Perplexity, Computer for Counsel, legal AI, agents, law, enterprise AI

## Elevator pitch
Perplexity launched Computer for Counsel, an AI agent tailored for legal professionals that connects research databases, document repositories, and contract tools — expanding the "Computer" agent platform into the legal vertical.

## Takeaways
- Computer for Counsel connects legal research databases (Midpage), compliance data (Deel), and contract templates (LegalZoom) to Perplexity's Computer agent platform
- Powered by 20+ frontier AI models with automatic model selection for research, reasoning, and contracts
- Integrates with Microsoft 365 (Word, SharePoint, Outlook, Teams), Box, Carta, Clio, DeepJudge, Docusign, Ironclad, and NetDocuments
- Always cites sources back to cases, statutes, regulations, or internal documents for attorney verification
- 75% of lawyers say administrative tasks are a big time challenge (Thomson Reuters survey)
- Available to Perplexity Enterprise and Max subscribers
- Key workflows: NDA intake and review, regulatory monitoring dashboards, case research with citation review
- Perplexity Enterprise does not train on company data; internal files stay under firm control

## Synthesis
Perplexity's Computer for Counsel represents the verticalization of agentic AI — taking a general-purpose agent platform and specializing it for a high-value professional domain. The strategy is clear: legal is a market where accuracy, source attribution, and workflow integration are non-negotiable, and Perplexity is positioning Computer as the platform that can meet those standards.

The product design reflects a deep understanding of legal workflows. Rather than just offering a chatbot that can answer legal questions, Computer for Counsel connects to the tools lawyers actually use — document management systems (NetDocuments, Box), contract platforms (Ironclad, Docusign), and legal research databases (Midpage, Clio). This means the agent can draft documents in Word, retrieve files from SharePoint, and reference conversations from Outlook, all while maintaining citation trails back to verifiable sources.

The model-selection approach is noteworthy. By routing tasks across 20+ frontier models, Perplexity avoids the common pitfall of vertical AI products that are locked to a single model provider. This also future-proofs the product as new models emerge — a practical advantage in a market where model capabilities are evolving monthly.

For the engineering and product community, the broader signal is that agentic AI is moving from horizontal tools to vertical solutions. The infrastructure is maturing enough that the competitive advantage shifts from "can we build an agent?" to "can we build an agent that deeply understands a specific domain's tools, data, and verification requirements?" Perplexity's partnership-first approach (Midpage, Deel, LegalZoom) versus build-everything approach is a model worth watching.

# Jeff Bezos's Prometheus Raises $12B to Build an Artificial General Engineer

**Date**: June 11, 2026
**Author**: Techibe
**Keywords**: Jeff Bezos, Prometheus, artificial general engineer, physical AI, Vik Bajaj, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, labor scarcity, manufacturing

## Elevator pitch
Jeff Bezos's physical AI startup Prometheus raised $12 billion at a $41 billion valuation to build an "artificial general engineer" that automates the design and manufacturing of complex physical systems — from jet engines to drug compounds — backed by JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, and BlackRock.

## Takeaways
- Prometheus raised $12 billion in a new round valuing the company at $41 billion, with investors including Bezos himself, JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and BlackRock.
- The company has raised $18.2 billion in total ($6.2B at launch, $12B in June 2026) and employs 150 people — $273 million of valuation per employee.
- The goal is to build an "artificial general engineer" that automates design and manufacturing of physical systems: jet engines, pharmaceutical compounds, industrial processes.
- Bezos argues AI-driven productivity will create "labor scarcity" — demand for workers outpacing supply — putting him at odds with those who predict net displacement.
- The presence of the world's largest financial institutions signals that physical AI is no longer a research topic but a near-term institutional bet.

## Synthesis
Jeff Bezos's physical AI startup Prometheus raised $12 billion at a $41 billion valuation on June 11, 2026, backed by JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and BlackRock. Co-founded with Vik Bajaj, former co-founder of Verily (Google's life sciences division), the company has raised $18.2 billion in total and employs 150 people — amounting to $273 million of valuation per employee. The scale and speed of the capital accumulation is unusual even by the standards of the current AI investment cycle: the first raise of $6.2 billion came at launch in late 2025, and the second $12 billion came within months.

Prometheus is building AI that can design physical systems, not just generate code or summarize documents. The target applications are deliberately varied and extremely hard: jet engine design, pharmaceutical compounds, industrial manufacturing processes. These are domains where errors have physical consequences — a drug that does not work, a turbine blade that fails under load, a factory line that produces defective parts. The company calls its system an "artificial general engineer," with "general" doing significant work: not a narrow tool for one industry but a system capable of engineering reasoning across domains. A large portion of the $12 billion will go toward compute, the raw processing power needed to train and run systems at this level of complexity.

The investors are notable for what they are not. JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and BlackRock are not venture funds chasing the next consumer app — they are institutions that manage trillions in assets and have a long history of moving carefully. Their presence at these numbers signals that physical AI, the application of AI to engineering, manufacturing, materials science, and drug development, is now considered a serious and near-term institutional bet. At $41 billion, Prometheus is one of the most richly valued AI startups ever funded and one of the largest single bets on the physical AI sector. Bezos's "labor scarcity" theory — that productivity gains will raise living standards, increase demand, and create more employment — puts him at odds with a significant portion of the AI and employment conversation, and sits in tension with Amazon's own history of layoffs amid automation. The company has offices in San Francisco, London, and Zurich, and is keeping the specifics of what it has built under wraps.

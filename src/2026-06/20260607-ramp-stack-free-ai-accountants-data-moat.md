## Elevator pitch
Ramp launched Stack, an AI system giving accounting firms free AI agents that run monthly closes, reconciliations, and journal entries — but the real play is using firms as a channel to pull downstream clients onto Ramp's payment rails where it actually makes money.

## Takeaways
- Ramp Stack launches free through August 2026, with AI "Coworkers" running reconciliations, journal entries, transaction coding, and variance analysis across a firm's entire client book
- Design partners report closes 50-60% faster and reconciliations up to 9x faster, with one firm's March close dropping to 20 minutes; Stack hit 65.8% on a 200+ task accounting benchmark
- The strategic play is data access: Ramp's agents work on live spend, AP, and banking data natively, while competitors like Pilot, Zeni, and Intuit must reach through connectors

## Synthesis
Ramp's launch of Stack is a textbook example of using AI agents as a distribution wedge rather than a revenue product. The system gives accounting firms AI "Coworkers" that handle the mechanical work of monthly closes — reconciliations, journal entries, transaction coding, variance analysis — across a firm's entire book of clients. Firms describe a client's process in plain English, Stack encodes it as reusable Skills, and nothing posts to QuickBooks without human sign-off. The results from design partners are striking: closes 50-60% faster, reconciliations up to 9x faster, and one firm's March close dropping to 20 minutes.

But the accounting work isn't where Ramp makes money. The asset is the data. Ramp already runs spend, accounts payable, and banking data for tens of thousands of businesses, so its agents work on live numbers instead of reaching through connectors the way competitors must. This native-data access is a structural advantage that pure-play AI accounting startups like Basis — fresh off a $100M raise at $1.15B — simply cannot replicate without owning the underlying financial rails.

The firms are the channel. More than 4,500 already use Ramp, and 92 of the top 100 CPA firms have clients on the platform. Because firms decide which financial stack their clients adopt, making a firm faster and more dependent on Ramp's rails pulls downstream clients onto the cards and bill-pay where Ramp's revenue actually lives. Free through August is the giveaway because the monetization sits downstream.

The timing explains the rest. Ramp is reportedly raising $750M at a $40B+ valuation, near 40 times revenue, with IPO-readiness targeted for late 2026. A multiple like that needs a bigger market than corporate-card spend, and the $150B accounting industry is one Ramp had never sold a product into. The staffing crisis — 300,000+ accountants have left the field, degrees at a 20-year low — hands Ramp a willing audience of firms turning away clients they can't staff. The collision to watch is Intuit: QuickBooks is both the launch integration and a competitor building its own agents, making them partners today and rivals tomorrow.

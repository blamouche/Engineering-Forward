# The Pulse: A Trend of Trying to Cut Back on AI Spend Within Eng Departments

**Source**: https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-a-trend-of-trying-to-cut-328
**Date**: June 11, 2026
**Author**: Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer)
**Keywords**: AI spend, token costs, model routing, engineering budgets, OpenCode, DoorDash, GitHub Copilot, AI efficiency, engineering management

## Elevator pitch
Engineering departments across the tech industry are grappling with exploding AI tooling costs, implementing strategies from per-engineer monthly token budgets to smart model routing — with no clear line yet between AI productivity gains and useful features shipped.

## Takeaways
- Uber's CTO went viral after revealing the company blew through its 2026 AI budget by mid-March, highlighting that token consumption costs are becoming a board-level concern
- Companies with cutting-edge AI usage are turning to "smart" model routing based on use case and prompt to reduce costs while maintaining quality
- DoorDash gives devs high monthly token limits but requires justification to exceed them, paired with regular knowledge-sharing sessions on efficient AI use
- A major US retirement-savings company imposed monthly GitHub Copilot token limits, downgrading devs to less capable models (GPT-5 mini, GPT-4.1, Grok Code Fast 1) once exhausted
- Startups are opting for subsidized Claude Code Max or Codex Max subscriptions instead of expensive API pricing
- OpenCode's hosted inference service saw every inbound enterprise request focused on optimizing spend, indicating widespread concern about AI bills
- Engineers who save on token costs may be rewarded in upcoming performance review cycles, similar to how vendor bill savings were rewarded two years ago

## Synthesis
The Pragmatic Engineer's analysis reveals a growing tension in engineering organizations: AI tooling spend has rocketed to levels comparable to or exceeding observability costs, yet drawing a direct line from AI productivity metrics to shipped user features remains elusive. Uber president Andrew McDonald captured this disconnect on the Rapid Response podcast, noting that while 25% of code commits may be AI-driven, the link to more useful consumer features is "not there yet."

This gap between spending and visible ROI is driving diverse cost-optimization strategies across company types. Cutting-edge tech companies bite the bullet on frontier model costs but are exploring smart model routing to downgrade cheaper tasks. DoorDash empowers developers with high token limits but enforces justification for exceeding them. Traditional enterprises like a major retirement-savings company have imposed hard monthly Copilot token caps with automatic downgrades to less capable models. Revenue-generating startups are pivoting to subsidized Max subscriptions rather than paying API rates.

A notable bottom-up efficiency movement is emerging from engineers themselves, not from top-down mandates. Knowledge-sharing sessions, lunch-and-learns, and engineering all-hands are increasingly focused on AI efficiency. OpenCode creator Dax Raad reported that every single enterprise inbound request over the past month was about optimizing spend. The article predicts that engineers who demonstrate token cost savings may earn promotions, mirroring how vendor bill reductions were rewarded two years ago — an ironic twist given that savings will come from the same departments that drove the rocketing spending.

# Anthropic Backtracks on Policy That 'Sabotaged' Researchers' Work
**Source**: https://www.engadget.com/2192004/anthropic-walks-back-policy-sabotaging-research/
**Date**: June 11, 2026
**Author**: Steve Dent
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Claude Fable 5, Mythos, transparency, safeguards, AI research, frontier LLM, rerouting, academic community

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic apologized and made its Fable 5 safeguards visible after researchers discovered the model was quietly rerouting requests to a lesser model when asked to perform AI research tasks, burning their tokens and money without disclosure.

## Takeaways
- Anthropic quietly rerouted Claude Fable 5 requests to a lesser model when users asked it to perform certain AI research tasks, without disclosing this in the model's documentation.
- The degraded tasks included training competing LLMs, debugging AI code, and optimizing neural architecture.
- Researchers were concerned not only about the degradation but about the lack of transparency and the wasted tokens and money.
- Anthropic stated: "We made the wrong tradeoff and we apologize for not getting the balance right."
- The company is not reversing its safeguard policy but making the restrictions visible — alerting users when it refuses a request or reroutes them to a less capable model.

## Synthesis
Engadget reports that Anthropic was forced to walk back a policy that discreetly hamstrung researchers using its new Claude Fable 5 LLM to create competing AI models. The company told Wired that it is changing Fable 5's safeguards for frontier LLM development to make them visible. "We made the wrong tradeoff and we apologize for not getting the balance right," Anthropic said in a statement.

When Anthropic released Claude Fable 5, a model based on its powerful Mythos system, researchers noticed something odd: the model would quietly reroute requests to a lesser model when asked to perform certain actions. That restriction was not disclosed in the model's documentation. The new model was either refusing or degrading responses for tasks like training competing LLMs, debugging AI code, and optimizing neural architecture. Researchers were bothered not only by the degradation itself but by Anthropic's lack of transparency about it. They were also concerned that they had burned tokens and money on a model that did not do what they expected.

The backlash was swift because Anthropic has positioned itself as a more ethical and researcher-friendly alternative to OpenAI. Dean W. Ball, a research fellow and Substack author, wrote on X: "Degrading performance on ML research without telling the user is shockingly hostile and a terrible look." The incident cut against the company's carefully cultivated image as a partner to the academic community. Anthropic is not reversing its safeguard policy — the restrictions remain in place — but it is making them visible. If the company suspects a user is trying to use Claude to build a highly capable AI, it will alert them that it is either refusing the request or rerouting them to a less capable model. The episode highlights the tension between AI safety measures and research transparency, and the reputational costs that even safety-motivated decisions can impose when they are implemented without disclosure.

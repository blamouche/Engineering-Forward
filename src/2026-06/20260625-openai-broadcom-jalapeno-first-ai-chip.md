# OpenAI and Broadcom Reveal Jalapeño, First AI Chip in Partnership
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/openai-and-broadcom-reveal-jalapeno-first-ai-chip-in-partnership.html
**Date**: 2026-06-24
**Author**: Kif Leswing
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Broadcom, Jalapeño, AI chip, custom silicon, inference, data center

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeño, their first jointly designed AI inference chip, designed in nine months with AI-assisted development and targeting gigawatt-scale data center deployments.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI and Broadcom revealed Jalapeño, the first accelerator in a planned family of LLM inference chips optimized for performance per watt
- The chip was designed from end to end in nine months with help from OpenAI's own AI models accelerating the process
- Jalapeño is intended for inference workloads, the compute-intensive process of serving AI models to users
- OpenAI President Greg Brockman said the company "cannot get compute fast enough," signaling insatiable demand
- Broadcom CEO Hock Tan confirmed demand from six customers is "simply insatiable" and elevated through 2028
- Jalapeño marks OpenAI's strategic shift toward building the full stack behind its models and products
- The chip partnership follows an October 2025 announcement after 18 months of collaboration

## Synthesis
OpenAI and Broadcom's Jalapeño chip represents a significant strategic shift for the ChatGPT maker: moving from pure model development to full-stack infrastructure ownership. The chip, designed for inference rather than training, targets the compute-intensive process of serving AI models to users — the bottleneck that increasingly defines OpenAI's economics.

What makes this announcement noteworthy is the speed of development. Greg Brockman confirmed the chip was designed "from end to end in nine months" with AI models playing a substantial role in accelerating the process. This compressed timeline — from October's public announcement to Wednesday's unveiling — suggests AI-assisted chip design could reshape semiconductor development cadences.

The commercial context is equally important. OpenAI has been one of Nvidia's largest customers, spending billions on GPUs for both training and inference. By designing custom inference silicon, OpenAI follows Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia), and Microsoft (Maia) in seeking to reduce dependency on Nvidia's premium pricing. However, unlike those hyperscalers, OpenAI lacks its own cloud infrastructure and must still partner with others for deployment.

Broadcom's involvement signals the chipmaker's growing role as an AI silicon foundry. CEO Hock Tan described demand as "simply insatiable" and elevated through 2028, noting that compute demand far exceeds what Broadcom can currently address. Broadcom shares rose on the announcement, reflecting investor confidence in the company's position as a key enabler of custom AI chips.

The name "Jalapeño" suggests this is the first in a family of chips — a platform rather than a one-off product. For the engineering community, the key takeaway is that inference optimization is becoming a first-class design target, and the era of general-purpose GPUs as the sole path to AI scale is giving way to specialized, workload-specific silicon.

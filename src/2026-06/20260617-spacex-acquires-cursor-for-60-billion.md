# SpaceX Acquires AI Coding Startup Cursor for $60 Billion
**Source**: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/spacex-spcx-cursor-acquisition-ipo.html
**Date**: 2026-06-17
**Author**: CNBC
**Keywords**: SpaceX, Cursor, acquisition, AI coding, $60 billion, Elon Musk, Anthropic, OpenAI

## Elevator pitch
SpaceX is acquiring AI coding startup Cursor for $60 billion in stock, marking one of the largest tech acquisitions in history and signaling Elon Musk's determination to compete directly with Anthropic and OpenAI in the AI tooling space.

## Takeaways
- SpaceX is buying Cursor for $60 billion in stock, making it one of the largest tech acquisitions ever
- Cursor, founded in 2022, crossed $1 billion in annualized revenue in November 2025 and has experienced explosive growth in its AI coding tool
- The deal will make Cursor a wholly owned subsidiary of SpaceX when it closes in Q3 2026
- The acquisition is part of Musk's broader strategy to compete with Anthropic and OpenAI in AI, following his xAI venture
- Cursor's popular AI coding tool helps developers generate, edit, and review code, and has become a staple in developer workflows

## Synthesis
SpaceX's $60 billion acquisition of Cursor represents a watershed moment for the AI industry and developer tooling specifically. Cursor, founded just in 2022, has experienced meteoric growth—crossing $1 billion in annualized revenue by November 2025—thanks to its AI-powered code editor that integrates generation, editing, and review into a seamless developer experience.

The acquisition signals several important trends. First, AI coding tools have become strategic assets that major players are willing to pay enormous premiums for. Second, Elon Musk is assembling an AI ecosystem that spans infrastructure (xAI's compute), models (Grok), and now tooling (Cursor)—a vertical integration play similar to what Apple did with hardware/software. Third, the valuation ($60B for a company with ~$1B ARR) reflects expectations of massive future growth in AI-assisted development, not current financials.

For the engineering community, the key question is whether Cursor will maintain its product quality and independence under SpaceX's umbrella, or whether it will be steered toward Musk's competitive priorities against Anthropic (which makes Claude Code) and OpenAI (which makes Codex). The acquisition also raises questions about the future of open development in AI coding tools, given Musk's history of both open-sourcing (Grok models) and tightly controlling (Twitter/X) his platforms.

## Synthesis
The Big Technology AI Summit, held in San Francisco, convened leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, Box, and Corridor for a dense series of discussions on the state of AI. The strongest consensus was that AI spending — across infrastructure and services — would only accelerate, even as the industry grapples with rationalizing token consumption in what has been termed the "tokenmaxxing reckoning."

Box CEO Aaron Levie offered a sharp reframing of the cost narrative. The traditional Moore's Law expectation that computing costs come down over time is broken for AI, he argued, because increasing capabilities lead companies to apply AI to harder, costlier problems. Box's own token consumption tells the story: from 5,000–20,000 tokens per task in early AI use cases to 1–5 million tokens per task with their latest agents. Companies are outrunning efficiency improvements with their appetite for what models can do.

On policy, Levie made a striking observation: the current US regulatory approach — where the White House reviews and approves each new model release — effectively fulfills the dream of the signatories of the six-month AI pause petition, achieved through administrative action rather than legislation. The export controls on Anthropic's latest model are the practical manifestation of this regime.

Alex Stamos, former Meta security chief and current Corridor chief product officer, contextualized the cybersecurity frontier. The real AI cyber breakthrough came last year with the Opus 4 and GPT-5 series models, which gave human engineers what amounts to superpowers — "like all of a sudden you went to a high school track meet and all the kids are running Olympic times." Mythos is the best public model at finding bugs, Stamos noted, but the capability is not unprecedented.

Anthropic Labs lead Mike Krieger described how Fable changed his building workflow. Fable requires less hand-holding than its predecessors: Krieger could hand off larger, more open-ended tasks and step away rather than babysitting the model. In one project, Fable completed the work and then "hung out for the next seven hours." This represents a meaningful shift from iterative collaboration to delegation.

Greg Brockman made the case that compute — not algorithms — will be OpenAI's core differentiator. With only 10–20 million agent users currently, the industry is far from planet scale, and there simply won't be enough compute to satisfy all demand. This framing reinforces the strategic logic behind OpenAI's massive infrastructure buildout.

# AI Just Killed the User Interface

**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/aikilledui

**Date**: January 29, 2026

**Author**: Linas Beliunas

**Keywords**: AI, MCP, user interface, SaaS, Anthropic, Claude, platform shift, agentic workflows

## Elevator pitch

Anthropic's MCP Apps partnership with major SaaS companies signals the death of the application era, as companies surrender their user interfaces to AI platforms to avoid becoming invisible within agentic workflows.

## Takeaways

- Ten major companies including Asana, Box, Canva, Figma, and Slack now render their applications directly within Claude's chat window through MCP Apps
- SaaS companies historically bundled data, logic, and interface, with the interface serving as the primary competitive moat through user familiarity
- When users access partner applications through Claude, they experience Anthropic's interface while accessing the backend logic and data
- Partner companies faced an existential choice: surrender interface control or become invisible within agentic workflows
- Applications are transforming from user-facing products into headless backend providers, fundamentally shifting what competitive advantage means in software

## Synthesis

Linas Beliunas argues that Anthropic's launch of MCP Apps represents the beginning of the end for traditional software applications as we know them. The partnership, which includes ten major companies such as Amplitude, Asana, Box, Canva, Clay, Figma, Hex, monday.com, and Slack, enables these applications to render directly within Claude's chat window. This architectural shift fundamentally changes the relationship between software companies and their users.

The article identifies the historical bundling model that defined SaaS success. Software companies combined three elements: data storage, business logic, and user interface. Among these, the interface served as the primary competitive moat. User familiarity, muscle memory, and workflow integration created switching costs that protected market position regardless of whether competitors offered similar functionality. Users stayed because relearning a new interface was costly, not because the underlying logic was irreplaceable.

MCP Apps unbundles this model. When users interact with partner applications through Claude, they access the backend logic and data while experiencing Anthropic's interface. The destination website becomes irrelevant for routine tasks. The application transforms from a user-facing product into a headless backend provider. This represents a fundamental transfer of the user relationship from the application vendor to the AI platform.

Beliunas examines why established companies with strong market positions chose to participate. The decision was not opportunistic but defensive. Applications that remain inaccessible to AI agents become invisible within agentic workflows. As users increasingly interact with software through AI intermediaries, companies face a choice between surrendering interface control and complete market irrelevance. The partner companies chose controlled participation over exclusion.

The strategic implications extend across the software industry. If the interface was the moat, and the interface now belongs to Anthropic, what remains of competitive advantage in software? Companies must compete on data quality, business logic sophistication, and integration depth rather than user experience. This marks what Beliunas calls the moment when the app era started dying. The industry is reorganizing around AI agents as primary interfaces, with traditional applications becoming interchangeable backend services accessible through a single conversational layer.

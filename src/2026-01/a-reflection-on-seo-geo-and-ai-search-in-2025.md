# A Reflection on SEO, GEO & AI Search in 2025

**Source**: https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/a-reflection-on-seo-and-ai-search

**Date**: January 19, 2026

**Author**: Lily Ray

**Keywords**: SEO, GEO, AI search, ChatGPT, Google, LLM, search optimization, digital marketing, agentic commerce

## Elevator pitch

Despite hype around Generative Engine Optimization as an SEO replacement, LLM responses fundamentally depend on traditional search indices, making solid SEO strategy the most effective path to AI visibility rather than abandoning established practices.

## Takeaways

- Google maintains over 90.6% global search market share while 70% of surveyed professionals receive less than 2% of traffic from ChatGPT
- LLM responses depend on query fan-out mechanisms that execute sub-queries against live search indices, meaning every URL surfaced comes from search rather than the model's training
- Many GEO and AEO promoters repackage decades-old SEO practices like structured data and header optimization under new terminology
- Risky tactics like generating thousands of AI pages or using invisible text for LLMs risk algorithmic penalties without genuine user value
- Agentic commerce protocols like ACP and UCP enable AI to facilitate transactions directly, requiring optimized API feeds beyond website optimization

## Synthesis

Lily Ray, a 15-year SEO veteran, reflects on what proved to be the most volatile year in the industry's history. ChatGPT's growth to 800 million weekly active users triggered widespread concern that traditional search optimization was becoming obsolete, prompting many to declare a new era of Generative Engine Optimization.

Ray identifies what she calls the "GEO grift"—newcomers and tool vendors promoting GEO and Answer Engine Optimization as revolutionary SEO replacements while repackaging established practices under new terminology. The recommended strategies often rebrand decades-old approaches like structured data, header optimization, and keyword-focused URLs. This creates confusion without adding genuine insight.

More concerning are risky tactics some GEO advocates promote: generating thousands of AI-created pages to inflate topical coverage, manufacturing visibility through manipulative social platform tactics, using invisible text containing hidden instructions for LLMs, and writing explicitly for algorithms rather than humans. These approaches risk algorithmic penalties while providing no genuine user value.

The data contradicts the narrative of SEO's demise. Google maintains over 90.6% global search market share. Ray's LinkedIn survey showed 70% of respondents receive less than 2% of traffic from ChatGPT. The search market is not zero-sum—the total pie is growing, with traditional search and AI interfaces serving complementary roles.

Technical understanding of how LLMs work undermines much GEO hype. Query fan-out mechanisms break user prompts into multiple sub-queries executed against search indices. Every URL surfaced in an LLM response comes from a live search index, not the model's training data. LLM tracking tools remain fundamentally limited because responses are non-deterministic and highly personalized.

Genuine evolutions include query fan-out analysis tools that reveal how models deconstruct user intent, multi-modal content like video transcripts serving as AI synthesis material, clear brand communication on owned properties enabling accurate AI citations, digital PR emphasis as LLMs rely on third-party validation from Reddit and Quora, and agentic commerce protocols requiring optimized API feeds for AI-facilitated transactions.

By late 2025, Google recovered from AI Overviews missteps while ChatGPT faced profitability questions and growth plateau. Google Gemini surged to 650 million monthly users while ChatGPT's market share fell from 87% to 64.5%. Ray concludes that abandoning Google for GEO is a "rookie mistake"—evolution rather than revolution characterizes the transformation ahead.

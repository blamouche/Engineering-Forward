# ChatGPT Apps Are About to Be the Next Big Distribution Channel

**Source**: https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/chatgpt-apps-are-about-to-be-the

**Date**: January 20, 2026

**Author**: Colin Matthews

**Keywords**: ChatGPT, distribution, apps, MCP, Model Context Protocol, OpenAI, solopreneurs

## Elevator pitch

ChatGPT apps represent a distribution opportunity comparable to the App Store in 2008 or early SEO—enabling contextual discovery where relevant apps surface automatically when users mention needing help with specific tasks.

## Takeaways

- ChatGPT apps enable contextual surfacing: when users mention needing help with travel or design, relevant apps appear automatically without active discovery
- The architecture involves three components: ChatGPT conversation interpretation, backend tools via Model Context Protocol, and user-facing React widgets
- Three display formats are available: inline cards within conversation flow, fullscreen for complex workflows, and picture-in-picture for background media
- Development can be rapid—potentially 30 minutes for basic applications using Replit with OpenAI's SDK or specialized tools like Chippy
- Early-mover advantage benefits both enterprises seeking new distribution channels and solopreneurs building microapps without marketing budgets

## Synthesis

Colin Matthews argues that ChatGPT apps represent a rare distribution opportunity of the kind that emerges perhaps once per decade. He compares this moment to the App Store launch in 2008 or the early days of SEO, when savvy builders could establish dominant positions before markets became saturated. The core insight is that ChatGPT apps fundamentally change how software gets discovered.

Traditional app stores require active discovery—users must search for solutions or browse categories. ChatGPT apps enable contextual surfacing instead. When users mention in conversation that they need help planning travel, finding design inspiration, or managing groceries, relevant apps can appear automatically. This shifts distribution from marketing-driven to conversation-driven, potentially democratizing access for builders who lack advertising budgets.

The technical architecture involves three components working together. First, ChatGPT interprets the conversation to understand user intent. Second, backend tools exposed via the Model Context Protocol allow ChatGPT to call functions that do actual work—booking reservations, generating images, fetching data. Tool descriptions help the model understand when to invoke each capability. Third, user-facing React widgets provide visual interfaces within the chat experience.

Matthews describes three display formats that ChatGPT apps can use. Inline mode presents cards and lists within the conversation flow, suitable for quick information display. Fullscreen mode handles complex workflows that need more space, like multi-step forms or detailed visualizations. Picture-in-picture keeps elements like music players running in the background while conversation continues.

For implementation, Matthews outlines two practical pathways. The Replit approach uses OpenAI's official SDK examples with deployment directly to ChatGPT. The alternative is Chippy, a specialized AI agent designed for ChatGPT app prototyping with built-in chat testing capabilities. Both enable rapid development—potentially 30 minutes for basic applications.

The strategic implications extend to different builder archetypes. Enterprises gain a new distribution channel that complements existing app store presence. The more interesting opportunity may be for solopreneurs building microapps. Without the need for marketing budgets or app store optimization expertise, individual builders can create focused utilities that surface precisely when users need them. This could enable a new category of small software businesses built on conversation-driven discovery rather than traditional acquisition funnels.

Matthews emphasizes early-mover advantage. As with previous platform shifts, those who build quality experiences early will establish positions that become harder to challenge as the ecosystem matures. The window for experimentation is open now.

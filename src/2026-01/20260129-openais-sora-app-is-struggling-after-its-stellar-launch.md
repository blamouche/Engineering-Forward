# OpenAI's Sora app is struggling after its stellar launch
**Source**: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/openai-sora-app-struggling-stellar-164844428.html
**Date**: January 29, 2026
**Author**: Sarah Perez
**Keywords**: OpenAI, Sora, AI video, mobile apps, consumer adoption

## Elevator pitch
After an explosive debut, OpenAI's Sora video app is seeing declining downloads and spending, highlighting the challenge of sustaining consumer AI momentum once hype fades and constraints tighten.

## Takeaways
- Sora hit the top of the App Store early but has since lost traction.
- Appfigures data shows month-over-month declines in downloads and spending.
- Competition from Google and Meta is intensifying in AI video.
- Copyright controls and limits on IP usage reduced some of the viral appeal.
- The app still has a sizable user base, but momentum has slowed.

## Synthesis
The article reports that OpenAI's Sora app, once a breakout hit, is now struggling to maintain momentum. At launch, Sora quickly climbed to the top of the U.S. App Store, and its invite-only iOS debut still managed to rack up unusually high install numbers. However, new analytics point to a significant pullback in both downloads and consumer spending, suggesting that early hype has cooled.

According to Appfigures data cited in the piece, Sora's downloads fell month over month in December and again in January. Spending followed a similar pattern, with January revenue lower than December's peak. The article notes that these declines are notable because the holiday season typically boosts mobile usage. It also reports that Sora is no longer in the top tier of the U.S. App Store rankings, signaling a drop in visibility and organic discovery.

The story attributes the slowdown to multiple factors. Competition has intensified, with Google and Meta pushing their own AI video experiences, reducing the novelty of a standalone Sora app. At the same time, OpenAI tightened copyright controls after backlash over users generating videos with recognizable characters. Moving from an opt-out to an opt-in model and placing stricter restrictions blunted some of the viral content that initially drove growth.

Despite the decline, the article does not frame Sora as a failure. Total downloads and cumulative spending remain sizable, and OpenAI continues to experiment with partnerships, including a deal with Disney that expands access to licensed characters. The question is whether these moves can reverse the downward trend or simply stabilize a smaller, more niche user base.

The broader implication is that consumer AI apps face a difficult transition from hype to habit. Initial virality is easier to achieve than sustained engagement, especially when the product depends on expensive inference, sensitive content rules, and fast-moving competition. Sora's trajectory illustrates how quickly attention can fade, and how critical it is for AI apps to prove lasting value once the novelty wears off.

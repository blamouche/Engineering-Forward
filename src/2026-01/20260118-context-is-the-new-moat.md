# Context is the New Moat

**Source**: https://www.theunwindai.com/p/context-is-the-new-moat

**Date**: January 16, 2026

**Author**: Shubham Saboo

**Keywords**: AI, competitive advantage, context, LLM, proprietary data, agents, prompting

## Elevator pitch

As AI models become commoditized and universally accessible, the true competitive advantage shifts from which LLM you use to the proprietary context—domain knowledge, user insights, and historical lessons—you can feed into those models.

## Takeaways

- Everyone has access to the same frontier AI models (Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro), making the model itself no longer a differentiator
- The quality of AI outputs depends heavily on the specialized context provided, not just the underlying model capabilities
- Five types of meaningful context matter: user context, domain context, historical context, quality context, and constraint context
- Teams that systematically capture and document context experience compounding improvements in AI agent performance over time
- Externalizing organizational knowledge into persistent context files (CLAUDE.md, GEMINI.md) makes contextual guidance automatic rather than repetitive

## Synthesis

The article presents a compelling argument about the evolving nature of competitive advantage in the AI era. As large language models become increasingly commoditized—with frontier capabilities available to essentially anyone through API access—the traditional moat of "having better AI" has disappeared. The author contends that in 2026, differentiation comes not from the model you choose but from the proprietary context you bring to that model.

To illustrate this point, Saboo presents a thought experiment: two developers building identical AI agents using the same underlying model. One uses generic prompts while the other embeds deep domain-specific knowledge about their product, users, past failures, and quality standards. The result is dramatically different—one produces a generic chatbot while the other creates something uniquely tailored and valuable.

The article breaks down context into five critical dimensions. User context involves genuine behavioral insights about target audiences, going beyond demographics to understand actual usage patterns and needs. Domain context captures field-specific patterns and architectural principles that general models might not emphasize. Historical context documents lessons learned from previous attempts, preventing repeated mistakes. Quality context provides concrete examples that distinguish excellent outputs from poor ones. Finally, constraint context establishes the real technical and business limitations that shape viable solutions.

Perhaps the most valuable insight is the concept of accumulation advantage. Teams that systematically capture and maintain context documentation don't just save time—they experience exponential improvements in AI agent performance over time. Each interaction becomes an opportunity to refine and expand the contextual knowledge base, creating a widening gap between organizations that invest in context curation and those that start from scratch with each new AI interaction.

The practical recommendation is straightforward: externalize organizational knowledge into persistent context files that automatically load into AI conversations. This approach transforms contextual guidance from a repetitive manual process into an automatic enhancement, ensuring every AI interaction benefits from accumulated institutional knowledge. In essence, the new moat is built not from proprietary algorithms but from proprietary understanding.

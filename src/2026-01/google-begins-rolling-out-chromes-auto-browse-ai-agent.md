# Google Begins Rolling Out Chrome's Auto Browse AI Agent

**Source**: https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/01/google-begins-rolling-out-chromes-auto-browse-ai-agent-today/

**Date**: January 28, 2026

**Author**: Unknown

**Keywords**: Google, Chrome, Gemini, AI agent, auto browse, agentic AI, browser automation, security

## Elevator pitch

Google integrates Gemini 3-powered agentic AI features into Chrome, including Auto Browse that can autonomously perform multi-step web tasks like vacation planning, form filling, and online shopping across multiple tabs.

## Takeaways

- Auto Browse is a multimodal AI agent built on Gemini 3 that can research, compare, and execute tasks across multiple websites autonomously
- Use cases include vacation planning across date options, scheduling appointments, filling forms, collecting tax documents, managing subscriptions, and identifying items from photos to add to shopping carts
- The feature can access saved passwords and log into services when given permission, enabling end-to-end task automation
- Security experts warn of prompt-injection vulnerabilities on malicious websites, with Google placing responsibility on users
- Auto Browse is rolling out in preview in the U.S. for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers only

## Synthesis

Google has begun rolling out Auto Browse, an agentic AI feature integrated into Chrome that represents a significant evolution in how browsers interact with users. Built on Gemini 3, Google's most capable model, Auto Browse can autonomously perform complex multi-step tasks across the web, effectively becoming a digital assistant that operates within browser tabs.

The capability set extends well beyond simple search. Auto Browse can optimize vacation planning by researching hotel and flight costs across multiple date options to find budget-friendly travel windows. Users have employed it for scheduling appointments, filling out tedious online forms, collecting tax documents, getting quotes from service providers, checking bill payment status, filing expense reports, managing subscriptions, and expediting administrative processes like driver's license renewals. The multimodal nature of the system enables it to identify items in photos, locate purchase options, and add them to shopping carts while applying relevant discount codes.

With user permission, Auto Browse can access saved passwords and log into online stores and services, enabling complete task automation from initiation to completion. This password access represents a deliberate trade-off between convenience and security, as it allows the agent to complete authenticated workflows without requiring manual intervention at each login screen.

Security concerns accompany this capability. Experts have identified prompt-injection attacks on malicious websites as a vulnerability, and Google has stated that users bear responsibility for the risks. Browser-based agents face inherent reliability challenges, and agentic AI performing web actions introduces attack surfaces that did not exist in traditional browsing. Google claims to have built Gemini in Chrome with rigorous security standards, including new defenses announced in December 2025. The company has implemented safeguards preventing the AI from performing sensitive tasks like completing purchases or publishing social media content without explicit user approval.

The competitive context is significant. Google is retrofitting Chrome with AI features as it faces pressure from OpenAI and Anthropic, recognizing that browsers serve as primary entry points for user interactions with AI services. Auto Browse is currently available in preview for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the United States, suggesting a premium monetization strategy for agentic capabilities. This rollout signals that autonomous web agents are transitioning from experimental research to consumer products, with implications for how users interact with online services and how those services will need to adapt to AI-driven traffic patterns.

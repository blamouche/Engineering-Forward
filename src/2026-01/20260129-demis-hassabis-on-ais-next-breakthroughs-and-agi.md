# Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis on AI's Next Breakthroughs, What Counts As AGI, And Google's AI Glasses Bet

**Source**: https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/google-deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis-946

**Date**: January 29, 2026

**Author**: Alex Kantrowitz

**Keywords**: AI, AGI, Google DeepMind, Demis Hassabis, smart glasses, continual learning, memory, reasoning

## Elevator pitch

Demis Hassabis defines AGI as systems exhibiting all human cognitive capabilities including novel scientific discoveries and artistic breakthroughs, achievable within five to ten years but requiring solutions to three critical unsolved problems in continual learning, memory, and context windows.

## Takeaways

- Internal DeepMind teams never questioned continued AI advancement; scaling existing architectures remains effective with room for improvement across pre-training, post-training, and reasoning paradigms
- AGI should not be a marketing term but a scientific standard requiring systems that can generate novel scientific theories or artistic movements, not merely solve existing problems
- Three major unsolved problems block AGI: continual learning from experience, enhanced memory systems, and efficient context windows
- Google's smart glasses partnership with Warby Parker and Samsung targets summer 2026 prototypes, with a universal digital assistant as the killer application
- Ad-supported AI assistants face trust challenges because users need confidence the system prioritizes their interests over advertiser revenue

## Synthesis

Demis Hassabis, CEO of Google DeepMind, provided substantive commentary on the state of AI research during a live podcast recording at Davos. The conversation with Alex Kantrowitz covered technical challenges, definitional questions around AGI, and Google's product strategy in wearables.

On the question of continued progress, Hassabis was direct: internal teams never questioned whether advancement would continue. He emphasized that scaling existing architectures remains effective, with substantial room for improvement across pre-training methodologies, post-training techniques, and emerging reasoning paradigms. This confidence contrasts with external discourse that periodically questions whether AI progress has plateaued.

The AGI discussion centered on definitional rigor. Hassabis explicitly rejected treating AGI as a marketing term deployed for commercial advantage, instead maintaining a scientific standard. True AGI requires systems exhibiting all human cognitive capabilities, encompassing creative breakthroughs and physical intelligence. The timeline estimate of five to ten years comes with an important caveat: AGI must demonstrate the ability to generate novel scientific theories or create new artistic movements, not merely solve problems that humans have already framed. This distinction separates narrow task performance from genuine general intelligence.

Hassabis identified three critical technical gaps that require breakthrough innovations before AGI becomes achievable. First, continual learning: current systems cannot meaningfully learn from ongoing experience after initial training. Second, enhanced memory systems: LLMs lack robust mechanisms for retaining and retrieving information across extended interactions. Third, efficient context windows: current limitations constrain how much information models can effectively process. These represent genuine architectural challenges rather than incremental engineering problems.

Google's smart glasses initiative reflects Hassabis's view of where AI will deliver consumer value. The partnership with Warby Parker and Samsung targets summer 2026 for prototype devices. The identified killer application is a universal digital assistant providing hands-free assistance across contexts. Notably, Hassabis expressed caution about advertising-supported AI assistants. Trust between users and AI assistants requires confidence that the system prioritizes user interests over advertiser revenue. This tension between Google's core business model and optimal assistant design represents an unresolved strategic question for the company.

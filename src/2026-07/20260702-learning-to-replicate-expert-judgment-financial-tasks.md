# Learning to Replicate Expert Judgment in Financial Tasks

**Date**: 2026-07-02
**Author**: Thinking Machines
**Keywords**: financial AI, expert judgment, fine-tuning, proprietary datasets, frontier models, differentiated intelligence, custom models

## Elevator pitch
Frontier models struggle with relatively simple financial tasks, but custom models fine-tuned on high-quality proprietary datasets labeled by expert investors perform better and are substantially cheaper, signaling a future of differentiated intelligence.

## Takeaways
- Frontier models struggle with relatively simple financial tasks, revealing gaps in domain-specific reasoning
- Custom models fine-tuned on high-quality proprietary datasets labeled by expert investors perform better than frontier models on financial tasks
- Custom models are also substantially cheaper to operate than frontier models for domain-specific work
- Models of the future will likely feature "differentiated intelligence"—organizations will have custom models tuned to their specific needs that outperform frontier models in their domain
- The key bottleneck is access to high-quality, expert-labeled proprietary data, not model architecture

## Synthesis
Thinking Machines' research on replicating expert judgment in financial tasks reveals an important shift in how organizations should think about AI model deployment. The study finds that frontier models—despite their broad capabilities—struggle with relatively simple financial tasks, exposing the limits of general-purpose intelligence when applied to specialized domains.

The solution is not larger frontier models but custom models fine-tuned on high-quality proprietary datasets. By having expert investors label training data, Thinking Machines was able to produce models that not only outperform frontier models on financial tasks but are also substantially cheaper to operate. This economic advantage is critical: the cost per inference for a fine-tuned specialized model can be orders of magnitude lower than running a frontier model, while producing better results in the target domain.

The most significant strategic insight is the concept of "differentiated intelligence." Rather than every organization using the same frontier model, the future involves organizations having custom models tuned to their specific needs—models that outperform frontier models in their domain because they have been trained on domain-specific data that the frontier models have never seen. This creates a new form of competitive moat: proprietary training data, not model architecture, becomes the key differentiator.

For engineering teams, this research suggests that the most impactful AI investments may not be in accessing the most powerful frontier model but in building the data pipelines and labeling infrastructure needed to create custom models. The bottleneck is not compute or model architecture but access to high-quality, expert-labeled proprietary data. Organizations that can systematically capture expert knowledge into training datasets will build models that outperform those relying on general-purpose frontier models.

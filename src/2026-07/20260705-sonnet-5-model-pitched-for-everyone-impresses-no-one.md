# Sonnet 5: A Model Pitched for Everyone Impresses No One
**Source**: https://every.to/vibe-check/sonnet-5
**Date**: 2026-07-02
**Author**: Katie Parrott
**Keywords**: Anthropic, Sonnet 5, Claude, LLM evaluation, AI models, agentic, Fable 5, GPT-5.5, Opus 4.8

## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's Sonnet 5 is capable but lacks a clear use-case advantage—it can draft, code, and analyze competently, but every task has a cheaper, faster, or smarter alternative.

## Takeaways
- Sonnet 5 was pitched as Anthropic's "just right" model—smarter than Haiku, cheaper and faster than Opus—but in practice, there's always a better option for any specific task
- In coding tests, Sonnet 5 got stuck on agentic builds that stronger models (Opus 4.8, Fable 5) handled better; in writing tests, it produced usable promo copy but editorial instincts went sideways
- The "Goldilocks problem" is real: for knowledge work, Sonnet 5's price point doesn't justify choosing it over Haiku for simple tasks, and for complex work, Opus 4.8 or Fable 5 are worth the extra cost
- Every's Reach Test—a standardized evaluation across coding, writing, knowledge work, and agent behavior—showed Sonnet 5 performing adequately but never distinguishing itself
- The model landscape is fragmenting: rather than converging on one general-purpose model, teams are assembling portfolios of specialized tools, each optimized for a particular type of work

## Synthesis
Katie Parrott's evaluation of Sonnet 5 crystallizes a growing tension in the frontier model market. Anthropic has historically positioned Sonnet as the Goldilocks model—smart enough for real work, affordable enough for daily use. But the release of Sonnet 5 into a field that now includes GPT-5.5, Opus 4.8, and Fable 5 exposes the weakness of this positioning: being adequate at everything is not enough when specialized alternatives exist for every category of work.

The detailed testing across coding, writing, knowledge work, and agent behavior tells a consistent story. In coding, Sonnet 5 got stuck in loops on agentic builds—exactly the kind of task where you'd want a model to shine. In writing, it produced functional copy but lacked the editorial judgment that makes output genuinely useful. In side-by-side email draft comparisons against Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5 held its own on simple tasks but fell behind on nuance and tone.

The broader implication is about where the model market is heading. The Every team's testing reveals that their own usage has shifted toward a portfolio approach: different models for different tasks, with intelligent routing (as covered in the Pragmatic Engineer's concurrent article on smart model routing) becoming the glue. Sonnet 5 may find its niche as a cost-effective middle layer in such routing systems, but as a standalone recommendation, it struggles to justify its existence against cheaper options for simple work and smarter options for complex work.

The most telling detail is the comparison with Fable 5, which returned online the same week and immediately demonstrated agentic capability by rebuilding Every's document editor Proof in about three hours. When the same team that found Sonnet 5 underwhelming can't stop talking about Codex and Fable 5, the competitive picture is clear: Anthropic's own portfolio is cannibalizing Sonnet's reason for being.

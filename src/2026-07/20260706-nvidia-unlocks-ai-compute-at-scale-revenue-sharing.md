# NVIDIA Unlocks AI Compute at Scale with Revenue-Sharing Partnership Model
**Source**: https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-unlocks-ai-compute-at-scale-capital-partners-to-power-ai-infrastructure-buildout/
**Date**: 2026-07-06
**Author**: NVIDIA
**Keywords**: NVIDIA, AI compute, AI factories, cloud providers, revenue sharing, Grace Blackwell, GPU infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
NVIDIA introduced a new business model where AI cloud providers procure NVIDIA infrastructure through revenue-sharing and credit-support agreements, earning NVIDIA both product revenue and a share of cloud revenue on supported capacity — essentially turning GPU supply into a recurring revenue stream.

## Takeaways
- NVIDIA is launching a new partnership model where AI cloud companies sell NVIDIA-powered cloud services and NVIDIA earns both standard product revenue and a share of the cloud revenue on the supported capacity.
- Sharon AI is deploying up to 40,000 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GB300 GPUs; Firmus is building a 360-megawatt DSX AI factory campus in Batam, Indonesia, with up to 170,000 NVIDIA GPUs.
- The model targets AI-native companies (Baseten, Fireworks AI, Together AI) that need immediate, large-scale compute access without the traditional 2-3 year site selection and construction timeline.
- This represents a shift from NVIDIA selling GPUs as hardware to selling them as a revenue-participating infrastructure asset — NVIDIA now earns both upfront and usage-linked income.
- NVIDIA plans to raise at least $20 billion in debt for general corporate purposes, signaling the scale of capital required to fund this infrastructure expansion.

## Synthesis
NVIDIA's new partnership model is the most significant evolution in GPU infrastructure economics since the cloud itself. By shifting from pure hardware sales to revenue-sharing arrangements, NVIDIA is effectively securitizing GPU capacity — turning compute into a financial product that generates recurring income proportional to actual usage.

The mechanics are straightforward but the implications are profound. AI cloud providers like Sharon AI and Firmus get access to Grace Blackwell GPU capacity without the traditional multi-year procurement and construction cycle. In exchange, NVIDIA gets a share of the cloud revenue generated on that capacity. This aligns incentives: NVIDIA profits when customers actually use the compute, not just when they buy it. It also gives NVIDIA a hedge against the GPU demand cycle — if demand shifts from training to inference, from large models to small ones, from frontier labs to enterprises, NVIDIA still captures value.

The timing reflects a structural change in AI compute demand. As the industry shifts from model development to production inference, the need is for continuously operating "AI factories" that generate tokens at scale. Traditional cloud procurement, with its long lead times and capacity planning, can't keep up with demand that grows 10x year-over-year. NVIDIA's model essentially provides supply-chain financing for compute, backed by the expected revenue from AI services.

The risk is concentration. If NVIDIA controls not just the hardware but also the financial terms under which that hardware is deployed, it becomes the central node in AI infrastructure. Competitors like AMD and the custom silicon efforts from Google, Amazon, and Microsoft are trying to break this concentration, but NVIDIA's software ecosystem (CUDA, NIM, NeMo) and now its financial engineering create a formidable moat. The $20 billion debt raise signals that NVIDIA views this not as an experiment but as the next phase of its business model.

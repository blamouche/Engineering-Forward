## Elevator pitch
Anthropic's Head of Claude Code Boris Cherny argues that traditional job titles are "melting" into five new archetypes—Prototyper, Builder, Sweeper, Grower, Maintainer—that cut across every function and shift over the life of a product in an AI-native world.

## Takeaways
- Boris Cherny, Head of Claude Code at Anthropic, has not personally written a line of code in more than six months
- Traditional job titles (engineer, PM, designer, analyst) are becoming lagging indicators of how work actually gets done in an AI-native world

## Synthesis
Linas Beliūnas unpacks a framework from Boris Cherny, the Head of Claude Code at Anthropic, that challenges the traditional way people think about their careers in the age of AI. Cherny's central claim is that job titles like "engineer," "PM," "designer," or "analyst" are becoming lagging indicators—descriptions of how work used to be organized rather than how it actually gets done now.

The five archetypes Cherny proposes are not job titles but functional roles that anyone can inhabit regardless of their formal position. The Prototyper focuses on rapid exploration and validation, using AI to quickly test ideas before committing resources. The Builder handles production implementation, turning validated prototypes into working systems. The Sweeper handles cleanup, verification, and quality assurance—increasingly important as AI-generated code requires human review. The Grower focuses on scaling and optimization, using data and feedback to improve systems. The Maintainer handles ongoing operations and reliability.

What makes this framework different from traditional job descriptions is its fluidity. The same person may be a Prototyper on Monday, a Builder on Tuesday, and a Sweeper on Wednesday, depending on what the product needs at that moment. This fluidity is enabled by AI tools like Claude Code, which lower the barrier to switching between roles by providing capabilities that previously required specialist knowledge.

The article connects Cherny's framework to broader trends: Klarna's publicly stated AI-driven workforce reductions, Shopify's internal memos shifting toward AI-first workflows, Meta's reorganization around AI, and Anthropic's own dogfooding of Claude Code. The data points suggest that companies adopting AI-native workflows are already seeing productivity shifts that make the five-role framework more descriptive of reality than traditional org charts.

The practical implication for individuals is clear: rather than optimizing for a single career track, the most resilient strategy is to develop competency across all five archetypes, using AI to fill gaps in areas where you lack specialist training. For managers, the framework suggests organizing teams around product phases rather than functional silos. For investors, it signals that companies that can fluidly reassign human capital across roles will outperform those locked into traditional org structures.

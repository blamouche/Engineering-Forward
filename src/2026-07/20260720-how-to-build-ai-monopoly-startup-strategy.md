# How to Build an AI Monopoly: The New Rules of Startup Strategy
**Source**: https://linas.substack.com/p/how-to-build-an-ai-monopoly
**Date**: 2026-07-20
**Author**: Linas Beliūnas
**Keywords**: AI monopoly, startup strategy, value capture, moats, Peter Thiel, AI startups, commoditization

## Elevator pitch
In an era where anyone can build an impressive AI product in a weekend, the real challenge isn't creating value—it's capturing it; this analysis translates Peter Thiel's monopoly framework to the AI age, showing why most AI startups will fail and how the few that succeed build compounding moats.

## Takeaways
- U.S. private AI investment hit $285.9 billion in 2025 with 1,953 newly funded AI startups, yet the cost of intelligence is collapsing—Opus-class models now sell for a third of their 2025 price.
- The defining trap of the AI age: technology massively increases the value being created while competition relentlessly drives value captured toward zero.
- Peter Thiel's "Competition Is for Losers" framework—value creation vs. value capture—is the most important strategy text for AI founders, and most are violating its every principle.
- Open-weight models like GLM-5.2 trail the closed frontier by roughly four months, meaning anyone can now build an impressive AI product—but an impressive AI product is worth almost nothing.
- The new moats in AI are context, memory, and evals—not the model itself; founders must answer whether their business is a value-creating commodity or a value-capturing monopoly.

## Synthesis
Linas Beliūnas delivers a comprehensive strategic framework that should be required reading for anyone building or funding AI companies. The core argument is deceptively simple: the AI age has created a massive gap between value creation and value capture, and most founders are building on the wrong side of that gap.

The data is stark. U.S. private AI investment hit $285.9 billion in 2025, with enterprise generative AI spending tripling to $37 billion. Yet the cost of matching any fixed level of model performance is falling by up to two orders of magnitude a year. Open-weight models trail the closed frontier by just four months. The implication is clear: building a better model or a fine-tuned wrapper in a crowded category adds up to a company worth approximately nothing.

Belūnas translates Peter Thiel's monopoly framework to the AI era with precision. The key distinction is between value creation (making something useful) and value capture (keeping the profit). AI makes it trivially easy to create value—any founder can spin up a demo with frontier APIs. But the commoditization of intelligence means that value is instantly competed away. The companies that will matter are those that build compounding moats: proprietary data loops, context accumulation, workflow lock-in, and evaluation infrastructure that gets better with use.

The practical tools are valuable: a monopoly scorecard, a "kill the idea" checklist, seven questions every AI founder must answer, and a 90-day operating plan. But the deeper insight is psychological—smart founders are drawn to competitive, well-defined markets precisely because those markets have been validated, which is exactly where AI makes it easiest to enter and hardest to differentiate.

The synthesis with current market dynamics is revealing. As model prices collapse (covered in our analysis of the price war facing frontier labs), the value shifts decisively to the application layer—but not to any application. Only those with genuine moats—proprietary data, network effects, workflow integration—will capture durable value. The rest will be outcompeted by margin pressure from below and feature parity from above.

# How GPT-5.6 Changes Knowledge Work
**Source**: https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work
**Date**: 2026-07-10
**Author**: Dan Shipper
**Keywords**: GPT-5.6, Sol, knowledge work, agentic loops, ChatGPT Work, Tend, Codex

## Elevator pitch
GPT-5.6 Sol is the first model that can reliably run whole loops of knowledge work autonomously, and Every's open-source Tend framework turns this capability into a practical system for tending rather than doing your work.

## Takeaways
- GPT-5.6 Sol crosses the threshold where continuous knowledge-work loops become practical: it can scan sources, identify relevance, carry out approved work, and build custom tools as needed
- The shift is from doing individual tasks to tending a system that does the work — from doing email one at a time to having an agent triage, research, draft replies, and compound your preferences
- ChatGPT Work (formerly Codex) integrates Sol with in-app browser, computer use, and Chronicle (periodic screenshots that learn how you work)
- The open-source Tend framework (prompt + repo) lets you build work loops in ChatGPT Work: connect Gmail, Slack, Cora, and other sources to run the "gather → decide → act → learn" cycle
- Knowledge work follows a three-step loop: gather/make sense of information, make decisions and take action, learn from results — and GPT-5.6 makes the model a capable participant at every stage

## Synthesis
Dan Shipper argues that GPT-5.6 Sol represents a genuine inflection point for knowledge work: it's fast enough, cheap enough, and reliable enough that the bottleneck has moved from "can the model do the work?" to "can you build the system that directs the work?" The article introduces "tending your loop" as the new paradigm for knowledge workers — not doing each task yourself, but setting up and supervising an autonomous agent that handles the routine middle of your workflow.

The email describes practical examples: GPT-5.6 in ChatGPT Work watching your inbox, deciding what deserves attention, doing research, and presenting each email with a summary and proposed reply. At the end of each sweep, the agent derives your preferences from your revisions and remembers them. This mirrors how managers already work: delegating, reviewing, and adjusting rather than executing every task personally.

Every released Tend, an open-source prompt and repository that lets you set up these loops for any knowledge work — from email to hiring to customer research. The framework connects to Cora, Gmail, Slack, and other information sources, and is designed for experimentation rather than production stability. The article positions this as the natural evolution of the "compound engineering" philosophy applied to knowledge work: creating the conditions for work to happen, rather than doing it yourself. The model supplies intelligence; the OS (or prompt) supplies honesty and structure. This is the clearest articulation yet of how the human role shifts from doer to tender in an agentic workflow.

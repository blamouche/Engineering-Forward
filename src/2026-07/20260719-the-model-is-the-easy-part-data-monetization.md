# The Model Is the Easy Part
**Source**: https://every.to/p/the-model-is-the-easy-part
**Date**: 2026-07-19
**Author**: Every Staff (Alex Duffy)
**Keywords**: AI commoditization, data monetization, model evaluation, enterprise AI, Good Start Labs, frontier models

## Elevator pitch
As AI models commoditize, the real challenge shifts from building a model to defining what "good" means for your business—and the companies that can measure and monetize their domain data will capture disproportionate value in the next phase of AI adoption.

## Takeaways
- Arkadium's Game Lab benchmark revealed that frontier models lose 90% of Gin Rummy games against casual players—models are shaped by what they've seen, and popular AI models may not have experience with whatever you work on.
- An expert model with 4.6 million parameters in an 18MB file beat human players 90% of the time at 1M requests/day for about $60/year—versus multi-millions for a frontier LLM approach.
- Reddit, Shutterstock, and News Corp have already turned their data into hundreds of millions in recurring revenue by selling to frontier labs.
- Anthropic asked big pharma for their data, and nearly everyone said no—companies whose data is their product must protect their IP from labs that might become competitors.
- Defining and measuring "good" is emerging as the next stage of AI adoption; companies that can set clear goals, build benchmarks, and measure progress will be the ones that capture value.

## Synthesis
Every's analysis, centered on Good Start Labs' work with Arkadium, makes a compelling case that the AI industry is entering its measurement phase. The premise is simple but powerful: getting AI tools into your team's hands was phase one; defining business-specific, measurable goals is phase two, and it's where the real value lies.

The Arkadium case study is instructive. When frontier models were benchmarked against simple games like Gin Rummy, they lost 90% of the time against casual players. This isn't a footnote—it's a structural insight. Models are shaped by their training data, and for most specialized domains, that training data is thin or nonexistent. An expert model with just 4.6 million parameters (compared to hundreds of billions for frontier models) dramatically outperformed GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 on the domain-specific task, at a fraction of the cost.

This has three implications. First, the frontier model isn't always the right tool. For well-defined, domain-specific tasks, a small expert model can be both more capable and dramatically cheaper. Second, the real moat is in the data and the evaluation framework, not the model. Arkadium's "good game" goal paid twice: better player experiences and a new revenue line selling anonymized data to frontier labs for millions. Third, the data market is already real—Reddit, Shutterstock, and News Corp have generated hundreds of millions in recurring revenue from lab partnerships.

The cautionary note is critical. Anthropic's chief product officer Mike Krieger left Figma's board days before Anthropic shipped a competing design tool; Cursor built on Claude while Claude Code was described as a "research effort" before becoming a direct competitor. If the lab you'd sell data to is entering your business, retaining a data edge is essential. The data market's long-term defensibility remains uncertain, but the near-term opportunity is clear: companies that can define "good," measure it, and structure their data accordingly will be positioned to capture value in both directions—better AI for themselves and monetizable data assets for the market.

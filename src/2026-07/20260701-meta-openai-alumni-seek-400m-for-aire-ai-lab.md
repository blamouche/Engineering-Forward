# Exclusive: Meta and OpenAI Alumni Seek $400m for New AI Lab Aire
**Source**: https://sifted.eu/articles/aire-openai-meta-aire-fundraise
**Date**: 2026-06-30
**Author**: Daphné Leprince-Ringuet
**Keywords**: aire, meta, openai, fundraise, ai-lab, startup, $400m

## Elevator pitch
A trio of Meta and OpenAI alumni are seeking $400 million for a new AI startup dubbed Aire, based between the US, France, and Israel, reflecting the ongoing talent exodus from frontier AI labs.

## Takeaways
- Three former employees of Meta and OpenAI are seeking $400m for a new AI startup called Aire.
- The company will operate across three countries: the US, France, and Israel.
- The fundraise reflects the continuing trend of top AI talent leaving frontier labs to start their own ventures.
- The multi-country structure (US/France/Israel) suggests Aire may be positioning itself to navigate different regulatory environments and access diverse talent pools.
- This comes amid a broader wave of AI lab spinouts, with ex-founders and researchers from major labs raising hundreds of millions to build competing organizations.

## Synthesis
The emergence of Aire — a stealth-mode AI startup founded by Meta and OpenAI alumni seeking $400m — is another signal of the dramatic talent reshuffling happening across the AI industry. The most capable people in frontier AI research are increasingly choosing to build their own organizations rather than continue within the large labs that trained them.

The $400m target puts Aire in the upper echelon of AI startup fundraises, suggesting the founders are planning to compete on frontier model development rather than building on top of existing models. The tri-country structure across the US, France, and Israel is notable: it provides access to the American market and capital, French AI talent (France has been aggressively building its AI ecosystem), and Israeli technical expertise (particularly from Unit 8200 veterans who are well-represented in AI startups).

This trend is accelerating across the industry. Meta has seen multiple high-profile departures to startups. OpenAI continues to lose researchers to ventures of their own. The pattern suggests that while large labs offer compute and data advantages, the most ambitious researchers are finding that the combination of abundant venture capital and model architecture expertise is sufficient to build competitive alternatives. The question is whether the resulting fragmentation of AI talent leads to faster progress through competition or slower progress through duplicated effort.

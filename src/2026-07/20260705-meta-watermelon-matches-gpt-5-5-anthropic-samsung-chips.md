# Meta Watermelon Matches GPT-5.5 Benchmarks; Anthropic Explores Samsung Chip Partnership
**Source**: https://tldr.ai/ (TLDR AI newsletter, 2026-07-03)
**Date**: 2026-07-03
**Author**: TLDR AI / Dan
**Keywords**: Meta, Watermelon, GPT-5.5, benchmarks, Anthropic, Samsung, chips, AI models, compute

## Elevator pitch
Meta's upcoming "Watermelon" model reportedly matches GPT-5.5 on key benchmarks, while Anthropic explores a Samsung chip partnership to diversify its compute stack beyond Google, Amazon, and Nvidia.

## Takeaways
- Meta's superintelligence chief Alexandr Wang claims the upcoming "Watermelon" model has caught up with OpenAI's GPT-5.5 on closely followed AI benchmarks, though it's still in training
- Watermelon reportedly uses an order of magnitude more compute than Muse Spark, Meta's previous flagship model, signaling an aggressive compute scaling strategy
- Anthropic is exploring a custom AI chip collaboration with Samsung, looking to diversify its compute stack beyond existing partnerships with Google, Amazon, and Nvidia
- Anthropic stated that chips from Google, Amazon, and Nvidia remain central to its hardware strategy—suggesting the Samsung partnership would be additive rather than a replacement
- Meta has not given a timeline for Watermelon's release, keeping the competitive pressure on without committing to a specific date

## Synthesis
Two stories from the frontier model race highlight how compute and chip strategy are becoming as important as model architecture. Meta's Watermelon represents a direct challenge to OpenAI's perceived lead: if a Meta model can match GPT-5.5 on benchmarks, the narrative that OpenAI holds a comfortable frontier advantage begins to erode. The reported order-of-magnitude compute increase over Muse Spark aligns with the broader scaling hypothesis—more compute still yields better models, and Meta has the resources to keep spending.

Anthropic's Samsung exploration is strategically significant for a different reason. Frontier model companies have been building increasingly tight partnerships with cloud providers (Google for Anthropic, Microsoft for OpenAI) that include chip access as part of the deal. By exploring Samsung as an additional chip partner, Anthropic is signaling that it wants to reduce dependency on any single compute provider. This is both a practical hedge against supply constraints and a negotiating lever with existing partners.

The combination of these stories points to a maturing competitive landscape. Meta's entry into the frontier tier with Watermelon adds another well-funded competitor to a race that already includes OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and emerging players. Anthropic's chip diversification reflects the same dynamic from the supply side: as demand for AI compute continues to outstrip supply, companies that can secure multiple chip sources will have a material advantage.

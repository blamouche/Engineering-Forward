# OpenAI in Early Talks to Give 5% Stake to US Government
**Source**: https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jul/02/openai-stake-us-government-ai-sam-altman
**Date**: 2026-07-02
**Author**: The Guardian
**Keywords**: OpenAI, US government, equity stake, sovereign wealth fund, Sam Altman, AI regulation, public ownership

## Elevator pitch
OpenAI is reportedly in early-stage talks to give a 5% equity stake to the US government, proposing that other major AI companies do the same, as the industry seeks to smooth relations with the Trump administration and share AI-generated wealth with the public.

## Takeaways
- OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has proposed giving a 5% equity stake in the company to the US government, with the idea that other major AI companies (Anthropic, Google, Meta) would give similar stakes.
- The proposal envisions a public wealth fund modeled on Alaska's Permanent Fund, which invests oil wealth and pays dividends to citizens — applying the same logic to AI-generated wealth.
- Talks are described as "conceptual" and in early stages; any deal would likely require an act of Congress to implement.
- The proposal comes amid growing government pressure on AI companies, including Anthropic's recent model suspension under export controls.
- Both OpenAI and Anthropic have previously suggested in policy papers that public or sovereign wealth funds may be needed to distribute AI wealth; both are reportedly preparing IPOs that could value them at over $1 trillion.

## Synthesis
OpenAI's proposal to give the US government a 5% stake is simultaneously a pragmatic lobbying move and a genuinely novel governance idea. On the surface, it's a way to preempt regulation by offering a seat at the table — if the government owns a piece of OpenAI, it has a direct financial incentive to see the company succeed. But the deeper implication is that AI companies are acknowledging what many critics have argued: the wealth generated by frontier AI will not be distributed equitably through market forces alone.

The Alaska Permanent Fund model is instructive. Alaska pays every resident an annual dividend from oil revenues, and the same principle could apply to AI — except the scale is potentially much larger. If OpenAI and its peers reach trillion-dollar valuations, a 5% stake held in a public fund could generate billions in annual dividends. The question is whether Congress would create such a fund, and whether the structure would actually benefit citizens or simply create a new channel for government patronage.

The timing is no accident. AI companies face a dual threat: regulatory risk from an administration that has shown willingness to use export controls and national security claims to restrict model access, and public backlash over AI displacement of jobs. Offering equity stake addresses both: it gives the government skin in the game (reducing regulatory risk) and provides a mechanism for wealth sharing (reducing political risk). It's a shrewd move that reframes the government-AI relationship from adversarial oversight to shared ownership.

The practical challenges are enormous. Would Google, which is already publicly traded, give the government 5% of its AI operations? Would Anthropic, which just raised at a $60 billion valuation, dilute its cap table? And would Congress, even in a pro-business administration, create the legal framework for what amounts to partial nationalization of the most valuable technology companies in history? The talks are early, but the fact that they're happening at all signals how quickly the AI-government relationship is evolving from suspicion to entanglement.
